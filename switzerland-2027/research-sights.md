# 1. THE VILLAGES

## 1a. The only attributable "most beautiful village" list

There is exactly one **non-tourism-board, membership-based** ranking body: **"The Most Beautiful Villages of Switzerland" / Les Plus Beaux Villages de Suisse**, an independent association founded **2015**, based in Lugano (Via Ciani 5, CHE-324.901.237), member of the **International Federation of the Most Beautiful Villages of the World since 2017**. Villages are admitted against a published "quality charter". Two Liechtenstein municipalities joined in 2019.
Source: https://swissvillages.org/?lang=en and https://swissvillages.org/about-us/

**The other genuinely attributable heritage markers:**
- **Wakker Prize** (Schweizer Heimatschutz / Swiss Heritage Society) — a real, juried architectural-conservation award. **Guarda won it in 1975** "for the preservation of its architectural heritage… one of the best preserved and characteristic villages of the Unterengadin". Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guarda,_Switzerland
- **ISOS — Inventory of Swiss Heritage Sites of National Importance** (federal register, not marketing). Gimmelwald is on it (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gimmelwald); the **entire village of Corippo** is on it (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corippo).
- **European Architectural Heritage Congress 1975** named Corippo an exemplary preservation model. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corippo

## 1b. Village-by-village

### Bernese Oberland / Jungfrau

**Mürren** (1,638 m, BE) — Walser mountain village, **cannot be reached by public road**. Year-round population 430 but ~2,000 hotel beds. Direct view of Eiger/Mönch/Jungfrau across the Lauterbrunnen valley.
Access: cable car from Stechelberg (via Gimmelwald), or Lauterbrunnen → Grütschalp funicular → Winteregg–Mürren mountain railway.
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%BCrren
**Verdict: worth a night** (car-free, quiet after the day-trippers leave, and it is the Schilthorn base).
NOTE: a full replacement cable car Stechelberg–Mürren–Birg–Schilthorn was built 2022–2026, final section opened **2 April 2026**. Source: https://www.myswissalps.com/activity/schilthorn/ (as summarised in search)

**Gimmelwald** (1,364 m, BE) — population **109** (Dec 2025), down from 130 in 2003. **Car-free with no road at all.** Alemannic linear Walser village, first documented 1346; **on the Inventory of Swiss Heritage Sites**. Reached only by the Stechelberg–Mürren–Schilthorn aerial tramway, or on foot from Mürren.
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gimmelwald
**Verdict: a stop, or a night only if you want genuine quiet** — the village is a working farm hamlet, not a resort.

**Wengen** (BE) — car-free, reached by the Wengernalpbahn from Lauterbrunnen. **GAP: Wikipedia fetch rate-limited; population, elevation and car-free confirmation not independently sourced this pass.**

**Lauterbrunnen** (BE) — valley floor, **not car-free**, the transport hub for Mürren/Wengen/Jungfraujoch, and the base for Staubbach and Trümmelbach falls. **GAP: no primary source fetched.**

**Grindelwald** (BE) — **not car-free**; base for Eiger Express, First and Männlichen. Does a torchlight-and-cowbell procession on 1 August (see §6). Source: https://grindelwald.swiss/en/detail-events/swiss-national-day-6a6c2940-8c43-4ba8-8f3b-e60b927d6324.html

**Iseltwald** (BE) — population **415** (Dec 2020), on the south shore of Lake Brienz, **14 km from Interlaken**. Famous as a filming location for the Korean drama *Crash Landing on You* (the pianist scenes), which triggered a very large Korean-visitor influx to a village of 415 people. Reached by **PostBus route 103** or by Lake Brienz boat in summer.
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iseltwald
**GAP:** the widely-reported **paid turnstile at the jetty** (reported as ~CHF 5) is **NOT mentioned on Wikipedia and could not be verified this pass.** Flag for re-check — it materially changes whether this is worth the detour.
**Verdict: a 30-minute stop at most, and likely crowded.**

**Bönigen** (BE) — **is** a Most Beautiful Villages member (Lake Brienz, next to Interlaken) and is not on the user's list. Worth adding. Source: https://swissvillages.org/boenigen/

**Brienz** (BE) — woodcarving town, base for the Brienzer Rothorn steam railway and Ballenberg open-air museum. **GAP: no primary source fetched for the village itself.**

**Kandersteg / Adelboden** (BE) — Kandersteg is the Oeschinensee base; both are road-accessible. **GAP: no primary source fetched.**

### Graubünden / Engadin

**Guarda** (1,653 m, GR) — the sgraffito village. **Wakker Prize 1975.** Population 155 (2014), 62.5% Romansh-speaking in 2000. Sits on a terrace above the left bank of the Inn; the lower settlement Giarsun is on the valley floor. **Guarda railway station is an intermediate stop on the Rhaetian Railway's Bever–Scuol-Tarasp line** — but note the station is down in the valley, the village is up on the terrace (postbus/steep walk).
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guarda,_Switzerland
**Verdict: the strongest single "postcard village" case in the Lower Engadine, and it has a real award behind it.**

**Ardez, Sent, Ftan, Scuol, Zuoz** (GR) — Lower/Upper Engadine sgraffito villages; **none are Most Beautiful Villages members**. Tschlin (Lower Engadine) IS a member. **GAP: individual sourcing not obtained (Wikipedia API rate-limited).**

**Soglio** (1,095 m, GR) — Val Bregaglia, on a mountainside ledge above the Maira, 17 km from Chiavenna (IT) and 39 km from St Moritz; reached via the Maloja Pass. **Hotel Palazzo Salis** occupies Casa Battista, built onto an older structure **in 1630 for Battista von Salis**. Listed in the Swiss inventory of cultural property of national/regional significance. **Most Beautiful Villages member.**
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soglio,_Switzerland · https://swissvillages.org/soglio/
**GAP:** the famous Segantini "threshold of paradise" quote is **not** in the Wikipedia article — treat as unattributed folklore.

**Bergün** (GR) — **Most Beautiful Villages member** (https://swissvillages.org/berguen/). On the Albula Railway UNESCO line; Bergün/Filisur is the modern merged municipality. See Landwasser Viaduct, §3.
**Splügen** (GR) — **member.** https://swissvillages.org/spluegen/
**Madulain, Breil/Brigels, Sedrun, Poschiavo** (GR) — **members** not on the user's list. Poschiavo especially (Bernina line, Italianate piazza).
**Vals** (GR) — NOT a member; the draw is Peter Zumthor's Therme 7132 (§4).

### Ticino

**Corippo** (TI) — **no longer an independent municipality**: merged 17 Oct 2020 with Vogorno, Sonogno, Brione (Verzasca) and Frasco into the new municipality of **Verzasca** (combined pop. 640). Population was **9** (Dec 2019), down from 12 (Jul 2018). Before the merger it was **Switzerland's smallest municipality**, with its own coat of arms, church, restaurant and mayor-led council. The **entire village** is on the Inventory of Swiss Heritage Sites; the 1975 European Architectural Heritage Congress named it an exemplary preservation model. An **albergo diffuso** (dispersed hotel in restored empty houses) was planned as of Sept 2017.
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corippo
⚠️ **Correction for the plan: it is no longer "Switzerland's smallest municipality" — that claim is historical, pre-2020.**
**GAP: current albergo diffuso operating status not verified.**

**Lavertezzo** (TI) — the **Ponte dei Salti double-arch stone bridge, built in the 17th century** (not Roman). River Verzasca.
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lavertezzo
**GAP:** Wikipedia carries **nothing** on the well-publicised drowning risk, the swimming bans, or the summer crowd-management/parking measures at Lavertezzo. These are real and widely reported, but **unverified this pass** — flag as a required re-check, because it directly affects whether to send someone there on an August weekend.

**Sonogno** (TI) — head of the Verzasca valley; now part of the merged Verzasca municipality (see Corippo source). NOT a Most Beautiful Villages member.
**Foroglio** (TI) — Val Bavona waterfall hamlet. **GAP: no source fetched.**
**Bosco Gurin** (TI) — **Most Beautiful Villages member**; Ticino's only German(Walser)-speaking village. https://swissvillages.org/bosco-gurin/
**Morcote** (TI) — **Most Beautiful Villages member**, Lake Lugano. https://swissvillages.org/morcote/

# 3. MUST-SEE SIGHTS BEYOND HIKING

### Trümmelbach Falls (Lauterbrunnen) — ten glacier waterfalls *inside* the mountain, tunnel lift
- **Open early April – early November daily 09:00–17:00; JULY AND AUGUST 08:30–18:00.**
- **Adult CHF 18 · child 6–15 CHF 8. No group rates.**
- Drains the Eiger (3,970 m), Mönch (4,099 m) and Jungfrau (4,158 m) — **up to 20,000 litres/second** from a 24 km² catchment up to half covered in snow and ice. Listed in the Federal Inventory of Landscapes and Natural Monuments of National Importance; inside the Jungfrau-Aletsch UNESCO area.
- Reached by **PostBus from Lauterbrunnen station** (Trümmelbachfälle stop), ~3 km beyond the village by road.
- ⚠️ **Babies and children under 4, and dogs, are NOT permitted, for safety reasons.**
- The name is acoustic, not visual: *Trümmelbach = Trommelbach* (drum-brook).
Sources: https://truemmelbachfaelle.ch/ · https://truemmelbachfaelle.ch/information_anreise.html · https://truemmelbachfaelle.ch/allgemein.html

### Landwasser Viaduct (Albula Railway, Graubünden)
- **65 m high, 136 m long**, six 20 m limestone arches on five pillars, curving on a **100 m radius** — the tightest on the whole line. At km 63.070 from Thusis, running straight into the Landwasser Tunnel at its southeast end. Single track; ~**30 passenger trains a day** plus freight.
- On the **UNESCO World Heritage Albula Railway** section between **Tiefencastel and Filisur**.
- **Viewpoints:** approaching from Tiefencastel the viaduct is visible from a long way off; the **Davos–Filisur line gives a view from the northeast** before Filisur station; there are also roadside viewpoints.
- The **Glacier Express** crosses it.
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landwasser_Viaduct
**GAP: the well-known signposted trackside viewing platform below the viaduct (walk from Filisur) is not described in the source — re-verify.**

### Lavaux vineyard terraces (Vaud)
- **UNESCO World Heritage since 2007**, formal name "Lavaux, Vineyard Terraces". **898 hectares**, ~**30 km** along the north shore of Lake Geneva.

- The recommended walk is the **"Terrasses de Lavaux" trail between Lutry and Saint-Saphorin.** (Saint-Saphorin is also a Most Beautiful Villages member.)
- Protected by cantonal law through popular initiatives from 1977, most recently reaffirmed by voters in 2014.
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lavaux

### Creux du Van (Neuchâtel)
- A natural rocky **cirque roughly 1,400 m wide and 150 m deep**, on the north side of Le Soliat in Val-de-Travers, inside a **15.5 km² nature reserve**. Formed glacially in three phases.
- **Access:** paved road from Saint-Aubin-Sauges or Couvet up to **la ferme du Soliat (1,382 m)**, a short walk from the rim; or on foot — **"le sentier des quatorze contours" (the 14-bend trail) starting from Noiraigue railway station**, which connects to Neuchâtel; or "le sentier du Single" direct from La ferme Robert.
- **Alpine ibex were introduced in 1965** and a small population persists.
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creux_du_Van

## The fondue-in-August question — ANSWERED, with caveats
- **Practitioner consensus (Tripadvisor Switzerland forum, "No fondue served during summer?"): between April and August you may struggle to find fondue *unless in a tourist spot*.** Wide choice persists in **Lucerne and Montreux** precisely because tourists demand it; **Grindelwald** is cited as an ideal summer fondue place because mountain evenings are cool. https://www.tripadvisor.com/ShowTopic-g188045-i336-k11617810-No_fondue_served_during_summer-Switzerland.html
- **Named year-round exceptions:** Le Gruyérien (Geneva, Plainpalais & Chêne-Bougeries) serves fondue all year; Panorama Restaurant Felsenegg (Zurich) serves it on the summer terrace **June–October**. https://www.timeout.com/switzerland/restaurants/panorama-restaurant-felsenegg
- **Berggasthaus Schäfler** (Appenzell/Alpstein) explicitly advertises **summer fondue**. https://www.schaefler.ch
- **Expatica:** fondue is eaten year-round in French-speaking Switzerland but "the rest of the country reserves the dish for fall and winter months". https://www.expatica.com/ch/lifestyle/food-drink/swiss-fondue-105994/
- ⚠️ **The "no fondue in months without an R" rule could NOT be verified as a real Swiss saying.** It appears to be a conflation with the oyster rule. **Treat as folklore, not fact.**
- **Practical:** fondue IS available in early August, but only at tourist-facing and mountain venues. Ask or book ahead; do not hike up expecting it.
- **Viator tip:** pick a restaurant with a separate fondue/raclette room, or eat on an open terrace so the smell disperses. https://www.viator.com/blog/The-Fon-Dos-and-Donts-of-Eating-Fondue-in-Switzerland/l93901

## Regional dishes, with attribution
- **Rösti** — originated as a Bernese (and part-Fribourg) farmers' breakfast; by the 1950s in official Swiss tourism material. **Röstigraben** = the German/French cultural-political divide, first used in WWI. https://www.myswitzerland.com/en-se/experiences/food-wine/recipe/recipe-for-roesti/ · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%B6stigraben
- **Älplermagronen** (Central Switzerland) — pasta + potatoes + cream + cheese + onions. Pasta arrived in northern Switzerland in the late 1800s with Italian **Gotthard Tunnel** workers; herdsmen adopted dried pasta because it kept well and was light to carry up. Recorded in Uri c. 1900 as "Magronä". Uri version omits potatoes; Obwalden calls it "Hindersi-Magronen". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%84lplermagronen · https://www.landjaeger.ch/en/blogs/news/alplermagronen
- **Graubünden** — **Bündner Nusstorte** (caramelised walnut tart, brought home by emigrant Graubünden pastry chefs), **Bündnerfleisch** (protected air-dried beef, Graubünden only), **capuns** (chard rolls with dried-meat dough, gratinated), **pizzoccheri** (buckwheat noodles, Italian-speaking Valposchiavo). https://www.graubuendenviva.ch/de/kulinarische-schaetze/buendner-nusstorte · https://www.chur.graubuenden.ch/de/kulinarik/buendner-spezialitaeten
- **Zürcher Geschnetzeltes** — veal in cream, mushrooms, white wine. **Earliest written reference traced to a 1947 cookbook** — much younger than it feels. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z%C3%BCrcher_Geschnetzeltes · served traditionally at https://www.zunfthaus-zur-waag.ch/en/news/zuercher-geschnetzeltes
- **Papet vaudois** — leeks + potatoes with cabbage sausage; the unofficial dish of Vaud, the white/green echoing the cantonal flag; on the **cantonal intangible-heritage inventory**. **Saucisson vaudois IGP** and **Saucisse aux choux vaudoise IGP** got IGP status **2004**; cooked ~45 min in simmering water. https://www.vd.ch/culture/patrimoine-mobilier-non-cantonal-et-immateriel-1/inventaire-cantonal-du-patrimoine-immateriel/pratiques-sociales/toute-lannee/papet-vaudois · https://www.aop-igp.ch/fr/saucisson-vaudois-igp
- **Ticino grotti** — simple taverns in shaded hideaways, with granite tables under trees. Typical: **polenta, luganighe sausage, Merlot risotto**; Merlot drunk from a **boccalino** jug; **gazzosa** the local soda. https://www.ticino.ch/en/explore/gastronomy/restaurants/grotto.html · https://www.ascona-locarno.com/en/what-s-on/stories/grotti — **GAP: no named "best grotti" shortlist obtained.**
- **Valais** — **Viande séchée du Valais IGP**: method documented to the **14th century**; salt/herbs rubbed into raw beef then air-dried in wooden barns for **min. 6 weeks**; **IGP since January 2003**; traditionally paired with **Fendant** or **Cornalin**. https://www.myswitzerland.com/en-us/experiences/food-wine/valais-air-dried-beef-igp-sublime/ · official spec: https://www.blw.admin.ch/dam/fr/sd-web/69ls1btl9XCs/Viande%20s%C3%A9ch%C3%A9e%20du%20Valais%20Cahier%20des%20charges.pdf · **Petite Arvine** and **Cornalin** are signature Valais varieties: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petite_Arvine · **Safran de Mund** grown at Mund in Oberwallis — **GAP: thin sourcing.**
- **Basler Läckerli** (honey, nuts, candied peel, cinnamon/cloves, kirsch) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%A4ckerli_Huus · **Berner Platte** (smoked pork/beef, belly, sausage, bacon, offal, juniper sauerkraut, potatoes, beans; the 5 March 1798 victory-meal origin is **folk-history, lightly sourced**) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berner_Platte · **Meringue with Gruyère double cream** (Fribourg) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gruy%C3%A8re_cream · **Zuger Kirschtorte** invented **1921** by Heinrich Höhn in Zug https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zuger_Kirschtorte · **Tirggel** (Zurich honey biscuits) is a **Christmas** item — low relevance in August.

## Supermarkets and Sunday closing
- **Legal basis: the Arbeitsgesetz (ArG), Art. 16 and 18** — night and Sunday work are prohibited in principle; exceptions require a permit.
- **The 26 cantons set weekday/Saturday hours independently.** ~15 cantons have restrictive laws (weekday close typically 18:30–22:00, tighter Saturdays); **Zurich is liberal (weekdays 06:00–23:00 unrestricted)**; ~8 cantons rely on the federal baseline.
- **Sunday exceptions: shops in RAILWAY STATIONS and AIRPORTS, and petrol-station shops** (kiosk/convenience range). Cantons may also authorise extended Sunday hours in **designated tourist zones** during specific seasons — **relevant for an August Alpine trip.**
Source: https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ladenöffnungszeit
- **Practical:** station Coop/Migros (Zurich HB, Bern, Geneva, Lausanne, Lugano) and airport shops are the reliable Sunday option; **Coop Pronto / Migrolino** petrol-station shops are the fallback.
⚠️ **GAP: no sourced price comparison across Coop / Migros / Denner / Aldi Suisse / Lidl Suisse / Volg** — moneyland.ch and comparis.ch fetches returned 404/403. The usual positioning (Denner/Aldi/Lidl = discount, Coop/Migros = mainstream, Volg = rural convenience) is **UNVERIFIED here.** Also GAP: cross-border shopping savings, and Coop/Migros to-go picnic strategy.

## Tipping, service, water
- **The separate service charge was abolished by the 1974 collective labour agreement for the Swiss hospitality sector (Gesamtarbeitsvertrag für das Gastgewerbe)** — service pay is built into wages and never itemised.
- **~95% of Swiss restaurant guests still round up, averaging ~6% of the bill**, informally shared among staff; legally, tips ≥10% of wages should appear on payslips and be taxed. Collectively **over CHF 1 billion/year**.
Source: https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinkgeld
- **Practical:** no obligation, but rounding to the next CHF 5/10 is the norm.
⚠️ **GAP: no source obtained on tap-water potability or public drinking fountains.** The common knowledge (Swiss tap water is spring/groundwater-based and safe; public *Brunnen* are widely potable unless marked "kein Trinkwasser") is **UNVERIFIED this pass.**
