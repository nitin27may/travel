Before day one

What you are actually going to see

The Alps exist because Africa is still pushing into Europe. The collision crumpled an ancient seafloor upward and, in Switzerland, drove sheets of older rock over younger rock — most visibly at the Glarus overthrust, where a 250-million-year-old layer sits on top of one 35 million years old. That is why the country reads as vertical rather than rolling: it is a range built by compression, then carved by ice into the U-shaped valleys and hanging waterfalls you see at Lauterbrunnen.

What Switzerland is actually famous for, though, is not the geology — it is that all of it is reachable. There are cogwheel railways to 3,089 m, a funicular climbing at 110%, a cable car with an open roof deck, and a rack line that has run to the foot of the Eiger since 1893. No other mountain country has decided, as national policy, that ordinary people should be able to get to the top of things. That decision is why this plan has no car in it.

The third thing is the huts. There are around 153 SAC mountain huts with 9,000 beds, staffed from mid-June to mid-October, where you can eat a cooked dinner at 2,800 m and sleep. Add four national languages, a country the size of Nova Scotia, and a train network that lets you cross most of it before lunch.

The network itself

The three decisions

Why there is no car in this plan

A 15-day second-class Swiss Travel Pass is CHF 499. An all-in mid-size rental car for the same fortnight is CHF 1,940–2,310. That alone would settle it, but three things make the car actively worse than useless.

Every base in this plan is car-free. Zermatt permits private cars only as far as Täsch. Wengen states plainly that it can be reached only by public transport, on foot or by bicycle. You would pay CHF 17 a day to park a car you cannot drive. The pass does not cover car-carrying trains, which is how you would cross the passes in bad weather. And TCS forecasts multi-hour queues at the Gotthard north portal in July and the south portal in August — this window straddles both. Trains use the base tunnel and are unaffected.

Break-even against the pass is roughly four paying adults. If driving the passes is itself the appeal, rent for three or four days — about CHF 400 — rather than fourteen.

But the Travel Pass may not be the right pass

A Half Fare Card is CHF 150 and covers a month from any start date. The Travel Pass only wins if you would otherwise generate CHF 700+ in fares — and with three bases and two transfers, you might not. The thing nobody mentions: the Travel Pass makes exactly three mountains free — Rigi, Stanserhorn and Stoos. Everything else is 50%, and Jungfraujoch is only 25%, where the Half Fare Card actually beats it by CHF 47–58. Price the real transfers before choosing. Buying early does not help either — Swiss domestic fares are fixed and never sell out.

The biggest lever is guest cards, not the national pass

Engadin gives you 10 lifts free with two nights, worth about CHF 230 a head over five — and it is the only affordable route to lifts that reject every national pass. Saas-Fee gives all nine cable cars free from the first night. Arosa gives three. Widely repeated but wrong: Aletsch Arena does not give free lifts — the feeder cable cars from the valley are discounted or free with a GA, but the summit lifts at Moosfluh, Bettmerhorn and Eggishorn are 20% off at best.

What runs the trip

How you get there is the plan

Nothing here is scheduled by the map. It is scheduled by first departures, last departures, and two dates that cannot move. Each day carries a badge for what governs it.

  • Rail Train, cogwheel or funicular does the work
  • Cable car A gondola or aerial cableway is the key — and its hours set the day
  • On foot No lift involved. You walk up, or there is nothing to ride
  • Dawn start Governed by first light, which runs hours before the first lift
  • Date-locked A fixed event you plan around rather than through

Sunday 1 August — Swiss National Day, on a Sunday

The only federal holiday, and it lands on the one day everything already closes. Banks, post offices, most retail and most supermarkets shut; station and petrol-station shops stay open. Trains run a Sunday and holiday timetable. Restaurants are open and packed. Accommodation guidance is to book four to six months ahead — when the 1st falls on a weekend it is the busiest travel weekend of the Swiss year. Buy food on Saturday 31 July, and do not move bases on the 31st, 1st or 2nd.

Saturday 7 August — Sierre-Zinal

A 31 km mountain race with 2,200 m of ascent, broadcast to over 100 countries. Published closures shut the Sierre–Zinal road twice before 06:30, Vissoie–Sierre 10:30–12:30, and Ayer–Chandolin from 06:00 to 14:00. Grimentz, St-Luc, Chandolin and Zinal are locked down for most of the day and the valley is fully booked. Val d'Anniviers and Lac de Moiry are out of this plan for that reason. The Zurich Street Parade and the Zermatt Folklore Festival both plausibly land the same weekend.

The 08:00 problem — book the high nights first

Sunrise is 06:01 on 27 July. Gornergrat's first train is 07:00. Grindelwald–First is about 08:00. Oeschinensee and Moosfluh are 08:30. Mürren–Allmendhubel is 09:00. Every marquee sunrise here needs either a night high up or a walk in the dark. Two exceptions worth knowing: the Zermatt Kirchbrücke is a five-minute walk from the village, and walking up to Oeschinensee from Kandersteg bypasses the mandatory booking system entirely — walkers need no slot.

Scrub the 05:30–07:00 frames from the previous three mornings on that last one — it tells you whether the Matterhorn is clearing at dawn, which no forecast will.

Hazard to check two days out: Spitze Stei above Kandersteg — roughly 16 million cubic metres of rock in motion, several metres a year, with a permanent restricted zone and a status report reissued weekly. It sits above Oeschinensee.

Rescue: dial 1414 (Rega) for a mountain emergency, not 112 — that is what the SAC prints on its own card. Rega patronage is CHF 40 a year per adult, children free, and foreigners can join; it covers air rescue inside Switzerland but not repatriation. In the Valais, Air Zermatt usually flies the mission — their card is CHF 35 and covers residual costs regardless of which organisation responds. Buy both.
