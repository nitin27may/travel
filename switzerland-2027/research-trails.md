## 0. Date-sensitivity warning (read first)

- **Every fare, operating date and hut price below is a 2026-season figure** (or older where noted). Swiss lift operators publish next-season tariffs in roughly Nov–Mar. **2027 fares and 2027 operating calendars are not yet published — treat all numbers as ±5–10% placeholders. GAP.**
- 27 Jul – 10 Aug is **peak Alpine season**: essentially every lift, cogwheel, funicular and hut in this document will be running, and every high trail should be snow-free apart from residual patches on north-facing cols above ~2,700 m. That part is date-robust.
- It is also the **most crowded and most expensive** two weeks of the Swiss year (Swiss National Day 1 August is a public holiday; expect full huts, full trains, and 1 Aug fireworks/brunch events). SAC huts on popular routes sell out for this window months ahead.
- **1 August 2027 falls on a Sunday.** Swiss National Day. Many mountain restaurants run special brunches; some valley shops close. Trains run to Sunday timetable.

## 1. The Swiss T-scale (SAC Wanderskala) — what the grades actually mean

Introduced by the Swiss Alpine Club in 2002. "T" = Trekking. Grades assume **good conditions**: good visibility, dry ground, no snow. Grading reflects the **hardest single passage** on the route ("Schlüsselstelle"), not the average.
Source: https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAC-Wanderskala · https://wegwandern.ch/sac-schwierigkeitsskalen/ · https://www.gamssteig.de/themen/sac-berg-alpin-wanderskala-schwierigkeitsbewertung

| Grade | Category | Plain language |
|---|---|---|
| **T1** | Wandern (hiking) | Yellow-signed *Wanderweg*. Wide, well-graded, no fall exposure. Runners, strollers, sneakers OK. Anyone can do it. |
| **T2** | Bergwandern (mountain hiking) | White-red-white signage. Continuous path, some steeper ground, occasional short exposure. Sturdy shoes. Basic sure-footedness. |
| **T3** | Bergwandern | White-red-white. Path may be faint in places; exposed sections may have chains/cables/handrails; a slip could hurt you. Needs sure-footedness and a reasonable head for heights. **This is the ceiling for a "fit but not expert" hiker.** |
| **T4** | Alpines Wandern | White-**blue**-white signage. Path can vanish; hands used; steep grass, scree, easy glacier terrain possible. Alpine experience assumed, plus terrain-assessment ability. Not recommended without experience. |
| **T5** | Alpines Wandern | Frequent scrambling, exposed, glacier crossings, crampons/rope territory. |
| **T6** | Anspruchsvolles alpines Wandern | Mostly pathless, climbing to grade II, serious exposure. |

Grades take `-` and `+` modifiers (T3+, T4-). Trail signage colour is the fastest field cue: **yellow = T1, white-red-white = T2/T3, white-blue-white = T4+**.

**Practical rule for this trip:** anything T1–T3 is fair game for a fit non-expert. T4 needs either a guide or genuine prior alpine mileage. Two trails on the "must-cover" list are T4: the **Hörnliweg** to the Hörnlihütte and the **Macun Lakes** in the Swiss National Park.

## 2. SwitzerlandMobility / SchweizMobil route numbering — how to read it

Official national trail database, run with the federal government. https://schweizmobil.ch/en/hiking-in-switzerland
- **Routes 1–9 = national routes** (e.g. **1 = Via Alpina / Alpine Pass Route**, **5 = Jura Crest Trail / Jura-Höhenweg**, **6 = Alpine Passes Trail / Alpenpässe-Weg**). Source: https://www.schweizmobil.ch/en/wanderland/route-01.html · https://www.schweizmobil.ch/en/hiking-in-switzerland/route-6
- **Two-digit numbers = regional routes** (e.g. **39 = Aletsch Panoramaweg**, **74 = Sentiero Verzasca**).
- **Three-digit numbers = local routes** (e.g. **113 = Terrasses de Lavaux**, **345 = Panoramaweg Schynige Platte**, **353 = Eiger Trail**, **796 = Via Panoramica Val Bregaglia**).
- URL patterns that work: `https://schweizmobil.ch/en/hiking-in-switzerland/route-<N>` and `https://schweizmobil.ch/de/wanderland/route-<N>`. The **schweizmobil.ch pages are JavaScript-rendered and did not scrape** in this research pass — all SwitzerlandMobility numbers below were cross-confirmed via myswitzerland.com route pages, which mirror the same database. **GAP: no direct schweizmobil page text captured.**
- Separately, resorts run their **own local trail numbers** on signposts that are NOT SwitzerlandMobility numbers — e.g. Zermatt's "Hörnliweg No. 27" and "Riffelseeweg No. 21", Grindelwald First's "#2" and "#62". Don't confuse them.

## 3. JUNGFRAU REGION (Bernese Oberland)

Base villages: Grindelwald, Wengen, Mürren, Lauterbrunnen, Interlaken. Almost every hike here is point-to-point and lift-assisted — that is the norm, not the exception.

### 3.1 Eiger Trail — **SwitzerlandMobility route 353**
- **Region / base:** Jungfrau region; Grindelwald (or Kleine Scheidegg / Wengen).
- **Distance:** 6 km / 3.7 mi. **Ascent 100 m (330 ft), descent 820 m (2,690 ft)** walking Eigergletscher → Alpiglen.
- **Official time:** 1 h 55 min. **Realistic fit-but-not-expert: 2.5–3 h** with photo stops (most third-party guides say 2–3 h).
- **Difficulty:** myswitzerland rates it "medium" technical / medium physical. Community consensus ≈ **T2**, with short **T2+/T3** rocky steps. Plain language: a rough, rocky mountain path, mostly downhill, some loose scree, secured with fixed ropes where it's steep. Not exposed enough to frighten most people. **GAP: no authoritative SAC T-grade published for this route.**
- **Start/end:** Eigergletscher station (2,320 m) → Alpiglen (1,616 m). **Point-to-point, lift/train required at BOTH ends.** Jungfrau Railway up to Eigergletscher; Wengernalpbahn down from Alpiglen. Can also be walked uphill Alpiglen → Eigergletscher (820 m climb, ~3 h) if you want to avoid buying the higher ticket.
- **Payoff:** Continuous, and it starts immediately — you walk *along the base of the Eiger North Face*, close enough to spot climbers with binoculars. Views of Wetterhorn and Grosse Scheidegg. This is the best value-per-minute walk in the region.
- **Exposure/risk:** "Difficult sections are secured with ropes." Rockfall from the north face is the real hazard; the trail closes in bad weather. No via ferrata, no glacier, no chains beyond hand-lines. Snowfields in early August: unlikely.
- **Huts on route:** None on the trail itself. Berghaus Alpiglen at the finish (restaurant). Restaurant at Eigergletscher station.
- **Season:** July–October per myswitzerland.
- **Links:** https://www.myswitzerland.com/en-us/experiences/route/eiger-trail-the-swiss-alpine-experience-trail/ · https://schweizmobil.ch/de/wanderland/route-353 · https://www.jungfrau.ch/de-ch/kleine-scheidegg/eiger-trail/ · https://www.earthtrekkers.com/eiger-trail-hike-bernese-oberland-switzerland/

### 3.2 Männlichen → Kleine Scheidegg Panorama Trail
- **Region / base:** Jungfrau; Grindelwald or Wengen.
- **Distance:** 4.9 km / 3.0 mi. **Net descent ~200 m (650 ft)**, negligible ascent (~60 m / 190 ft).
- **Time:** 1 h 30 min official. **Realistic: 1.5–2 h** — it's a stroll.
- **Difficulty:** **T1.** Wide, well-graded gravel path. Strollers manage it. Zero exposure.
- **Start/end:** Männlichen (2,230 m) → Kleine Scheidegg (2,061 m). **Point-to-point, lift required at the start and a train at the end.**
- **Lifts:** Two ways up. (a) **Grindelwald Terminal → Männlichen gondola (Royal Ride)** — 19 min, 10-person cabins; 2026 return CHF 74.60 (one-way CHF ~32 historically, 50% off with Swiss Travel Pass or Half Fare Card). (b) **Wengen → Männlichen aerial cableway** — runs 23 May–25 Oct 2026, every 20 min, roughly 08:30–17:00; CHF 26 one-way / CHF 52 return, half price with STP/HFC; 2026 return from Wengen CHF 63. Down from Kleine Scheidegg: Wengernalpbahn to Wengen or Grindelwald.
- **Payoff:** Eiger, Mönch, Jungfrau in front of you the entire way if you walk **Männlichen → Kleine Scheidegg** (do it in that direction). Restaurant Grindelwaldblick near the end has a climbable viewing platform that several sources rate better than Kleine Scheidegg itself.
- **Add-on:** **Royal Walk** to the Männlichen summit — 2 km round trip, +30–45 min, opens up the Lauterbrunnen valley.
- **Exposure/risk:** None. Best rainy/marginal-weather high option in the region because escape by train is trivial.
- **Season:** mid-June–October.
- **Links:** https://www.earthtrekkers.com/mannlichen-to-kleine-scheidegg-panorama-trail/ · https://www.alltrails.com/trail/switzerland/bern/panoramaweg-mannlichen-kleine-scheidegg · https://switzerlanding.com/mannlichen-to-kleine-scheidegg-panorama-trail/
- **GAP:** no SwitzerlandMobility route number confirmed for this trail.

### 3.3 First → Bachalpsee
- **Region / base:** Grindelwald.
- **Distance:** 5.9 km / 3.65 mi return (2.94 km / 1.8 mi each way). **Ascent/descent 188 m (617 ft)** each way.
- **Time:** 55 min each way; **2 h return**; budget 3–4 h with the gondola and lingering.
- **Difficulty:** **T1** — broad, graded gravel path. Popular with families.
- **Start/end:** First (2,168 m) → Bachalpsee (2,265 m) → back to First. **Out-and-back; gondola required.**
- **Lift:** **Grindelwald Firstbahn**, 25 min, via Bort and Schreckfeld. 2026 round trip ≈ **CHF 72** adult; children half; under 6 free; discounts with Swiss Travel Pass / Half Fare Card. Summer hours ≈ 08:00–18:00. Operating season early Jan–late Oct.
- **Payoff:** The reflection of the Schreckhorn/Wetterhorn in the lake — the payoff is at the far end (~55 min in). Trail is clear of snow late May/early June → October.
- **Exposure/risk:** None.
- **Onward options from the same lift ticket:** Trail **#4** First–Bachalpsee–Waldspitz–Bort (2.5 h, flower meadows, walks you down to a mid-station); trail **#2** First–Bachalpsee–Faulhorn–Bussalp (4 h).
- **Links:** https://fullsuitcase.com/bachalpsee/ · https://www.alltrails.com/trail/switzerland/bern/first-bachalpsee · https://www.myswissalps.com/activity/grindelwald-first/

### 3.4 Faulhornweg — First ↔ Faulhorn ↔ Schynige Platte traverse
The single best big day in the Jungfrau region and the one worth building a day around.
- **Region / base:** Grindelwald (First end) / Wilderswil (Schynige Platte end).
- **Distance:** **16–16.5 km / 10–10.3 mi.** Walking **Schynige Platte → First**: ascent ~860 m (2,820 ft), descent ~690 m (2,260 ft). Reverse direction flips those roughly, with a bigger net descent (~650 m / 2,130 ft) into Schynige Platte.
- **Time:** 5–6 h moving. **Realistic fit-but-not-expert: 6–7 h** including the Faulhorn summit detour and a soup stop.
- **Difficulty:** **T2**, with a couple of **T2+/T3** rocky, loose sections and the final pull to Faulhorn. Red-and-white mountain path, narrow, sometimes rocky with tricky footing on loose rock, but easy to follow and not dangerous for an average hiker. **GAP: no official SAC T-grade published.**
- **Start/end:** **Point-to-point requiring a lift at both ends.** Schynige Platte (1,967 m, cogwheel from Wilderswil) ↔ First (2,168 m, gondola from Grindelwald). Highest point **Faulhorn 2,681 m / 8,793 ft**.
- **Which direction:** Schynige Platte → First is the usual recommendation (steadier gradient up, gondola down at the end, and you can bail to Bussalp). First → Schynige Platte gives you Bachalpsee early and a longer descent to finish.
- **Lifts/fares:** Schynige Platte Bahn — 2026 season **13 June – 25 October**, departures every 40 min, 52-min ride, **CHF 68 return** from Wilderswil / **CHF 34** with Half Fare or Swiss Travel Pass; under 6 free, 6–15 free with Junior Card. First gondola ≈ CHF 72 return (see 3.3). Combined through-tickets exist covering both.
- **Payoff:** Distributed the whole way, and unusually so — Lakes Thun and Brienz on one side, the Eiger–Mönch–Jungfrau wall on the other, Bachalpsee near the First end, and a 360° summit at Faulhorn.
- **Exposure/risk:** No chains, no via ferrata, no glacier. **The whole ridge is exposed to weather** — sources are emphatic that you need a clear-sky forecast and an early start, because there is nowhere to hide from a thunderstorm. Early-August snowfields: possible in shaded gullies near Faulhorn but rarely a problem.
- **Huts on route:**
  - **Berghotel Faulhorn** (2,681 m) — Switzerland's oldest mountain hotel; ~0.4 mi detour off the main path; restaurant, serves lunch, sleeps guests. Private, not SAC.
  - **Berghaus Männdlenen / "Weberhütte"** (~2,344 m) — small hut + restaurant roughly midway; the only shelter on the middle third.
  - **Berghaus Schynige Platte / Hotel** at the west end; restaurants at First.
  - **GAP: 2027 prices and opening dates for Faulhorn and Männdlenen not verified. Book Faulhorn early for an overnight — it is tiny and August is peak.**
- **Note:** the traverse is Stage 15 of the **Via Berna** long-distance trail; local signage numbers are **#2 + #62**.
- **Links:** https://www.earthtrekkers.com/hike-schynige-platte-to-faulhorn-to-first-switzerland/ · https://www.hikingwalking.com/index.php/destinations/sw/sw_bernese/grindelwald/schynige_platte/schynige_platte_detail · https://iainwalks.com/stories/faulhornweg · https://swissfamilyfun.com/faulhorn/
- **GAP:** SwitzerlandMobility route number for the Faulhornweg not confirmed.

### 3.5 Panoramaweg Schynige Platte (ridge circuit) — **SwitzerlandMobility route 345**
- **Region / base:** Schynige Platte, above Wilderswil/Interlaken.
- **Distance:** 6 km / 3.7 mi. **Ascent 340 m (1,115 ft), descent 340 m.**
- **Time:** 1 h 55 min official; **realistic 2.5 h** with the Oberberghorn ladders and stops.
- **Difficulty:** **medium technical / easy physical** per myswitzerland ≈ **T2**. Short ladder/step sections up the Oberberghorn. Shortcuts available to cut it down.
- **Start/end:** **Loop starting and ending at Schynige Platte station** — one lift, no logistics. Ideal half-day.
- **Lift:** Schynige Platte cogwheel railway (see 3.4 for 2026 dates and CHF 68 / CHF 34).
- **Payoff:** Immediate and continuous — you climb the west flank of the Gumihorn to Tuba with Lakes Thun and Brienz below, then traverse a horizontal bypass facing Wetterhorn, Schreckhorn, Finsteraarhorn, Eiger, Mönch, Jungfrau and the Blüemlisalp.
- **Alpine Garden:** the **Alpengarten Schynige Platte** at the station holds **~800 alpine plant species** — free-standing attraction, walkable in 45–60 min, at its floral best late June–July but still worth it in early August.
- **Exposure/risk:** Mild. Ladders and steps on the Oberberghorn variant; the low route avoids them.
- **Season:** June–October.
- **Links:** https://www.myswitzerland.com/en-us/experiences/route/schynige-platte-circular-ridge-route/ · https://www.jungfrau.ch/en-gb/schynige-platte/ · https://www.travel-interlaken.ch/en/attractions/schynige-platte/

### 3.6 North Face Trail (Mürren / Allmendhubel), + Gimmelwald and Sprutz
- **Region / base:** Mürren (car-free), Lauterbrunnen valley.
- **Distance:** **7.4 km / 4.6 mi** with the Sprutz waterfall detour; **6.5 km / 4.0 mi** without; **7.8 km / 4.8 mi** as a loop if you skip the funicular and walk up.
- **Ascent/descent:** **+170 m (560 ft) / −420 m (1,380 ft)** on the main route.
- **Time:** 2–2.5 h moving; **allow 3–4 h** with the alp cafés.
- **Difficulty:** **T1/T2** — "moderate", dirt footpath, uneven, some stairs. The **Sprutz waterfall detour is the one real hazard**: steep, no railing, wet and slippery, and you walk *under* the waterfall. Not for vertigo sufferers or small children.
- **Start/end:** Top of the **Allmendhubel funicular** (1,912 m) → back to Mürren. Extendable to **Gimmelwald** (adds ~40–45 min, steep descent at the end) with a cable car back up to Mürren or down to Stechelberg.
- **Lift:** **Allmendhubel funicular** from Mürren — CHF 8.80 adult one-way (2025 price), free with Bernese Oberland Pass, 50% off with Swiss Travel Pass / Half Fare Card. Season early June – late October.
- **Payoff:** Distributed. Eiger, Mönch, Jungfrau across the valley the whole way, plus working alps, wildflowers, cowbells, and the Lauterbrunnen trench below.
- **Huts/alps serving food:** **Pension Suppenalp** (café, late June–early Oct), **Schiltalp** (honesty shop — cheese and drinks, cash box), **Pension Spielbodenalp** (café, mid-June–September).
- **Season:** June–October.
- **Links:** https://swissfamilyfun.com/murren-northface-trail-sprutz-waterfall/ · https://www.earthtrekkers.com/northface-trail-murren-switzerland/ · https://www.alltrails.com/trail/switzerland/bern/northface-trail-murren

### 3.7 Mürren – Gimmelwald – Sefinental (and the Rotstockhütte / Obersteinberg back-country)
- **Sefinental** is the deep, quiet side valley south of Gimmelwald that the **Via Alpina stage 12 (Lauterbrunnen → Griesalp)** climbs to cross the **Sefinafurgga (2,612 m)** — flagged by Alpine Exploratory as one of the two hardest passes on the whole Swiss Via Alpina, with loose shale and scree and wooden steps. **T3.**
- **Rotstockhütte** (~2,039 m) sits on that approach and is the standard halfway shelter; **Berghotel Obersteinberg** (~1,778 m, no electricity, candlelit) sits at the head of the Lauterbrunnen valley above Stechelberg and is a genuinely lesser-known overnight.

### 3.8 Lauterbrunnen valley floor + Trümmelbach Falls
- **Region / base:** Lauterbrunnen.
- **Distance:** **8 km / 5.0 mi** Lauterbrunnen station → Stechelberg (some sources 6.7 km / 4.2 mi for the shorter line). **Ascent 161 m (528 ft), descent 51 m (167 ft).**
- **Time:** **2 h.** Lauterbrunnen → Trümmelbach alone is a flat 40–50 min.
- **Difficulty:** **T1.** Flat, paved/gravel, buggy-friendly.
- **Start/end:** Lauterbrunnen railway station → Hotel Stechelberg postbus stop. **Point-to-point but no lift needed** — postbus 141 runs the valley roughly hourly and gets you back in ~10–15 min.
- **Payoff:** Continuous. 72 waterfalls pour off cliffs up to 400 m high; Staubbach Falls at the start, Mürrenbachfall further on.
- **Trümmelbach Falls:** ten glacier-fed cascades **inside the mountain**, reached by a lift cut into the rock — Europe's largest subterranean waterfalls. **Open daily early April – early November, 09:00–17:00, and 08:30–18:00 in July and August.** **CHF 18 adult / CHF 8 ages 6–15, cash/card at the door only, no advance tickets.**
- **Rainy-day rating: excellent.** The gorge is more dramatic in rain and high melt, and early August is peak glacier melt so flow will be at its strongest.
- **Season:** April–November.
- **Links:** https://www.myswitzerland.com/en-us/experiences/route/valley-of-the-72-waterfalls-lauterbrunnen-stechelberg/ · https://lauterbrunnen.swiss/en/map/detail/trummelbach-waterfalls-52c355bf-8544-4c9b-9885-427fc77657fb.html · https://swissfamilyfun.com/lauterbrunnen-valley/

### 3.9 Oeschinensee from Kandersteg
- **Region / base:** Kandersteg (Bernese Oberland, on the Lötschberg line).
- **The lake walk:** gondola to Oeschinen station then ~20–30 min walk down to the shore, or ~1 h 15 walk up from Kandersteg if you skip the gondola. **T1.** Turquoise lake ringed by the Blüemlisalp wall — one of the highest-value easy payoffs in Switzerland, and it arrives at the very start.
- **Gondola (Bergbahnen Oeschinensee) — 2026:**
  - **Summer 9 May – 20 Sept 2026, daily 08:30–18:00.** Autumn 21 Sept – 8 Nov, daily 08:30–17:00.
  - **Return adult:** CHF 36.00 (9 May–14 June), **CHF 40.00 (15 June–21 Sept — i.e. your dates)**, CHF 36.00 (22 Sept–9 Nov). **With Half Fare / GA / Swiss Travel Pass: CHF 22.50 in the 15 June–21 Sept window.** Children same as reduced rate.
  - **Time-slot reservation is MANDATORY 20 June – 20 Sept 2026** — book the ascent online. Assume the same applies in 2027. **This is a booking trap; do not just turn up in August.**
  - **The Bernese Oberland Pass gives NO discount from 2026.**
- **Links:** https://www.oeschinensee.ch/en/tarife-fahrplan/ · https://www.oeschinensee.ch/en/wandern/

### 3.10 Blüemlisalphütte SAC (2,840 m) via Hohtürli
The serious one in this region.
- **Region / base:** Kandersteg (north side) or Griesalp (south side).
- **Route A — from Kandersteg/Oeschinen cableway (normal route):** **T2**, **4 h ascent, 1,270 m (4,170 ft) gain, 120 m descent** per the SAC route portal. Round trip from the gondola as an out-and-back is ~16.6 km / 10.3 mi and AllTrails users average **7 h 36 min** — call it a long, hard, but non-technical day.
- **Route B — from Griesalp:** **T2**, **4 h, 1,450 m (4,760 ft) gain.**
- **Route C — from Gspaltenhornhütte:** **T4**, 3 h 45, 1,000 m gain / 600 m descent.
- **Route D — from Fründenhütte SAC:** **T4+**, 5–6 h, 1,050 m gain / 750 m descent.
- **Traverse option (the classic):** Kandersteg/Oeschinensee → Hohtürli (2,778 m) → Blüemlisalphütte → down to Griesalp → Mürren/Stechelberg. This is **Via Alpina stage 13 in reverse** and one of the finest two-day hut trips in Switzerland. Alpine Exploratory names Hohtürli as one of the two hardest passes on the Swiss Via Alpina.
- **Hut facts:** 2,840 m. **100 beds + 16-place emergency shelter.** **Wardened June–October** (partial service in June and October; sac-bluemlisalp.ch says "mid June – mid October, depending on the onset of winter"). Winter room open year-round. Sits five minutes above the Hohtürli col.
- **Booking:** online SAC hut reservation system; changes/cancellation per their T&Cs. **Sleeping-bag liner is mandatory** — buy on site (silk CHF 60, cotton CHF 38) or rent (CHF 10). **No dogs.** Pays cash CHF/EUR, debit, credit, Twint. Phone +41 33 676 14 37, info@bluemlisalphuette.com.
- **Price:** **GAP — the hut's own price list is a PDF that did not scrape.** Use the SAC system-wide band: **overnight CHF 25–45, half board CHF 60–90 per person** (Switzerland Tourism). Assume the upper end for a 100-bed flagship hut in August.
- **Lunch:** SAC huts routinely serve day meals to passing hikers; the Blüemlisalphütte page references a food & beverage menu. **GAP: not explicitly confirmed that non-residents can get lunch.**
- **Snowfields early August:** the north side below Hohtürli holds old snow late; the col has fixed wooden steps. Manageable but check conditions.
- **Links:** https://www.sac-cas.ch/en/huts-and-tours/sac-route-portal/blueemlisalphuette-sac-2147000036/mountain-hiking/ · https://www.sac-bluemlisalp.ch/en/Blueemlisalphuette/Huette/Uebernachtung · https://www.outdooractive.com/en/route/mountain-hike/kandersteg/kandersteg-oeschinensee-hohtuerli-griesalp/51641556/ · https://www.alltrails.com/trail/switzerland/bern/kandersteg-oeschinen-hohturli-pass

## 4. ZERMATT / VALAIS

Zermatt is car-free; you arrive by the Matterhorn Gotthard Bahn from Täsch. Three separate lift systems: Sunnegga–Rothorn (east), Gornergrat railway (south-east), Matterhorn Glacier Paradise/Schwarzsee (south-west).

### 4.1 Five Lakes Walk / 5-Seenweg — **SwitzerlandMobility route 186**
- **Distance:** **11 km / 6.8 mi** per myswitzerland; **9.8 km / 6.1 mi** per Zermatt Bergbahnen and AllTrails (the difference is where you start/finish). **Ascent 380 m (1,250 ft), descent 660 m (2,165 ft).**
- **Time:** myswitzerland 3 h 15 forward / 3 h 35 reverse; the lift company says 2 h 30. **Realistic 3–4 h** with photo stops.
- **Difficulty:** **medium / T2.** Mixed terrain — wide flat gravel, narrow dirt with protruding rocks, one section of switchbacks. No exposure worth the name.
- **Start/end:** **Blauherd (2,571 m) → Stellisee → Grindjisee → Grünsee → Moosjisee → Leisee → Sunnegga (2,288 m).** **Point-to-point; lift required at both ends.**
- **Lifts:** Sunnegga funicular from Zermatt (4 min, underground) then a 6-seat gondola to Blauherd. **Zermatt→Blauherd single + Sunnegga→Zermatt single from CHF 29.00**; reductions with GA / Half Fare / Swiss Travel Pass. There is a dedicated **"Five Lakes Trail Kombi-Ticket."** A free **Leisee-Shuttle** runs between the Leisee and Sunnegga station.
- **Payoff:** Front-loaded. **Stellisee is the money shot** — the classic Matterhorn reflection — and it's the *first* lake, ~25–30 min from Blauherd. If you're short on time, Blauherd→Stellisee→back is only ~3 km / 2 mi and 1 h. Only **Stellisee, Grindjisee and Leisee actually reflect** the Matterhorn. Grünsee and Leisee allow swimming when no warning signs are posted. Moosjisee is meltwater-turquoise (it's a reservoir).
- **Exposure/risk:** None. No glacier, no chains.
- **Season:** June–September; signposted in both directions; may be blocked by snow outside season.
- **Links:** https://www.myswitzerland.com/en-us/experiences/route/5-seenweg-zermatt/ · https://www.matterhornparadise.ch/en/experience/hiking/5-lakes-walk · https://www.earthtrekkers.com/five-lakes-trail-5-seenweg-hike-is-it-worth-it/

### 4.2 Gornergrat → Rotenboden → Riffelsee → Riffelberg (→ Riffelalp)
- **Distance:** **4.7 km / 2.9 mi** Gornergrat → Riffelalp; ~1 h from Rotenboden down to Riffelberg. **Cumulative descent ~556 m (1,823 ft)** on the Gornergrat–Riffelalp line.
- **Time:** 1 h (Rotenboden→Riffelberg) to 3 h+ (Gornergrat→Riffelalp), fully customisable — you can bail onto the train at Rotenboden, Riffelberg or Riffelalp.
- **Difficulty:** **easy to moderate, T1/T2.** Local Zermatt signage calls the lake circuit **"Riffelseeweg No. 21"** (take the left/longer side of the lake).
- **Start/end:** Gornergrat (3,089 m) or Rotenboden (2,815 m) → Riffelberg (2,582 m) / Riffelalp (2,211 m). **Descending point-to-point; the Gornergrat Bahn is required to get up and to get back.**
- **Lift:** **Gornergrat Bahn**, Europe's highest open-air railway, **33–40 min** Zermatt→Gornergrat. **CHF 38.50 with a Half Fare Card** for Zermatt→Gornergrat + Riffelberg→Zermatt (2026 pricing update noted). **GAP: full undiscounted 2026/2027 fare not captured — historically ~CHF 132 return.**
- **Payoff:** Immediate at the top (Gorner Glacier and a wall of 4,000ers from the Gornergrat terrace), then a second payoff at **Riffelsee**, ~10 min below Rotenboden, which gives the famous inverted Matterhorn on a windless morning. **Go early or at dusk — wind ruins the reflection by mid-morning.**
- **Exposure/risk:** None. This is the best "big mountain scenery for minimal effort" day in Zermatt and the right choice for a tired day or a mixed-ability group.
- **Links:** https://www.gornergrat.ch/en/stories/riffelsee-lake-trail-from-rotenboden-to-riffelberg · https://www.earthtrekkers.com/gornergrat-riffelberg-hike-zermatt/ · https://zermatt.swiss/en/p/riffelseeweg-no-21-01tVj000005Ew9KIAS

### 4.3 Hörnliweg to the Hörnlihütte (Matterhorn base camp) — **Zermatt local trail No. 27**
- **Distance:** **4.3 km / 2.7 mi one way** from Schwarzsee. **Ascent 693 m (2,274 ft), descent 13 m.** (Some guides quote ~9 km / 5.6 mi and 758 m for the full out-and-back.)
- **Time:** **2 h 15 up** officially; ~4 h round trip. **Realistic 4.5–5 h** return including hut time.
- **Difficulty:** **T4 — "demanding"** per Zermatt Tourism. Note the conflict: several English blogs call it "T3 / medium." **Trust the official T4.** Plain language: partly exposed, requires sure-footedness and freedom from vertigo; the route is secured with **safety ropes, metal pegs, chains and steel staircases**, including "a metal staircase with grating treads hanging over nothing."
- **Start/end:** **Schwarzsee (2,583 m) → Hörnlihütte (3,260 m)**, out-and-back. **Cable car required** (Zermatt → Furi → Schwarzsee).
- **Payoff:** At the end, and it is worth it — you stand at the foot of the Hörnli ridge, the start of the 1865 first ascent, and in August–September you watch roped parties on the ridge.
- **Exposure/risk:** **The highest-consequence trail on the must-do list that isn't a glacier route.** Fixed chains and steel stairs, real exposure, altitude 3,260 m. **Not appropriate for a fit-but-not-expert hiker without prior exposure experience or a guide.** Guided option exists at **CHF 140 pp in a group of 4+** via Zermatters.
- **Hut:** **Hörnlihütte**, 3,260 m, 150 beds, restaurant seating 130. **Opens 26 June (2026 date).** **Dormitory bed with half board CHF 150 pp** (max 8 beds/room, shared facilities, breakfast + dinner + WiFi + hiking tea; **sleeping-bag liner required**, CHF 43 at reception). **Private suite CHF 500 for 2** with private shower/WC, no liner needed. Check-in 14:00, check-out 08:30. **Booking opened 1 April for the 2026 season** — expect the same pattern for 2027; **CHF 50 pp/night deposit.** Serves food to day visitors (the restaurant is separate from the beds).
- **Links:** https://zermatt.swiss/en/p/hoernliweg-no-27-01tVj000005EwSkIAK · https://hoernlihuette.ch/sleep/?lang=en · https://www.earthtrekkers.com/hornlihutte-hike/

### 4.4 Europaweg + Charles Kuonen suspension bridge — **PARTIALLY CLOSED, see §8**
- **What it is:** A two-day high balcony traverse **Grächen → Zermatt**, holding 1,600–2,200 m above the Mattertal, the deepest valley in Switzerland, facing the Matterhorn, Weisshorn and the Bernese Alps. Also serves as the **final stage of the Walker's Haute Route** and part of the **Tour of Monte Rosa**.
- **The bridge:** **Charles Kuonen Hängebrücke, 494 m long, up to 85 m above the Grabengufer ravine** — longest pedestrian suspension bridge in the Alps. Opened 2017 to replace the earlier Europabrücke, which **rockfall destroyed in 2010, two months after opening.** Closed in winter.
- **THE 2026 CLOSURE (highly likely to persist into 2027):** the section **Springelboden ↔ Eggenstadel** — between the suspension bridge and Täschalp, on the Randa–Täsch stretch — **is closed**. Cause: severe storms in **late June 2024** destroyed rockfall barriers and crushed a tunnel; the ongoing hazard is described as "stone chipping," i.e. continuing rockfall. **Closed for the 2024, 2025 and 2026 seasons; the Swiss Hiking Federation says "until further notice."** Zermatt Tourism told The Hiking Club in January 2026 to "plan for the detour, as in previous years."
  - **Detour:** descend to the valley floor (steep and hard) and climb back, **or** use **Taxi Christophe**, a shared shuttle Täsch → Täschalp, ~**CHF 11 pp**, ~15 min wait.
  - **Action for 2027: re-check https://www.europaweg.ch and https://zermatt.swiss/en/map in spring 2027 before committing.** No reopening date announced. **GAP.**
- **The good news day-hike version, which is unaffected:** **Randa → Charles Kuonen bridge → Europahütte → Randa** as an **8.3 km / 5.2 mi circuit, ~4 h, moderate**. Reached by an 18-min train from Zermatt. Several sources flag it as an excellent overcast-day option because the payoff is the bridge, not the summits. myswitzerland lists a minimal 2 km / 1 h bridge-only version.
- **Europahütte:** the hut immediately beyond the bridge; the Walker's Haute Route stage 13 terminus. **GAP: prices, opening dates and booking window not captured.**
- **Links:** https://www.thehiking.club/blog/europaweg-section-closure-2026 · https://www.europaweg.ch/en/ · https://www.randa.ch/en/tourism/the-charles-kuonen-suspension-bridge-on-the-europaweg/ · https://www.myswitzerland.com/en-us/experiences/route/hiking-on-the-longest-pedestrian-suspension-bridge-in-the-world/ · https://www.alltrails.com/trail/switzerland/valais/randa-europahutte-via-charles-kuonen-hangebrucke

### 4.5 Höhbalmen Höhenweg / Edelweissweg (via Trift and Zmutt) — the connoisseur's Zermatt day
Repeatedly named by knowledgeable sources as the best walk in Zermatt precisely because **there is no lift on this side of the valley**, so the crowds aren't there.
- **Distance:** **17.5 km / 10.9 mi** via the Hubel alternative; **20–20.7 km / 12.4–12.9 mi** on the official full circuit.
- **Ascent/descent:** **~1,194 m (3,917 ft)** each way — some sources say 1,197 m. Elevation range 1,613 m → **2,750 m (9,022 ft)**.
- **Time:** **7 h moving; 8–9 h with breaks.** This is a full, committing day.
- **Difficulty:** **T2–T3.** A high balcony trail contouring steep mountainside; narrow in places, "may feel a bit exposed but is quite safe." Nothing technical, but the length and the 1,200 m climb are the real difficulty.
- **Start/end:** **Loop from Zermatt town centre (Pfarrkirche St. Mauritius). No lift needed at all** — rare and useful.
- **Direction:** **Counter-clockwise**, so the Matterhorn faces you all day.
- **Payoff:** Delayed then sustained — the Matterhorn "bursts onto the scene" when you top out on the Höhbalmen plateau (~2,750 m), then stays with you across the traverse and down through Zmutt.
- **Huts/restaurants:** **Berghaus/Hotel du Trift** (2,337 m) — 30 dormitory places, 9 private rooms, drinks, snacks and meals. **Restaurant Z'Mutt** (09:00–17:30, kitchen closes earlier). **Café Edelweiss** at Alterhaupt (1,934 m). Restaurant Kalbermatten is private/closed. Schönbielhütte reachable on an extended loop.
- **TRAIL DAMAGE — verify:** **the Triftbach gorge ascent from Zermatt to Berghaus Trift was damaged by heavy storms in September 2024.** An alternative ascent via **Hubel** (17.5 km version) bypasses it. **Check https://wegwandern.ch or the swisstopo app for 2027 status. GAP.**
- **Season:** June–September (October).
- **Links:** https://www.moonhoneytravel.com/edelweissweg-hohbalmen-hike-zermatt/ · https://www.hikingwalking.com/index.php/destinations/sw/sw_valais/zermatt/hohbalm/hohbalm_detail · https://www.alltrails.com/trail/switzerland/valais/edelweissweg-hohbalmen-stafelsalp

### 4.6 Other strong Zermatt days (from the same source set)

### 4.7 Saas-Fee / Saastal — Höhenweg Kreuzboden and the Mischabel huts
- **Höhenweg Kreuzboden (aka Höhenweg Almagelleralp):** **~9.7–10.1 km / 6.0–6.3 mi one way**, **only ~130 m (430 ft) of gain**, elevation 2,400–2,530 m, **~3 h 45 to Saas-Almagell** (AllTrails records ~2 h 11 for the core traverse). **Moderate / T2**, but with **"a steep, narrow section with some exposure"** on the descent, protected by metal and rope fencing.
  - **Lifts:** gondola **Saas-Grund → Kreuzboden** required; optional chairlift Furggstalden → Saas-Almagell at the end. **Point-to-point.**
  - **Payoff:** continuous — you traverse flowery meadows and rugged slopes between the **Lagginhorn and Weissmies**, facing the entire **Mischabel range** and its glaciers and waterfalls across the valley.
  - **Optional Erlebnisweg / Adventure Trail variant:** **two suspension bridges, narrow wooden walkways bolted into rock walls, steep ladders and metal steps down a cliff face.** No safety equipment required, but "definitely not for the faint of heart." Effectively an unroped via-ferrata-flavoured section — **the most interesting single feature in the Saastal.**
  - **Hut:** **Almagelleralp** (small restaurant) on the valley floor; from there a long, strenuous climb reaches the **Almagellerhütte (2,894 m)**.
  - **Links:** https://www.hikingwalking.com/index.php/destinations/sw/sw_valais/saas_fee/kreuzboden_almagell/kreuzboden_almagell_detail · https://swissfamilyfun.com/almagelleralp-hike/ · https://wegwandern.ch/wanderung/saas-almagell-almagelleralp-almagellerhuette-kreuzboden-wanderung-wandern/
- **Mischabelhütte (3,340 m):** the classic brutal steep haul above Saas-Fee. **GAP — distance, T-grade, time, price and opening dates NOT verified in this pass.** Widely reported to be a T4-ish, fixed-rope, 1,550 m climb. Verify at https://www.mischabel.ch before using.

### 4.8 Val d'Anniviers, Lac de Moiry and the Cabane de Moiry

## 5. BERNESE OBERLAND / ALETSCH / GRIMSEL

### 5.1 Aletsch Glacier Panorama Trail — **SwitzerlandMobility route 39** (regional, 3 stages)
The Great Aletsch is **~22–23 km long**, the longest glacier in the Alps; UNESCO World Heritage since 2001.
- **Full route 39 — three day-stages, Belalp → Bellwald:**
  1. **Blatten, Belalp → Riederalp, Riederfurka** — **10 km / 6.2 mi, +700 m (2,300 ft) / −740 m (2,430 ft), 3 h 45, medium.** Best views of the **glacier snout and lower glacier**; crosses a **124 m suspension bridge over the Massa gorge, 80 m above the river**.
  2. **Riederalp, Riederfurka → Märjelensee** — AllTrails records **8.5 km / 5.3 mi, ~3 h, challenging.** This is the balcony section past **Moosfluh and Bettmerhorn** — the classic view.
  3. **Märjelensee → Bellwald.**
- **The popular single-day version (what most people actually do):** **Moosfluh (2,644 m) → Grosses Gufer → Rote Chumma → Märjelensee → Talligrat → Fiescheralp.** **12 km / 7.5 mi, +510 m (1,675 ft) / −630 m (2,065 ft), 3 h 45, medium.** Well-graded **T2** balcony path along the glacier's northern edge between Bettmerhorn and Eggishorn, with the ice **800 m below** you, continuously, the whole way. Passes **Restaurant Gletscherstube** at Märjelensee; the Talligrat variant adds Fiescher Glacier views and goes **through a lit tunnel** before dropping to Fiescheralp. **Cable car required at both ends** (Riederalp/Moosfluh up, Fiescheralp down). Season **July–October**.
- **Also on route 39:** the **Aspi-Titter suspension bridge (160 m, built 2016)**, and the protected **Aletschwald** with several-hundred-year-old (up to 1,000-year) stone pines. Four viewing platforms: **Hohfluh, Moosfluh, Bettmerhorn, Eggishorn.**
- **Base villages:** **Riederalp, Bettmeralp and Fiescheralp are all car-free**, reached only by cable car from the Rhône valley (Mörel, Betten, Fiesch). Excellent low-stress base.
- **Aletsch Arena official trail stats:** Aletsch Glacier Viewing Trail 6.84 km / +399 m / 2 h 50 (medium); Bettmerhorn via Riederfurka 7.65 km / +778 m / 2 h 40 (medium); Riederalp→Blatten "Knebel bridges" 12.12 km / +959 m / 4 h 30 (medium); **Moosfluh Geology Trail 706 m / 30 min / easy**; **UNESCO ridge trail Bettmerhorn→Eggishorn, 3 km, alpine grade.** 300 km of trails total. **Cable car singles from CHF 2.20; hiking packages CHF 17–37; guided experiences incl. glacier ice-cave tours CHF 14–140 pp.**
- **PERMAFROST/LANDSLIDE WARNING — the important one:** **six kilometres of hiking routes on the north slope of Moosfluh have been closed since 2016** because glacier retreat de-buttressed the slope and it is actively sliding. A dozen sensors monitor ground movement there. This is the textbook example of glacier-retreat-driven trail closure in Switzerland. **Verify which specific Moosfluh-area paths are open for 2027 before planning. GAP.**
- **Links:** https://www.myswitzerland.com/en-ch/experiences/route/aletsch-panoramaweg-39_01/ · https://www.myswitzerland.com/en-us/experiences/route/aletsch-panoramaweg-the-swiss-glacier-trail/ · https://schweizmobil.ch/en/hiking-in-switzerland/route-39/stage-2 · https://www.aletscharena.ch/en/planning-booking/summer-autumn/hiking · https://www.swissinfo.ch/ger/gesellschaft/naturgefahren_wird-das-wandern-in-den-schweizer-alpen-gefaehrlicher/43482582

### 5.2 Märjelensee
Glacial lake sitting directly on the edge of the Aletsch, reached on **stage 2/3 of route 39** or as a shorter out-and-back from **Fiescheralp** (roughly 2 h each way). **Restaurant Gletscherstube** on the shore serves food. **T2.** The lake gives the most intimate ice contact on the whole panorama route without stepping on a glacier.

### 5.3 Trift suspension bridge (Triftbrücke), Gadmen
- **Distance:** **6 km / 3.7 mi round trip** from the Triftbahn upper station. **Ascent/descent 695 m (2,280 ft).**
- **Time:** **3 h 20** official; most people report ~1.5 h uphill to the bridge.
- **Difficulty:** **Medium / T2–T3.** Steep and rocky — "straight uphill on a super rocky trail." Not technical, but a real climb.
- **Start/end:** **Trift gondola upper station**, out-and-back. **Cable car mandatory** — there is no reasonable walking alternative.
- **The bridge:** **170 m long, 100 m high**, over the Triftsee. It replaced a 2004 bridge that became unusable **because glacier retreat cut off the access** — one of the clearest visible climate signals on any Swiss trail.
- **Triftbahn cable car:** **only 8 people per cabin, departures every ~12 minutes — book online, this is the bottleneck.** Fares reported at **CHF 26 return (2024)** rising to **~CHF 30 (recent)**. **The cable car was renovated and did not reopen until end of June 2025 at the earliest, and timetable/ticket info changed after reopening — re-verify for 2027. GAP.**
- **Payoff:** at the end (turquoise glacier lakes and the glacier tongue after ~1.5 h), plus the bridge crossing itself.
- **Huts:** the return route passes **SAC Hut Windegg**. **Trifthütte was destroyed by an avalanche in 2021 and reopens June 2026** — worth confirming, it would be a fresh option for 2027.
- **Season:** June–October.
- **Links:** https://www.myswitzerland.com/en-ch/experiences/route/trift-bridge-a-spectacular-pedestrian-bridge/ · https://www.newlyswissed.com/trift-suspension-bridge/ · https://swissfamilyfun.com/trift-suspension-bridge/ · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trift_Bridge

### 5.4 Gelmersee and the Gelmerbahn
- **Gelmerbahn:** **the steepest open funicular in Europe** (gradient up to 106%), 12 min, Handegg → Gelmersee. **2026 season: 6 June – 25 October.** Hours **08:36–16:48 in July/August**, 09:00–16:00 in June/Sept/Oct. **Fares: adult CHF 40 return / CHF 20 single; children 6–15 CHF 20 / CHF 10.** **Online booking strongly recommended; tickets must be exchanged at the valley station at least 15 min before departure; no refunds or changes.**
- **2026 CONSTRUCTION — check for 2027:** (a) **the valley station is a building site** — access is via steep stairs and a temporary trail, **add ~15 min**; (b) **the Gelmersee dam itself will be inaccessible in summer 2026**, though **the lakeside circuit trail remains open**. The Gelmerbahn turns 100 in 2026. **GAP: 2027 status of both works unknown.**
- **The walk:** an easy circuit of the reservoir from the top station; you can also walk down (or up) to Handegg instead of riding.
- **Links:** https://www.grimselwelt.ch/en/railways/gelmerbahn/ · https://swissfamilyfun.com/gelmerbahn/

## 6. ENGADIN / GRAUBÜNDEN

### 6.1 Val Roseg (Pontresina)
- **Distance:** the flat valley walk Pontresina → Hotel Roseg Gletscher is **~5.7 km / 3.5 mi each way with essentially no elevation gain (2 m)** — rated **Blue / Easy / T1**. Continuing to **Lej da Vadret** at the glacier makes it **7.2 km / 4.5 mi with 316 m (1,036 ft) gain, 2–2.5 h**, moderate.
- **Start/end:** Pontresina station, out-and-back. **No lift.** A **horse-drawn carriage** runs the valley track (see Val Fex note for the operator model). The valley is closed to cars.
- **Payoff:** at the far end — the Roseg glacier amphitheatre with Piz Roseg and Piz Bernina — but the whole walk is pleasant larch-forest-and-river stuff.
- **Best use in an itinerary:** the ideal **acclimatisation or recovery day** in the Engadin, and a strong rainy-day option because it stays low and forested.
- **Links:** https://www.alltrails.com/trail/switzerland/grisons/pontresina-val-roseg-lej-da-vadret · https://www.trailforks.com/trails/val-roseg-hike/

### 6.2 Piz Languard (3,262 m) — Pontresina
The Engadin's classic non-technical 3,000er and one of the best-value summits in Switzerland.
- **Two approaches:**
  - **From Alp Languard (chairlift):** **7.9 km / 4.9 mi, 885 m (2,903 ft) of gain, 4.5–5 h**, rated **hard**.
  - **From Pontresina (no lift):** **7.6 km / 4.7 mi, 1,392 m (4,566 ft) of gain, 4–4.5 h**, rated **hard**.
- **Difficulty:** **T3.** A regular hiking trail runs to ~2,900 m and continues to the summit; steep, relentless zigzags on scree near the top. No chains or glacier. **GAP: no official SAC T-grade captured — T3 is inferred from route character and the "hard" ratings.**
- **Hut:** **Chamanna Georgy (Georgyhütte), >3,200 m**, sits just below the summit — **food, drink and accommodation**, with a terrace facing the entire Bernina massif. Sleeping here for sunrise over Piz Palü/Bernina is a genuinely special, lesser-known option. **GAP: prices, capacity and opening dates not captured.**
- **Payoff:** entirely at the summit — a 360° view over the Bernina 4,000ers and their glaciers.
- **Links:** https://www.alltrails.com/trail/switzerland/grisons/piz-languard · https://www.hikingwalking.com/destinations/sw/sw_engadin/st_moritz/piz_languard · https://www.alpenwild.com/staticpage/piz-languard-a-classic-viewpoint-in-the-engadine/ · https://www.summitpost.org/piz-languard/150947

### 6.3 Lej da Staz and the Engadin lake walks
- **Lej da Staz (1,809 m)** sits in the **Stazerwald** between St. Moritz, Celerina and Pontresina. From **St. Moritz station** it's a **~90-min round trip, T1**, past the dairy. Trails also start **directly from Pontresina station** (~3 km to the lake).
- Moor lake, protected high moor around it, **swims to ~20 °C in summer**, jetty, nostalgic changing cabins, BBQ and picnic areas, restaurant, playground. Connecting trails let you finish in Celerina or Pontresina rather than doubling back.
- **Best use:** family/easy day, evening walk, or a swim after a hard day. Spectacular in October (larches) but that's outside your window.
- **Links:** https://www.pontresina.ch/en/guide/mountains-and-valleys/the-stazerwald-woodland-the-lej-da-staz · https://www.hikingwalking.com/destinations/sw/sw_engadin/st_moritz/lejstaz_celerina · https://swissfamilyfun.com/stazersee-st-moritz-lake-walk/

### 6.4 Swiss National Park (Zernez) — and its unusually strict rules
**This is the strictest protected area in the Alps. Read the rules before you plan anything here.** Officially, in the park:
- **"It is strictly forbidden to leave the marked paths as well as the resting areas marked with posts."** No off-trail, no shortcutting, no sitting down wherever you like.
- **Dogs are not allowed in the Park, not even on a lead.**
- **No natural object may be picked or removed: animals, plants, sticks, stones.**
- **Bathing in lakes, pools, streams and rivers is not permitted.**
- **No camp fires. No camping. No overnight in vehicles** except along the Pass dal Fuorn road during permitted daylight hours.
- **No cycling. No flying of any kind, including drones.**
- **Visits restricted to daylight hours** (civil twilight to civil twilight).
- **Park wardens enforce this and issue administrative fines.**
- The park is **closed in winter** as a wildlife protection area; the hiking season is roughly **June to late October**, which covers your dates fine.
- **There is exactly one place to sleep inside the park: Chamanna Cluozza.** **GAP: not verified in this pass — check nationalpark.ch.**
- **Trails:**
  - **Val Trupchun** — the popular one, in the south near S-chanf. **14 km / 8.7 mi, ~446 m (1,463 ft) gain, 4 h 30, moderate / T2.** Best wildlife in the park: red deer, ibex, chamois, marmots. Golden larches in autumn.
  - **Lais da Macun (Macun Lakes)** — a high glacial basin with **20+ lakes and tarns** beneath 3,000 m peaks, above Lavin/Zernez. **White-blue-white / difficult = T4.** ~**1,184 m (3,885 ft) of gain**, or ~700 m if you take a taxi to the trailhead. **"A long and challenging high mountain excursion, suitable only for seasoned, sure-footed walkers with a head for heights."** **Icy paths or snow must be reckoned with at all times around Munt Baselgia — including in August.** This is the lesser-known Engadin highlight but **it is a T4 and needs to be treated as such.**
  - **Il Fuorn nature trail** — 45 information boards, family-grade, at the Pass dal Fuorn.
- **Links:** https://nationalpark.ch/en/protection-regulations/ · https://nationalpark.ch/en/visit/educational-trails/ · https://www.alltrails.com/trail/switzerland/grisons/val-trupchun-alp-trupchun · https://www.hikingwalking.com/index.php/destinations/sw/sw_engadin/scuol/macun_lakes

### 6.5 Val Fex (Sils Maria)
- A quiet hanging valley at **1,800–2,000 m running up to the Fex glacier**, closed to private cars. **Easy to moderate / T1–T2**, adjustable from a gentle stroll to a longer push to **Alp Muot Selvas**.
- **Horse-drawn omnibus** on a set summer timetable from Sils: **~20 min to Fex-Platta, ~30 min to Fex-Crasta, ~50 min to the last stop Fex-Curtins.** Operator: https://www.coretti.ch

### 6.6 Sentiero Panoramico / Via Panoramica Val Bregaglia — **SwitzerlandMobility route 796**
The best "lesser-known but genuinely first-rate" long day in Graubünden, and it's Italian-speaking Switzerland with chestnut woods and granite.
- **Distance:** **15 km / 9.3 mi** (myswitzerland) or **14.2 km / 8.8 mi** (bergfex). **Ascent 600 m (1,970 ft), descent 950 m (3,120 ft).** Highest point **1,460 m** at the Fopeta creek.
- **Time:** **4 h 30** official (5 h return direction); **realistic 5–6 h.** bergfex says "about five hours."
- **Difficulty:** **T2** — explicitly stated by bergfex: "T2 mountain hiking, sturdy shoes, some surefootedness," medium fitness. myswitzerland rates it medium technical / **difficult physical** (it's the length and descent).
- **Start/end:** **Casaccia (1,458 m) → Soglio (1,090 m). Point-to-point, no lift needed — postbus at both ends.** Take the bus over the **Maloja pass** and get off at Casaccia, the first village in the valley; an hourly bus links Soglio (Promontogno stop) back to Casaccia.
- **Payoff:** repeated and building — you contour the sunny north side of the Bergell with the **Sciora group and Piz Badile** opposite; **Alp Durbegia at the halfway point** is a tranquil, isolated spot with the full panorama; the finish at **Soglio** is a museum-piece stone village (Segantini painted here) with chestnut specialities.
- **Shortcuts/extensions:** shortcut via Vicosoprano (−1.5 h) or Vicosoprano Nasciarina (−1 h); extend from Maloja (+45 min), onward to Castasegna (+45 min), or into Italy to Savogno (+2 h) and Chiavenna (+1 h more).
- **Season:** April–October.
- **Links:** https://www.myswitzerland.com/en-us/experiences/route/via-panoramica-val-bregaglia/ · https://www.bregaglia.ch/en/our-tips/71-sentiero-panoramico-from-casaccia-to-soglio · https://it.bergfex.com/sommer/graubuenden/touren/wanderung/26669,via-panoramica/ · https://www.hikr.org/tour/post40884.html

### 6.7 Morteratsch / Diavolezza — the accessible glacier front
- **Morteratsch Glacier Trail (Gletscherweg):** a **3 km path with 16 stations** documenting the glacier's retreat; **the glacier tongue is ~50 min walk from Morteratsch railway station.** **T1/T2, no equipment.** There is also a **2-hour interpretive trail with five discovery points** about the Pers and Morteratsch glaciers. **The single best "see a glacier without any risk" walk in Switzerland**, and quietly devastating as a climate exhibit.
- **Diavolezza (2,978 m) and Munt Pers:** cable car up from the Bernina Pass road; short walk to **Munt Pers (3,207 m)** for the Piz Palü / Piz Bernina / Morteratsch panorama. Non-glacial, T2–T3.
- **Links:** https://www.myswitzerland.com/en-us/experiences/diavolezza-isola-pers-morteratsch-glacier/ · https://www.corvatsch-diavolezza.ch/en/experiences/glacier-experience-trail · https://thephotohikes.com/hike-to-diavolezza-and-munt-pers-on-the-bernina-pass/

### 6.8 Heidi country (Maienfeld)
- **"In the footsteps of Heidi" / Heidi Trail:** **12 km / 7.5 mi, ascent and descent 639 m (2,096 ft), 3 h 50.** **Easy technical / medium physical (T1).** **Loop from Maienfeld railway station.** **Buggy-friendly.** Season April–November. Suitable ages 6+.
- The short village circuit alone is **~6.5 km / 4.0 mi in ~1 h 30**; the extension to **Heidialp (Ochsenberg)** adds ~1 h 45 and has **twelve storytelling stations**; **Bovel–Heididorf–Heidialp** is **9.5 km / 5.9 mi with 463 m (1,519 ft) gain, 3.5–4 h.** Ends at the Heidi Fountain honouring Johanna Spyri.
- **Honest assessment:** this is a low-altitude, high-kitsch, family-oriented walk in vineyards and pasture, not an alpine day. Include it only if travelling with children or if you want a Graubünden rest day; Maienfeld is also a serious wine village.
- **Links:** https://www.myswitzerland.com/en-us/experiences/route/in-the-footsteps-of-heidi/ · https://www.heididorf.ch/en/experience/heidi-trail/ · https://www.alltrails.com/trail/switzerland/grisons/bovel-heididorf-heidialp

## 7. CENTRAL SWITZERLAND, APPENZELL, TICINO, JURA / WEST

### 7.1 Stoos Ridge (Gratwanderung) — Klingenstock → Fronalpstock
The best short ridge walk in Switzerland and an easy day-trip from Lucerne or Zurich.
- **Distance:** **4.5 km / 2.8 mi** point-to-point (komoot's longer variant with the walk-out: 10.6 km / 6.6 mi).
- **Ascent/descent:** **+282 m (925 ft) / −302 m (990 ft)** on the ridge itself. Elevation 1,731–1,927 m.
- **Time:** **2 h 10 moving; ~4 h door-to-door** including all the cableways and a rest.
- **Difficulty:** **T2, explicitly stated.** "No technical difficulty," but lots of short ups and downs and **some steep drop-offs beside the path** — described as unsettling for vertigo sufferers but "not precarious, or exposed."
- **Start/end:** **Klingenstock (1,935 m) → Fronalpstock (1,922 m). Point-to-point; three separate lifts involved.**
- **Lifts:** **Stoosbahn — the steepest funicular in the world**, 1.7 km, Schwyz → Stoos, **daily year-round, every 30 min at :10 and :40.** Then the **Klingenstock chairlift** (2026: **daily 13 June – 11 October, 09:00–16:00**, weather permitting) up, and the **Fronalpstock chairlifts sections 1 and 2** (2026: **daily 1 May – 1 November, normally 09:00–16:00**) down.
- **Fares (2026):** **"Stoos Peak Experience Ticket" CHF 60 adult, CHF 20 children 6–15, under 6 free**, bought at the Schwyz Stoosbahn valley station. A funicular-only return Schwyz/Morschach → Stoos village is **CHF 23.20**. Discounts with Swiss Travel Pass and Half Fare Card.
- **Payoff:** continuous for the entire 2 hours — the serpentine **Lake Lucerne** below you, **Rigi and Pilatus** across the water, and a wall of Alps on a clear day. Density of view per minute of effort is arguably the best in the country.
- **Links:** https://www.moonhoneytravel.com/stoos-ridge-hike/ · https://swissfamilyfun.com/stoos-ridgeline-hike/ · https://fullsuitcase.com/stoos-ridge-hike-switzerland/ · https://www.novo-monde.com/en/stoos-hike-fronalpstock-klingenstock/

### 7.2 Rigi Panoramaweg (Rigi Kulm → Rigi Scheidegg)
- **Distance:** **8.2 km / 5.1 mi one way.** **Ascent 230 m (755 ft), descent 320 m (1,050 ft).**

### 7.3 Pilatus — Tomlishorn and the Blumenpfad

### 7.5 Appenzell / Alpstein — Seealpsee, Ebenalp, Äscher, Schäfler
The most photographed non-alpine landscape in Switzerland. Small, steep, dense, and it can be done in a day from Zurich.
- **Ebenalp → Berggasthaus Aescher:** **1.2 km / 0.75 mi, 324 m (1,063 ft), 1 h, easy.** Start at Ebenalp cable-car station. The path goes **through the Wildkirchli cave system** and emerges at the **Äscher guesthouse glued to the base of the cliff.** **This is a 25-minute walk from Ebenalp — the payoff is essentially immediate.**
- **Schäfler Ridge:** **5.5 km / 3.4 mi out-and-back, 372 m (1,220 ft), 2 h 15, easy–moderate.** Elevation 1,587–1,925 m. **Exposure:** the path **beyond Berggasthaus Schäfler is exposed but secured with fixed cables and stairs** — only for the sure-footed with no fear of heights.
- **Seealpsee circuit:** **7.3 km / 4.5 mi, 372 m (1,220 ft), 2 h 45, easy–moderate**, from **Wasserauen — no lift needed.** Two mountain inns on the lake.
- **Big combined loop:** **Wasserauen – Äscher – Ebenalp – Schäfler – Seealpsee – Wasserauen, ~14–14.5 km / 8.7–9.0 mi, ~1,030 m (3,380 ft) of gain, ~6 h 52, hard** — "requires very good fitness, sure-footedness, sturdy shoes and alpine experience." Recommended for **experienced hikers only** because of the narrow trail on a steep slope.
- **Cable car:** **Wasserauen → Ebenalp, 6 min, 40 people, every 15 min during operating hours.** **GAP: 2026/2027 fares and operating dates not captured — see https://www.ebenalp.ch.** Note: staying multiple nights in the region earns an **"Appenzeller Ferienkarte"** giving **one free cable car ride**.
- **Huts serving food:** **Berggasthaus Ebenalp (1,587 m)**, **Alp Chlus (1,726 m)**, **Berggasthaus Schäfler (1,925 m — food served until 19:30, cash and card)**, **Berggasthaus Aescher**, **Berggasthaus Seealpsee**, **Berggasthaus Meglisalp**, **Berggasthaus Bollenwees** at Fälensee. All private, none SAC. **Äscher and Seealpsee huts book out far ahead in August.**
- **Wild camping and drones are forbidden.**
- **Hoher Kasten → Staubern → Saxer Lücke → Fälensee → Bollenwees → Brülisau:** **19.3 km / 12.0 mi, +505 m (1,657 ft) / −1,385 m (4,544 ft), ~6 h, moderate–difficult.** Alternative stats: 16.7 km / 10.4 mi, 590 m gain, 6.5–7.5 h. **Cable car up to Hoher Kasten required; point-to-point, bus back from Brülisau.** Official **white-red-white** mountain trail (T2–T3), with **fixed cables or handrails on exposed ridge sections.** **Saxer Lücke** — the notch in a knife-edge limestone fin — is the iconic viewpoint, roughly midway. This is the best full day in Appenzell and much less crowded than Seealpsee.
- **Other Alpstein routes worth noting:** **Lisengrat ridge** (Säntis → Rotsteinpass, **5.1 km / 3.2 mi, 400 m, 2 h 30, moderate–difficult** — genuinely exposed, cabled); **Marwees to Meglisalp circuit** (14.5 km / 9.0 mi, 1,225 m / 4,020 ft, 6 h 45, **difficult**, no lift); **Alpstein High Trail 3-day, 33 km / 20.5 mi**.
- **Links:** https://www.moonhoneytravel.com/appenzell-hikes/ · https://www.moonhoneytravel.com/schafler-ridge-hike-appenzell/ · https://www.moonhoneytravel.com/seealpsee-hike-appenzell-switzerland/ · https://www.journeyera.com/saxer-lucke-hike/ · https://www.alltrails.com/trail/switzerland/appenzell-inner-rhodes/brullisau-bollenwees-staubern-hoher-kasten

### 7.6 Ticino
- **Sentiero Verzasca — SwitzerlandMobility route 74.** **35 km / 21.7 mi** through the entire Verzasca valley from **Sonogno** down to Locarno/the lake, **split into two stages** (stage 1 Sonogno → Lavertezzo). Follows an old mule trail past stone rustici, chapels, little bridges, waterfalls and vineyards. **T1/T2, low altitude.** **Lavertezzo** is the famous stop: the **double-arched Ponte dei Salti** over emerald pools. **Postbus runs the valley**, so any section can be walked and bussed back — this is the key logistical point.
  - **Warning:** Lavertezzo is **extremely crowded on summer weekends** and the "emerald pools" have a real drowning history (flash releases and cold water). The recommendation from Ticino sources is to go **on a weekday and early**, or choose Val Bavona instead.
  - **Links:** https://www.myswitzerland.com/en-us/experiences/route/sentiero-verzasca-74_01/ · https://www.ticinotopten.ch/en/trekking/sentierone-verzasca-valley · https://www.alltrails.com/trail/switzerland/ticino/sentiero-verzasca-tappa-1-sonogno-lavertezzo · https://www.wanderwego.com/hiking/sentiero-verzasca
- **Val Bavona — the quiet alternative and arguably the more interesting valley.** A steep 10 km side valley of the Vallemaggia between **Cavergno and San Carlo**, with **12 rustic hamlets** wedged among gigantic landslide boulders. **Famously has no mains electricity in the hamlets.** **Foroglio** is the star: a **50 m waterfall** dropping right behind the stone village; **steep stone stairs climb to the top of the falls.** A good linking walk is **Sonlerto → Foroglio** past the boulder-built hamlets.
  - **Robiei cable car** from **San Carlo** at the head of the valley climbs to **Robiei**, where a trail circles **Lago Robiei with the Basodino glacier** behind. **GAP: Robiei cable-car 2026/2027 dates and fares not captured.**
  - **Sentiero Cristallina (SwitzerlandMobility route 59)** is the multi-day route through this area — **GAP: stats not captured, the myswitzerland page failed to load.** https://www.myswitzerland.com/en-us/experiences/route/sentiero-cristallina-59_01/
  - **Links:** https://www.ticinotopten.ch/en/trekking/bavona-valley · https://www.myswitzerland.com/en-ch/experiences/bavona-valley-and-foroglio-waterfall/ · https://www.touringswitzerland.com/our-first-hike-in-the-wild-bavona-valley/
- **Cardada–Cimetta (Locarno's local mountain).** Funicular **Locarno → Orselina**, then cable car to **Cardada** and a chairlift to **Cimetta (1,671 m)**. Trail from Cardada over **Alpe Cardada** to Cimetta; several grotti/mountain huts en route. **From Cimetta you can see the lowest and the highest point in Switzerland at the same time.** There's a **suspension-bridge-style panoramic walkway** at Cardada.
  - **Fares:** **CHF 47 return Locarno → Cimetta** = CHF 8 funicular Locarno–Orselina return + CHF 39 cable car + chairlift return.
  - **Best use:** half-day, arrival/departure day, or a hot-day escape from Locarno.

- **Monte Generoso (1,704 m).** **Cogwheel railway from Capolago** on Lake Lugano, dating from **1890**, **35-min ride**, terminating at Mario Botta's "Fiore di pietra" summit building. **2026 return fare CHF 72 adult in summer season (2 May – 25 October 2026)**; CHF 54 in winter season. Summit views over Valle di Muggio (Switzerland's southernmost valley), the Lombardy lakes and, on a clear day, the Po plain to the Apennines. Walkable down; several trails from the summit.
  - **Links:** https://www.montegeneroso.ch/en/plan/ferrovia/high-season-railway-prices · https://www.myswissalps.com/activity/monte-generoso/

### 7.7 Jura and Lake Geneva
- **Creux du Van (Neuchâtel).** A **rock cirque roughly 1 km+ across and 160–200 m high** carved by ice and water — the single most impressive non-alpine landform in Switzerland.
  - **From Noiraigue (the classic):** the **"14 Contours"** trail — **~15 km / 9.3 mi round trip, ascent and descent 780 m (2,560 ft), ~4 h 30**; some sources rate the harder variants **12.9 km / 8.0 mi, 798 m (2,618 ft), 5.5–6 h.** **Difficulty T2 (medium)**; the 14 Contours climb is rocky, rooted and slippery when wet.
  - **Via Ferme Robert / Les Oeuillons:** an alternative approach through beech forest from the **Ferme Robert mountain inn (972 m, built 1750)**.
  - **komoot's standard loop:** 12.2 km / 7.6 mi, 4 h 30, moderate.
  - **Point-to-point vs loop:** loop from Noiraigue station — **train to the trailhead, no lift needed.** That's unusual and convenient.
  - **The payoff is at the top and it's abrupt** — you walk through forest and then the ground simply ends at the rim. **Unfenced cliff edge; ibex and chamois are frequently on the rim.** Season May–October.
  - **Links:** https://www.alltrails.com/trail/switzerland/neuchatel/le-creux-du-van · https://www.bergfex.com/sommer/jura-drei-seen-land/touren/wanderung/616594,von-noiraigue-nach-creux-du-van/ · https://www.komoot.com/smarttour/e1404807455/creux-du-van-trail · https://www.alpenwild.com/staticpage/le-creux-du-van-a-scenic-overlook-in-the-jura/

Wine cellars (*caveaux*) in Rivaz, Epesses, Riex, Cully. **Ideal arrival/departure-day walk or a hot-weather evening walk.**

Trails from Plan-Francey and a **via ferrata** on the summit pyramid. Gruyères itself (walled medieval town, cheese dairy, Château, HR Giger museum) pairs with a short walk.

## 8. MULTI-DAY HUT-TO-HUT TREKS AS 2–4 DAY SEGMENTS

### 8.1 The SAC hut system — booking mechanics (applies to all of the below)
- **153 SAC huts, 9,000+ bed spaces.**
- **Prices: overnight CHF 25–45 per adult** depending on hut standard, room type and season; **half board (dinner, bed, breakfast) CHF 60–90 per person.** Non-SAC/private huts (Berggasthaus, Berghotel, Cabane) are generally more and set their own rates — e.g. **Hörnlihütte dorm + half board CHF 150.**
- **Member discount:** SAC members (and reciprocal-rights clubs — Alpine Club of Canada has reciprocity with UIAA clubs) normally pay materially less. **GAP: the myswitzerland SAC page does not state member discount amounts. Verify per hut.**
- **Dinner:** communal tables, fixed menu — soup, main, dessert, sometimes salad. Pasta, rösti, sausage, cheese specialities, baked dishes. **"Hiking tea" for the next day is provided and often included in the room rate.** Sandwiches and trail provisions purchasable.
- **Booking:** phone, email, or the **online SAC reservation system** (built by ELCA). **Reservations "strongly recommended"** — in your window they are effectively mandatory. **Timely cancellation is free at all SAC huts** — so book early and speculatively; there is no financial penalty for changing your mind in good time.
- **BOOKING WINDOW — the key number: "reservations for 2027 will be available starting November 2026"** (Britannia Hütte SAC). Treat **November 2026** as the general opening for the 2027 summer season. **Private huts differ: the Hörnlihütte opened its 2026 bookings on 1 April 2026.** Book the popular ones (Blüemlisalp, Hörnli, Cabane du Mont Fort, Cabane de Prafleuri, Europahütte, Faulhorn) **the week booking opens** for late-July/August dates.
- **Bring:** **sleeping-bag liner is mandatory at most huts** (Blüemlisalp sells silk CHF 60 / cotton CHF 38, rents CHF 10; Hörnli sells at CHF 43). **Cash in CHF** — some huts are cashless but not all, and phone charging is often unavailable. Indoor shoes/crocs provided. **Dogs generally not accepted.**

## 9. GLACIER SAFETY — what crosses ice, what needs a guide, what is a walk-up

### Genuine walk-ups (NO glacier travel, no equipment, no guide)
Everything in this document is a walk-up **except** the items in the next two sections. Specifically confirmed safe non-glacier routes that *look* glacial:
- **Aletsch Panorama Trail (route 39)** — you walk on a **balcony path 800 m ABOVE the glacier**, never on it. **T2.** Guided *ice-cave* tours onto the Aletsch are sold separately from CHF 14–140 pp.
- **Matterhorn Glacier Trail (Trockener Steg → Schwarzsee)** — beside and between glacier-fed lakes, on rock and moraine. Moderate.
- **Morteratsch Glacier Trail** — moraine and interpretive path to the glacier snout, ~50 min from the station. **Best zero-risk glacier encounter in Switzerland.**
- **Trift bridge** — over the glacier valley, not the ice.
- **Märjelensee**, **Cabane de Moiry viewpoint**, **Val Roseg / Lej da Vadret**, **Gornergrat** — all rock/moraine viewing positions.
- **Hörnliweg** — **no glacier**, despite the altitude. Its difficulty (T4) is exposure and fixed cables/steel stairs, not ice.

### Requires a mountain guide — do not attempt independently
- **Diavolezza → Pers Glacier → Isla Persa → Morteratsch Glacier → Morteratsch station.** **~5 h, 1,100 m of altitude difference, roped glacier travel across two glaciers.** Guide is **mandatory** — booked through **Go Vertical / Bergsteigerschule Pontresina**. **From CHF 99.00 per person. Runs July to October.** Suitable for "average" hikers *with* professional guidance; high-altitude hiking experience recommended. **This is the single most accessible real glacier crossing in Switzerland and the one to book if you want that experience.** https://www.corvatsch-diavolezza.ch/en/experiences/glacier-experience-diavolezza · https://bergsteiger-pontresina.ch/engadin-stmoritz/gletscherwanderung-diavolezza-morteratsch/?lang=en
- **Any route beyond a hut onto the ice** — e.g. Blüemlisalp glacier tours from the Blüemlisalphütte, Mischabel glacier routes from Saas-Fee, anything above the Cabane de Moiry. The huts sit at the *edge* of glacier terrain: walking to the hut is fine, walking past it is not.
- **Anything graded T5/T6.**

### Grey zone — sure-footedness and no vertigo, but no glacier gear
- **Hörnliweg (T4)** — fixed ropes, metal pegs, chains, and a grated steel staircase over a drop. Guided option CHF 140 pp in a group of 4+.
- **Macun Lakes, Swiss National Park (T4, white-blue-white)** — "icy paths or snow must be reckoned with **at all times**" near Munt Baselgia, **including August.**
- **Lisengrat, Alpstein** — exposed cabled ridge.
- **Saas Erlebnisweg (Adventure Trail)** — ladders, cliff-bolted steps and suspension bridges, unroped.
- **Sprutz waterfall detour, Mürren** — steep, unrailed, wet rock.
- **Sefinafurgga and Hohtürli (Via Alpina 12/13)** — loose shale and scree, wooden steps, **residual snow on the north sides into August.**

### Early-August snow expectation
Late July/early August is the **minimum-snow window** of the Alpine year. Expect: **no snow below ~2,600 m**; **patchy old snow on north-facing cols above ~2,700 m** (Hohtürli, Sefinafurgga, Fenêtre d'Arpette, Macun/Munt Baselgia, Col de Torrent); **permanent snow/ice above ~3,000 m** on shaded aspects. **Microspikes are unnecessary for T1–T3 routes but are cheap insurance for the T4s and the high Haute Route cols.** **GAP — this paragraph is expert inference, not a sourced claim; verify against live conditions on schweizmobil.ch and the SAC portal in July 2027.**

## 10. CLOSURES, DAMAGE AND CONSTRUCTION — 2026/2027

**Systemic context (sourced):** **More than 70 hiking trails in Switzerland are closed at any given time due to natural hazards** — rockfall, landslides, debris flows. Climate change drives this through heavy rainfall, **thawing permafrost** and drier summers destabilising mountain slopes. Christoph Hegg (WSL, the Swiss Federal Institute for Forest, Snow and Landscape Research): relocations and closures of hiking routes **will become more frequent** because permafrost thaw makes rock more brittle and unstable. Christian Huggel (University of Zurich): smaller and occasionally larger rockfalls have become **more frequent over the last 10–30 years** and should be expected more often than 50–60 years ago. The SAC has **abandoned certain hiking and climbing routes** outright over rockfall and landslide hazard.
