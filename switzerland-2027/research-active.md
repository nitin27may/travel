### 1.2 PubliBike (national bike-share, dock/station based, SwissPass or app unlock)

Source: https://www.publibike.ch/en/subscriptions

- **B-Quick National** (pay as you go, no subscription):
  - Bike: **CHF 3 for the first 30 min**, then CHF 0.10/min
  - E-Bike: **CHF 5 for the first 30 min**, then CHF 0.20/min
  - E-Scooter: CHF 1 unlock + CHF 0.20/min
- **Day Pass: CHF 29** for 24 h, one bike or e-bike.
- B-Fit Local annual subs ~CHF 69/yr (e.g. Zurich: bike free first 30 min then CHF 0.10/min; e-bike CHF 2 first 15 min, CHF 1 second 15 min, then CHF 0.20/min). Premium e-bike subs CHF 169–199/yr.
- Up to 5 vehicles at once per account; max rental duration 24 h.
- Unlock via app or SwissPass card; return to any station in the network.
  Source: https://www.sbb.ch/en/offers/rental-bikes-bikesharing
- **Verdict for a 14-night trip:** the CHF 29 day pass is poor value against Rent a Bike CHF 38/day for a proper bike. PubliBike is a city-hop tool, not a touring tool.

### 2.1 Rhone Route — No. 1 — Andermatt → Geneva. THE headline "ride down" route.

Source (stage table): https://www.schweizmobil.ch/en/cycling-in-switzerland/routes/route-01.html (via aggregated snippet)
Source (totals, surface): https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhone-Route

- **350 km, 8 stages. 321 km asphalt / 29 km natural surface** (DE Wikipedia says 26 km unpaved — minor discrepancy).
- **Total ascent 3,700 m; total descent 4,700 m riding Andermatt → Geneva.** Reverse direction 3,400 m ascent.
- "The route mostly runs downhill with some climbs, and **from Brig it is almost completely flat and well suited for leisure riders**."
- **Direction: ride Andermatt/Oberwald → Geneva (east→west, downhill-dominant).**

| Stage | Section | km | Ascent | Descent |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Andermatt – Oberwald | 38 | 1,000 m | 1,100 m |
| 2 | Oberwald – Brig | 50 | 560 m | 1,250 m |
| 3 | Brig – Sierre | 42 | 160 m | 300 m |
| 4 | Sierre – Martigny | 44 | 90 m | 150 m |
| 5 | Martigny – Montreux | 48 | 160 m | 240 m |
| 6 | Montreux – Morges | 42 | 560 m | 560 m |
| 7 | Morges – Genève | 58 | 620 m | 600 m |
| 8 | Genève – Bois de Chancy | 28 | 340 m | 320 m |

- **Furka Base Tunnel car-train "Veloverlad" (Realp↔Oberwald)** lets you skip **890–1,070 m of climbing** depending on direction — i.e. skip Stage 1's Furka climb entirely and start the pure descent at Oberwald.
  Source: https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhone-Route
- **Best family/easy segment: Stages 3–5 (Brig → Sierre → Martigny → Montreux), 134 km, only ~410 m total ascent over 690 m descent, all valley-floor, with SBB stations at every town for a train back.**
- Valais tourism version of the route: https://www.valais.ch/en/explore/activities/road-cycling/routes/rhone-route
- Andermatt-region version: https://www.outdooractive.com/en/route/bike-riding/ferienregion-andermatt/rhone-route/17746223/
- GPS/described version: https://www.biroto.eu/en/cycle-route/switzerland/rhone-cycle-route/rt00000238

### 2.2 Rhine Route — No. 2 — Andermatt → Basel
Source: https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhein-Route

- **430 km. 362 km asphalt / 68 km unpaved.**
- **Descending toward Basel: 2,000 m of climbing. Reverse (Basel→Andermatt): 3,200 m.** So again, ride east→west/north.
- Andermatt → Oberalppass → Vorderrhein → Alpenrhein → Lake Constance → High Rhine → Basel. Passes Chur, Rorschach, Schaffhausen, Koblenz.
- Veloland itself recommends rail bike-transport for two sections: **Andermatt–Oberalppass (saves 610–900 m of climbing)** and the **Ruinaulta gorge near Ilanz**.
- Continues internationally as the Rhine cycle route to Rotterdam.

### 2.3 North–South Route — No. 3 — Basel → Chiasso, over the Gotthard
Source: https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nord-S%C3%BCd-Route

- **365 km, ~3,600 m of climbing in each direction. ~30 km natural surface, remainder paved.**
- Basel → Aarau → Lucerne → Andermatt → **Gotthard Pass** → Bellinzona → Lugano → Chiasso.
- Since summer 2019 there is a dedicated hiking/cycling path through the **Schöllenen Gorge (Göschenen ↔ Andermatt)**, which removed a previously compulsory bike-transport section for uphill riders.
- Scenic stretches along Lake Lucerne.
- **The Tremola** (see §3) is the historic cobbled south ramp of the Gotthard and is on/adjacent to this route.

### 2.4 Aare Route — No. 8 — Gletsch → Koblenz
Source: https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aare-Route

- **305 km. 1,700 m ascent / 3,100 m descent riding Gletsch → Koblenz. 64 km natural surface.**
- Crosses the **Grimselpass** at the start, then follows the Aare through **Thun, Bern, Biel, Solothurn, Aarau** to the Rhine confluence at Koblenz.
- Strongly downhill-biased in the Gletsch→Koblenz direction. Bern–Thun and Bern–Solothurn are flat, well-served by trains.

### 2.5 Lakes Route — No. 9 — Montreux → Rorschach
Source: https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seen-Route

- **505 km. 4,200 m ascent west→east. 64 km natural surface.**
- Thirteen lakes incl. Lake Geneva, Lake Thun, Lake Lucerne, Lake Zug, Lake Zurich, Lake Constance. Through Thun, Lucerne, Zug, Sargans.
- **Using "Veloverlad" (bike-carriage on trains/boats) cuts total climbing to 1,430 m westbound / 1,490 m eastbound** — a >65% reduction. This is the single clearest official endorsement of the "ride down, train up" pattern.

### 2.6 Engadin valley path & Val Poschiavo descent from Bernina
- **GAP.** The Engadin valley cycle path (Maloja→Zernez/Scuol, largely flat, along the Inn) and the Bernina→Poschiavo→Tirano descent (~1,880 m of descent over 37 km on the road side — see §3 Bernina) could not be sourced to an official page in this pass.
- Confirmed related: the **Bernina Express (673) mountain-bike route** is catalogued at https://www.maps.engadin.ch/en/tour/mountainbiking/bernina-express-673-/23435889/
- The rail return from Tirano to Pontresina/St. Moritz is on the Rhaetian Railway — **but see §2.8: the Bernina Express panoramic service does NOT take bikes; the ordinary RhB regional trains on the same line do.**

### 2.7 Lake Geneva & Lavaux vineyard riding
- **GAP.** Rhone Route stages 5–7 (Martigny–Montreux–Morges–Genève, 148 km) run along the north shore and through/below the Lavaux terraces. Lavaux-specific cycling route detail is at https://www.vaud.ch/en/experiences/lavaux-vineyards-by-bike (redirected from myvaud.ch; not fetched).
- Lavaux is a UNESCO World Heritage vineyard terrace landscape between Lausanne and Montreux; the through-vineyard roads are steep and narrow, the lakeside Rhone Route is the flat option. **Unverified detail — confirm.**

### 2.8 BIKES ON SWISS TRAINS — "ride down, train up" (WELL VERIFIED)

**Tickets** — source: https://www.sbb.ch/en/offers/bike-day-pass and https://www.myswitzerland.com/en-us/planning/offers/bike-day-pass-sbb/
- **Bike Day Pass: CHF 15** — one bicycle or e-bike, all day, on trains and most public transport, unrestricted.
- **Multiple Day Bike Pass: CHF 90** for 6 days of your choosing. (Best value for a 14-night trip with 5–6 riding days.)
- **Annual bike pass: CHF 260/yr.** https://www.sbb.ch/en/offers/annual-bike-pass
- **Bike space reservation: CHF 2** on any Swiss train that requires one; bookable up to shortly before departure.
- You must also hold a valid passenger ticket/travelcard for the same class.
- Children under 6 and holders of a GA Travelcard for Children carry a bike free.

**Reservations** — source: https://www.sbb.ch/en/travel-information/individual-needs/travelling-with-bikes/carriage-bikes-train.html
- **21 March – 31 October: bike reservations are MANDATORY on IC trains on certain routes.** On all other InterCity lines they are mandatory **Friday–Sunday**, plus Maundy Thursday, Easter Monday, the Wednesday before Ascension, Ascension Day, Whit Monday, and **1 August (Swiss National Day)**.
  → **This directly hits the trip window: 1 August 2027 is a Sunday, and the whole 27 Jul–10 Aug period is inside the mandatory season. Assume every IC leg with a bike needs a CHF 2 reservation.**
- **Self-service:** you load and unload the bike yourself. Look for coach doors with the bicycle pictogram.
- Tandems and cargo bikes: only on **RE, S and R** category SBB trains — not IC/IR.
- Excluded: bikes over 2 m long; e-bikes with combustion engines.
- **Folding bikes, folded, count as hand luggage — free, no ticket.** Same for bikes packed in a transport bag with the front wheel detached.

**Trains that refuse bikes**
- **Bernina Express: self-service bicycle transport is NOT allowed.** Bikes CAN be carried on the ordinary RhB regional trains over the same Bernina line. Bikes also cannot go on the Bernina Express **Bus**.
  Source: https://berninaexpresstickets.com/frequently-asked-questions/ and https://www.rhb.ch/en/panoramic-journeys/bernina-express/
- **Glacier Express: bicycles are not permitted on board** (along with very large suitcases, skis, snowboards, e-scooters).
  Source: https://www.myswissalps.com/activity/glacier-express/
- **GAP:** GoldenPass Express, Gotthard Panorama Express and Luzern–Interlaken Express bike policies not verified.

## 4. MOUNTAIN BIKING AND BIKE PARKS

### 4.1 Lenzerheide — "Bike Kingdom" (UCI MTB World Cup venue)
Source: https://www.bikekingdom.ch/en/Regions/Lenzerheide and https://www.bikekingdom.ch/en/Resort-Lifts/Tickets-rates

- Bike Kingdom is a **three-region network: Arosa + Lenzerheide + Chur.**
- Lenzerheide bike park: **five graded gravity lines**
  - **FLOWline** — beginner
  - **PRIMEline** and **SHOREline** — intermediate
  - **STYLEline** — black
  - **STRAIGHTline** — **the UCI World Cup track**, steep and fast
  - **HIKINGline** — walking path alongside the park

## 8. GLACIER AND HIGH-ALPINE FOR NON-ALPINISTS

### 8.1 Breithorn 4,164 m — "the easiest 4,000er" (FULLY VERIFIED, and the numbers matter)
Source: https://zermatt.swiss/en/p/breithorn-ascent-4164m-zermatters-01tVj000004sJoFIAU (operator: ZERMATTERS, Bahnhofstrasse 58, Zermatt, +41 27 966 24 66, info@zermatters.ch)

- **Price: CHF 240.00 per person, in groups of 3–6.** **Mountain railway tickets are NOT included** (you need the Matterhorn Glacier Paradise lift on top).
- **Duration: 6 hours total; 3–4 h for the ascent depending on fitness.** Back in Zermatt by early afternoon.
- **Group: minimum 3 participants, maximum 6 per guide.**
- **Requirements: "experienced mountain hiker"; NO prior climbing experience needed. Acclimatisation hiking beforehand is recommended. Minimum age 18.**
- **Gear provided: crampons, climbing harness, ski pole.**
- Bring: mountain boots with ankle support, 30 L pack, weather-resistant layers, gloves, hat, sunglasses, sun cream, snacks, water.
- Meeting point: Matterhorn Express station, **08:30** (seasonal variation — confirm).
- Route: glacier crossing to the Breithorn south face, descent by the same route.
- Two other Breithorn listings exist in the Zermatt directory (different providers/formats): https://zermatt.swiss/en/p/breithorn-01tVj000005EwcUIAS and https://zermatt.swiss/en/p/breithorn-01tVj000005DpTJIA0

**Responsibility note for the synthesiser:** the operator itself frames this as suitable for an *experienced mountain hiker with no climbing experience*, guided, roped, with crampons supplied, on a glacier, at 4,164 m, minimum age 18. It is a legitimate recommendation **only** with a guide, only with prior acclimatisation, and only for someone comfortable with a full day at altitude. The lift does most of the vertical (Matterhorn Glacier Paradise is at 3,883 m), which is exactly why it is "easy" — and also why altitude sickness is the main realistic risk rather than technical difficulty. Do not present it as a casual add-on.
