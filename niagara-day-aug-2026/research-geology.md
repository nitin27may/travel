# Research: What this place physically is (Aug 28 2026)

The orientation section rests on this. Sources at the bottom.

## The gorge is the Falls' own footprint
Niagara Falls is not where it started. Since the last glaciation the Falls have been
eating their way upstream, cutting the gorge behind them as they retreat. **The Niagara
Glen is a stretch of gorge the Falls carved and then abandoned** — you are standing on
the floor of a canyon the waterfall dug on its way past, roughly 4,000+ years ago.
That is the single most useful fact for a visitor: the Glen is not near the Falls, it is
where the Falls *used to be*.

## The Whirlpool and St. David's Buried Gorge
- Before the last (Wisconsin) glaciation, an older river had already carved a valley here
  — **St. David's Buried Gorge**. Roughly 1,200 m long, 300 m wide, 91 m deep.
- The glacier's retreat, about 12,000 years ago, **filled that valley with glacial silt**
  and it never reopened.
- About **4,200 years ago** the retreating Niagara Falls cut backwards into the buried
  gorge. Hitting loose silt instead of hard rock, the river tore sideways into the old
  valley and flushed the fill out.
- The result is the **Niagara Whirlpool** — the river makes a near right-angle turn and
  spins, because it is running through a re-excavated ancient channel rather than a
  channel it cut itself.

## Why the forest is unusual
The gorge floor is **Carolinian forest** — the northern tip of a zone that properly
belongs to the Ohio Valley and the American southeast. The gorge walls trap heat and
shelter it from wind, creating a microclimate warmer than the tableland above. This is
why the vegetation at the bottom of the stairs looks different from the vegetation at
the top, over only 60 m of descent.

## The boulders
The huge blocks scattered across the Glen floor are talus — roof collapse. The gorge is
capped by hard dolostone over softer shale; the shale erodes, undercuts the cap, and
slabs of dolostone drop. Those blocks are why the Glen is one of Ontario's few
established bouldering areas (Niagara Parks issues permits for it).
