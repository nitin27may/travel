Before the days start

What this place physically is

Chamonix sits at about 1,000 m in a valley running roughly north-east to south-west. On the south-east side is the Mont Blanc massif: Mont Blanc at 4,805–4,808 m, the Aiguille du Midi at 3,842 m, the Aiguilles de Chamonix and the Mer de Glace. On the north-west side are the Aiguilles Rouges: Brevent at 2,525 m, Flegere, Lac Blanc and the Index. The two sides face each other across about 1,000 m of valley floor. The exact bearing of the valley is a GAP.

Why it matters to a hiker. The famous Chamonix views are taken from the Aiguilles Rouges, looking across at the massif. The Mont Blanc faces point north-west toward the valley, so they are back-lit in the morning and front-lit from about 14:00 to sunset (sun azimuth 190–283 degrees). That one fact sets the hour of most days below. The Aiguilles Rouges are named for their colour in early-morning light.

What you cannot get anywhere else. A cable car from 1,033 m to 3,842 m in one continuous rise, and a lake, Lac Blanc at 2,352 m, that mirrors the whole massif on calm evenings. You can also see glacier retreat measured in stairs: the Mer de Glace surface has dropped about 4–6 m a year, and the stair count down to the ice cave grows every season.

Why 9, not 7 or 11

Why 9 and not 7. Day 7 to day 9 is where the upper valley and a third swap day come in. Neither crosses the lift risk line.

Why 9 and not 11. Day 10 is the first day that could hit the 2028 closing Sunday, and day 11 the first that could have no Brevent or Flegere at all.

Why arrive Fri 1 rather than Sat 2. Both keep lift days before 10 Sep. Arriving on the 1st makes the last full day Fri 8, so even a 9 Sep surprise closure costs nothing. The departure day also catches the Saturday market. A UTMB on 28 Aug–3 Sep would overlap either arrival date equally (UTMB 2028 dates are a GAP).

Decision rule. If CMB publishes 2028 dates with the Brevent, Planpraz and Index group closing on 17 Sep, nothing in this plan changes. You could add a day, but flights will already be bought by then.

The full Tour du Mont Blanc: no

  • Length. Official site: 5–12 days, most commonly 7–10, about 170 km and close to 10,000 m of ascent. A 10–11 day TMB fills the whole 1–14 Sep window with no buffer.
  • Lift timing works against it. Starting anticlockwise from Les Houches on 2 Sep puts the Chamonix-side stages on about 11–13 Sep, exactly when their lift shortcuts close.
  • Huts close around 20 Sep. Refuge de la Croix du Bonhomme (stage 2) is closed for renovation until about June 2029.
  • Booking. 2027 bookings opened 15 Oct 2026; by the same pattern 2028 would open about 15 Oct 2027 (inference, unpublished). Peak huts book 6–9 months ahead.
  • Rockfall. The Swiss section between Prayon and Branche (Orsieres) has been rerouted since July 2026.

Hut-free alternative (recommended). Walk the Chamonix side of the TMB as day hikes from valley beds: Grand Balcon Sud (Day 2), Flegere to Lac Blanc (Day 4), Col de Balme (Day 7). The optional Lac Blanc night matches the official site’s “2-day/1-night taster”. A full TMB is better as its own trip in July or August.

Booking, cut-off, hazards, decision rule

Booking. Timed slot online, with new tickets released D-1; same-day box office subject to availability. Pass holders must reserve too. GAP: whether a summit return lets you get off at the mid-station. Ask; the official “North Balcony package” is EUR 68.40. Both lifts are covered by the Multipass.

Weather rule. At 07:00, check the Meteo-France Chamonix bulletin and the summit. If it is clouded in, move this day to Day 7 or Day 8 and do Day 7 now.

Cut-off. If you are not past Signal Forbes by 13:00, skip the cave and catch the train. The last train from the summit was 16:30 in 2026.

Cold. Summit estimated -9 to +1 C before wind chill, from a standard lapse rate (inference). Bring gloves and a hat.

Hazards. The ride gains about 2,800 m in one go. Altitude symptoms can appear from about 2,500 m on a fast ascent (third-party source). Children under 3 are not allowed and under 5 is not recommended. On the walk take the right fork at 2,071 m via Signal Forbes (2,198 m); the left shortcut goes through couloirs the tourist office calls dangerous in snow. At Montenvers the stairs down to the ice cave are given as 100, 200, about 430–440 and about 580 steps in different sources, metal openwork, and the climb back up is the hard part. Do not walk out onto the ice beyond the cave: in September the bare ice and crevasses are most exposed.

Wet-weather swap. Montenvers and the ice cave alone, which works in cloud.

  • In Jan 2026 ATMB said 2027 and 2028 repeat the autumn closure model: one cycle of about 3.5 months (15 weeks) each autumn.

Physical risk

Hazards and gotchas

Hazards

  • Altitude, Aiguille du Midi. A rise of about 2,800 m in one ride. Symptoms (nausea, dizziness, fatigue, rapid pulse) can start from about 2,500 m, and fitness is no protection (third-party source). Keep the visit short if you feel unwell. GAP: official medical guidance; see a doctor if anyone has a heart condition or is pregnant.
  • Cold. The summit is near or below 0 C in a normal September (estimated -9 to +1 C before wind chill).
  • Mer de Glace stairs. Sources give 100 to about 580 steps. Metal openwork, steep, not wheelchair-accessible. The count grows each year. Stay off the open ice without a guide.
  • Ladders, Cheserys to Lac Blanc. Exposed; climb them, do not descend them; avoid when wet. The Index route to Lac Blanc has narrow, exposed ledges and loose scree; this plan does not use it.
  • Grand Balcon Nord couloirs. The left shortcut at 2,071 m is dangerous in snow. Take the right branch via Signal Forbes.
  • Grand Balcon Sud. Steep pegged steps with a handrail.
  • Rockfall. 2026 was a record rockfall year in heat. Gorges de la Diosaz closes for rockfall and the TMB has been rerouted at Orsieres. Hot spells raise the risk.
  • Thunderstorms. Localized after 15:00, widespread from 17:00, with a sharp temperature drop. Lifts stop in lightning and high wind.
  • Early snow. Possible above about 2,500–3,000 m (a single 2007 report; no climatology).
  • Emergency. 112 everywhere; PGHM is the French high-mountain rescue.

Gotchas and known issues

  1. The guest card makes trains free between Servoz and Vallorcine only. Buses are discounted, not free, and older flyers that say otherwise are out of date.
  2. The Multipass excludes Panoramic Mont-Blanc, and holders still need an Aiguille du Midi slot.
  3. Last lift descents are 16:45–17:00. Miss the Flegere one and Les Praz is about 800 m below on foot.
  4. “Sunset from Brevent” is impossible by lift in September.
  5. The Lac Blanc hut is cash only, takes no children under 8, and books by phone only after 31 May.
  6. The Tramway du Mont-Blanc’s Nid d’Aigle top station is reserved for mountaineers with a hut booking, so day visitors get Col de Voza or Mont Lachat. Left out for that reason.
  7. On Sundays most shops close. Carrefour Chamonix shuts at 12:30; Super U Les Pelerins opens daily. Argentiere has only small grocers.
  8. A Swiss vignette is needed if the rental car leaves from the Swiss side of GVA.
  9. “Les Bains de Chamonix” does not exist under that name. The options are QC Terme (no under-14s) or the Richard Bozon pool.
  10. “Lac de Peclerey” does not exist; the walk is the Montagne de Peclerey.
  11. AllTrails figures here come from search snippets (the site returned 403 to page fetches). Official and AllTrails figures differ; ranges are given where they disagree.
  12. Tourist tax rates change on 1 Jan 2027; the only grid found is from 2019.

Open questions for you

  1. Group size and ages. These limits decide who can do what: Aiguille du Midi (no children under 3; under 5 not recommended); Lac Blanc hut (no children under 8); paragliding (minimum age 4–12 by operator; weight cap about 100–120 kg); guided glacier walks (12–15+); QC Terme spa (14+).
  2. Car or no car? The plan works without one, except that Day 3 then becomes the Chapeau hike or Peclerey.
  3. Fitness ceiling in km and ascent per day. The plan peaks at 9.8 km and +765 m (official figures; up to 12 km and +1,000 m on another official listing).
  4. Do hut nights appeal? That decides whether Day 4 is a hut night or a day hike, and whether a TMB is worth a separate July or August trip.
