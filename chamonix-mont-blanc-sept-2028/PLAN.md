# Chamonix and Mont Blanc: Fri 1 to Sat 9 September 2028

8 nights, 9 days, inside a 1-14 Sep window. Fly out of Toronto (YYZ) on Thu 31 Aug. Fly into Geneva (GVA) with one stop, or nonstop to Zurich (ZRH) and onward by train (see Getting there). Two valley bases and one optional hut night. A car helps but is not required.

Every price, timetable and closing date here is a 2026 (or older) figure used as a pattern. Nothing for 2028 has been published. GAP means not verified. "Inference" means my reasoning, not a source.

## The answer first: how many days

**Book 9 days: arrive Fri 1 Sep and leave Sat 9 Sep.** Seven of the nine days are usable. Every lift-dependent hike falls on or before Fri 8 Sep, which is ahead of the earliest likely 2028 closing Sunday (10 Sep). The trip also has 3 swap days for the 4 weather-critical outings.

Assumptions behind the table:
- Each length counts arrival day and departure day.
- From Toronto, arrival day is half usable at best. Departure day is lost to the transfer.
- September has about 10 rain days in 30 (climatestotravel 1991-2020 normals; seechamonix says the same). Over 7 usable days that is about 2-3 wet days (inference, proxy arithmetic).

**Why 9 and not 7.** Day 7 to day 9 is where the upper valley and a third swap day come in. Neither crosses the lift risk line. **Why 9 and not 11.** Day 10 is the first day that could hit the 2028 closing Sunday, and day 11 the first that could have no Brevent or Flegere at all.

**Why arrive Fri 1 rather than Sat 2.** Both keep lift days before 10 Sep. Arriving on the 1st means the last full day is Fri 8, not Sat 9, so even a 9 Sep surprise closure costs nothing. The departure day, Sat 9, also catches the Chamonix Saturday market. A UTMB on 28 Aug-3 Sep would overlap either arrival date equally (UTMB 2028 dates are a GAP).

**Decision rule.** If CMB publishes 2028 dates with the Brevent, Planpraz and Index group closing on 17 Sep, nothing in this plan changes. You could add a day, but flights will already be bought by then.

### Does the full Tour du Mont Blanc belong in this trip? No.

- **Length.** The official TMB site says 5-12 days, most commonly 7-10, about 170 km and close to 10,000 m of ascent. At 7 days that is about 24 km and 1,400 m a day; at 10 days, about 17 km and 1,000 m a day. Add two travel days from Toronto and a 10-11 day TMB fills the entire 1-14 Sep window with no buffer.
- **Lift timing works against it.** Starting anticlockwise from Les Houches on 2 Sep puts the Chamonix-side stages on about 11-13 Sep. Those are stage 9 (Col de Balme), 10 (Flegere) and 11 (Brevent and Planpraz), and their lift shortcuts are exactly the ones that close that week. Stage 1's Bellevue shortcut closes the same week.
- **Huts.** They close around 20 Sep, so 1-14 Sep is the safe end, but the margin is gone. Refuge de la Croix du Bonhomme (stage 2) is closed for renovation until about June 2029. Rifugio Maison Vieille ran 9 Jun-21 Sep in 2025.
- **Booking.** Bookings for the 2027 season open 15 Oct 2026. By the same pattern, 2028 bookings would open about 15 Oct 2027 (inference, unpublished). AllTrails reports a possible rule from 2027 requiring a reservation for every night on the French section ("nothing is official"; page returned 403, unconfirmed). Peak huts book 6-9 months ahead.
- **Weather.** About 10 wet days a month, and the high cols (Col de la Seigne, Grand Col Ferret) have no September snow data (GAP).
- **Rockfall reroute.** The Swiss section between Prayon and Branche (Orsieres) has been rerouted for rockfall since July 2026.

**Hut-free alternative (recommended).** Walk the Chamonix side of the TMB as day hikes from valley beds. This plan already does it:
- the Grand Balcon Sud, stage 11 between Planpraz and Flegere (Day 2)
- Flegere to Lac Blanc, stage 10 (Day 4)
- Col de Balme, the end of stage 9 (Day 7)

All of these use lifts that are open in the 2-8 Sep window, with no luggage moves and no hut bookings. If you want one hut night, the optional Lac Blanc night on Day 4 matches the official site's suggestion of a "2-day/1-night taster" before committing to a full TMB. A full TMB is better as its own trip in July or August with bookings made the October before.

## Orientation

**What the place is.** Chamonix sits in a valley running roughly NE-SW. On the SE side is the Mont Blanc massif (Mont Blanc 4,805-4,808 m, Aiguille du Midi 3,842 m, the Aiguilles de Chamonix and the Mer de Glace). On the NW side are the Aiguilles Rouges: Brevent (2,525 m), Flegere, Lac Blanc and the Index (orientation from the timing research; the exact bearing is a GAP). The two sides face each other across about 1,000 m of valley floor.

**Why it matters for a hiker.** The famous Chamonix views are taken from the Aiguilles Rouges, looking across at the massif. The Mont Blanc faces point NW toward the valley. That makes them back-lit in the morning and front-lit from about 14:00 to sunset (sun azimuth 190-283 degrees). This one fact sets the hour of most days below. The Aiguilles Rouges are named for their colour in early-morning light.

**What you cannot get anywhere else.** A cable car from 1,033 m to 3,842 m in one continuous rise, and a lake (Lac Blanc, 2,352 m) that mirrors the whole massif on calm evenings. You can also see glacier retreat measured at the stairs: the Mer de Glace surface has dropped about 4-6 m a year, and the stair count down to the ice cave grows every season.

**Sub-regions**

| Area | What it is for | Days |
|---|---|---|
| Aiguilles Rouges balcony (Brevent, Planpraz, Flegere, Lac Blanc, Cheserys) | The face-on Mont Blanc views; afternoon light | 2, 4, 5 |
| Mont Blanc massif side (Aiguille du Midi, Plan de l'Aiguille, Montenvers, Mer de Glace) | High altitude, glacier, Grand Balcon Nord | 6 |
| Lower valley (Les Houches, Servoz) | Lift-free Aiguillette des Houches; Gorges de la Diosaz as the wet option | 3 |
| Upper valley (Argentiere, Le Tour, Balme, Vallorcine) | Quieter base; Col de Balme on the Swiss border; Peclerey | 5, 7, 8 |
| Optional: Switzerland (Emosson) and Italy (Courmayeur) | Reserve-day trips only; Italy depends on the tunnel | 8 |

**The trip in numbers.**
- 8 nights; 2 bases plus 1 optional hut.
- Highest point 3,842 m.
- Longest hike: Aiguillette des Houches, 9.8 km (6.1 mi) and +765 m on the official figure. Another official listing gives 12 km and +1,000 m.
- Total ascent on foot about 2,500-2,700 m. This is the sum of the sourced figures and depends on which source you take for each hike.

**Know before reading the days**
1. Lifts set the calendar. In 2026 the early-September timetable ran 31 Aug-13 Sep, with first ascents about 08:30 and last descents 16:45-17:00. Nobody watches sunset from a lift top station unless they walk down.
2. Aiguille du Midi tickets are timed. Tickets are released regularly, notably the day before (D-1). Multipass holders still need a slot.
3. Afternoon storms. A late-August UTMB bulletin gave "very localized thunderstorms after 3 PM, widespread from 5 PM to 10 PM". Get off exposed ground by 15:00 on unstable days. September frequency is a GAP.
4. The guest card (carte d'hote) gives free trains between Servoz and Vallorcine only. Buses are discounted, not free.
5. The valley floor loses direct sun early, roughly 17:30-18:15 in September (inference; no sourced figure). Summits keep their colour until well after the flat-horizon sunset.

**Correction to the brief: "Lac de Peclerey" does not exist.** No source shows a lake of that name. The nearest match is the Montagne de Peclerey loop above Le Tour, a mountain-pasture walk with no lake (Day 7).

# The plan

| Base | Nights | Dates | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Les Praz | 3 | Fri 1, Sat 2, Sun 3 | Quiet hamlet at the foot of the Flegere gondola. You walk to the lift, so the small free Flegere car park (capacity GAP; a low-quality tip says arrive by 08:30) stops mattering. On the Mont-Blanc Express line. |
| Refuge du Lac Blanc (optional) | 1 | Mon 4 | Evening and dawn at the lake after the day crowd has left. Half board EUR 80 pp (undated), cash only, no children under 8. If declined, take a 4th night in Les Praz. |

**Not Chamonix centre.** Parking is EUR 19.90 per 24 h (chamonix.net says EUR 22.50, a conflict), evenings are busy and the noise is unsourced but likely (inference). The stay research recommends a quiet hamlet as the main base.

Prices are proxies (2026, aggregators): holiday rentals average EUR 159 a weeknight and EUR 218 a weekend night (Kayak), with September the cheapest rental month. The average 3-star hotel is about EUR 132 (Skyscanner, inconsistent). Tourist tax rates are being reset for 1 Jan 2027, so no current figure exists (GAP).

### Day 1, Fri 1 Sep: arrive, settle in Les Praz

- **Arrival.** From GVA it is 80-90 min by road. From ZRH, the nonstop lands 06:30 (2024 SWISS times), then 2h45 by train to Geneva (unsourced), then the shuttle. A valley arrival around midday is plausible but unverified.
- **Errands.** Check in, collect a guest card per person, and buy groceries: Super U Les Pelerins is open daily, and Carrefour Chamonix is open Sunday until 12:30.
- **Evening.** A flat walk into Chamonix. The Musee du Mont-Blanc runs 10:00-13:00 and 14:00-18:00 and is closed Tuesdays (one source gives afternoons only in September, a conflict). Afterwards, watch the summit colour on Mont Blanc from the valley, about 20:12-20:43.
- **Why this order.** No lifts and no altitude on a jet-lagged day.

### Day 2, Sat 2 Sep: Brevent, then the Grand Balcon Sud home to Les Praz

| | |
|---|---|
| Time | 10:00 Planpraz gondola from Chamonix; Brevent cable car to 2,525 m; lunch at Planpraz; 12:30-15:00 walk; Flegere gondola down by 17:00 at the latest |
| Walk | Grand Balcon Sud, Planpraz to Flegere: 5.5 km (3.4 mi), about 300-350 m up (loopi 299 m, AllTrails 346 m), 2-2.5 h |
| Lifts (2026) | Planpraz return EUR 25; Brevent cable car return EUR 43; Flegere one way EUR 19. All are covered by the Multipass. |
| Last descents (2026) | Brevent 16:45, Planpraz 17:00, Flegere 17:00 |
| Booking | None |

- **Why this direction and hour.** Walking toward Flegere ends the day at your own door, so there is no dash for the last Planpraz descent. It also puts the walk in the early afternoon, when the massif turns from side-lit to front-lit. The Brevent summit in late morning is side-lit, not ideal light, but it is the widest panorama in the valley.
- **No sunset from Brevent.** The usual "sunset from Brevent" advice does not work in September: the last descent is 16:45.
- **Why Day 2.** Moderate effort for jet legs, and the highest point so far (2,525 m) starts the altitude ladder toward Day 6 (inference; no sourced acclimatisation schedule).
- **Hazards.** Steep pegged steps with a handrail about 1 km in; no exit points once you are walking.
- **Weather rule.** If cloud sits on Brevent at 09:00 but the forecast clears after midday, skip the summit and walk only the Balcon. If it is raining, swap with Day 3's wet option or the Montenvers part of Day 6.
- **Wet-weather swap.** Montenvers train and the ice cave (works in cloud), plus the Musee du Mont-Blanc.

### Day 4, Mon 4 Sep: Flegere to Cheserys to Lac Blanc, hut night

| | |
|---|---|
| Time | 09:00 check out; drop bags at the Argentiere lodging (by car or free train) and ask them to store them; back at the Flegere gondola by 11:30; walk 12:00-15:00; at the lake by 15:00 |
| Walk | Flegere (1,877 m) via the Lacs de Cheserys, then up the ladders to Lac Blanc (2,352 m). The full Flegere-Cheserys-Lac Blanc loop is 7.8 km (4.8 mi), +570 m round trip (randos-montblanc). This day walks the climbing half, and Day 5 walks the direct descent. The split distance is inference. |
| Lift | Flegere gondola one way up, EUR 19 (2026); last ascent 16:30 |
| Booking | Refuge du Lac Blanc: online via refugedulacblanc.fr until 31 May, then by phone only (07 67 56 74 14). About 40 places, half board only, cash only. The 2028 booking opening date is a GAP; book the day it opens. |

- **Why this hour.** You reach the lake at 15:00, ahead of the storm window, and you are there more than 2 hours before sunset (about 20:05). That is the photographers' rule for calm water. Golden light on the massif runs about 17:30-19:30.
- **Why Monday.** It is a weekday, and the day crowd leaves on the last gondola anyway.
- **Hazards.** Ladders on the Cheserys-Lac Blanc stretch are exposed. Climb them on this day rather than descending them. Swimming is banned by prefectural order, and dogs are banned in the reserve.
- **Weather rule.** If storms are forecast from early afternoon, skip Cheserys and take the direct route: Flegere to Lac Blanc, about half of the official 7 km / +500 m return route, 1h45 up. Decide the hut night itself 48 h out; its cancellation terms are a GAP.
- **Hut-free version.** Walk the 7.8 km loop as a day hike from Les Praz, back on the Flegere gondola by 17:00, and keep the 4th night in Les Praz.
- **Wet-weather swap.** Cascade de Berard from Le Buet (Vallorcine): 3.8 km (2.4 mi), +203 m, 1h40, reached by train or bus.

### Day 5, Tue 5 Sep: dawn at Lac Blanc, down, move to Argentiere

- **Dawn.** First colour on the high summits is about 06:28-06:40 (inference, interpolated between 1 and 14 Sep). From here Mont Blanc itself is back-lit at dawn; the colour is on the Aiguille Verte and Drus side.
- **Descent.** Lac Blanc to Flegere by the direct path, about 3.5 km (2.2 mi), -500 m, 1-1.5 h (inference from the official route). Then the Flegere gondola down (EUR 19 one way), collect anything left in Les Praz, and continue to Argentiere.
- **Afternoon.** Short sleep, so rest. Shop on the way, because Argentiere has only small grocers. The museum is closed on Tuesdays.
- **Optional (only if fresh and clear).** Col de Balme from Le Tour, which gets Day 7's main sight in early.
- **Wet-weather swap.** Descend early and spend the afternoon at the cinema (Cinema Vox adds afternoon showings on bad-weather days) or at the spa.

### Day 6, Wed 6 Sep: Aiguille du Midi, Grand Balcon Nord, Mer de Glace

| | |
|---|---|
| Time | First cabin 08:10 (2026, 31 Aug-13 Sep band; last ascent 16:00). Summit until about 10:00, back down to Plan de l'Aiguille (2,310 m), walk about 10:30-13:30, Montenvers 13:30-16:00, train down by 16:30. |
| Walk | Grand Balcon Nord, Plan de l'Aiguille to Montenvers: 5.7 km (3.5 mi), +140 m / -523 m, 2h30 (official) |
| Lifts (2026) | Aiguille du Midi return EUR 83; Montenvers hiking package (train one way plus gondola and cave) EUR 42. Both are covered by the Multipass. |
| Booking | Aiguille du Midi timed slot: online, with new tickets released D-1; same-day box office subject to availability. Pass holders must reserve too. GAP: whether a summit return lets you get off at the mid-station. Ask; the official "North Balcony package" is EUR 68.40. |

- **Why a morning start.** The air is clearest and convection builds in the afternoon. The Vallee Blanche is lit from the east.
- **Why Day 6.** You have climbed through 2,525 m and slept at 2,352 m, so this is not the first altitude day (inference; no sourced acclimatisation guidance). Midweek is quieter than the weekend (anecdotal).
- **Summit cold.** An estimated -9 to +1 C before wind chill, from a standard lapse rate (inference). Bring gloves and a hat.
- **Hazards.** The ride gains about 2,800 m in one go. Altitude symptoms can appear from about 2,500 m on a fast ascent (third-party source). Children under 3 are not allowed and under 5 is not recommended. On the walk, take the **right** fork at 2,071 m via Signal Forbes (2,198 m); the left shortcut goes through couloirs the tourist office calls dangerous in snow. At Montenvers, the stairs down to the ice cave are given as 100, 200, about 430-440 and about 580 steps in different sources. They are metal openwork, and the climb back up is the hard part. Do not walk out onto the ice beyond the cave: in September the bare ice and crevasses are most exposed.
- **Cut-off.** If you are not past Signal Forbes by 13:00, skip the cave and catch the train. The last train from the summit was 16:30 in 2026.
- **Weather rule.** At 07:00, check the Meteo-France Chamonix bulletin and the summit. If it is clouded in, move this day to Day 7 or Day 8 and do Day 7 now.
- **Wet-weather swap.** Montenvers and the ice cave alone, which works in cloud.

- **Why morning at Balme.** The col looks down the valley toward Mont Blanc, which means looking into the sun in the afternoon.
- **Why Day 7.** In 2025 the Balme lifts closed 14 Sep and in 2026 on 20 Sep, so Thursday is safe under either pattern. The Swiss border is at the col (Schengen; carry ID).

- **Wet-weather swap.** Lac d'Emosson by train (see Day 8), or Cascade de Berard.

### Day 8, Fri 8 Sep: reserve day

**Use it in this order:** first any skipped headliner (Aiguille du Midi, Lac Blanc, Brevent), then one of the options below.

- **Lac d'Emosson (Switzerland).** Mont-Blanc Express to Le Chatelard, changing at Vallorcine (about 1 h from Chamonix). Then VerticAlp Emosson: funicular every 30 min 08:35-16:05 (2026), then a panoramic train to the dam (1,930 m). Price GAP; pay in CHF; the guest card does not cover anything beyond Vallorcine. The dam was open until 31 Oct in 2026.
- **The Chapeau from Les Tines.** 5.3 km (3.3 mi), +503 m, 2h40, no lift, close to Argentiere. A viewpoint over the Mer de Glace side.
- **Tandem paraglide from Planpraz.** About EUR 100-148 for 15-20 min. Minimum age 4-12 depending on the operator; weight cap about 100-120 kg. Wind-dependent; book ahead with flexibility. One operator flew daily until 13 Sep in 2026.
- **Courmayeur.** Only if the tunnel is open (see Getting there). Skyway EUR 59 return in low season (2026). Last return departures were 14:30 from 8 Sep in 2025 and 13:45 from 7 Sep in 2026, so this is a morning-only option.

Keep this day light; tomorrow is travel.

If the flight leaves late afternoon, the Chamonix Saturday market runs about 07:00/08:00-13:00 on Place du Mont-Blanc. The transfer to GVA takes 80-90 min from Chamonix (Argentiere adds time; GAP). Allow a margin for Saturday traffic.

Each evening at 18:00, read the Meteo-France Chamonix bulletin (en.chamonix.com/meteo) and MeteoBlue, then sort tomorrow:

| Forecast | Do |
|---|---|
| Clear and stable | Aiguille du Midi, if not done yet |

| Cloud above about 2,500 m but dry | The Balcon Sud, the Aiguillette or Peclerey (below the cloud) |
| Rain | The wet list |

The hut night is the one fixed piece; decide it 48 h out.

## Getting there and around

**Flights from YYZ.**
- **No Toronto-Geneva nonstop.** None was found for 2026: Air Canada flies Montreal-Geneva, not Toronto.
- **Toronto-Zurich is nonstop.** Air Canada daily (787-9, 2026). SWISS is seasonal, 5 weekly in summer 2026; 2024 times were YYZ 16:35 to ZRH 06:30 and ZRH 09:55 to YYZ 13:00. Flight time is 8h50-9h15.
- **When 2028 goes on sale.** Schedules and fares open about 11 months out, roughly Sep-Oct 2027.
- **Fare snapshot.** Air Canada showed "from CAD 968" to Geneva, a snapshot only (2026).
- **Recommendation: nonstop to ZRH outbound, one-stop from GVA on the way home**, if fares are close.
  - Outbound, ZRH lands early, so Day 1 gets the most daylight.
  - Homebound, a 09:55 ZRH departure cannot be reached from Chamonix the same morning. It would cost Day 8 as a transfer to Zurich.
  - GAP: one-stop routings, ZRH-Chamonix door-to-door time and fares.
- **Avoid Milan Malpensa.** It has no Toronto nonstop and it depends on the tunnel.

**Mont Blanc tunnel (affects Courmayeur only).**
- In Jan 2026 ATMB said 2027 and 2028 repeat the autumn closure model: one cycle of about 3.5 months (15 weeks) each autumn.
- The 2025 closure ran **1 Sep to 12 Dec**. The 2028 start date is unpublished.
- **Assume the tunnel may be shut for the whole trip.** When it is shut, there is no bus to Courmayeur and no practical day trip. The alternatives are the Great St Bernard tunnel or the Little St Bernard pass (drive times GAP). The only other route is the lift chain: Aiguille du Midi, Panoramic Mont-Blanc (EUR 125.50, morning only, not on the Multipass), then Skyway down.
- When open, the toll is about EUR 56.40 one way or EUR 70.40 return (valid 7 days), and the bus takes 35-50 min (EUR 9-35).
- Check atmb.com in early 2027 and again about 12 months out.

**In the valley.**
- **Guest card (carte d'hote).** Free from your host, one card per person. It gives free Mont-Blanc Express rides **between Servoz and Vallorcine only**; St-Gervais and the Swiss section are not covered. **Buses are not free.** The card gives a discount on passes of 2+ days (2-day EUR 5 instead of EUR 9; weekly EUR 10 instead of EUR 18), with no discount on single rides or on night buses after 21:00. Validate on every boarding.
- **Timetables.** The summer bus timetable ended 6 Sep in 2026 and the Mont-Blanc Express summer timetable 13 Sep (2028: GAP). The Chamo'nuit night bus ran Les Houches-Argentiere every 45 min, 20:50-midnight.

## Hazards

- **Altitude, Aiguille du Midi.** A rise of about 2,800 m in one ride. Symptoms (nausea, dizziness, fatigue, rapid pulse) can start from about 2,500 m, and fitness is no protection (third-party source). Keep the visit short if you feel unwell. Third-party advice discourages visits with a severe cold or heart problems. GAP: official medical guidance; see a doctor if anyone has a heart condition or is pregnant.
- **Cold.** The summit is near or below 0 C in a normal September (estimated -9 to +1 C before wind chill).
- **Mer de Glace stairs.** Sources give anywhere from 100 to about 580 steps. They are metal openwork, not wheelchair-accessible, and steep. The count grows each year as the glacier drops. Stay off the open ice without a guide.
- **Ladders between Cheserys and Lac Blanc.** Exposed; climb them, do not descend them; avoid them when wet. The Index route to Lac Blanc has narrow, exposed ledges and loose scree; this plan does not use it.
- **Grand Balcon Nord couloirs.** The left shortcut at 2,071 m is dangerous in snow. Take the right branch via Signal Forbes.
- **Grand Balcon Sud.** Steep pegged steps with a handrail.
- **Rockfall.** 2026 was a record rockfall year in heat. Gorges de la Diosaz closes for rockfall, and the TMB has been rerouted at Orsieres. Hot spells raise the risk.
- **Thunderstorms.** The late-summer pattern is localized storms after 15:00 and widespread ones from 17:00, with a sharp drop in temperature. Lifts stop in lightning and high wind.
- **Early snow.** Possible above about 2,500-3,000 m (a single 2007 report; no climatology).
- **Emergency.** 112 everywhere; PGHM is the French high-mountain rescue.

## Gotchas / Known issues

1. The guest card makes trains free between Servoz and Vallorcine only. Buses are discounted, not free, and older flyers that say otherwise are out of date.
2. The Multipass excludes Panoramic Mont-Blanc, and holders still need an Aiguille du Midi slot.
3. Last lift descents are 16:45-17:00. Miss the Flegere one and Les Praz is about 800 m below on foot.
4. "Sunset from Brevent" is impossible by lift in September.
5. The Lac Blanc hut is cash only, takes no children under 8, and books by phone only after 31 May.
6. The Tramway du Mont-Blanc's Nid d'Aigle top station is reserved for mountaineers with a hut booking, so day visitors get Col de Voza or Mont Lachat. It is left out of this plan for that reason.
7. On Sundays most shops close. Carrefour Chamonix shuts at 12:30; Super U Les Pelerins opens daily. Argentiere has only small grocers.
8. A Swiss vignette is needed if the rental car leaves from the Swiss side of GVA.
9. "Les Bains de Chamonix" does not exist under that name. The options are QC Terme (no under-14s) or the Richard Bozon pool.
10. "Lac de Peclerey" does not exist; the walk is the Montagne de Peclerey.
11. AllTrails figures in this plan come from search snippets (the site returned 403 to page fetches). Official and AllTrails figures differ; ranges are given where they disagree.
12. Tourist tax rates change on 1 Jan 2027; the only grid found is from 2019.

## Open questions for you

1. **Group size and ages.** These limits decide who can do what:
   - Aiguille du Midi: no children under 3; under 5 not recommended
   - Lac Blanc hut: no children under 8
   - Paragliding: age 4-12 minimum depending on operator; weight cap about 100-120 kg
   - Guided glacier walks: 12-15+
   - QC Terme spa: 14+
2. **Car or no car?** The plan works without one, except that Day 3 then becomes the Chapeau hike or Peclerey.
3. **Fitness ceiling** in km and ascent per day. The plan peaks at 9.8 km and +765 m (official figures; up to 12 km and +1,000 m on another official listing).
4. **Do hut nights appeal?** That decides whether Day 4 is a hut night or a day hike, and whether a TMB is worth a separate July or August trip.
