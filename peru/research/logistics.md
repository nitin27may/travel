# Peru — logistics, access, and the booking constraints

## Machu Picchu is the most rule-bound site on the trip. Book it first, plan around it.

### Capacity
- **4,500 tickets/day** standard for 2026; **5,600/day** on 1 Jan, 2–5 Apr, **19 Jun – 2 Nov**,
  30–31 Dec.
- Buy at **tuboleto.cultura.pe** (official). Operators recommend **at least 60 days ahead**;
  in the 19 Jun – 2 Nov window treat that as a floor, not a target.

### Circuits — you are locked into one
Three circuits (**Panoramic, Classic, Royalty**), subdivided into **10 sub-routes** with
colour-coded signage. Each visitor gets **one circuit, one direction, one fixed entry hour**,
in hourly slots from **06:00 to 15:00**, with a **30-minute grace period**. Deviating from your
assigned circuit can get you *and your guide* removed and your entry cancelled.

Practical consequence: **Machu Picchu is no longer a place you wander.** If you want the classic
postcard view *and* the Sun Gate *and* Huayna Picchu, you are choosing between them at the moment
of purchase, months in advance — or buying two entries on two days.

### The 2026 change that catches people
**From 2026 the Inca Trail permit no longer includes the Machu Picchu entrance ticket.** They are
two separate, separately capacity-controlled purchases. Trekkers who booked the trail assuming
entry was bundled will arrive at the gate without a ticket.

### Inca Trail permits
- **500 people per day**, closed all February, permits for 2026 released 17 Nov 2025.
- Book **5–6 months ahead**; peak months sell out almost immediately.
- GAP: the 500/day cap is widely reported to include porters, guides and cooks — which would leave
  roughly 200 trekker places. Not verified against the official DRC-Cusco regulation.

### Prices (2026)
- Machu Picchu entry: **~S/152 (~US$62)** classic circuit; **~S/200 (~US$80)** for circuits
  including Huayna Picchu / Machu Picchu Mountain. **No price increase for 2026.**
- Train Ollantaytambo ↔ Aguas Calientes (1 h 21 m):
  - Economy (PeruRail Expedition / Inca Rail Voyager): **from ~US$80 return**
  - Vistadome / 360°: **US$140–180 return**
  - Sacred Valley train: US$280–350 · Hiram Bingham: US$500–600
- Sources: https://www.perurail.com/tickets-to-machu-picchu/ ·
  https://www.machupicchu.org/train-to-machu-picchu-cost-2026-complete-price-guide.htm ·
  https://www.tuboletocultura.com/

### The access corridor is fragile — this is the real risk, not the weather
The Aguas Calientes shuttle-bus concession (Consettur's contract expiry and the tender that
followed) triggered protests from **16 September 2025**. Rail blockades shut the route **twice, in
September and November 2025**: about **1,400 tourists were evacuated on overnight trains and
roughly 900 were left stranded** in Aguas Calientes. Some visitors walked up rather than wait.

Plan accordingly: **do not put Machu Picchu on the last possible day before an international
flight.** Leave a buffer day in Cusco after it.
Sources: https://adept.travel/news/2025-12-03-machu-picchu-rail-blockades ·
https://www.adiosadventuretravel.com/blog/2025/09/many-tourists-walk-to-machu-picchu-when-buses-are-suspended/

## Getting around

### Flying is not optional
Distances are large and mountain roads are slow. Domestic carriers: **LATAM** (widest network —
Arequipa, Ayacucho, Cajamarca, **Jaén**, Chiclayo, Cusco, Iquitos, Juliaca, Piura, Puerto Maldonado,
Pucallpa, Tacna, Tarapoto, Trujillo, Tumbes), **Sky Airline** (~20% of domestic capacity by
mid-2026, fares from under US$30 one-way if booked early on Lima–Cusco and Lima–Arequipa),
**JetSMART**, and **Star Perú**.

Almost everything routes **through Lima**. Cusco–Puerto Maldonado and Cusco–Juliaca are the useful
exceptions. Assume a same-day transfer between two regional cities means a Lima connection and
half a day gone.
Source: https://newperuvian.com/best-domestic-airlines-in-peru/

### Cusco's new airport is not open yet
**Chinchero International (AICC)** — in the Sacred Valley, intended to take international flights
directly — was ~**65–70% complete in mid-2026** and is now slated for **late 2027**, having already
slipped from 2024 and 2026. Assume Cusco = **CUZ (Velasco Astete)** and a Lima connection.
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinchero_International_Airport

### Buses
Long-distance buses are good (Cruz del Sur, Oltursa, Movil) and are the only sensible way to reach
Huaraz. **Lima → Huaraz: ~8 hours, ~400 km, around S/100 (~US$27).** Overnight services exist.
Canada's advisory explicitly flags **bus accident risk from speeding, poor maintenance and driver
fatigue** — worth paying for a full-cama seat on a reputable line.

## Entry and safety (Canadian passport)

- **No visa** for stays under **90 days per 365-day period**. Passport valid **6 months** from arrival.
- Canada's advisory (last updated **20 August 2026**): **"Exercise a high degree of caution"** with
  regional advisories.
  - **Avoid all travel:** the **VRAEM** (Apurímac, Ene and Mantaro river valleys).
  - **Avoid non-essential travel:** Upper Huallaga valley; within **20 km of the Colombian border**;
    within 20 km of the Ecuadorian border except the Tumbes crossing (landmines).
  - **States of emergency currently in effect in Lima, Callao and several other provinces** in
    response to violent crime.
  - Specific warnings: **express kidnapping in unlicensed taxis** (never street-hail — airport
    counters or apps only), **theft on intercity buses**, and a marked rise in violent crime in Lima.
- None of the trip's target regions (Cusco, Sacred Valley, Ancash/Huaraz, Arequipa, Puno, Amazonas,
  the Amazon lodges) fall inside an avoid-travel zone.
- Source: https://travel.gc.ca/locations/peru
