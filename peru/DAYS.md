# How many days does Peru actually need?

## The short answer

**There is no honest version of "properly visit Peru" in under about three weeks — and the
specific trip that Instagram post describes, Huayhuash *and* Machu Picchu, needs 24 to 26 days
door-to-door.**

That is not padding. The Huayhuash circuit alone consumes 14–16 days from Lima once you count the
bus down, the mandatory acclimatisation, the trek itself and a weather buffer. Machu Picchu and
Cusco is a separate 6-night block 1,100 km away, reachable only via Lima. The two things the post
puts in one sentence are, logistically, two holidays.

## The tiers, with what each one actually buys you

| Days | What fits | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **5** | Cusco, Sacred Valley, Machu Picchu | A site, not a country. Also the most altitude-risky version — no room to acclimatise |
| **8–10** | Lima + Cusco/Valley/MP + **one** of: Puerto Maldonado Amazon, or the south coast | The standard first trip. Works. Honest about being a highlights run |
| **14** | The southern circuit properly: Lima · Sacred Valley · Machu Picchu · Cusco · Arequipa · Colca · Titicaca | The point where Peru stops being a checklist. Still no trekking beyond day hikes, still no Amazon |
| **17–18** | The above **plus 3 Amazon nights**, or **swap** the Arequipa/Titicaca leg for Huaraz + the 4-day Santa Cruz trek | The best trip available in under three weeks. Pick two of: south circuit, Amazon, trekking |
| **24–26** | **Lima · Huaraz · full Huayhuash circuit · Cusco/Valley/Machu Picchu** | What the post is actually describing. Also the version where the trekking is the trip and Machu Picchu is the coda |
| **35–40** | Everything above **plus the north** — Chachapoyas/Kuélap/Gocta (5–7 days) and the Moche north coast, Trujillo + Chiclayo (3–4 days) | "Properly visited Peru." Almost nobody does this on one trip |

## The one sequencing decision that saves you three days

**Do Ancash (Huaraz) before Cusco, not after.**

Huaraz is 3,050 m and the acclimatisation hikes go to 4,600 m. Come off a Cordillera Blanca or
Huayhuash trek and you arrive in Cusco (3,399 m) **already fully acclimatised** — which means:

- no need for the two soft Sacred Valley acclimatisation days
- Vinicunca (5,036 m) is available on your first Cusco day instead of your third
- the whole Cusco block compresses from 6 nights to 4 without losing anything

Done the other way round, you acclimatise twice and pay for it twice.

The counter-argument, stated fairly: it front-loads the trip's biggest failure risk. If altitude or
weather ends the Huayhuash early you have a lot of unstructured days. Mitigate with a Huaraz
operator who runs the 4-day Mini Huayhuash as a fallback rather than a refund.

## Two shapes worth costing

### A — 17 days, "trekking and Machu Picchu, no compromise on either"
Lima 1 · Huaraz 3 (Laguna Churup, Laguna 69) · **Santa Cruz trek 4** · Huaraz 1 ·
fly to Cusco via Lima · Sacred Valley 2 · Aguas Calientes/Machu Picchu 1 · Cusco 2 (Vinicunca or
Palcoyo) · buffer 1 · Lima 1.

Gets you a genuine multi-day Andean trek over a 4,750 m pass, the full Cusco block, and a buffer
against the Machu Picchu access disruptions — in under three weeks.

### B — 24–26 days, "the post's trip, done properly"
Lima 1 · Huaraz 3 acclimatising · **Huayhuash 10–12** · Huaraz 1 recovery · transit 1 ·
Sacred Valley 2 · Machu Picchu 1 · Cusco 2 · buffer 1 · Lima 1.

This is the trip. It is also four weeks of leave once you add the flights from Toronto.

## Non-negotiables that fall out of the research

1. **Book Machu Picchu before anything else.** 4,500 tickets/day (5,600 on peak dates), one fixed
   circuit, one fixed entry hour, 60+ days lead time. The itinerary is built around that ticket, not
   the other way round.
2. **From 2026 an Inca Trail permit no longer includes Machu Picchu entry.** Two separate purchases.
3. **Never sleep the first night in Cusco.** Transfer straight down to the Sacred Valley.
4. **Leave a buffer day after Machu Picchu.** Rail blockades shut the corridor twice in late 2025;
   ~1,400 people were evacuated and ~900 stranded.
5. **Go in May–September, and September is the best single month** — dry, but past the June–August
   crowd peak. October is not the crowd relief the post claims: Machu Picchu runs at its 5,600/day
   high-season capacity until **2 November**.
6. **Don't shoulder-season a committing trek.** April/October on the Huayhuash means snow on twelve
   passes above 4,500 m.
