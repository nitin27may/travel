Before the days start

What this place physically is

What this place is. (This paragraph is general geology, not from the research files.)

  • The ice field. Southern Patagonia is the Andes at about 49-55 degrees south, where the westerly winds come off the Pacific with nothing in their way. They dump snow on the crest. The result is the Southern Patagonian Ice Field, the largest ice mass in the Southern Hemisphere outside Antarctica, about 350 km long. Its outlet glaciers spill east into long glacial lakes: Lago Argentino (Perito Moreno, Upsala, Spegazzini) and Lago Viedma.
  • The steppe. The air that crosses the ice field arrives on the Argentine side dry and fast. That is why El Calafate sits in brown steppe and why the wind defines every day of this trip.
  • The spires. Fitz Roy (about 3,400 m) and Cerro Torre (about 3,100 m) are granite intrusions. The ice stripped away the softer rock around them and left the hard cores standing as near-vertical towers.
  • Perito Moreno. It is fed by a large, high, snowy catchment on the ice field, so it has long held its size while most neighbouring glaciers retreated (recent studies report it has started thinning; GAP). Its front keeps pushing across the lake toward the Magallanes Peninsula. When it touches the far shore it dams the Brazo Rico arm, the water rises, and the ice dam eventually ruptures.
  • Tierra del Fuego. Further south, the Andes bend from north-south to east-west and run out into the sea. The Beagle Channel is a glacier-carved channel along that bend. Ushuaia sits on its north shore under the Martial range, among the last mountains of the chain.

Why it is famous, specifically.

  • The Fitz Roy skyline at sunrise. Around sunrise the east-facing granite turns red for a few minutes. Photographers call it the "Fitz Roy fire". The classic photographs are taken from Laguna de los Tres, Laguna Capri and the road into El Chalten.
  • Perito Moreno calving. You watch from metal walkways a short distance across the water from a 5 km-wide ice wall (front width from general knowledge), while blocks fall into Lago Argentino.
  • Ushuaia's end-of-the-road geography. Ruta 3 ends at Bahia Lapataia, inside Tierra del Fuego National Park.

What you cannot get elsewhere.

  • An 11-year-old walking on a glacier with crampons. Minitrekking takes ages 8-65.
  • Walking among Magellanic penguins. On Isla Martillo you walk a fixed path a few steps from the birds. Gentoo penguins are possible but not guaranteed.
  • World-class spires as day hikes from a village bed. El Chalten's two main trailheads are at the two ends of town, so you walk out of the door.
  • The cultural layer (general knowledge, not from the research):
    • the estancia, Patagonia's sheep-ranch culture; Harberton was founded by the Bridges missionary family and houses the Acatushun marine-mammal museum
    • the asado, the grill-house meal you will be offered everywhere
    • Buenos Aires itself, a European-looking capital 2,500 km north of the ice

What to know before reading the days

  • 1Wind runs the schedule. In El Chalten, days without wind are rare until about 20 March, and gusts matter more than the average. Wind can cancel boats (Martillo, the Beagle, Safari Nautico), make the last climb to Laguna de los Tres unpleasant or unsafe, and hit cars on RN40. A calm morning in town tells you little about the exposed ground.
  • 2Cash is a real problem in El Chalten. It has one ATM (Red Link, Banco Santa Cruz), often empty, and no exchange office. Card payment fails when the signal drops. Withdraw pesos in El Calafate or Ushuaia. The old "bring dollars for the blue rate" trick is mostly dead: the gap was 1-3% in September 2026.
  • 3Park tickets are bought online and shown as a QR code. El Chalten's trail portals check them, and there is no mobile signal on the trails. Buy on Wi-Fi the night before at the latest.
  • 4Domestic flights have a 15 kg checked-bag limit, and since May 2026 Aerolineas' cheapest fare includes no carry-on, only a 3 kg personal item. Hiking gear for a family breaks that fast.
  • 5Children cost adult prices earlier than you expect. National parks charge full price from age 7. Child fares stop at 11 on the Beagle catamaran, at 12 on the train and at 16 on the Upsala cruise. Get everyone's exact age on the travel dates.
  • 6Every peso price here will be higher in 2027. Budget from the USD figures, not the ARS ones.

On the ground

Ground transport: rental car or buses

Recommendation: a rental car from FTE airport, Wed 3 to Tue 9 Mar, same-location return. This assumes a group of four or more; group size is an open question.

For the car:

  • Perito Moreno is about 80 km (1h15-1h30) with no public bus; the alternative is a tour or transfer.
  • Piedras Blancas and Piedra del Fraile trailheads are 16-17 km out of El Chalten.
  • The car saves two bus-terminal transfers.
  • Cost, 2026 aggregator figures: Sandero about USD 50 a day (Vipcars), Kayak from ARS 72,370 a day, SUV average ARS 138,200 a day. Peak season is higher.

Against the car:

  • Enterprise takes a USD 2,200 deposit on an SUV or pickup. National's waiver deductible runs up to USD 4,500.
  • Basic cover excludes tyres, windscreen and keys. Confirm gravel cover in writing.
  • The 37 km of Ruta 41 gravel to Lago del Desierto is the one road to take by tour, not by rental.
  • An International Driving Permit is recommended by Global Affairs Canada.
  • There is no fuel in El Chalten. Fill up in El Calafate. Tres Lagos has fuel; La Leona roadhouse fuel is a GAP.

The bus alternative:

  • Operators: Cal Tur and Chalten Travel, departures about 08:00 and 17:00, 2h40-3h30.
  • Fares conflict: USD 39-40, ARS 35,500, or ARS 50,000 plus ARS 3,000 (Calafate) and ARS 2,000 (Chalten) terminal fees.
  • What it costs the plan: the Day 6 arrival moves to about 20:00 on the 17:00 bus, which loses the Condores sunset. The glacier day becomes a tour with pickup (minitrekking includes urban pickup).

Ushuaia: no car.

  • National park buses leave from Fadul and Maipu in central Ushuaia (timetable GAP).
  • The Laguna Esmeralda shuttle leaves the bus terminal at 09:00, 10:00 and 11:00 and returns about 16:00, minimum 3 people (search snippet).
  • Remises (cars with drivers) and taxis cover the rest. Piratour includes transport.

Risk

Hazards

  • Wind.
    • Chalten has few windless days before about 20 March, and gusts matter more than the average. The exposed final climb to Laguna de los Tres, Condores and Pliegue Tumbado are the wind-critical places.
    • RN40 has strong crosswinds.
    • Boats (Martillo, the Beagle, Safari Nautico, Lago del Desierto) cancel in wind.
    • Ushuaia averages 22.7-23.6 km/h.
  • Laguna de los Tres final climb. Steep and loose, about 450 m in 2.4 km, on an exposed slope. Use poles and descend slowly. The turnaround rule is on Day 8.
  • Laguna Torre.
    • Steep rocky moraine.
    • GLOF warning boards; the river is high and fast.
    • The route beyond the lagoon is closed.
  • Cold and fast-changing weather. Chalten averages 16 C high and 5 C low in March, and snow is possible at altitude on the same day as sun. Ushuaia is 11 C high and 5 C low in March. Carry a windproof shell, an insulated layer, a hat and gloves every day.
  • Mud. Laguna Esmeralda has peat bog, beaver dams and a steep muddy third forest.
  • Remoteness and medical care.
    • No mobile signal on most trails.
    • El Chalten has a basic health facility only. Serious cases go to the SAMIC hospital in El Calafate, 2-3.5 h away, which has basic services and a mixed reputation.
    • A helicopter evacuation from Fitz Roy may cost up to EUR 12,000, often paid upfront. Buy insurance with no evacuation ceiling and with trekking cover.
  • Town dogs. They follow hikers; rangers ask you not to let them.
  • Buenos Aires. Pickpocketing and phone snatching. Watch Retiro station and La Boca outside the tourist strip, and Line D at rush hour. Use app rides at night.
  • Emergency numbers. GAP (not in the research). Look them up before travel.

Paperwork

Entry and documents (Canadian citizens)

  • No visa for tourism, up to 90 days (Argentine consulate, Montreal, updated 28 Oct 2025).
  • No reciprocity fee (suspended from 1 Jan 2018; no reinstatement found).
  • No electronic sworn declaration (DDJJ); it was abolished in August 2022.
  • At the border: biometrics (fingerprints and photo) on arrival and departure. You may be asked for an onward ticket and proof of funds.
  • Passport: valid for the stay per Global Affairs Canada. 6 months beyond the trip is the safe rule; the official Argentine rule is a GAP.
  • Health insurance: all foreigners must hold insurance covering hospitalisation and medical care (consulate page). Documents may be in English. How often it is checked is a GAP.
  • Children: the minor-consent rule applies only to children born or resident in Argentina.
  • Vaccinations: hepatitis A and B recommended; no yellow fever proof required.
  • Other passports: any traveller who is not a Canadian citizen needs a separate check (GAP).
