What this place actually is

Five national parks in eleven days, with one base per park and no day spent only in the car.

The Colorado Plateau is a slab of sedimentary rock roughly the size of Poland, lifted almost intact and almost level about 1,500 m into the sky. Because it rose without crumpling, the layers stayed flat — so rivers cut down through them instead of being deflected sideways. Everything you look at follows from that: canyons with squared-off walls, mesas with flat tops, arches and spires left standing where soft rock eroded from beneath a hard cap. The colours are iron and manganese staining the sandstone.

One place breaks the pattern, and it is on this route. The Waterpocket Fold at Capitol Reef is a hundred-mile wrinkle where the crust did buckle, tilting the layers on edge so you drive past a cross-section of 200 million years in an afternoon.

Why it is famous — the specific things

Reflected light. In a deep canyon the sun never reaches the floor directly; it hits one wall and bounces, and the bounced light is orange. That is the whole reason Mesa Arch is photographed at sunrise — the underside of the arch is lit from below, by light reflected off a cliff 500 m beneath it.

Density. The Windows section of Arches has the highest arch density per step anywhere. Bryce is a huge field of hoodoos seen from above; Zion is the opposite view, from the floor of the canyon looking up.

What was left out, and why it is not a loss. The earlier version of this plan also ran through Page and Monument Valley. Antelope Canyon, Horseshoe Bend and Monument Valley are among the most photographed places in the Southwest, and cutting them is a real trade. What they cost was four nights, an hour of clock confusion twice, the trip's hardest sell-out and a Navajo-time booking. They make a good trip of their own later.

Six things to know before reading the days

1 · Nights per park, and the evidence

No official source publishes a recommended number of days for any of these parks — the split here comes from NPS hour and distance figures plus about a dozen trip reports. Bryce: one night, near-universal. Capitol Reef: two, the park people most often wish they had given more. Moab: three, the common split for Arches plus Island in the Sky. Zion: three, because the Narrows is a firm goal — two is the mainstream answer without it.

2 · Highway 12 is the longest day

Bryce to Torrey is 123 mi and about 3 h without stopping; add Lower Calf Creek Falls and it is a full day. The route is fully paved and peaks near 9,600 ft. The Hogback section has no guardrail — drive it, do not photograph it from the driver's seat.

3 · Fuel gaps are short but real

One source describes a 40-mile stretch between Torrey and Escalante with no services; whether Boulder has a working pump is unverified. Fill up in Escalante or Torrey. Between Torrey and I-70 the only fuel is Hanksville (about 81 mi), which has two public stations, Hollow Mountain and Stan's.

4 · Everything is paved now

Dropping the Hanksville badlands and Moki Dugway takes out every dirt road on the plan, and with them the rental-insurance problem on unpaved roads. Burr Trail is paved only to the Capitol Reef boundary and Hell's Backbone is gravel, so both stay off the list. Mapping apps will still offer the Shafer Trail — see day 9.

5 · “Later is quieter” is false here

NPS visitation data shows October is busier than September at Zion and Arches. Shifting the trip later in autumn buys you nothing crowd-wise in Utah — it only costs you warmth and daylight.

6 · Almost nothing pins the dates

With the Navajo parks gone, the only date-sensitive item found in the research is the Narrows, which turns dry-suit cold around 1 October. Utah's parks are open year-round, and no 2026–27 closure was found on UT-12 or UT-24. The other real constraints are heat in summer and the Kanarra Falls permit cap.

When to go — the window is wide

Best: the second half of September. Utah has cooled from its 100–110 °F summer to roughly 75–85 °F, the Narrows is still in the wetsuit-and-boots window, and Bryce is near freezing only at dawn.

The one date that bites is 1 October: Narrows outfitters switch to dry suits as the water hits 3–7 °C. If the Narrows is the point of the Zion days, book before it. If it is not, the plan barely cares.

Also workable: mid-May to early June — before the heat and with long days, though spring runoff can close the Narrows entirely and Kanarra Falls runs cold and high.

Avoid July and August. Moab at 105 °F is not a holiday, and Zion and Arches are at their busiest.

Assumptions taken

Dates are not booked and a few decisions were left open, so the plan takes a position rather than stalling. Every one is a real choice that can be reversed — none is a silent default.

Utah only: Page, Antelope Canyon, Horseshoe Bend and Monument Valley are out Four nights, two clock changes and the trip's worst sell-out saved. To put one back, the cheapest is Page for one night between Bryce and Torrey — but that gives up Highway 12, so it is a swap, not an addition.

Three nights at Zion, two at Capitol Reef, three at Moab Zion gets two full days (Narrows; Scout Lookout and Emerald Pools) plus an arrival night. Capitol Reef gets one full day, which the official trail list fills, and the second night is the arrival evening. Moab could be two nights on the official figures — Arches is about a day and Island in the Sky half a day — but Mesa Arch at dawn and Dead Horse at sunset cannot share a day comfortably. Cutting Moab to two nights makes this a 10-day trip.

Bryce: Queen's Garden out-and-back, not the classic combination The Navajo Loop has been closed since Feb 2026 with no reopening date. Re-check at the visitor centre; if it has reopened, do the loop instead.

Canyon Overlook is on SR-9 and is not shuttle-restricted, so you drive to it. That makes it the one piece of the day you control entirely — which is why it holds the sunset slot rather than competing with shuttle queues at dawn.

Day 3

Zion — the water day

The Narrows, bottom-up from Temple of Sinawava. This is an out-and-back, so the 12 km ceiling manages itself — turn round when you have had enough.

Water is 3–7 °C. Outfitters switch to dry suits on 1 October; in mid-September you are in the wetsuit-and-boots window. Rent boots and a stick — trainers do not work on that riverbed.

Narrows conditions
Decision rule — flash flood. The Narrows closes on flow rate and forecast, not on whether it is raining where you stand. Check the flash-flood potential rating that morning. If it reads “Expected” or above, the Narrows is off — substitute the Watchman Trail and move Canyon Overlook into this day.

Day 5 · midday

Lower Calf Creek Falls replaces the Hanksville detour

A 126 ft waterfall at the end of a sandy canyon walk. 6.0 mi (9.7 km) round trip, about 500 ft of gain, and BLM calls it a good introductory Escalante hike suitable for families. It is under the 12 km ceiling but it is soft sand and usually unshaded, so start with all the water you own.

Budget 3–3.5 hours including the walk to the trailhead. The day-use fee is $5 per vehicle, and the pass does not cover it.

Day 5 · afternoon

The Hogback, Boulder Mountain and into Torrey

The road tops out near 9,600 ft on Boulder Mountain and crosses the Hogback, a narrow ridge with no guardrail. It is fully paved, and the one UDOT culvert job on it (mile 6–7) finished in June 2026; no other 2026–27 closure was found on UT-12.

Fuel: one guide describes a 40-mile stretch between Torrey and Escalante with no services, and Boulder's pump is unverified. Fill in Escalante or Torrey.

Day 6 · midday

The Scenic Drive, Grand Wash and Fruita no permits, no lottery

The Scenic Drive runs 8 mi south along the fold, with Grand Wash — a flat walk into a narrow canyon, easy and shaded — off it. Cassidy Arch is the bigger objective at ~5.5 km with 670 ft of climb, ending on top of the arch rather than under it.

Fruita is a Mormon orchard settlement inside the park. In season you can pick fruit in the historic orchards, and the Gifford House sells pie that is not a gimmick.

8 mi Scenic Drive5.5 km Cassidy Arch RT670 ft gain
Why this park is empty and the others are not

Capitol Reef takes roughly a fifth of Zion's visitors. It has no timed entry, no shuttle and no permit lottery, because it has never needed them. After five days of queues and reservation windows, that is the actual attraction.

Cathedral Valley is out. The loop fords the Fremont River and NPS lists it Impassable. Do not let a blog route you into the ford. Unverified: the Scenic Drive fee and the Gifford House seasonal closing date.

Day 7 · optional

Optional: Goblin Valley State Park optional

Not part of the core plan — take it only if the morning is going well. It sits on the Torrey → Moab drive: a 12-mile paved dead-end spur off UT-24 (Temple Mountain Road, then Goblin Valley Road), about 20 mi north of Hanksville. The valley is a free-roaming field of small mushroom-shaped hoodoos, and the best place on the route for an 11-year-old to run around. If the GPS sends you onto dirt, it is the wrong road.

Time added: about 2–2.5 h — 24 extra miles (30–35 min, my estimate) plus about 1.5 h on foot. Three Sisters is 250 yd; a family covered ~1.3 mi in the valleys in 90 minutes; add Carmel Canyon (2.4 km loop, scrambling) for a 2-hour visit. Leave Torrey by about 08:30 and you reach Moab around 14:30, which still leaves Delicate Arch at sunset (about 19:18).

$20 per vehicle+24 mi extra drivingno shade 90–100 °F possible
Goblin Valley
Pass and rules. It is a Utah state park: the America the Beautiful pass is probably not valid (sources conflict — call 435-259-3710). Torrey → Moab is 145–156 mi, 2h20–2h40, east through Hanksville. Fuel at Hanksville — it is the only stop between Torrey and I-70.

Optional pause: Factory Butte is visible from UT-24 for miles, and BLM's kiosk and restroom stop is 12 mi west of Hanksville, no fee. The close-up viewing is dirt road and is dropped.

Day 10 · optional

Optional: Corona Arch before you leave optional

Not part of the core plan — skip it if anyone is tired after day 9. The trailhead is on UT-279 (Potash Road), about 14 mi from Moab and 25–30 minutes by car. 3 mi (4.8 km) round trip, about 2 hours, free. The trail crosses a railroad track, then climbs slickrock with two safety cables, steps cut into the rock and one short ladder, past Pinto Arch and Bowtie Arch to Corona, whose opening is 140 ft wide and 105 ft high.

Time added: about 3–3.5 h including both drives. Start by 07:00 — it is sun all day with no shade — and you are back in Moab about 10:00, leaving for Salt Lake City around 10:15 and arriving mid-afternoon.
