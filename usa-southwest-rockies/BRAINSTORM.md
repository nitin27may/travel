## Rule-zero findings — what's wrong with the reel's list

1. **It is not a route, it is a highlight reel.** The stop order backtracks twice.
   Stop 1 (Page, AZ) is ~2h45 east of stop 2 (Zion) and stop 3 (Kanarraville) is the
   westernmost point of the whole trip. Driving Page -> Zion -> Kanarra -> Bryce and
   then east again to Hanksville wastes roughly half a day.

2. **Stops 5, 6 and 7 are one place.** Bentonite Hills, Factory Butte and
   "Long Dong Silver Spire" (officially **Blue Valley Spire**, ~7 mi west of Hanksville
   off UT-24) all sit within a 30-minute radius of Hanksville. Moonscape Overlook is in
   the same cluster and is arguably the best of the four. That is **one half-day + a
   sunset**, not three stops. VERIFIED via AllTrails/Tripadvisor (see Sources in chat).
   Spire hike: 2.2 mi, ~190 ft gain, easy.

3. **It is two trips, not one.** Breckenridge -> Grand Teton is **~570 mi / 8h30** through
   empty Wyoming with nothing on the list in between. The red-rock half (stops 1-10, 17)
   and the mountain half (stops 11-16) are joined only because a creator compressed a
   year of travel into a list.

4. **Stop 17 is a routing artifact.** Stansbury Island is 45 min west of Salt Lake City.
   It is on the list because SLC is where they flew home from — it is an exit stop, not a
   destination. Fine to keep as the last afternoon; do not build a day around it.

5. **Monument Valley is misplaced at stop 10.** Coming after Canyonlands it is a
   2h45 detour south and back. It belongs between Page and the Moki Dugway.

## The routing fix

Page/Monument Valley sit on the SOUTHERN arc; Hanksville sits on the NORTHERN arc.
Naively you must pick one. The join is **UT-261 (Moki Dugway) + UT-95**: Monument Valley
-> Natural Bridges -> Hanksville is ~3h and picks up a spectacular gravel switchback
climb as a bonus. That single leg makes all 17 stops routable.

Corrected order:
LAS -> Zion -> Kanarraville -> Bryce -> Page -> Monument Valley -> [Moki Dugway /
Natural Bridges] -> Hanksville cluster -> Moab -> Durango -> Million Dollar Hwy ->
Ouray -> Breckenridge -> [WY transit night] -> Grand Teton -> Yellowstone ->
Stansbury Island -> SLC out.

## When — this is the binding constraint, not the day count

Recommended window: **mid-September to early October.**
- Yellowstone/Teton interior roads are seasonal (broadly late May to early November).
  A June trip risks roads still opening; November closes them. GAP: confirm exact
  2027/2028 opening and closing dates once a year is chosen.
- Colorado aspens peak around Ouray/Breckenridge roughly Sept 20 - Oct 1. Free upgrade.
- Yellowstone elk rut is September.
- Utah red rock in July/August runs 100-110F. Moab in July is genuinely unpleasant.
  September drops it to 75-85F.
- Second-best window: late May to mid-June, but the Colorado alpine (Yankee Boy Basin)
  and parts of Yellowstone may still be snowed in.

## Constraints and gotchas to carry into the real plan

- **Bentonite clay is impassable when wet.** Bentonite Hills, Factory Butte and the
  Cathedral Valley loop turn to grease in rain. Needs a same-day decision rule.
  Cathedral Valley proper also fords the Fremont River — 4x4 + high clearance only.
- **Rental class matters.** Moki Dugway, Factory Butte, Shafer Trail, Yankee Boy Basin.
  An AWD SUV with real clearance, not a compact. Check the one-way drop fee LAS->SLC.
- **Bookings that sell out months ahead:** Antelope Canyon, Angels Landing permit
  (seasonal lottery ~3 months out + a day-before lottery), Kanarra Falls (150/day,
  paid permit), The View Hotel at Monument Valley, in-park Yellowstone lodging (12+ months).
- **Monument Valley is Navajo Nation land** — tribal park fees and hours, separate from
  the NPS pass. An America the Beautiful pass does NOT cover it.
- Hiking ceiling from prior trips is ~12 km round trip. Everything above fits:
  Angels Landing 8.7 km, Delicate Arch 4.8 km, Blue Valley Spire 3.5 km.
- Durango & Silverton narrow-gauge railway is an 8h round trip — it costs a whole day
  in Option A/C. Decide explicitly, don't let it ambush the schedule.

## Lane 3 (logistics) complete — 1,917 lines, 122 GAPs. Two findings that change the plan.

### 1. THE RENTAL CONTRACT VOIDS COVER ON THE ROUTING FIX
Quoted verbatim from the agreements: **Avis §15 and Budget §1.5 automatically void all
liability protection and the LDW** on unpaved roads; **Enterprise bans it in §4 and voids the
waiver in §17.** That puts **the Moki Dugway, Shafer Trail and Factory Butte outside cover** —
and the Moki Dugway is the single leg that makes all 17 stops routable.

Proposed fix: a **paved-road major rental** plus a **1–2 day Jeep hire in Moab (~$330–345/day,
sourced)** for the dirt. Needs a decision — it also means the Hanksville cluster and the
Moki Dugway day may need re-sequencing around where the Jeep is picked up.

**Also: "Full-size SUV" buys neither AWD nor clearance.** National publishes its plain SUV
classes as **2 Wheel Drive**. And National/Enterprise/Alamo is the **only** group publicly
stating "typically no drop charge between airport locations" for LAS→SLC.

### 2. FIRST-SNOW DATA, COMPUTED NOT QUOTED
20 seasons of NRCS SNOTEL snow-depth records: **Red Mountain Pass median first snow 9 Oct**
(5 of 20 seasons by 30 Sep), Hoosier Pass 7 Oct, Togwotee 25 Sep. With Dunraven closing
~12 Oct this is the strongest driving-logistics case yet for 2028 over 2027.

### The time-zone trap, now quantified
Page is MST year-round; Monument Valley and Antelope Canyon are on Navajo MDT. **Monument
Valley's sunrise is 6 minutes EARLIER in real time but reads 54 minutes LATER on the clock —
and the tribal park gate does not open until 08:00.** Sunrise from inside the valley is
therefore only possible if staying at The View, inside the gate. That settles the lodging
question for night 7.

### A consequence of my own Frisco substitution
Re-routing Ouray→Frisco instead of Ouray→Breckenridge **quietly swaps Monarch and Hoosier
passes for I-70 over Vail Pass.** Faster and lower, but it drops two of the best passes on
the route. **Needs an explicit decision — do not let a lodging choice silently rewrite the
drive.**

### Distances are solid, times are not
Google Maps was unreachable; every leg was routed twice through OpenStreetMap engines
(Valhalla + OSRM). **Distances agree within 1%; durations diverge and are flagged per leg.**
Point-to-point transit **2,396.7 mi / 3,857 km**, consistent with my ~2,820 mi estimate once
in-park driving is added. Valhalla is badly optimistic on Silverton→Ouray and everywhere
inside Yellowstone (45 mph NPS limit).

## GAP-CLOSE 3 (vehicle) — complete. The routing fix survives, on a technicality that matters.

### RECOMMENDATION: National, class "Full Size SUV AWD" (FFDR), LAS→SLC, on Emerald Club, plus one Canyonlands Jeep day in Moab (~$410 all-in) for the Shafer Trail — NOT for the Dugway.

**No major rental company permits UT-261. That is the honest answer.** Verbatim clauses now
captured for all eight: Avis/Budget §1.5(a)(7) "on unpaved roads"; National/Enterprise/Alamo
§16(10) "unpaved road or off-road"; Hertz §5(d) "USE OFF PAVED ROADS OR ON ROADS WHICH ARE
NOT REGULARLY MAINTAINED"; Sixt "other than a paved road or graded **private** road"; Turo
"undeveloped or unimproved."

### The decisive finding: picking up in NEVADA changes the exposure
**NRS 482.31555** is an *exhaustive permissive list* of what a damage waiver may exclude, and
**NRS 482.3159 makes it non-waivable.** Hertz, National/Enterprise/Alamo and Sixt all publish
matching Nevada blocks under which unpaved driving voids the LDW **only "if the damage or loss
is a direct result of the road or driving conditions."**

**Read precisely:** this does NOT make the Moki Dugway covered. Rock-strike damage caused by
the Dugway is still excluded. What it does is stop the mere act of driving unpaved from
voiding the whole waiver for everything else. **The Dugway becomes a bounded, calculated risk
rather than a contract-voiding act.** Avis and Budget publish no Nevada block — they rank last
and should not be booked.

**Separate trap:** NRS 482.31555(2)(e) excludes operation outside NV/AZ/CA/ID/OR/UT. **The
Colorado and Wyoming legs are safe only because the contract expressly permits US-wide use —
confirm that at the counter.**

### AWD comes only from the class name
Verified against National's live list: **plain Standard / Midsize / Full Size SUV are 2WD.**
The real options are the explicit AWD/4WD variants, "Jeep Wrangler 4 door", and "Premium
All-Terrain" (Rubicon/Defender — **4 seats only**, so it fits a party of three but not luggage
for 25 days). All are "or similar", none model-guaranteed. **Emerald Aisle at LAS lets you
inspect the badge before taking the keys** — that is the practical mitigation.
Correction: **Hertz's Adrenaline Collection is V8 performance cars, not 4x4.**

Pair this with NPS's spec from the Hanksville lane: **"high clearance" = ≥ 8 inches.**

### Child seats — settled
**No booster is required for an 11-year-old in any of NV, UT, AZ, CO or WY.** The two nearest
thresholds are Colorado's tightened 1 Jan 2025 rule (under-9 booster, 9–18 belt) and Wyoming's
under-9 rule. Both clear.
