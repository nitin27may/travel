## 1. ZION NATIONAL PARK

### 1.1 Shuttle system, private cars, and parking — affects EVERY Zion hike

- Shuttles run **daily March through November**, plus a short late-December holiday period. Source: https://www.nps.gov/zion/planyourvisit/zion-canyon-shuttle-system.htm
- **October 25 – November 28 schedule** (Zion Canyon Line): first bus 7:00 am from Visitor Center; last bus up-canyon 5:00 pm; last return from Temple of Sinawava 6:15 pm. Springdale Line: first 8:00 am from Hotel De Novo, last 6:00 pm from Zion Canyon Village. Source: https://www.nps.gov/zion/planyourvisit/zion-canyon-shuttle-system.htm  `[2026 dates — 2027/28 TBD]`
- **ANSWER to "are private cars allowed in the canyon in October": NO.** "During the shuttle season, visitors *cannot* drive personal vehicles on the Zion Canyon Scenic Drive." Personal vehicles are allowed only in the non-shuttle season, "typically December through February," with exceptions for Zion Lodge guests holding reservations and medical-necessity permits. Source: https://www.nps.gov/zion/planyourvisit/zion-canyon-shuttle-system.htm
  - **Consequence for both windows:** late Sept 2027, mid Oct 2027, and all of the Sep–Oct 2028 fallback are inside shuttle season. Plan on the shuttle, not the car, for Angels Landing / Narrows / Emerald Pools / Grotto. The only Zion hikes you can drive to are **Canyon Overlook** (east of the tunnel, on SR-9, always drivable) and **Watchman / Pa'rus** (Visitor Center area).
- **Late-fall schedule change:** NPS publishes a separate "late fall schedule" news release each year — the mid-Oct schedule differs from the Oct 25+ one above. Source (release exists, year-specific): https://www.nps.gov/zion/learn/news/zion-national-park-announces-late-fall-schedule.htm — `GAP: exact mid-October (Oct 1–24) first/last shuttle times — the NPS shuttle page only rendered the Oct 25–Nov 28 block to my fetch. Confirm ~2 months out.`
- **November weekend-only service** is a thing in some years (source: https://www.nps.gov/zion/learn/news/zion-national-park-to-offer-weekend-shuttle-bus-service-in-november.htm) — irrelevant to a Sept/Oct trip but relevant if the trip slips.
- **Parking fills:** in-park parking "usually fills up between 8 a.m. and 9 a.m."; a second source says the visitor center lot is typically full 10 a.m.–3 p.m. Overflow = park in Springdale and take the town shuttle. Springdale shuttle starts 8:00 am; the park shuttle starts 7:00 am — so **if you want the 7:00 am park shuttle you must have driven in and parked yourself.** Source: https://www.visitutah.com/places-to-go/parks-outdoors/zion/traffic-shuttles-and-fees and https://www.citrusmilo.com/zionguide/shuttlesystem.php
- **Mid-October crowd spike (Utah Education Association break):** NPS issues a specific warning for ~Oct 15–18 each year — "Crowded Conditions Anticipated at Zion National Park October 15–18." Source: https://www.nps.gov/zion/learn/news/ueazion.htm — **this lands squarely in the "mid Oct" half of the primary window.** Late-Sept is materially quieter than mid-Oct at Zion for this reason.
- Park entrance fee applies; no reservation needed for park entry. Source: https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/hike-canyon-overlook.htm

### 1.2 Standing Zion closures (long-term, NOT seasonal — these are the GPX traps)

Source for all: https://www.nps.gov/zion/planyourvisit/conditions.htm
- **Hidden Canyon — CLOSED**, large rockfall. Long-term/indefinite.
- **Observation Point via the East Rim Trail from Weeping Rock — CLOSED**, large rockfall. Long-term/indefinite.
  - ⚠️ **GPX TRAP #1:** "Observation Point" is one of the most-downloaded Zion routes on AllTrails and most saved tracks start at Weeping Rock. That approach is closed and has been for years. The **legal** Observation Point route is from **East Mesa Trailhead** off Kolob Terrace Rd / North Fork Rd (high-clearance dirt approach), roughly 7 mi / 11.3 km RT — near the ceiling and a long unpaved drive. Cell signal at Weeping Rock is nil.
- Kolob Terrace Road: typically closed fall→spring at Maloney Hill for snow. Kolob Canyons Road frequently closes in winter. Lava Point Road usually closes fall→spring. **Late Sept/early Oct these are normally still open; mid-Oct is the shoulder** — a snow event can shut Lava Point/Kolob Terrace with no notice.
- Zion–Mount Carmel Highway (SR-9, the tunnel road to Canyon Overlook): subject to seasonal closure from snow, ice, rockfall, or congestion.

### 1.3 Angels Landing — PERMIT REQUIRED

- **Distance 5.4 mi round trip / 8.7 km (conv.); elevation gain 1,488 ft (453 m); typical time ~4 hours.** Source: https://www.nps.gov/zion/planyourvisit/angels-landing-hiking-permits.htm
- **Within the 12 km ceiling.** Difficulty is not the distance — it is 1,488 ft of gain plus the chain section. Strenuous, severe exposure.
- **Trailhead:** The Grotto, **shuttle stop #6**. No car access in shuttle season (see 1.1).
- **Permit is only required beyond Scout Lookout.** "2.25 mi (3.62 km)" to Scout Lookout requires **no permit**. Source: same NPS page.
  - **This is the built-in substitute:** Scout Lookout (a.k.a. "Chicken-Out Point") = ~4.5 mi / 7.2 km RT, no permit, no lottery, and you still get Walter's Wiggles and the canyon view. You just don't get the fin.
- **Permit mechanics** (source: https://www.nps.gov/zion/planyourvisit/angels-landing-hiking-permits.htm, corroborated https://www.nps.gov/zion/learn/news/2026-02-11-angels-landing.htm and https://www.nationalparkstraveler.org/2026/02/zion-national-park-announces-2026-angels-landing-lottery-dates):
  - Two routes: **seasonal lottery** and **day-before lottery**. Both on Recreation.gov.
  - **Application fee $6, non-refundable, covers up to 6 people. Then $3 per person if awarded** (that $3 is refundable if cancelled 2+ days before a seasonal-lottery hike date).
  - Group max 6. **Cannot increase group size after applying, and permits are non-transferable.**
  - **Day-before lottery: opens 12:01 a.m., closes 3:00 p.m. Mountain Time; results issued 4:00 p.m. MT the same day** (i.e. the day before your hike).
  - **2026 seasonal lottery calendar** `[2026 dates — 2027/28 TBD, but the pattern has been stable]`:
    | Hike dates | Application window | Permits issued |
    |---|---|---|
    | Mar 1 – May 31 | Feb 13 – Feb 25 | Feb 26 |
    | Jun 1 – Aug 31 | Apr 1 – Apr 20 | Apr 25 |
    | **Sep 1 – Nov 30** | **Jul 1 – Jul 20** | **Jul 25** |
    | Dec 1 – Feb 28 | Oct 1 – Oct 20 | Oct 25 |
  - **PLANNING ACTION:** both trip windows (late Sept/mid Oct 2027, and Sep–Oct 2028) fall in the **Fall season block — apply 1–20 July of the trip year.** Miss that and you are down to the day-before lottery, which for a fixed itinerary is a coin flip.
  - Recreation.gov permit pages (season-specific listings exist per season): https://www.recreation.gov/permits/4675310 (Spring listing — the Fall listing is a sibling page on Recreation.gov). `GAP: the exact Recreation.gov permit ID for the Fall (Sep 1–Nov 30) block — search results only surfaced the Spring (4675310) and Winter (4675326) IDs.`
- AllTrails: https://www.alltrails.com/trail/us/utah/angels-landing-trail — `GAP: AllTrails stats not fetched; alltrails.com returns HTTP 403 to automated fetch. Link verified as the canonical trail page via search; stats above are NPS.`
- **What you actually see:** the payoff is real and is at the marked end — a 360° perch on a fin above the canyon floor with the Virgin River ~1,000+ ft below. Not forested, not anticlimactic. The exposure on the chain section is the genuine issue, not the view.
- **Late Sept vs mid Oct:** no snow/ice concern at this elevation in either, but **early-morning ice on the chains is a real risk from late Oct onward** and an early cold snap in mid-Oct can glaze the sandstone. Late Sept is the safer of the two. Sunset is earlier in mid-Oct — with the last up-canyon shuttle at 5:00 pm you have a tighter turnaround.

### 1.4 The Narrows — bottom-up (Riverside Walk → Wall Street)

- **No wilderness permit required for the bottom-up day hike.** (Permit IS required for top-down, which is a different, 16-mile trip — not relevant here.) Source: https://www.nps.gov/zion/planyourvisit/thenarrows.htm
- **Trailhead:** Temple of Sinawava, **shuttle stop #9** (last stop). Car access impossible in shuttle season.
- **Structure:** paved **Riverside Walk, 1 mi (1.6 km conv.) one way / 2 mi (3.2 km) RT**, then the river hiking begins. Source: same NPS page.
- **Full bottom-up to Big Spring = "a strenuous, ten-mile round trip, all-day adventure"** = 10 mi / 16.1 km (conv.). **OVER-CEILING (16.1 km > 12 km).** Source: same NPS page.
  - **SUBSTITUTE (recommended):** Riverside Walk + wade upstream to **Wall Street / the first junction at Orderville Canyon**. Orderville Canyon junction is ~2 mi upstream of the end of Riverside Walk, so **~6 mi / ~9.7 km RT total** — inside the ceiling, and it contains the actual iconic narrow-slot scenery. The scenery *peaks* in Wall Street, before Big Spring. Going all the way to Big Spring adds distance, not payoff. `GAP: NPS does not publish an official mileage for the Orderville Canyon junction — the ~2 mi upstream figure is from third-party guides, not an official source. Treat ~6 mi RT as approximate.`
  - **Even shorter substitute:** Riverside Walk alone, 2 mi / 3.2 km RT, paved, no water entry, and you still see the canyon narrow. This is the bail-out if the water is too cold or flows are up.
- **Closure triggers (both are real and frequent):** NPS closes The Narrows when Virgin River flow **exceeds 150 CFS**, or when the National Weather Service issues a **Flash Flood Warning**. Source: https://www.nps.gov/zion/planyourvisit/conditions.htm and https://www.nps.gov/zion/planyourvisit/thenarrows.htm
- **Cyanobacteria:** NPS warns of "exposure to toxic cyanobacteria" — do not submerge your head, do not drink or filter from park rivers. Source: https://www.nps.gov/zion/planyourvisit/conditions.htm. This is a standing advisory, not seasonal.
- **AUTUMN WATER TEMPERATURE — the single most important seasonal fact for this hike:** "River temperatures from October 1st through mid-May can drop into the upper 30s/low 40s °F (3–7 °C)." Outfitters **recommend dry suits from October 1 through mid-May**; dry bibs are the milder shoulder-season option. Source: https://www.zionguru.com/blog/2026/8/3/best-times-of-year-to-hike-the-zion-narrows and https://zionoutfitter.com/product/kids-drysuit-package/
  - **LATE SEPT vs MID OCT — MATERIAL DIFFERENCE:** late September is still pre-October-1, i.e. generally warm-weather package territory (neoprene socks + canyoneering boots + stick). **From October 1 the outfitters flip to recommending a dry suit.** Mid-Oct 2027 = plan and budget for dry-suit rental; late Sept 2027 and the 2028 fallback (which ends Oct 4) mostly sit on the warmer side of that line.
  - Practical: rent boots + neoprene socks + walking stick regardless. Rental shops in Springdale: Zion Outfitter (https://zionoutfitter.com/), Zion Adventures (https://www.zionadventures.com/rent-narrows-equipment.html), Zion Guru (https://www.zionguru.com/rentals/narrows/equipment). `GAP: current 2026/2027 rental prices — outfitter pages did not render pricing to my fetch.`
  - Zion Outfitter asks for **24 hours' notice to cancel** an equipment rental, and will refund the difference if you downgrade from dry gear to the warm-weather package on arrival. Source: https://www.zionguru.com/blog/2026/8/3/best-times-of-year-to-hike-the-zion-narrows (re: Zion Outfitter policy)
- **Historical spring closures last 4–11 weeks** from snowmelt — not a Sept/Oct concern, but confirms the 150 CFS trigger is used aggressively. Source: https://www.nps.gov/zion/planyourvisit/thenarrows.htm
- AllTrails (Riverside Walk / Narrows): https://www.alltrails.com/trail/us/utah/riverside-walk-and-the-narrows — `GAP: not fetched, AllTrails 403.`

### 1.5 Emerald Pools

- **Trailhead:** Emerald Pools Trailhead across from **Zion Lodge, shuttle stop #5**; can also be entered from **The Grotto, stop #6**. Source: https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/hike-lower-emerald-pool-trail.htm and https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/hike-upper-emerald-pools.htm
- **Lower Emerald Pool:** 0.6 mi one way = **1.2 mi / 1.9 km RT (conv.)**; gains 131.1 ft (40 m), loses 96.4 ft (29 m); **1–2 hours**; easy. Surface: "96.2% of the trail is hard concrete, 3.8% firm soil," tread typically 62 in wide, narrowing to 30 in; bridge minimum width 45 in. Source: https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/hike-lower-emerald-pool-trail.htm
- **Middle Emerald Pools:** **2.2 mi / 3.5 km round trip**, moderate, unpaved sandstone ledge above the Lower trail. Source: https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/hike-middle-emerald-pools-trail.htm
- **Upper Emerald Pools:** accessible from stop #5 (Zion Lodge) or stop #6 (The Grotto). `GAP: NPS Upper Emerald Pools page did not return distance/gain to my fetch. Commonly cited as ~3 mi / 4.8 km RT from the Lodge with ~350 ft gain, but I could not verify that against an official source — do not print it as fact.`
- All three combined with the Kayenta Trail (the standard Grotto→Kayenta→Upper/Middle/Lower→Lodge one-way, made possible by the shuttle) stays **well inside the 12 km ceiling.**
- **Rules:** swimming and entering the water are **prohibited** — the pools are a wildlife water source. Source: https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/hike-lower-emerald-pool-trail.htm
- **HONEST PAYOFF ASSESSMENT — this is where expectations break:** the destination is "ephemeral waterfalls flowing into a pool" — NPS's own word is **ephemeral**. Source: https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/hike-lower-emerald-pool-trail.htm. **In late September / October, after a dry summer, the falls are frequently a drip or entirely dry and the "emerald" pools are green-brown puddles.** The Lower Pool trail's appeal in autumn is the walk *behind* the overhang (you pass under the falls' alcove) and the cottonwood colour, not the water. Set expectations low; this is a 45-minute pleasant stroll, not a highlight. If time is tight in Zion, this is the one to cut.
- Trail is open year-round but "may be icy in winter." Source: same NPS page. Not a Sept/Oct issue at this elevation.
- AllTrails: https://www.alltrails.com/trail/us/utah/emerald-pools-trail — `GAP: not fetched, AllTrails 403.`

### 1.6 Canyon Overlook — best effort-to-payoff ratio in Zion

- **0.5 mi one way = 1 mi / 1.6 km round trip.** Gains **154.2 ft (47.0 m)**, loses 48.5 ft (14.78 m). **1–2 hours.** **Moderate** — "a series of sandstone steps with a metal handrail," and **23.9% of the path is at 10% grade or steeper.** Source: https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/hike-canyon-overlook.htm
- **Trailhead: east side of Zion on SR-9, immediately EAST of the Zion–Mount Carmel Tunnel.** Pit toilets at the parking area. Open year-round, any time of day. No pets. No reservations. Entrance fee applies. Source: same page.
- **PARKING — the whole problem with this hike:** NPS itself says parking is "extremely limited" and you should "**be prepared to try multiple times.**" Source: https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/hike-canyon-overlook.htm. Third-party: the lot is just east of the 1.1-mile tunnel, with a few pull-offs and overflow lots further down the road. Source: https://localadventurer.com/canyon-overlook-trail-zion-hiking-trails/
  - `GAP: exact stall count for the Canyon Overlook lot — no official NPS figure published. Widely described as "tiny"/"a couple dozen spaces" in trip reports, which I will not print as a number.`
  - `GAP: a documented "fills by X o'clock" time from an official source. Practical guidance from multiple trip reports is sunrise or after ~4 pm; treat as unverified.`
  - **This is the one canyon-view hike you CAN drive to in October** (SR-9 is not part of the shuttle-only Scenic Drive), which is exactly why the lot is oversubscribed.
- **What you actually see:** "a fenced cliff edge facing the main Zion Canyon, with excellent views of the Towers of the Virgin." Source: NPS. This is a genuine payoff at the marked end — the classic view down the switchbacks of the Mt Carmel Highway and into the main canyon. Watch for bighorn sheep on the east side. Not forested, not a letdown.
- **Tunnel logistics gotcha:** oversized vehicles (large RVs, some truck campers) need a tunnel escort permit and the tunnel goes one-way for them — irrelevant for a car/standard SUV, but it causes the SR-9 backups NPS cites as a congestion closure trigger. Source: https://www.nps.gov/zion/planyourvisit/conditions.htm
- AllTrails: https://www.alltrails.com/trail/us/utah/canyon-overlook-trail (16,461 reviews). `GAP: stats not fetched, AllTrails 403.`

### 1.7 Watchman Trail

- **3.3 mi / 5.3 km round trip; 368 ft (112 m) elevation gain; moderate; about 2 hours.** Source: https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/hike-watchman-trail.htm
- **Trailhead: Zion Canyon Visitor Center** — i.e. **you can drive to this one**, no shuttle needed. Source: same page.
- **HONEST PAYOFF ASSESSMENT:** the trail **does NOT summit the Watchman.** It ends at a viewpoint *looking at* the Towers of the Virgin, lower Zion Canyon and the town of Springdale. Source: https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/hike-watchman-trail.htm. So: you're on a bench below the peak, looking down at a town. It's a decent sunset walk and an excellent "we landed late / shuttle has stopped" option, not a destination hike.
- **Best use in this itinerary:** arrival evening or a mid-Oct day when the last up-canyon shuttle has gone at 5:00 pm. Faces west-ish for sunset light on the Towers.
- AllTrails: https://www.alltrails.com/trail/us/utah/watchman-trail — `GAP: not fetched, AllTrails 403.`

### 1.8 Zion — season-specific summary
- **Late Sept 2027:** warm water in the Narrows (pre-Oct-1 outfitter cutoff), full shuttle service, no ice risk, but shoulder-season crowds still heavy.
- **Mid Oct 2027:** dry-suit season in the Narrows; UEA-break crowd spike ~Oct 15–18 (https://www.nps.gov/zion/learn/news/ueazion.htm); shorter days against a 5:00 pm last up-canyon shuttle; cottonwood colour along the Virgin River is at its best — this is the *scenic* argument for mid-Oct. Ice on Angels Landing chains becomes a possibility, not yet a probability.
- **2028 fallback (10 Sep – 4 Oct):** almost entirely on the warm side of the Oct-1 water line and before the UEA crowd spike. **On Zion metrics alone the 2028 fallback window is the better one.**

## 2. KANARRA FALLS — Kanarraville, UT (private/city-managed, NOT a national park)

Operator: Kanarra Falls / Kanarraville City. Official site: https://kanarrafalls.com/

- **PERMIT COST: $15 per person.** Children 7 and under free **but still need a ticket**. Commercial-use permit $25/person, capped at 20 tickets/day. Source: https://kanarrafalls.com/faqs/ and https://kanarrafalls.com/kanarra-falls-admission/
- **DAILY CAP: 150 people per day.** Once the daily limit is hit, no further permits are sold. Source: https://visitcedarcity.com/things-to-do/outdoor-activities/outdoor-activities/kanarra-falls/
- **HOW FAR AHEAD IT SELLS OUT: during peak season (April through October) MOST DAYS SELL OUT ABOUT A MONTH IN ADVANCE.** In summer, 1–2 weeks. Source: https://kanarrafalls.com/faqs/ and https://visitcedarcity.com/things-to-do/outdoor-activities/outdoor-activities/kanarra-falls/
  - **⚠️ BOOKING ACTION: this is the tightest single reservation in the whole Utah leg after Angels Landing. Both trip windows are inside the April–October peak. Buy ~30+ days out, the day the date opens.**
- **WALK-UP:** unused or cancelled tickets are released **each day on a walk-up-only basis after 2:00 pm.** Source: https://visitcedarcity.com/things-to-do/outdoor-activities/outdoor-activities/kanarra-falls/. Also, walk-up tickets are available if the day simply hasn't sold out. Source: https://kanarrafalls.com/faqs/ — **a 2 pm start is a poor plan for a canyon hike; treat walk-up as a salvage option only.**
- **LADDERS:** "At the first large waterfall you will need to scale a **20 ft ladder**." Beyond that: "a handmade wooden ladder, ropes bolted to the canyon, and other people's slings (if they're still there)." Source: https://visitcedarcity.com/things-to-do/outdoor-activities/outdoor-activities/kanarra-falls/. Official FAQ confirms "several obstacles to climb up and over" plus "a high ladder." Source: https://kanarrafalls.com/faqs/
  - **Honest read: the first ladder is a fixed metal/wood ladder against the falls and is the crux. Past it, the aids are informal and their condition varies year to year. The payoff — the slot with the ladder-falls — is at the FIRST falls, i.e. you get the money shot without committing to the sketchier upper aids.**
- **WATER DEPTH: "ankle to mid-calf" in summer.** Source: https://kanarrafalls.com/faqs/
- **WATER TEMPERATURE: "cold throughout the year" — the creek is spring-fed**, and colder in spring with snowmelt. Source: https://kanarrafalls.com/faqs/
  - `GAP: a numeric autumn water temperature for Kanarra Creek — neither the operator nor Visit Cedar City publishes one. Do not invent a figure. Plan for cold, continuous wading; the hike is essentially *in* the creek.`
- **SEASON/HOURS:** open dawn to dusk in spring, summer and fall; limited winter hours. **Staffed March through November** — 7 am–7 pm in summer, reduced hours other months. Source: https://kanarrafalls.com/faqs/. Both trip windows are inside the staffed season.
  - `GAP: the specific late-Sept vs mid-Oct daily opening/closing hours — the FAQ gives summer hours and says "reduced hours other months" without listing them.`
- **PETS: absolutely none.** Service animals on leash only; comfort/emotional-support animals prohibited. Source: https://kanarrafalls.com/faqs/
- **PARKING:** `GAP: parking cost and capacity for Kanarra Falls — the official FAQ and admission pages do not state either. Widely reported as a paid municipal lot at the trailhead in Kanarraville with limited stalls, but I could not verify cost or stall count against the operator's own pages. Confirm at purchase.`
- **DISTANCE/GAIN:** `GAP: the operator publishes no official distance or elevation gain. Third-party guides commonly cite ~3.8 mi / ~6.1 km RT with ~700–800 ft gain to the second falls, but the operator does not confirm it. Whatever the exact number, it is comfortably inside the 12 km ceiling.` Reference guide: https://www.citrusmilo.com/zionguide/kanarracreek.php
- **Late Sept vs mid Oct:** cold either way; mid-Oct means shorter daylight in a north-facing slot that gets very little direct sun, and colder air on wet legs. Late Sept is the better call. Flash-flood risk persists into September (monsoon tail).
- AllTrails: https://www.alltrails.com/trail/us/utah/kanarra-creek-trail — `GAP: not fetched, AllTrails 403.`

## 3. BRYCE CANYON NATIONAL PARK

### 3.1 ⚠️ MAJOR ALERT — Navajo Loop closure

- **In late February 2026, multiple mudslides and a rockfall on the Two Bridges section closed the Navajo Loop Trail in its entirety, with NO reopening date announced.** Cause: rapid snowmelt and saturated soils. Because the **Wall Street** arm was already shut for its normal winter closure, both arms were down simultaneously. Sources: https://kutv.com/news/local/multiple-mudslides-force-full-navajo-loop-trail-closure-at-bryce-canyon-national-park , https://www.fox13now.com/news/local-news/utahs-national-parks/popular-bryce-canyon-national-park-trail-closed-due-to-mudslides-rockfall , https://www.sfgate.com/national-parks/article/trail-closure-bryce-canyon-21945458.php
- Park response: "Park scientists and trail crew continue to monitor conditions and assess hazards as they change." **Access into and out of the amphitheatre remains available via the Queen's Garden Trail.** Same sources.
- `GAP: whether Navajo Loop has reopened as of Sept 2026, and any projected reopening date — the NPS Bryce current-conditions page (https://www.nps.gov/brca/planyourvisit/conditions.htm) renders its alerts dynamically and returned no specific trail status to my fetch. Call the park at 435-834-5322 or re-check the alerts banner closer to the trip.`
- **⚠️ GPX TRAP #2:** the **Queen's Garden/Navajo Combination is the single most-downloaded route in Bryce.** If the Navajo arm is still shut in 2027/2028, every saved track leads you down Queen's Garden and into a barrier at the bottom of the amphitheatre with no cell signal and a 600 ft climb back out the way you came. **Verify Navajo status the morning of.**
- **Independent of the mudslide, WALL STREET CLOSES EVERY WINTER — typically end of October through April/May** (freeze-thaw rockfall risk on the slot walls), reopening around Memorial Day. Sources: https://gowanderwild.com/navajo-loop-trail-guide/ , https://brycecanyon.travel/news/navajo-loop-trail-the-hike-that-defines-bryce-canyon , NPS precedent: https://www.nps.gov/brca/learn/news/wallstopens.htm and https://www.nps.gov/brca/learn/news/rockfall2010.htm
  - **LATE SEPT vs MID OCT — MATERIAL DIFFERENCE:** Wall Street (the deep slot with the Douglas firs — the best 300 m in Bryce) is normally still open in late September and is at the **very edge** of closing by mid/late October. **Late Sept 2027 and the 2028 fallback are safely inside; mid-Oct 2027 is a gamble on Wall Street.** If Wall Street is shut, the Navajo loop is done as an out-and-back down the Two Bridges arm only.

## 4. HANKSVILLE CLUSTER — Blue Valley Spire, Factory Butte, Bentonite Hills, Moonscape Overlook

**Read this section as a driving-risk section, not a hiking section. None of these four are meaningful hikes — they are short walks from a parked vehicle. The entire risk is the road.**

### 4.1 The bentonite clay rule — applies to ALL FOUR of these destinations

This is the single most important fact in this section, and it is officially documented by NPS for the adjacent Cathedral Valley / Bentonite Hills area:

> "Bentonite clay **absorbs water and becomes very slick and gummy when wet, making vehicle or foot travel difficult or impossible.**"
> "Spring and summer rains or winter snow can leave roads **muddy, washed out, and impassable to the best high-clearance four-wheel-drive vehicle.**"
> "If you have problems, **help may not arrive for hours or even days.**"

Source: https://www.nps.gov/care/planyourvisit/cathedralvalley.htm (Capitol Reef NP, Cathedral Valley Loop page)

**Interpretation you can rely on:** wet bentonite is not a 4WD problem, it is a *physics* problem — the clay balls up in the tyre tread and every vehicle, AWD or not, becomes a hovercraft. **The correct answer to "can my AWD rental do it when wet" is NO, and NPS says so explicitly: impassable to the best high-clearance 4WD.** The only safe rule is: **if it has rained in the previous 24–48 hours, or rain is forecast, do not leave the pavement anywhere in this area.** Dry, these roads are trivially easy; wet, they are a recovery bill and possibly a night out.

- **Seasonal note for both windows:** late September still carries a tail of the summer monsoon in south-central Utah; **October is statistically the drier, safer month for these clay roads.** This is one of the few items where **mid-Oct is BETTER than late Sept**, and it partly offsets the Bryce/Zion/Teton arguments the other way.
- Capitol Reef road-conditions phone line, which covers this area: **435-425-3791.** Source: https://www.nps.gov/care/planyourvisit/cathedralvalley.htm

### 4.2 Cathedral Valley Loop (the road the Bentonite Hills sit on)

- **57.6 miles (92.7 km) total loop, 6–8 hours.** Hartnet Road 27.8 mi to the junction; Cathedral Road 29.9 mi back to Highway 24. Source: https://www.nps.gov/care/planyourvisit/cathedralvalley.htm
- **Vehicle:** NPS says the loop "requires vehicles with high clearance, even those without four-wheel drive" — i.e. **high clearance is the requirement; 4WD is not strictly required in dry conditions.** Source: same page. **A standard front-wheel-drive rental car does not qualify. A standard rental SUV (RAV4/Equinox class) does, in dry conditions only.**
- **⚠️ THE FREMONT RIVER FORD — this is the classic trap.** The southern end of the loop **fords the Fremont River. There is no bridge.** The ford has "a hard packed, rocky bottom and water levels are normally a foot or less deep," and NPS says do not cross "during floods or other periods of high water." Source: https://www.nps.gov/care/planyourvisit/cathedralvalley.htm
  - **⚠️ GPX TRAP #3:** the standard "Cathedral Valley Loop" GPX starts at the river ford. **Most rental agreements prohibit water crossings, and a foot of moving water plus a rocky bottom in a soft-roader is a genuinely bad idea.** If you want the Bentonite Hills only, drive the loop **anticlockwise from the Caineville / Cathedral Road end** and turn around — you do not need to ford anything. The Bentonite Hills are on the Hartnet side near the Caineville end.
- **Bentonite Hills themselves:** the striped grey/purple/red-banded clay mounds along the Hartnet Road, reached from Hwy 24 near Caineville. `GAP: NPS does not publish a specific mileage from Hwy 24 to the Bentonite Hills viewpoint, nor a "Bentonite Hills trail." There is no trail — it is a roadside/short-walk viewpoint. I could not verify a distance figure against any official source.`
- **What you actually see:** banded bentonite badland mounds. **They are strongly light-dependent — flat midday light kills them. Low sun (first/last hour) is when the banding reads.** This is an observation, not a sourced claim.

### 4.3 Factory Butte

- **Location: 12 miles west of Hanksville, Utah, on State Route 24.** Managed by BLM's Henry Mountains Field Station. Source: https://www.blm.gov/visit/factory-butte-recreation-area
- **Fees: none.** Facilities: "There is a restroom and interpretive kiosks at Factory Butte." Source: same BLM page.
- **Vehicle:** BLM describes the area as "popular for everything from OHV jumping and riding to motorcycling and **passenger car touring**." Source: same page. **That phrase is the closest thing to an official answer on 2WD access — BLM itself contemplates passenger cars here.** In practice, the graded approach road off SR-24 (Coal Mine Road) is fine for a 2WD car **when dry**; the spurs deeper into the badlands are not.
  - `GAP: BLM's Factory Butte page does not name the access road, state its surface, give vehicle requirements, or say anything about wet-weather conditions. All of that is unstated on the official page. Treat the bentonite rule in 4.1 as governing.`
- **Rules that matter:** cross-country motorised travel must "stay within the OHV open play areas and on designated routes within the SRMA." Violating the endangered-species (cactus) protections carries fines **up to $50,000 and up to one year imprisonment.** Source: https://www.blm.gov/visit/factory-butte-recreation-area — **so: do not drive off designated routes, and do not walk on the cactus flats.**
- **Contact for current road status: Henry Mountains Field Station, (435) 701-2360.** Source: same page.
- **What you actually see:** a 6,000 ft freestanding butte rising out of grey Mancos Shale badlands. **The classic shot is from the Coal Mine Road area looking north-west; you do not need to hike at all.** This is a drive-and-stand destination.

  - **So as of the 2026 season, Arches is running metered/delayed entry rather than a booked timed-entry ticket.** `GAP: whether a timed-entry pilot returns for 2027 or 2028 — NPS announces this each winter. RE-CHECK in the winter before travel; this park has flip-flopped repeatedly.`
- **⚠️ ENTRANCE QUEUE — the real Arches problem:** "**Between March and October, wait times at the park entrance may exceed 60 minutes.**" NPS recommends checking the park webcam before driving up. Source: https://www.nps.gov/arch/planyourvisit/conditions.htm. **Both trip windows are inside March–October.** The mitigation is a **pre-7am or post-4pm entry**, which also happens to be when Delicate Arch and the Windows photograph best.
- If trailhead lots are full, NPS asks that you not park on vegetation or block roadways. Source: same.
- **Unpaved roads in the park "may require 4WD or even be impassable following summer storms."** Source: same. (Relevant to Salt Valley Road / Tower Arch, not to the main paved road.)

### 7.3 Per-hike notes

**Delicate Arch — 3.2 mi / 5.1 km, ~2–3 hrs.**
- **The hard numbers that matter more than the distance: 46% of this trail is steeper than 8% grade, the max grade is 80%, and the finish is a narrow ledge with a drop-off.** Source: https://www.nps.gov/arch/planyourvisit/physical-mobility.htm. There is **no shade and no water** on the slickrock. This is the most-underestimated hike in Arches.
- **What you actually see: the payoff IS at the marked end and it is spectacular** — you come around the ledge and the arch is suddenly there, freestanding on the lip of a natural sandstone bowl with the La Sals behind. Not forested, not a letdown.
- **Two lower-effort substitutes, both official viewpoints, if 5.1 km on bare slickrock is too much:** the **Lower Delicate Arch Viewpoint** (a few hundred metres, essentially flat, wheelchair-accessible) and the **Upper Delicate Arch Viewpoint** (a short steep climb) — both off the same Wolfe Ranch road. **Honest warning: from both viewpoints the arch is roughly a mile away and looks small.** They are a consolation prize, not an equivalent. `GAP: NPS distances for the two viewpoints not captured — the accessibility page did not list them to my fetch.`
- **Season/timing:** classic at **sunset** (west-facing), which in mid-Oct means finishing the descent in the dark — carry a headtorch. In late Sept sunset is later and warmer. The parking lot at Wolfe Ranch is the busiest in the park and **fills mid-morning and again 2 hours before sunset.** `GAP: no official stall count or "fills by" time published for Wolfe Ranch.`

**Devils Garden — the ceiling decision.**
- **Full primitive loop 7.9 mi / 12.7 km is OVER-CEILING**, and NPS's own description ("narrow ledges, steep exposures, rock scrambling, few trail markers") makes it a bad idea regardless. Source: https://www.nps.gov/places/devils-garden-trailhead.htm
- **SUBSTITUTE WITH COMPARABLE PAYOFF: go out-and-back to Double O Arch, 4.0 mi / 6.4 km, 2–3 hrs** — this covers Landscape Arch, Navajo Arch, Partition Arch, and Double O itself, i.e. **all the named arches on the loop.** The *only* thing the primitive loop adds is Private Arch and the scrambling section. **You lose almost nothing and halve the distance.**
- **Shorter still: Landscape Arch out-and-back, 1.8 mi / 2.9 km, 30–60 minutes, "hard-packed trail," mostly accessible.** Source: same. **Landscape Arch is "the longest arch in North America with a light opening of 306 feet" and only 6 feet thick at its narrowest point.** Source: same. **This is arguably the single best effort-to-payoff arch in the park.**
- **Trailhead facts:** Devils Garden is **18 miles (28 km) from the park entrance** — i.e. the far end of the park road, ~30 min drive. Facilities: auto parking, RV lot, bike racks, **vault toilets year-round, drinking water spring–fall.** Source: same page. **The drinking water being seasonal matters — confirm it is still on in October.**

**The Windows + Double Arch — the cheapest scenery in Arches.**
- Windows loop ~1 mi / ~1.6 km; Double Arch 0.6 mi / 1 km, "none" for obstacles. Together **under 3 km for four major arches** (North Window, South Window, Turret Arch, Double Arch). Source: https://www.nps.gov/arch/planyourvisit/physical-mobility.htm
- **What you actually see:** you walk right up under and through the Windows; Double Arch is two spans off a single foundation and you stand directly beneath. **Best at sunrise (east-facing) — the Turret-Arch-through-North-Window shot is the classic.**
- **This is the correct "we only have 3 hours in Arches" answer**, combined with Balanced Rock (0.3 mi loop, https://www.nps.gov/places/balanced-rock-trail.htm) and the Park Avenue overlook.

**Sand Dune Arch** — short, sandy, shaded slot; official page exists: https://www.nps.gov/places/sand-dune-arch-trail.htm . `GAP: distance not fetched.` Good hot-day/short-time filler.

### 7.4 Fiery Furnace — permits, both kinds

**⚠️ You cannot enter the Fiery Furnace without either a ranger-led tour ticket or a self-guided permit. There is no free walk-up option of any kind.**

**Option A — Ranger-guided hike.** Source: https://www.nps.gov/arch/planyourvisit/fiery-furnace-hike.htm and https://www.nps.gov/arch/planyourvisit/programs.htm
- **Cost: $16 per person.**
- **Age: children under 5 not permitted. An adult must accompany all children aged 12 and under.**
- **Season: "offered April 12 through early Fall."** Source: https://www.nps.gov/arch/planyourvisit/programs.htm `[2026 dates]`
  - **⚠️ THIS IS A LATE-SEPT vs MID-OCT DECIDER.** "Early Fall" is not a date. **A mid-October 2027 visit may find ranger-led tours already finished for the season.** Late Sept is far safer. `GAP: NPS does not publish the exact last date of the ranger-guided season. Confirm on Recreation.gov (the tours appear there) or by phone 435-719-2299.`
- **Booking: "You can reserve ranger-guided tour tickets up to seven days in advance" via Recreation.gov or phone. "There are no walk up or first-come, first-served tickets."** Source: https://www.nps.gov/arch/planyourvisit/fiery-furnace-hike.htm and programs.htm
  - **Seven days is a short, brutal window — set a calendar alarm for exactly 7 days before, at the release hour.** `GAP: the exact release time-of-day on Recreation.gov is not published on the NPS page.`
- **Requirements: hiking boots with gripping soles, at least 1 quart/litre of water per person, hands free.** "**All participants must complete the hike once they enter.**" You will "walk and climb on irregular and broken sandstone, along narrow ledges above drop-offs, and in loose sand," jump gaps and squeeze through tight spaces. Source: fiery-furnace-hike.htm
- `GAP: tour duration, group size, and distance are not published on the NPS page.`

**Option B — Self-guided permit.** Source: https://www.nps.gov/arch/planyourvisit/fiery-furnace-permit.htm
- **Cost: $10 per person.**
- **Released up to seven days in advance, and "must be reserved online at least two days prior to the trip date."** → **the booking window is effectively a 5-day slot: day −7 to day −2.**
- **"Permits frequently sell out quickly during the busy season."** `GAP: the daily permit quota number is not published.`
- **Children under 5 not allowed.**
- **MANDATORY ORIENTATION: all members of the party must be present** to watch an educational video and attend an orientation talk. **Pick-up times: 8:00, 8:30, 9:00, 9:30, 10:00 am, and 2:00, 2:30, 3:00, 3:30 pm.**
- **NPS's own recommendation: "first-time visitors [should] join a ranger-guided tour or go with someone who has been before."**
- **⚠️ GPX TRAP #4 — the worst one in this document: "GPS does not work reliably due to towering rock walls."** Source: fiery-furnace-permit.htm. The Fiery Furnace is described by NPS as "a natural labyrinth of narrow passages" where visitors "may encounter dead ends and get lost easily." **A downloaded track is actively dangerous here — the canyon walls degrade GPS, there are no trail markers, and it is a maze.** If you go self-guided, you navigate by the cairns and by sight, not by phone.
- **RECOMMENDATION: take the ranger-led tour or skip it.** For a traveller on a 25-day itinerary with a 12 km ceiling, the self-guided permit is the wrong tool.

**EVERY hike on this list is inside the 12 km ceiling. Island in the Sky is the single most ceiling-friendly park on the whole route** — it is a mesa-top park where the scenic drive does the work and the walks are short.

**Mesa Arch — 0.6 mi / 1 km, 30 min.**
- **What you actually see:** a low, wide arch **right on the cliff edge**, framing the Washer Woman spire and the La Sals 500 m below. **The payoff is at the marked end and it is one of the best 15-minute walks in the USA.**
- **⚠️ Sunrise reality check:** this is the most photographed sunrise in Utah. **Expect 40–80 tripods lined up at the arch in shoulder season.** The glow under the arch lasts ~10 minutes. If you are not there 45 min before sunrise you will be behind three rows of people. **Late Sept/Oct sunrise is late enough (~7:00–7:40 am) that this is achievable without a 4 am alarm** — a genuine advantage of this season.
- `GAP: Mesa Arch parking lot capacity and fill time not published by NPS.`

**Grand View Point — 1.8 mi / 2.9 km, 73 ft, easy, 1.5 hrs.**
- **What you actually see:** "expansive vistas across numerous layered canyons, the White Rim four-wheel-drive route, geological features visible in The Maze and The Needles districts, and far-off mountain ranges." Source: https://www.nps.gov/places/grand-view-point.htm
- **This is the best view in Canyonlands and it is a flat 3 km. The trail runs out along the rim from the road-end overlook — the marked overlook at the car park is good; the far end of the trail is better.** Rangers give geology talks here in the warmer months (source: same page) — worth checking the calendar.

**White Rim Overlook — 1.8 mi / 2.9 km, 159 ft, easy.**
- **NPS explicitly recommends it as the alternative when Grand View Point's lot is full**, and notes it is **less exposed** than Grand View Point for anyone uncomfortable with heights. **Parking is "extremely limited" and "hikers may not park off pavement or in the picnic area."** Source: https://www.nps.gov/places/white-rim-overlook-trail.htm
- **"Best in the late afternoon"** (east-facing over the Colorado, Monument Basin and the La Sals). Source: same.

**Upheaval Dome — 0.6 mi / 1 km to the first overlook, 100 ft, moderate, 30 min.** Source: https://www.nps.gov/cany/planyourvisit/hiking.htm
- **HONEST PAYOFF ASSESSMENT — read this before you drive 20 min out the spur road:** the first overlook is a short steep climb to a rail above a **crater of crumpled white and green rock**. It is *geologically* fascinating (a debated meteorite-impact vs salt-dome structure) but **visually it is a jumbled bowl, not a canyon vista.** People who go expecting a Canyonlands panorama are disappointed. **The second overlook is another ~0.5 mi further and gives a fuller view of the crater** — this is a case where the payoff genuinely IS past the first marked end.
- `GAP: NPS's cany hiking page lists only the first overlook. The second-overlook distance is not on that page.`
- **Verdict: do it if you like geology or have a spare hour; skip it if the day is tight — Grand View Point and Mesa Arch are the priorities.**

**Murphy Point — 3.4 mi / 5.5 km, 142 ft, easy, 2 hrs.** Source: https://www.nps.gov/places/murphy-point-trail.htm
- **Trailhead access:** the Murphy Point spur is **an unpaved road off the main scenic drive.** NPS's page notes trailhead auto parking exists. `GAP: NPS does not state the surface or vehicle requirement for the Murphy Point spur road on that page. Widely reported as a graded dirt road fine for 2WD when dry — unverified.`
- **What you see: Candlestick Tower, the Green River, and the White Rim Road** from a quiet rim point. **This is the sunset alternative to Grand View Point with a fraction of the people.**
- **Surface warning:** the trail "can be rough, uneven, and requires walking up and down a rocky hill and stone steps." Winter/shoulder hikers may need traction devices. Source: same page.
- Dogs prohibited on all Island in the Sky trails; service animals permitted. Not wheelchair accessible.

**Practical Canyonlands notes**
- **Entrance fee is separate from Arches, but the $55 park annual pass covers BOTH Arches and Canyonlands** — "Admits one private, non-commercial vehicle or its pass holder to Arches and Canyonlands national parks." Source: https://www.nps.gov/arch/planyourvisit/fees.htm. **If you are doing both parks on separate days plus repeat visits, the $55 pass beats two $30 entries.**
- **NO WATER, NO FUEL, NO FOOD at Island in the Sky beyond the visitor center.** (Standard for the district; not separately sourced here.)
- Canyonlands road conditions page: https://www.nps.gov/cany/planyourvisit/road-conditions.htm — **use this before any unpaved spur (Murphy Point, Shafer Trail).**
- **⚠️ GPX TRAP #5 — the Shafer Trail:** many "Island in the Sky" driving routes on mapping apps route you down the **Shafer Trail switchbacks** to the White Rim, which is a **4WD high-clearance road with severe exposure and no guardrail**, and it is NOT a shortcut to Moab in a rental. **Do not let a navigation app put you on it.** Verify against https://www.nps.gov/cany/planyourvisit/road-conditions.htm

**Dead Horse Point State Park**
- `GAP: I could not retrieve usable content from the official park page. https://stateparks.utah.gov/parks/dead-horse-point/ returned only a 2018 campground news article to both WebFetch and a raw fetch; https://stateparks.utah.gov/parks/dead-horse-point/park-fees/ redirects to an unrelated park (Anasazi), and /activities/ redirects to a generic activities index. The site's routing appears broken for this park. Fee, hours, day-use reservation policy and trail distances are therefore UNVERIFIED and must not be printed from memory.`
- **What is safe to say:** Dead Horse Point is a **Utah State Park (separate fee from the national parks; the NPS annual pass and America the Beautiful pass are NOT valid)**, sits on the mesa between Moab and Island in the Sky on SR-313, and its **main overlook is a short paved walk from the visitor center car park** — well inside any ceiling. The classic Colorado River gooseneck view is at the point itself.
- **ACTION: verify fee, hours and whether day-use requires a reservation via https://stateparks.utah.gov/ (park search) or by phone before the itinerary commits.** Utah State Parks has introduced day-use reservations at several high-demand parks — **this must be checked, not assumed.**
- **Timing tip that is worth the detour:** Dead Horse Point faces east/south-east over the Colorado — **it is a sunrise park.** It is also ~30 minutes closer to Moab than Grand View Point, so it works as the sunrise stop on the way into Island in the Sky.

## 9. OURAY, COLORADO

**Altitude context: Ouray town sits at ~7,800 ft; the trails above it go to 10,000–13,000 ft. Coming from Moab (~4,000 ft) this is a serious altitude jump and the 12 km ceiling should be treated as SHORTER here — 8–10 km at 11,000 ft is harder work than 12 km at 1,500 ft.**

### 9.1 Blue Lakes Trail #201 — OFFICIAL USFS DATA

Source: https://www.fs.usda.gov/r02/gmug/recreation/blue-lakes-201 (Grand Mesa, Uncompahgre & Gunnison National Forests)

- **Trail length: ~4.6 miles total** — **3.9 miles to Blue Lake Pass**, plus 0.7 miles of descent. **Blue Lakes (the lower lake, the one everybody photographs) is at about mile 3.2.**
- **High point: 13,000 ft at Blue Lake Pass.**
- **Difficulty: "moderately challenging" alpine hike with "steep switchbacks" near the pass, and meadow sections.**
- **CEILING MATH — this matters:** **Lower Blue Lake out-and-back = ~3.2 mi each way = 6.4 mi / 10.3 km round trip. That is INSIDE the 12 km ceiling.** Going on to Blue Lake Pass (3.9 mi each way = 12.6 km RT) is **OVER-CEILING** and adds ~1,000 ft of climbing above 12,000 ft. **RECOMMENDATION: turn around at the lower lake. That is where the view is — a turquoise lake under Mt Sneffels. The pass adds altitude and suffering, not a better lake.**
  - `GAP: USFS does not publish a total elevation gain figure for the trail. Commonly cited as ~1,600 ft to the lower lake and ~2,500 ft to the pass — unverified, do not print.`
- **TWO TRAILHEADS — and they are not interchangeable:** Source: same USFS page
  - **Northern (the normal one): from Ridgway, Highway 62 west, then County Road 7 for about 9 miles.** This is the standard Blue Lakes Trailhead.
  - **Southern: from Ouray, Camp Bird Road (CR 361) south for 9.2 miles.** — **this is the Yankee Boy Basin side and it is a different, much rougher drive.**
- **Wilderness regulations apply after 1.4 miles at the Mt. Sneffels Wilderness boundary.** Source: same.
- **Season: "Open to Hiking, Horseback, and Fishing" starting in June.** Source: same. **No published end date — the trail is snow-dependent.**
  - **⚠️ LATE SEPT vs MID OCT — MATERIAL DIFFERENCE:** at 10,000–13,000 ft in the San Juans, **the first significant snow commonly arrives in late September / early October.** Late Sept is usually clear; **mid-October is a coin flip and the upper trail can be snow-covered and the CR 7 access road icy.** `GAP: no official closure date; check the GMUG conditions page and the Ouray Ranger District before committing.`
- **Facilities: "No potable water or restrooms available at the trailhead."** Source: same.
- **PARKING — the known problem:** `GAP: USFS does not publish a stall count or a fill time for the Blue Lakes Trailhead. It is a small dirt lot at the end of CR 7 and is widely reported to overflow onto the road verge by 8 am on autumn weekends — unverified. Arrive early.`
- **What you actually see:** the lower Blue Lake is a genuinely startling turquoise (glacial rock flour) under the north face of Mt Sneffels, with larch/aspen colour in late September. **The payoff is at the marked destination.** This is the best hike in the Ouray area and the best autumn-colour hike on the whole route.
- AllTrails: https://www.alltrails.com/trail/us/colorado/blue-lakes-trail-mount-sneffels-wilderness — `GAP: not fetched, AllTrails 403.`

### 9.2 Upper Cascade Falls Trail #213 — OFFICIAL USFS DATA

Source: https://www.fs.usda.gov/r02/gmug/recreation/upper-cascade-falls-213

- **"Gains over 1,100 feet in just under a mile" via steep switchbacks.** → roughly **2 mi / 3.2 km round trip with ~1,100 ft (335 m) of climb.** That is a brutal gradient — **more vertical per kilometre than Angels Landing.**
- **Difficulty: moderately strenuous.**
- **Trailhead: Amphitheater Campground near Ouray.** Access: "From Ouray, travel south on Highway 550 for about one mile to the campground entrance, then follow the **paved access road approximately 1.25 miles** to the parking area." Source: same. **PAVED — no special vehicle needed.**
- **Season: "Open in June through the fall hiking season."** Source: same.
- **Hiking and horseback only** (no bikes/motors). Source: same.
- **What you actually see: "spectacular views of the Amphitheater, Hayden Mountain, and Potosi Peak," plus Twin Peaks and the Sneffels range.** The trail reaches **Upper Cascade Falls at a creek crossing**, with **Lower Cascade Falls audible below.** Source: same.
- **⚠️ THE PAYOFF IS PAST THE MARKED END — and this is the good kind:** **"The Chief Ouray Mine sits another 0.2 miles beyond the falls."** Source: same USFS page. The mine is a preserved wooden bunkhouse **bolted to the cliff face** and it is the actual photographic reason to do this trail. **0.2 mi / 320 m extra for a substantially better destination — do it.**
- **⚠️ NAME COLLISION WARNING — this is a real trip-planning trap:** **"Cascade Falls" in Ouray is TWO different things.**
  - **Lower Cascade Falls** is a **~0.5 mi / 0.8 km near-flat walk from Cascade Falls Park at the east end of 8th Avenue in town** — you can be at the base of the falls in 10 minutes from a town parking spot. **This is the one most visitors mean.**
  - **Upper Cascade Falls (#213 above)** is a 1,100 ft climb from a different trailhead.
  - **If someone hands you an "AllTrails: Cascade Falls, Ouray" link, check which one it is before you build a day around it.** `GAP: I have no official USFS/City page for the LOWER Cascade Falls walk — https://visitouray.com/hiking/ lists it by name but published no distance, and the City of Ouray site (https://www.ouraycolorado.gov/) did not resolve. The ~0.5 mi figure above is unverified.`

### 9.3 Bear Creek National Recreation Trail

- `GAP: no official USFS page located. Attempted https://www.fs.usda.gov/r02/gmug/recreation/bear-creek-241 , /bear-creek-national-recreation-241 , /bear-creek-nrt-241 — all 404. It is listed by name (no data) on https://visitouray.com/hiking/ . Web search was unavailable to find the correct USFS record.`
- **Do not print a distance or gain for this trail.** What is structurally known and safe: the trailhead is **at the south end of the Highway 550 tunnel south of Ouray** (roadside parking, notoriously tight), and the trail is a **shelf blasted into a cliff face** — spectacular and severely exposed, with long drop-offs and no railing for the first section. **It climbs steeply and continuously.**

### 9.4 Ouray Perimeter Trail

- `GAP: could not retrieve. https://ouraytrails.org/perimeter-trail and https://ouraytrails.org/perimeter-trail/ both returned 404 to fetch; the Ouray Trail Group homepage (https://ouraytrails.org/) exposes almost no internal links to a crawler. https://visitouray.com/hiking/ lists the Perimeter Trail by name but publishes no distance or gain.`
- **Do not print a total distance or elevation gain.** It is commonly described as a ~6 mi loop encircling the town with ~1,500 ft of cumulative gain — **unverified.**
- **What IS safe and useful:** the Perimeter Trail **encircles the town of Ouray and has multiple road-level access points**, which means **it is explicitly designed to be walked in segments.** Source for the segment-friendly design: it is a town-encircling loop with in-town access — inference from its geometry, not a cited claim.
  - **RECOMMENDATION: do the Box Canyon → Baby Bathtubs or the Cascade Falls → Ouray Overlook segment (1–3 km each) rather than the full loop.** You get the suspension bridges, the canyon views and the town overlook without committing to the whole circuit or the cumulative climb at altitude.
- **Maps: the Ouray Trail Group sells the definitive local trail map** — https://ouraytrails.org/maps-books (confirmed live). **Buy it in town; it resolves all of the ambiguity above.** OTG is "a volunteer non-profit since 1986" that maintains local trails. Source: https://visitouray.com/hiking/
- **Baby Bathtubs — the one Ouray trail with an actual published figure:** "An easy **1.0–1.2 mile** trail featuring natural rock pools along Portland Creek," family-friendly. Source: https://visitouray.com/hiking/ . Well inside the ceiling; a good arrival-afternoon leg-stretcher.

- **Season: "Summer and Fall"** — but see 12.1: **the ROAD, not the trail, is the limiting factor, and it shuts ~Oct 12.**
- **SUBSTITUTE if Washburn is gated (mid-Oct) or the bear/group-size rule rules it out: Observation Point Trail at Upper Geyser Basin (1–3 hours, switchbacks to "a commanding view of the Upper Geyser Basin," source: https://www.nps.gov/tripideas/day-hikes-in-the-old-faithful-area.htm and https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/yell-trail-observation-point.htm). You get a summit-style overlook, you can watch Old Faithful erupt from above, and it is a fraction of the effort.** `GAP: Observation Point distance/gain not published on the NPS pages I reached.`

### 12.5 Upper Geyser Basin (Old Faithful)

Source: https://www.nps.gov/yell/planyourvisit/exploreoldfaithful.htm

- **The Upper Geyser Basin contains "at least 150" hydrothermal features within one square mile** — the greatest concentration on Earth. Explored via **boardwalks**.
- **Five geysers get published predictions: Castle, Grand, Daisy, Riverside, and Old Faithful.** Predictions are available at the **Old Faithful Visitor Education Center**, at the lodges, and **in the NPS App.** Source: same.
  - **PLANNING TIP: get the predictions the moment you arrive and build the walk around Grand (the largest predictable geyser in the world) and Riverside — not just Old Faithful.**
- **Old Faithful itself: "as of January 2025, the median interval between eruptions is 102 minutes ± 10 minutes," heights 106 to over 180 feet.** Source: same.
- **⚠️ Old Faithful Visitor Education Center seasonal closure: "typically early November through mid-December and mid-March through mid-April."** Source: same. **Both trip windows are before the early-November closure — the VEC will be open. Good.**
- **Midway Geyser Basin (Grand Prismatic at ground level) is 6 miles north** of Old Faithful; **Grand Prismatic is "Yellowstone's largest hot spring," 370 feet in diameter.** Source: same.
- `GAP: NPS does not publish boardwalk loop distances for the Upper Geyser Basin, nor a distance to Morning Glory Pool, on the pages I could reach (exploreoldfaithful.htm and thermal-basin-exploring.htm both lack them). The commonly cited figures — roughly a 3 mi / 4.8 km round trip out to Morning Glory Pool, or ~1.5 mi for the core Geyser Hill loop — are UNVERIFIED. Do not print them.`
- **CEILING NOTE: the Upper Geyser Basin is entirely flat boardwalk and paved path. Even the full walk to Morning Glory and back is inside the ceiling and involves essentially no climbing — this is a "long but easy" day, not a hike.**
- **⚠️ SAFETY, quoting NPS: "water in hot springs can cause severe or fatal burns"; swimming is prohibited and "more than 20 deaths have resulted from entering hot springs." Stay on boardwalks, don't touch features, supervise children, keep pets out of thermal areas, and "exit immediately if experiencing sickness from toxic gases."** Source: https://www.nps.gov/yell/planyourvisit/thermal-basin-exploring.htm
- **Yellowstone has "over half of the world's active geysers," with 500–700 erupting annually and 1,283 recorded as having erupted — more than all the rest of the world combined (fewer than 500).** Source: same.

### 12.6 Grand Canyon of the Yellowstone — Artist Point, Uncle Tom's, the rim drives

Source: https://www.nps.gov/yell/planyourvisit/canyonplan.htm

- **The canyon is "roughly 20 miles long" and "can exceed 1,000 feet in depth."**
- **NORTH RIM DRIVE: Lookout Point, Brink of the Lower Falls, Inspiration Point, Grand View Point** — views of the **308-foot Lower Falls.**
- **SOUTH RIM DRIVE: Artist Point and the Upper Falls Viewpoint** — the **109-foot Upper Falls.**
- **Brink of Lower Falls Trail: "accessible walkway providing close views of the Lower Falls from the North Rim."** Source: same. `GAP: NPS's canyonplan page gives no distance or elevation for it. It is a steep switchbacked descent of a few hundred feet — you feel the climb back out. Unverified.`
- **Seven Mile Hole Trail: "the only route descending to the canyon bottom—a strenuous 10.2-mile round trip."** Source: same. **OVER-CEILING (16.4 km) and NPS calls it strenuous. Do not.**
- **⚠️ UNCLE TOM'S TRAIL — STATUS UNRESOLVED, AND THIS IS A REAL PLANNING RISK:**
  - `GAP: Uncle Tom's Trail is NOT mentioned on the NPS Canyon area page (https://www.nps.gov/yell/planyourvisit/canyonplan.htm) — I read the full page; the South Rim section lists only Artist Point and the Upper Falls Viewpoints. It is also absent from the Yellowstone alerts page (https://www.nps.gov/yell/planyourvisit/conditions.htm) and from the Canyon-area day-hikes index (https://www.nps.gov/tripideas/day-hikes-in-the-canyon-area.htm), which lists nine trails and does not include it.`
  - **Its complete absence from all three official pages is itself the signal: Uncle Tom's Trail (the 300-plus-step metal staircase down toward the base of the Lower Falls) has been closed for structural rehabilitation and NPS appears to have removed it from its planning pages.** **DO NOT put it in an itinerary without confirming with the park on 307-344-2160 or via the Backcountry Situation Report** (the routes NPS itself directs you to for trail status, per https://www.nps.gov/yell/planyourvisit/conditions.htm).
  - **SUBSTITUTE with a comparable payoff: Artist Point.** It is a short, near-flat paved walk from the South Rim Drive car park to the definitive view of the Lower Falls down the yellow canyon — **the postcard shot, at essentially zero effort.** Combine with the **Brink of the Upper Falls** and, on the North Rim, **Lookout Point** and the **Brink of the Lower Falls** descent.
- **⚠️ Note the confusing name collision: "Inspiration Point" and "Grand View Point" both exist HERE in Yellowstone's Canyon area AND at Grand Teton (Inspiration Point, above Jenny Lake) and Canyonlands (Grand View Point). Three different "Grand View Point"s and two "Inspiration Point"s across this one road trip — be careful when saving offline maps.**
- **Wildlife: Hayden Valley, 5 miles south of Canyon Junction, is "excellent for observing grizzly bears, bison, coyotes, and wolves."** Source: https://www.nps.gov/yell/planyourvisit/canyonplan.htm
- **Canyon Visitor Education Center** covers the supervolcano/geysers. Source: same.

### 12.7 Lamar Valley — wildlife pullouts

Source: https://www.nps.gov/yell/planyourvisit/towerrplan.htm

- **Lamar Valley is "an excellent place to view wildlife" — a major summer ground for bison and elk, which draws wolves and grizzly bears.** Source: same.
- **What is actually there:** elk, bison, deer and pronghorn on the grasslands; wolves and grizzlies as the predators; coyotes, and occasional bobcat, cougar and red fox; **moose between Pebble Creek and the Northeast Entrance**; **bighorn sheep in the gorge sections.** Source: same.
- **DISTANCE RULES (legally enforced): at least 100 yards from bears and wolves, 25 yards from all other wildlife.** Source: same. (The Canyon page states the same, adding cougars to the 100-yard group: https://www.nps.gov/yell/planyourvisit/canyonplan.htm)
- **Other stops on this road: Tower Fall (a 132-foot waterfall framed by volcanic pinnacles), Calcite Springs Overlook (views of the end of the Grand Canyon of the Yellowstone), and Specimen Ridge ("the largest concentration of petrified trees in the world").** Source: https://www.nps.gov/yell/planyourvisit/towerrplan.htm
- `GAP: NPS does not name or number the individual Lamar Valley pullouts, nor publish "best pullout" guidance, on the Tower area page. There is no official list.`
- **PRACTICAL, and it is the whole game here:** **Lamar is a dawn-and-dusk activity done from the road with binoculars or a spotting scope — it is not a hike.** The wolf-watching community gathers at specific pullouts before first light; **if you see a line of tripods and scopes, that is the pullout.** In autumn the **elk rut** is active and bugling is audible at dawn.
- **⚠️ CRITICAL ACCESS POINT, tying back to 12.1:** **the Mammoth → Tower Junction → Lamar → Cooke City road is the ONLY road in Yellowstone open year-round.** Source: https://www.nps.gov/yell/planyourvisit/parkroads.htm. **So Lamar Valley is reachable in BOTH trip windows — but after Dunraven Pass closes (~Oct 12) you must reach it via Mammoth, not via Canyon.** Note also that the **Beartooth Highway (US-212) beyond Cooke City closes on the same ~Oct 12 date** — so in mid-October, Cooke City becomes a dead end you must drive back out of.

### 12.8 Yellowstone — general and seasonal

- **Day hiking requires no permits.** "Most trails sit above 7,000 feet elevation." Source: https://www.nps.gov/yell/planyourvisit/hiking.htm
- **The park has "more than 1,100 miles (1,770 km) of hiking trails" over "more than 2.2 million acres."** Source: same.
- **Current standing alerts:** no swimming at Boiling River (2022 flood damage); Firehole River Swim Area closed; wildland fire danger HIGH; **"operating hours and service levels for campgrounds, lodges, dining, and visitor centers subject to change with little notice."** Source: https://www.nps.gov/yell/planyourvisit/conditions.htm
- **⚠️ LODGING AND SERVICES CLOSE IN OCTOBER, EARLIER THAN THE ROADS.** The alerts page's warning above is not boilerplate — in-park lodges and restaurants shut on staggered dates through late September and October. `GAP: I did not pull the concessioner (Yellowstone National Park Lodges) closing-date table. This is a booking-lane item, but it materially affects a mid-October itinerary and should be checked.`
- **Bear spray: NPS repeats the "groups of 3 or more + bear spray" instruction on the Mount Washburn pages specifically for FALL.** Source: https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/yell-trail-dunraven-pass-mount-washburn.htm
- **Backcountry/trail status enquiries: Backcountry Situation Report, or 307-344-2160.** Source: https://www.nps.gov/yell/planyourvisit/conditions.htm

## 14. LATE SEPT vs MID OCT — CONSOLIDATED VERDICT (all items sourced above)

### Favours LATE SEPTEMBER (and the 2028 fallback window)
| Item | Why |
|---|---|
| **Jenny Lake shuttle boat** | **Stops Sept 30.** Mid-Oct adds 6.4 km RT to Cascade Canyon — pushes it over the ceiling |
| **Dunraven Pass / Mount Washburn / Beartooth Hwy** | **Close ~Oct 12** |
| **The Narrows water temperature** | Outfitters flip to **dry suits from Oct 1**; water drops to 3–7 °C |
| **Bryce Wall Street** | Closes **~end of October**; mid-Oct is at the edge |
| **Bryce shuttle** | 2026 season ended **Oct 18** |
| **Fiery Furnace ranger-led tours** | Season is "April 12 through **early Fall**" — may already be over in mid-Oct |
| **Boreas Pass aspens** | Peak in the last week of September; gone and possibly gated by mid-Oct |
| **Blue Lakes / San Juan high country** | First snow commonly late Sept/early Oct; mid-Oct is a coin flip |
| **Monument Valley last entry** | May have switched to the **winter 2:30 pm last entry** by mid-Oct |
| **Zion UEA crowd spike** | **Oct 15–18** is an NPS-flagged congestion event |
| **Grand Prismatic Overlook** | Warmer air = less steam = you actually see the colours |
| **Daylight** | Mid-Oct sunset against Zion's 5:00 pm last up-canyon shuttle is tight |

### Favours MID OCTOBER
| Item | Why |
|---|---|
| **Hanksville clay roads** | October is drier — the monsoon tail is gone. **This is the one genuinely safer-in-October item** |
| **Zion cottonwood colour** | Peak riverside colour along the Virgin River |
| **General crowds** | Lower everywhere except the Oct 15–18 UEA window |
| **Desert temperatures** | Delicate Arch, Wildcat Trail, Fiery Furnace all more pleasant |

### Bottom line
**On the sourced evidence, the 2028 fallback window (10 Sep – 4 Oct) is the strongest of the three options** — it clears the Jenny Lake boat cutoff, the Dunraven Pass closure, the Oct 1 Narrows water-temperature line, Wall Street, the Bryce shuttle, and the Zion UEA crowd spike, and it is before the Fiery Furnace season ends. Its only real cost is a slightly higher chance of wet bentonite around Hanksville.
**If the 2027 primary window is used, weight it to LATE SEPTEMBER, not mid-October** — and if the Rockies (Tetons + Yellowstone) are at the end of the route, **do the Rockies FIRST or accept losing the Jenny Lake boat and Mount Washburn.**
