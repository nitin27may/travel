# Peru — when to go

## The traveller's claim, checked

The Instagram post says: *"Best time to go is May to September... the Inca Trail is open and at its
most beautiful. Although, if you want fewer crowds and better prices, aim for April or October, you
get nearly the same weather with SOO much less chaos."*

**Three things in that are wrong or dangerously incomplete.**

### 1. "The Inca Trail is open [May–September]" implies it closes otherwise. It doesn't.
The Inca Trail is open **year-round except February**, when it closes for maintenance —
1–28 February 2026, reopening 1 March. That closure covers the **entire trail network including the
Short 2-Day Inca Trail**. Everything else — Salkantay, the 1-Day Inca Trail, all train routes —
runs normally in February.
Sources: https://machupicchutourguides.com/inca-trail-closed/
https://www.samtravelperu.com/inca-trail-permits/

### 2. "October = fewer crowds" is false *for Machu Picchu specifically*.
Machu Picchu's own capacity calendar tells you where the crowds actually are. For 2026 the site
admits **5,600 visitors per day** on: 1 January; **2–5 April**; **19 June – 2 November**; and
30–31 December. Every other date is capped at **4,500**.

October sits inside the high-capacity window. The crowd relief doesn't start until **3 November**.
Source: https://www.perurail.com/tickets-to-machu-picchu/

GAP: one operator (tierrasvivas.com) lists the April high-capacity block as 17–20 April rather than
2–5 April. Easter Sunday 2026 falls on 5 April, so PeruRail's 2–5 April is the one consistent with
Semana Santa; 17–20 April looks like a stale 2025 date. Verify against tuboleto.cultura.pe before
booking around Easter.

### 3. "April or October, nearly the same weather" is true for Cusco day trips and **false for the
very trek the post is recommending.**
For the Huayhuash, the dry season is **May–September**, with **June–August the most reliable**.
April and October carry a *significantly higher* risk of rain or snow — and on this route that means
snow on twelve passes above 4,500 m, with afternoon storms becoming frequent in October, plus
landslide and river-crossing risk. A shoulder-month gamble on a day hike costs you a view. The same
gamble on a 12-day committing circuit is a different category of decision.
Source: https://bushop.com/peru/guides/best-time-visit-cordillera-huayhuash/

**September is the genuinely correct answer to what the post was reaching for**: weather still
holds, crowds thin, and it is still inside the dry season rather than on the edge of it.

## Season by season

| Window | Andes | Coast | Amazon | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **May** | Dry season begins, landscape still green from the rains | Cool, grey (*garúa*) | Wet-ish, high water | Best-value month. Green *and* dry |
| **Jun–Aug** | Peak dry, clearest skies, coldest nights | Grey and cool | Drier, better trails | Peak everything: weather, crowds, prices |
| **Sep** | Still dry, crowds falling away | Clearing | Low water, good beaches | The sweet spot |
| **Oct** | Transition. Afternoon storms return | Warming | Warming | Fine for Cusco. Risky for committing treks |
| **Nov–Mar** | Wet. Muddy trails, cloud on the peaks | **Sunny and hot** — this is coastal summer | Wettest | Coast season, Andes off-season |
| **Feb** | Wettest | Peak beach | Wettest | **Inca Trail shut all month** |

The single counter-intuitive fact: **the Peruvian coast is best in December–March**, exactly when the
mountains are worst. Lima is under a low grey overcast for most of the June–September window that
everyone flies in for.

## Altitude cold
Dry season = clear skies = hard radiative cooling overnight. Huayhuash camps sit above 4,000 m and
routinely go below freezing in June–August. Cusco nights in July run cold and most budget and
mid-range hotels have **no heating**.

## Festivals worth planning around or avoiding
- **Inti Raymi**, Cusco, **24 June** — the Inca sun festival, the largest event in the Andean
  calendar. Cusco is at maximum capacity and maximum price for roughly a week around it.
- **Semana Santa** — Easter Sunday is **5 April 2026**. Ayacucho's is the biggest in Peru.
- **The World's 50 Best Restaurants awards, Lima, 4 November 2026** — Lima hotel and restaurant
  demand will spike that week.
