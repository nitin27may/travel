# Peru — trekking and altitude

## Altitude governs the itinerary. Everything else is negotiable.

| Place | Altitude |
|---|---|
| Lima | 0 m |
| Arequipa | 2,328 m |
| Ollantaytambo | ~2,792 m |
| Urubamba | ~2,870 m |
| Pisac | ~2,980 m |
| Huaraz | 3,050–3,090 m |
| **Cusco** | **3,399 m** |
| Machu Picchu citadel | 2,430 m — *lower than Cusco* |
| Lake Titicaca / Puno | 3,812–3,856 m |
| Laguna 69 | ~4,600 m |
| Punta Unión (Santa Cruz) | 4,750 m |
| Salkantay Pass | 4,630–4,650 m |
| Vinicunca viewpoint | ~5,036 m |
| Huayhuash high point | ~5,020 m |

### The two rules that follow

**1. Do not sleep your first night in Cusco.** Flying Lima (sea level) → Cusco (3,399 m) is an
instantaneous 3.4 km gain, well beyond the CDC guidance to ascend sleeping altitude gradually above
~2,500 m. Transfer straight from the airport down to the **Sacred Valley (2,792–2,980 m)** for the
first one or two nights, do market strolls and low-effort ruins, then come *up* to Cusco. This
costs nothing and it is the single highest-leverage decision in a Peru itinerary.
Source: https://www.peru-spirit.com/guides/cusco-altitude-vs-sacred-valley/

**2. Machu Picchu is low.** At 2,430 m it is a *relief* from Cusco, not a challenge. People who
think of it as the altitude test have it backwards — the hard days are Vinicunca, Titicaca and
everything in Ancash.

Practical sequencing for Cusco: Humantay Lake after 24–48 h at altitude, Vinicunca only after
48–72 h, then the Sacred Valley and Machu Picchu.
Source: https://howtoperu.com/answers/rainbow-mountain-or-humantay-lake-first-altitude-difficulty-weather-compared/

## Cordillera Huayhuash — the trek from the post

**What it is:** a compact 30 km range south-east of Huaraz holding **Yerupajá (6,634 m)**, Peru's
second-highest peak, and **Siula Grande** — the mountain Joe Simpson crawled off in *Touching the Void*.

| | |
|---|---|
| Full circuit distance | **135 km** |
| Duration | **10–12 days** typical; 8, 9, 11 and 14-day variants exist |
| Passes | **12, all above 4,500 m** |
| High point | **~5,020 m** |
| Most walking | above 4,000 m |
| Difficulty | Strenuous. Steep passes, camping at altitude, remote |
| Permits | **None.** But **land-use fees to 9 separate communities, ~S/205 (~US$62) total**, 20–50 soles each. Carry cash |
| Guided cost | Wide spread — **from ~US$989** (local Huaraz operator) to **US$3,550** (Western outfitter, 12-day Aug 2026 departure) |

**Shorter versions exist and are legitimate:**
- **Mini Huayhuash, 4 days** — the range's best lakes and passes without the full loop
- **5-day** version — shorter, but you miss the full panorama of the range
- **8-day** variants — including one from the eastern side via the Huánuco Viejo Inca ruins, with
  rest days built in; described as the least physically demanding option while still crossing four
  high passes

**Access:** Lima → Huaraz by bus, ~8 h / 400 km / ~S/100. Then Huaraz → **Llamac** (the usual
trailhead) by public bus, **5 hours, departing 05:00 daily, arriving ~10:30**, ~S/70. Cuartelwain
is the other common start.

**Acclimatisation is not optional and the post doesn't mention it.** The standing advice is
**2–3 full days in Huaraz first**, with at least one acclimatisation hike above 4,000 m — Laguna 69
(4,600 m) or Laguna Churup — before starting a multi-day trek. Skipping this is the most common
cause of a trek ending early.

**Door-to-door budget from Lima: 14–16 days.** 1 travel down + 3 acclimatising + 10–12 trekking
+ 1 return + 1 buffer.

## Cordillera Blanca — the shorter Ancash options

- **Laguna 69** — ~4,600 m, **6–7 hours round trip**, the standard acclimatisation day hike and a
  worthwhile objective in its own right. Turquoise lake under Chacraraju.
- **Santa Cruz trek** — **4 days / 3 nights, ~50 km**, crossing **Punta Unión at 4,750 m**,
  4–8 hours walking per day. The correct answer for anyone who wants a real multi-day Andean trek
  without surrendering half the holiday.
- Also: Laguna Churup, Laguna Parón, Pastoruri glacier, Huascarán National Park.
Source: https://www.explorandes.com/blog/santa-cruz-trek

## Machu Picchu treks compared

| Trek | Days | High point | Character |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Classic Inca Trail** | 4 | Dead Woman's Pass 4,215 m | The only route that walks *into* the site through the Sun Gate. 500 permits/day, book 5–6 months out, closed February |
| **Short Inca Trail** | 2 | — | Same Sun Gate arrival, far less commitment. Also closed in February |
| **Salkantay** | 5 (4–6 variants) | Salkantay Pass 4,630–4,650 m | No permit cap. Harder at altitude than the Inca Trail; 6–9 h days. Glacier to cloud forest in one route |
| **Lares** | 4 | — | Easiest of the four. Cultural — Quechua weaving communities, hot springs |
| **Choquequirao** | 4–7 | — | The hardest on the legs: repeated 1,500 m descents and climbs in the Apurímac canyon. ~30 visitors a day at the site |
| **Ausangate** | 4–6 | above 5,000 m | The most remote and the highest. Rainbow-mountain country, glacial lakes, alpaca herders. No ruins |

## Vinicunca (Rainbow Mountain) — the honest version
**~5,036 m viewpoint**, higher than Everest Base Camp. Standard day trip is a **3:00–4:30 a.m.
pickup and a 14-hour day dominated by driving**. Peak-season days see **1,500+ visitors**; the crush
runs **08:30–11:00**. Requires **two full days at Cusco altitude behind you** as a minimum.
**Palcoyo** is the quieter striped-ridge alternative and is markedly less brutal.
Source: https://bushop.com/peru/guides/humantay-lake-vs-rainbow-mountain-difficulty-crowds-views-and-best-start-times/
