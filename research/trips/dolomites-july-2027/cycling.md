### 1.11 Booking lead time, helmets, delivery — practical rules
- **Helmets are generally NOT included.** Papin €2/day; On the Rocks €3–4/day. Budget for it or
  bring your own (also better fit/hygiene).
- **One-way is a solved problem in South Tyrol**: Papin €7 flat to any station incl. Lienz;
  Südtirol Rad allows return at any of its 20 stations. This is what makes the Drava and
  Dolomiti path rides work without a second car.
- **Hotel delivery** is not advertised by the big networks; individual Val Gardena / Alta Badia
  shops will often deliver locally — ask directly. NOT verified.
- **Booking lead time:** e-MTB full-suspension and road bikes in the popular sizes go first.
  For the 26 Jun–9 Jul window, and especially the Maratona weekend (2–5 Jul 2027), book
  **at minimum 4–8 weeks out; for Maratona week treat it as 3+ months**. Papin/Südtirol Rad
  both take online reservations. (Judgement call from event demand — no operator publishes a
  formal lead-time policy.)

## 2. THE EASY FAMOUS RIDES

### 2.1 San Candido/Innichen → Lienz (Austria), Drava/Drau cycle path — THE classic
- **Distance 43.4 km**; **+15 m / −517 m**; start 1,242 m (San Candido) → finish 672 m (Lienz).
- Surface: "mostly asphalted cycle path, short sections along byroads at the beginning."
- Difficulty **easy**, "particularly suitable for families", runs mainly downhill.
- Riding time ~**2 h 50 min** (add 2 h for stops; it's a 4–5 h day trip).
- Route: San Candido → Versciaco → Prato alla Drava (border) → Sillian → Heinfels → Strassen →
  Abfaltersbach → Lienz.
- Source: https://www.pustertal.org/en/leisure-activities/mountain-biking-and-cycling/cycle-paths/drava-cycle-path-san-candido-lienz/
- Also: https://www.dreizinnen.com/en/activity/drava-cycle-path-drau-radweg-innichen-san-candido-lienz-aut-_631

**Getting back — three options**
1. **Drop the bike in Lienz and train home.** Papin Sport has a station **at Lienz railway
   station**; drop fee **€7**. Then ÖBB Lienz → San Candido as a foot passenger.
   https://www.papinsport.com/en/papin-rent-a-bike-your-bike-rental-in-south-tyrol-east-tyrol-carinthia-and-belluno/cycle-track-dobbiaco-san-candido-lienz/
2. **Take the bike on the ÖBB train back.** Lienz–San Candido is a **cross-border** section, so
   südtirolmobil fares do NOT apply: "ÖBB and VVT sell special tickets for journeys to North or
   East Tyrol (… Innichen/San Candido – Lienz)". The combined **"Rad & Person"** (bike +
   passenger) ticket for this section is bought at **Lienz station**.
   https://www.suedtirolmobil.info/en/tickets/ticketing-system-and-ticket-validation/bring-your-bike
   and https://www.pustertal.org/en/leisure-activities/mountain-biking-and-cycling/bike-train/
   Bike-carriage rules: **no reservations**, space-limited (~16–20 bike racks/train), staff can
   refuse bikes on crowded trains. Children's bikes <20", folded folding bikes, unhooked
   trailers and handbikes travel free.
   A user-reported adult return fare on Lienz→San Candido was **€16.90** (unverified, via
   https://www.tripadvisor.com/Attraction_Review-g194896-d21226593-Reviews-Drava_Cycle_Path_San_Candido_Lienz-San_Candido_Province_of_South_Tyrol_Trentino_.html).
3. **Bike shuttle bus.** "bikes are not admitted in overcrowded trains, however a Bikeshuttle
   service is available" — Val Pusteria excursion buses with racks for 5–6 bikes are marked
   with an **"A"** in the timetable and bike-loading stops carry a bicycle symbol.
   https://www.suedtirolmobil.info/en/tickets/ticketing-system-and-ticket-validation/bring-your-bike
   Papin Sport also organises transfers — +39 0474 913450.

**Gotcha:** the Innichen–Lienz railway had a full closure 6–26 April 2026 with bikes banned from
the replacement buses (https://www.suedtirol.info/en/en/information/mobility-and-barrier-free-accessibility/traffic-restrictions/traffic-restrictions-railway).
Re-check the südtirolmobil "Closure 2026/2027" page nearer the time — South Tyrol does annual
spring line closures, and they occasionally slip.

### 2.2 Val Pusteria / Pustertal cycle path (Rio Pusteria/Fortezza → Dobbiaco)
- Follows the Rienza then the Drava; undulating, mostly asphalt, family-grade. It continues
  seamlessly into the Drava path at San Candido ("the continuation of the Pusterbike cycle route").
- **Train links are the point.** The Fortezza–San Candido FLIRT trains run **every 30 min
  Fortezza–Brunico and hourly Brunico–Alta Pusteria**; renovated stations are step-free for
  bikes; designated bike areas in the coaches.
  https://www.pustertal.org/en/leisure-activities/mountain-biking-and-cycling/bike-train/
- **Bike day ticket €7.50** (2026 südtirolmobil tariff), **€3.50** with a südtirolmobil Pass.
  (The pustertal.org page still quotes the older €7.00 — use €7.50.)
  https://www.suedtirolmobil.info/en/tickets/ticketing-system-and-ticket-validation/bring-your-bike
- The Mobilcard South Tyrol covers public transport but bike carriage is a separate charge:
  https://www.pustertal.org/en/service/local-public-transport-in-south-tyrol/mobilcard-south-tyrol/
- Strategy: ride one direction, train back. Rental stations sit next to Brunico (1 min from the
  station) and Versciaco stations.

### 2.3 Lunga Via delle Dolomiti / Dolomiti cycle path (Dobbiaco – Cortina – Calalzo)
- **Total ~72 km Dobbiaco → Calalzo** with **~350 m of climbing**, **~5 h** riding
  (Papin's own figures): https://www.papinsport.com/en/papin-rent-a-bike-your-bike-rental-in-south-tyrol-east-tyrol-carinthia-and-belluno/dolomiti-cycle-path-dobbiaco-cortina-calalzo/
- Built on the **1921 Calalzo–Cortina–Dobbiaco narrow-gauge railbed**; old stations, tunnels and
  a spectacular iron bridge at Ospitale; passes Cimabanche (the high point).
  https://www.atvo.it/en/blog/discover-the-cycle-path-of-the-lunga-via-delle-dolomiti
- **Surface: only about half is paved.** From the hospital junction in Cortina northwards the
  road becomes **unpaved** through woodland; Cortina–San Vito partly uses the old *strada regia*.
  → **Gravel or MTB tyres, not a road bike.** https://visitcadoredolomiti.com/en/cyclopath-lunga-via-delle-dolomiti/
- **Direction:** ride **Dobbiaco → Cortina → Calalzo**, i.e. north to south. "From Dobbiaco to
  Cortina d'Ampezzo you face the longest and most demanding stretch" (the first ~13 km climb to
  Passo Cimabanche), and **from Cortina the route goes downhill** to Calalzo.
  Riding Cortina→Dobbiaco is the short uphill-ish option; riding Cortina→Calalzo is the pure
  downhill option. https://www.dolomiti.it/en/itineraries/cycling-holidays/the-dolomites-cycle-path
- Segment stats from the 3 Zinnen DMO
  (https://www.dreizinnen.com/en/activities-winter-summer/cycling-mountain-biking/all-bike-routes):
  - **Dobbiaco → Cortina: 30.7 km, +563 m / −603 m, intermediate**
  - **Villabassa → Cortina: 36.1 km, +500 m / −478 m, easy**
- **Return:** bus. Papin names **Dolomitibus** (http://www.dolomitibus.it/) and
  **Cortina Express** (http://www.cortinaexpress.it/) for the Toblach–Cortina–Calalzo corridor.
  Calalzo di Cadore also has a **railhead** (line to Belluno/Venice) if you're heading out.
- Papin one-way €7 makes Dobbiaco-pickup / Calalzo-or-Cortina-drop trivial.

### 2.4 Val Gardena and Val Badia valley-floor paths
- **Val Gardena — "Rodaval"**: the valley's dedicated cycle path, leisurely, links Ortisei –
  Santa Cristina – Selva; used as the family/rest-day option and as the return leg off the
  Sellaronda MTB tour. https://www.valgardena.it/en/summer-holidays-dolomites/mountainbike/
- **Val Badia**: valley-floor riding La Val / Badia / La Villa / Corvara / Colfosco is on the
  Alta Badia route/gravel pages: https://www.altabadia.org/en/bike/gravel and
  https://www.altabadia.org/en/bike/alta-badia-cycling-routes-dolomites
- GAP: neither DMO publishes km/elevation for the valley-floor paths on the pages fetched.
  Treat both as ~10–15 km each way, gently graded, mixed asphalt/compacted gravel — verify.

### 2.5 Adige/Etsch cycle path
- **Adige Cycle Path (Via Claudia Augusta), Brennero → Salorno via Bolzano: 96 km**, "from the
  Alpine north to the Mediterranean south", stations along the whole line for train returns.
  https://www.suedtirol.info/en/en/information/mobility/by-bike
- **Bolzano → Trento** (~60 km) continues south on the same corridor; südtirolmobil bike fares
  are valid "for regional trains to Trento" — south of Trento, Trenitalia fares apply.
  https://www.suedtirolmobil.info/en/tickets/ticketing-system-and-ticket-validation/bring-your-bike
- **Bolzano → Merano** (~30 km) is the short, flat, very popular half-day; the Val Venosta
  extension Merano → Malles is served by the **BikeLiner** bike bus (18 Apr–4 Oct 2026,
  departures from Merano station 09:10 / 10:10 / 15:10 / 16:10).
  https://www.suedtirol.info/en/en/information/mobility/by-bike
- Ride **north→south** (net downhill), train back. ~140 rental points sit near stations.

### 3.1 Typical times (rule of thumb, no source — flag as estimate)
A fit recreational rider at ~3.0 W/kg climbs at roughly 750–850 Vm/h; a strong rider 1,000+.
So Pordoi from Arabba ≈ 45–55 min; Giau from Selva di Cadore ≈ 65–80 min (it's relentless — no
respite even in the hairpins per Climbfinder reviews); Passo delle Erbe from Bressanone ≈ 2 h.

### 3.2 The Sella Ronda as a road ride
- **The classic loop: 4 passes — Campolongo, Pordoi, Sella, Gardena.**
- **Distance/gain figures in circulation:**
  - Maratona "Sellaronda course": **55 km, 1,780 m, 4 passes** —
    https://www.alta-badia.org/en/leisure-activities/mountain-biking-and-cycling/maratona-dles-dolomites/
    and https://epicroadrides.com/cycling-blog/maratona-dles-dolomites-route-guide/
  - Sellaronda Bike Day organisers: **53 km, 1,650 m** —
    https://www.bike-magazin.de/en/tours/italy/car-free-pass-roads-in-the-dolomites-20th-sellaronda-bike-day-the-biggest-holiday-for-pass-fans/
- **Direction:** the Maratona and Sellaronda Bike Day both run it from Corvara/Selva
  **anticlockwise** (Campolongo → Pordoi → Sella → Gardena). Dolomites Bike Day explicitly
  recommends anticlockwise "to experience each climb and each view at its best"
  (https://www.dolomitesbikeday.it/en/bike-route/falzarego-valparola-campolongo). Anticlockwise
  also puts the Pordoi hairpin wall and the Sella descent the "good" way round.
- Start/finish options: Corvara, Selva Val Gardena (Nives square), Arabba, Canazei — all four are
  official Bike Day start points (https://www.sudtirol.com/en/events/sellaronda-bike-day.htm).

### 3.3 Traffic and motorcycle danger
- This is a **serious, under-discussed hazard**. The Sella passes are a motorcycle-touring
  mecca; SS242/SS243/SS244/SS48 carry heavy motorbike traffic on summer weekends, plus coaches
  (the Giau road is explicitly "passable by trucks and coaches, unlike some alternative routes"
  — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giau_Pass).
- Mitigations that are actually documented:
  - Ride **early** — passes are quiet before ~09:00.
  - Use the **car-free days** (§5.2). The whole reason Sellaronda Bike Day exists is that ~20,000
    riders want these roads without engines
    (https://www.cyclingweekly.com/news/sellaronda-bike-day).
  - Prefer the quieter side roads: Valparola/La Villa, Passo delle Erbe, Passo Valles.

### 3.4 Car-free day availability in the trip window — see §5.2
**Short answer: probably none.** In 2026 the car-free days were 6 June, 20 June, 5 July
(race day, closed *for* the race, not for you), 12 September. Nothing recurring falls inside
26 Jun–9 Jul except the Maratona itself.

### 3.5 Tre Cime toll road — is it rideable?
- Yes, and it's a famous finish (Giro d'Italia summit finish). **10 km at 7.5% from Misurina
  with the steepest 100 m at 15.7%**; the final ramps to Rifugio Auronzo are the brutal bit.
  https://climbfinder.com/en/climbs/tre-cime-di-lavaredo-misurina
- The full climb from Auronzo di Cadore is **29.6 km / 1,524 m / 5.1%**.
  https://climbfinder.com/en/climbs/tre-cime-di-lavaredo-auronzo
- GAP — NOT VERIFIED: the exact 2026/2027 **toll tariff** for cars/motorbikes, whether cyclists
  are exempt (they are normally toll-free), the opening season/hours, and the Misurina/Dobbiaco
  shuttle bus. The tourism pages I could reach did not carry it. **Must be re-checked** — the
  Tre Cime road also has periodic car-access-restriction schemes.

### 3.6 Monte Grappa / Zoncolan — worth the detour?
- Both are **outside the Dolomites proper**. Monte Grappa (Semonzo, 19.1 km @ 8.1%) is ~2 h
  drive south toward Bassano; Zoncolan (Ovaro, 9.8 km @ **12.3%**, max 23.3%) is ~2.5 h east in
  Friuli. Zoncolan is Climbfinder's **#80 toughest climb in the world**.
- Verdict for a 14-day Dolomites trip with a Maratona-week disruption in the middle: **skip
  both.** They cost a full travel day each and Fedaia-from-Caprile (12.8 km @ 7.8%, max 15.8%)
  plus Giau-from-Selva-di-Cadore (9.8 km @ 9.5%) already deliver the "genuinely hard" box.

### 5.4 Lodging scarcity ranking for the window
1. **Maratona weekend 2–5 July 2027 — Alta Badia/Corvara/La Villa/San Cassiano/Arabba/Colfosco:
   severe.** Minimum stays, premium rates, sold out early.
2. Early July generally across the whole Dolomites — Italian and German school holidays begin;
   Lake Braies parking requires **advance reservation from 1 July** (§6.7).
3. Late June (26–30 June) is comparatively the calm part of the trip.

### 6.7 Lakes — swimming, boats, SUP
- **Lago di Braies / Pragser Wildsee** — rowing boats via **La Palafitta**
  (https://www.la-palafitta.com/en/sunrise-boat-experience):
  - **Shared boat €20 (45 min); private boat €55 (45 min); Bike & Boat package €75 (all day);
    private shoot €600 (1.5 h); VIP shooting zone €120 (60 min).** Children <4 free, 4–10 €10.
  - Hours **08:00–18:00 in peak season (20 June – 6 September 2026)**; later opening in shoulder.
  - **Mostly in-person booking only** (only the Bike & Boat and shoot packages are bookable
    online); **queues of up to 2 hours in high season.**
  - Max 5 people/boat; under-16s need an adult; dogs only on private boats; **drones banned**;
    **your own boat or SUP is NOT permitted.**
  - **Access:** park at P3/P4 (paid). **From 1 July – 15 September 2026 advance parking
    reservation is required** (parking@lagodibraies.com); bus line 442 from the railway stations.
    Shuttle info: https://www.prags.bz/en/offer/detail/shuttle-pragser-wildsee-lake_21
  - Swimming: not confirmed either way in the sources reached; Braies is a protected natural area
    and swimming is generally discouraged/regulated. **NOT VERIFIED.**
- **Lago di Caldaro / Kalterer See** — South Tyrol's warmest bathing lake; the official portal
  says of the south "in some areas such as in Caldaro and Appiano … there are even some beautiful
  ponds and lakes where you can take a refreshing swim."
  https://www.suedtirolerland.it/en/leisure-activities/water-sports/swimming/
- **Lago di Fiè / Völser Weiher** and the **Renon/Ritten plateau ponds** — also named as
  swimmable in that source. Both are close to Bolzano/Alpe di Siusi.
- **Lago di Dobbiaco** and **Lago di Anterselva** — scenic, cold, mostly walking-loop lakes with
  small boat hire; swimming status **NOT VERIFIED** in the sources reached.
- GAP: lake-by-lake swimming permissions, water temperatures and lido prices were not obtainable.
  **Follow-up needed** — try https://www.suedtirolerland.it/en/leisure-activities/water-sports/
  regional sub-guides ("Swimming in the Val Pusteria", "Swimming in Bolzano & surroundings").
