## 0. CROSS-CUTTING FACTS (verified by lane 1 directly)

### 0.1 Late-June snow / season reality for a 26 Jun – 9 Jul 2027 trip

- Winter snow normally melts below ~2,600 m by mid-June, but **persists on shadowed north-facing slopes, cirques and high passes to the end of June**. Source: https://monttrekking.com/info/hiking_season_dolomites.php
- Rifugi generally **begin opening mid-June**; the bulk of the high huts open ~20 June. Many are still in soft-opening / limited-service mode in the last week of June. Source: https://huttohuthikingdolomites.com/blog/dolomites-in-june
- Alta Via 1 hikers in the **last two weeks of June should expect snow on the trail**; microspikes recommended and hut-by-hut condition advice should be sought each morning. Source: https://altavia1dolomites.com/hiking-the-dolomites-in-june/
- Typical high-altitude temps 10–15 °C at 2,000–2,500 m; mornings almost always clear, afternoon thunderstorms are the standard pattern — start early. Source: https://huttohuthikingdolomites.com/blog/dolomites-in-june
- **Planning consequence:** 26 Jun – 9 Jul is right on the season boundary. Front-load lower/south-facing hikes (Cinque Torri, Adolf Munkel Weg, Alpe di Siusi, Braies, Federa) in days 1–5 and save the high/north-facing ones (Croda del Becco, Puez plateau, Pale altopiano, Gartl/Passo Santner) for days 8–14.

### 0.3 Rifugio booking mechanics (whole region)

- Most rifugi **open their booking calendar in January**; some open as early as **October** of the preceding year, especially the Alta Via huts. Source: https://kimberlykepharttravels.com/how-to-book-rifugio-dolomites/
- For peak summer and for AV1/AV2, book **5–7 months ahead** — i.e. for a 26 Jun 2027 start, book **January 2027 at the latest**, and check October 2026 for the AV huts. Sources: https://traveloutlandish.com/blog/alta-via-1-rifugios/ , https://wildbeeoutdoors.co.uk/when-to-book-mountain-huts-hut-to-hut-hike/
- Most huts run **late-mid June to late September / early October**. Source: https://www.in-moments.com/dolomites/2026-dolomites-hut-booking-guide

### 0.4 Tre Cime toll road / parking — 2026 official numbers

- Toll: **car €40, motorbike €26, campervan €60, bus ≤30 seats €80, bus >30 seats €160**.
- **Advance online booking is MANDATORY**, no same-day bookings, must reserve by 23:59 the day before. Booking site: https://auronzo.info/en/parking-tre-cime-di-lavaredo/
- Ticket valid **12 hours only**.
- Rifugio Auronzo car park capacity **~700 vehicles**.
- Road season: late May/early June – late Oct/early Nov, snow-dependent. 2026 toll road opened **25 May**.
- Bus alternative: **DolomitiBus line 31** from Lago di Misurina (20 min); **shuttle bus 444** from Dobbiaco/Toblach (book at tre-cime.bz); from Cortina take **bus 30** to Misurina then **31** up.
- Sources: https://www.moonhoneytravel.com/rifugio-auronzo-toll-road/ , https://auronzo.info/en/tre-cime-di-lavaredo-dolomites/

### 0.6 Lago di Sorapis — lane-1 verified detail (supplements part 1)

- **Trail 215 only**: 5.6 km (3.5 mi) one-way, ~2 h one-way; ~114 m (374 ft) gain to the lake — the trail is a long traverse, not a climb.
- **Full circuit 215 / 216 / 213**: **13.2 km, ~5 h+, 846 m (2,776 ft) total gain** — rated *difficult*; the 216 descent is a treacherously steep rubble slope with an exposed ledge and **no cables/aids**. Do not take the loop as a default.
- Distances disagree across sources: 11 km / 475 m gain up to 12.6 km / 630 m gain for the plain out-and-back. Report as a **range: ~11–12.6 km (6.8–7.8 mi), ~475–630 m (1,560–2,067 ft)**.
- **Exposure**: metal ladders, boards and cabled ledge passages on 215. Genuinely uncomfortable for anyone with vertigo.
- **Lake altitude** 1,923 m (6,309 ft); trailhead Passo Tre Croci 1,809 m.
- **Parking**: free roadside on SR48 at Passo Tre Croci plus the "Parcheggio per Sorapis" overflow lot to the west. Fills by ~06:00–06:30 in high season. No published capacity.
- **Bus**: Dolomiti Bus lines 30/31, Cortina d'Ampezzo ↔ Passo Tre Croci ↔ Lago di Misurina.
- **Rifugio Vandelli**: season late June – late September; lunch served 11:30–14:30; **cash only, no credit cards**.
- Sources: https://www.moonhoneytravel.com/lago-di-sorapis-hike/ , https://dolomites-guide.com/hiking-dolomites/lago-di-sorapis/ , https://www.alltrails.com/trail/italy/veneto/passo-tre-croci-lago-sorapis

### 0.7 Cadini di Misurina — lane-1 verified detail (supplements part 1)

- Trail **117** = the first section of the **Sentiero Alberto Bonacossa**.
- Viewpoint hike from Rifugio Auronzo (2,326 m): **3.9 km (2.4 mi) out-and-back, ~144 m (472 ft) gain**, min 2,256 m / max 2,334 m. **~40 min each way**.
- **The famous view is NOT at a summit** — it is a short spur off the trail roughly 30–40 min from Rifugio Auronzo, after the descent to Forcella Longeres, standing on a small rocky promontory looking back at the Cadini spires.
- Exposure: narrow, rocky and **exposed in several places**; the viewpoint promontory itself is a drop-off. Not for vertigo sufferers or unsupervised kids.
- Continuing the full Bonacossa traverse is a serious, exposed, partly-aided route — different proposition from the viewpoint walk.
- Sources: https://www.outdooractive.com/en/route/hiking-trail/belluno/panoramic-viewpoint-cadini-di-misurina-via-auronzo-refuge/810966498/ , https://www.moonhoneytravel.com/cadini-di-misurina-viewpoint-hike/ , https://www.moonhoneytravel.com/sentiero-alberto-bonacossa-north-trail/ , https://www.alltrails.com/trail/italy/veneto/rifugio-auronzo-cadini-di-misurina

## 1. Tre Cime di Lavaredo loop (Sentiero 101/105/104/AV4)

- **Name + trail numbers**: Tre Cime di Lavaredo loop — Sentiero 101 (out via Forcella Lavaredo/Rifugio Locatelli) + 105 (return via Malga Langalm/Rifugio Lavaredo), AV4 overlaps this segment.
- **Sub-region / nearest valley / base town**: Ampezzo Dolomites, Auronzo di Cadore valley; base town Misurina or Auronzo di Cadore.
- **Distance**: ~9.5–10.5 km loop (~5.9–6.5 mi). Sources vary: 9.5 km/400 m gain per one guide vs 9.7 km per another. **Elevation gain**: ~400 m (1,300 ft), some report up to 480 m depending on whether Forcella Lavaredo detour is included. [Range cited, sources disagree slightly] ([earthtrekkers.com](https://www.earthtrekkers.com/hiking-tre-cime-di-lavaredo-loop/), [moonhoneytravel.com](https://www.moonhoneytravel.com/tre-cime-di-lavaredo-hike/))
- **Difficulty + realistic round-trip time**: Easy-moderate, well-graded gravel/rock path, no exposure on standard loop. Realistic time for fit-but-not-expert with photo stops: 3.5–4.5 hours.
- **Trailhead**: "Rifugio Auronzo" car park (Google Maps: Rifugio Auronzo, 32041 Auronzo di Cadore BL), reached by car via the Misurina–Tre Cime toll road, or by shuttle bus from Misurina/Dobbiaco (summer bus lines run by Dolomiti Bus / SAD). No cable car on this route.
- **PARKING**: Rifugio Auronzo lot — mandatory ONLINE pre-booking (12-hour time slot) via the official Municipality of Auronzo di Cadore portal auronzo.info. 2026 price: **€40/car**, €26/motorbike, €60/campervan (toll road + parking combined). Booking opens end of April for the season; fills fast on weekends and July/August — book well ahead for a late-June/early-July 2027 visit. ([auronzo.info](https://auronzo.info/en/parking-tre-cime-di-lavaredo/), [moonhoneytravel.com toll-road update](https://www.moonhoneytravel.com/rifugio-auronzo-toll-road/))
- **The payoff**: Full 360° views of all three Cime peaks are seen progressively around the loop — Forcella Lavaredo (2,454 m) gives the classic postcard shot of the north faces, NOT visible from the Rifugio Auronzo trailhead itself. The three towers are best seen mid-route, not at start/end.
- **Exposure/risk**: None on the standard 101/105 loop — wide gravel track throughout. Late-June snow patches possible near Forcella Lavaredo (highest point ~2,454 m) — UNVERIFIED exact snow persistence for this specific date; check locally.
- **Rifugi on/near route**: Rifugio Auronzo (2,333 m, at trailhead, lunch yes, bookings yes) — opens "indicativamente fine maggio" (approx. late May) per 2026 season notice; Rifugio Lavaredo (on 105 return leg, lunch yes) — 2026 opening June 13; Rifugio Locatelli (side detour, see Trail 11 below). ([auronzo.info news](https://auronzo.info/news/apertura-impianti-e-rifugi-ad-auronzo-misurina-e-tre-cime/))
- **Links**: [AllTrails](https://www.alltrails.com/trail/italy/veneto/rifugio-auronzo-cadini-di-misurina) (Cadini variant shares trailhead); official: [auronzo.info parking](https://auronzo.info/en/parking-tre-cime-di-lavaredo/); [Rifugio Auronzo official site](https://rifugioauronzo.it/en/).

## 2. Cadini di Misurina viewpoint (Sentiero 117 / Bonacossa)

- **Name + trail numbers**: Cadini di Misurina viewpoint — short spur off Trail 117 (Sentiero Attrezzato Alberto Bonacossa) from Rifugio Auronzo.
- **Sub-region / nearest valley / base town**: Ampezzo Dolomites, Auronzo/Misurina.
- **Distance**: Short viewpoint version ~3.2 km RT (2 mi) with ~144–210 m gain (sources disagree: one cites 210 m gain/3.2 km, another 144 m gain min 2256/max 2334 m — **range 144–210 m reported**). Full Bonacossa traverse (117) is a separate, much harder route: 16.0 km, ~7h27, EEA-rated via ferrata linking Rifugio Auronzo to Rifugio Col de Varda. ([moonhoneytravel.com](https://www.moonhoneytravel.com/cadini-di-misurina-viewpoint-hike/), [trailforks.com](https://www.trailforks.com/route/sentiero-attrezzato-bonacossa-117/))
- **Elevation gain**: 144–210 m (472–690 ft) for the viewpoint spur only.
- **Difficulty + realistic round-trip time**: Viewpoint spur — moderate, narrow trail with some rock scrambling near the end; ~1.5–2 hours RT. Full Bonacossa (117) traverse — EEA/via ferrata grade, full kit required, NOT suitable for non-expert hikers.
- **Trailhead**: Same as Tre Cime — Rifugio Auronzo car park, via the same toll road.
- **PARKING**: Shares the Rifugio Auronzo lot — same €40 booking system as Trail 1 above (see auronzo.info).
- **The payoff**: The famous jagged-spires viewpoint is roughly 1.5 km from Rifugio Auronzo along a side spur off 117 — NOT at a summit, just a ledge/viewpoint partway along the traverse. Best light early morning.
- **Exposure/risk**: The short viewpoint spur has some narrow/eroded sections with mild exposure near the final overlook — care needed but no cables required for the spur. The continuation into full Trail 117 is EEA-graded with cables/ladders — do not continue past the viewpoint without via ferrata gear.
- **Rifugi on/near route**: Rifugio Auronzo at trailhead (see Trail 1 for opening date); Rifugio Col de Varda (far end of full 117 traverse only) — 2026 opening June 18.
- **Links**: [AllTrails](https://www.alltrails.com/trail/italy/veneto/rifugio-auronzo-cadini-di-misurina); official/context: [auronzo.info](https://auronzo.info/en/parking-tre-cime-di-lavaredo/).

## 3. Lago di Sorapis (Sentiero 215, loop options via 216/213)

- **Name + trail numbers**: Lago di Sorapis — CAI Trail 215 from Passo Tre Croci.
- **Sub-region / nearest valley / base town**: Ampezzo Dolomites, between Cortina d'Ampezzo and Misurina.
- **Distance**: Sources disagree — 12.6 km RT (7.8 mi) with 630 m gain per one source, vs 11.5–12 km RT with ~460 m gain per another. **Report range: 11.5–12.6 km RT, 460–630 m gain.** ([moonhoneytravel.com](https://www.moonhoneytravel.com/lago-di-sorapis-hike/), [alpventurer.com](https://alpventurer.com/lago-di-sorapis-hike-passo-tre-croci/))
- **Elevation gain**: 460–630 m (1,510–2,067 ft).
- **Difficulty + realistic round-trip time**: Moderate, with exposed cable-secured scree traverses. Realistic RT for fit-but-not-expert: 4.5–6 hours including lake time.
- **Trailhead**: Passo Tre Croci (Google Maps: Passo Tre Croci, 32043 Cortina d'Ampezzo), reached by car on the SR48 between Cortina and Misurina, or by summer bus (Dolomiti Bus Cortina–Misurina line).
- **PARKING**: Free gravel car park near the bridge at Passo Tre Croci, only ~20 spaces. Arrive before 7:00 AM in July/August — fills very early. No known toll/booking requirement as of 2026. ([alpventurer.com](https://alpventurer.com/lago-di-sorapis-hike-passo-tre-croci/))
- **The payoff**: The turquoise glacial lake itself IS the endpoint payoff, at Rifugio Vandelli (1,926 m) on the shore — consistent with route end.
- **Exposure/risk**: Trail narrows onto exposed scree slopes secured with fixed steel cables and metal pegs — real exposure for anyone with vertigo, though technically non-alpine (no via ferrata kit needed, just care on cables). Late-June snow/ice on these cabled traverses is a known seasonal hazard — UNVERIFIED for exact late-June 2027 conditions, check locally/CAI Cortina before going.
- **Rifugi on/near route**: Rifugio Vandelli at the lake (lunch yes, bookings yes) — 2026 opening **June 20** per Auronzo-area rifugi notice (nearby group, same operator region). ([auronzo.info news](https://auronzo.info/news/apertura-impianti-e-rifugi-ad-auronzo-misurina-e-tre-cime/))
- **Links**: [AllTrails](https://www.alltrails.com/trail/italy/veneto/lago-di-sorapis) (search "Lago di Sorapis" — multiple listed variants); official: [Rifugio Vandelli / dolomiti.org Cortina hiking page](https://cortina.dolomiti.org/en/); trail-feel detail: [moonhoneytravel.com](https://www.moonhoneytravel.com/lago-di-sorapis-hike/).

## 5. Lagazuoi — WWI tunnels (Galleria Lagazuoi / Kaiserjäger path) + descent to Passo Falzarego, cable car

- **Name + trail numbers**: Galleria del Lagazuoi (mine tunnel) + Kaiserjägersteig, no single CAI number — signed "Percorso 6" (Lagazuoi tunnels) on the official Lagazuoi trail map; connects to path 402/401 for the walk-up alternative.
- **Sub-region / nearest valley / base town**: Passo Falzarego, Ampezzo Dolomites side, base town Cortina d'Ampezzo or Andraz.
- **Distance / elevation**: Tunnel descent from Rifugio Lagazuoi (2,752 m) to Passo Falzarego (2,105 m) is ~647 m descent over roughly 3 km; typically done as a one-way descent after riding the cable car up. ## GENERAL LATE-JUNE CONDITIONS NOTE (cross-cutting, applies to all trails above)

- Late June (roughly 20–30 June) is described by multiple sources as close to ideal — most rifugi open, trails accessible, wildflowers peaking, crowds not yet at July/August peak. [huttohuthikingdolomites.com](https://huttohuthikingdolomites.com/blog/dolomites-in-june)

Hail or light snow above 2,500 m is "not uncommon" even in summer. Microspikes/poles advised as a contingency, and always check local trail-condition reports (rifugio websites, valgardena.it, suedtirol.info) close to the travel date. [source discussion](https://altavia1dolomites.com/hiking-the-dolomites-in-june/)

Confirm exact 2027 opening dates 4–6 weeks before travel.

- **Links**: [rifugiocarestiato.com official](https://www.rifugiocarestiato.com/wordpress/en/home/) + [caiagordo.it](https://www.caiagordo.it/rifugi/rif-bruto-carestiato)

### 5.2 Laghi di Colbricon + Cima Cavallazza (from Passo Rolle)

- **Name + trail numbers**: Passo Rolle/Malga Rolle → Cavallazza Piccola → Cavallazza → Laghi di Colbricon loop, trail 348 for the lakes section.
- **Sub-region / base town**: Passo Rolle, Lagorai border, near San Martino di Castrozza/Val Venegia scope.
- **Distance/elevation**: ~500 m elevation gain total for the circuit. [itineraridimontagna.it](https://www.itineraridimontagna.it/cima-cavallazza-laghi-colbricon/) ≈ 1,640 ft. Exact km not confirmed — UNVERIFIED.
- **Difficulty + time**: ~2 hours to Cavallazza summit from Passo Rolle, ~2 hours for the full lakes circuit — moderate, well within fit-hiker range, roughly 4h combined.
- **Trailhead**: Parking at "Malga Rolle" (1,910 m), ~1 km from Passo Rolle.
- **PARKING**: At Malga Rolle lot — capacity/cost UNVERIFIED.
- **The payoff**: Two glacial lakes (1,909m and 1,922m) set among junipers and rhododendrons, with Great War fortifications encountered en route on Cavallazza Piccola — a WWI-history-plus-nature combo that's overlooked relative to the Pale/Civetta big names, and it also delivers Pale di San Martino panoramic views from the Cavallazza ridge.
- **Exposure/risk**: None significant — an easy-moderate ridge walk.
- **Rifugi**: Rifugio Colbricon (1,927 m) — restaurant-only (no overnight beds implied by source), good lunch stop.
- **Archaeological note**: Mesolithic hunter-gatherer finds on the lake shores — a genuine hidden-gem detail most guidebooks skip.
- **Links**: [itineraridimontagna.it](https://www.itineraridimontagna.it/cima-cavallazza-laghi-colbricon/) + [magicoveneto.it detailed route](https://www.magicoveneto.it/lagorai/Colbricon/Passo-Rolle-Tognazza-Cavallazza-Laghi-Colbricon.htm)

## SECTION 6: VAIA STORM (OCT 2018) DAMAGE — CURRENT STATUS

- **Scale**: The 26–30 October 2018 storm devastated ~41,000 hectares of forest across the Triveneto (some sources cite 30,000 ha specifically for the "30,000 ettari di bosco" figure used in a 2025 retrospective), felling ~15 million trees / 8.6 million m³ of timber, with gusts up to 200 km/h. [ladige.it 7-years-later retrospective](https://www.ladige.it/cronaca/2025/10/28/sette-anni-fa-la-grande-paura-e-le-devastazioni-di-vaia-distrutti-30mila-ettari-di-bosco-1.4211106) [it.wikipedia.org](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tempesta_Vaia_in_Italia)
- **Areas hit hardest**: Agordino, Cadore, Feltrino, Comelico, Carnia, Val di Fassa, Val di Fiemme — this directly covers the Val di Zoldo/Agordino/Civetta scope and touches the Primiero/Pale periphery. [it.wikipedia.org](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tempesta_Vaia_in_Italia)
- **Trail status**: General sources note that while roads were restored relatively quickly, numerous numbered hiking trails remained impassable for an extended period post-storm, and some routes were permanently rerouted rather than restored to their original line. No source found gives a definitive, current (2025/2026) list of which specific numbered trails in Val di Zoldo/Agordino/Primiero remain closed — this is UNVERIFIED at the specific-trail level and should be checked against current CAI section bulletins (CAI Agordo, CAI Zoldo, SAT sections for the Trentino side) or the relevant Parco Naturale Paneveggio Pale di San Martino / Parco Nazionale Dolomiti Bellunesi trail-status pages before finalizing the itinerary.
- **Ongoing secondary threat**: Post-Vaia bark beetle (bostrico) infestation has continued damaging surviving spruce stands in the years since, per a 2022 retrospective — this affects forest health/views along lower-elevation approach trails (e.g., the Val Canali and Cant del Gal forest sections) rather than the high-alpine objectives themselves. [ladige.it 2022](https://www.ladige.it/montagna/2022/10/28/quattro-anni-fa-le-devastazioni-di-vaia-ora-l-allarme-per-il-bostrico-che-minaccia-le-foreste-1.3344088)
- **Recommendation**: Before finalizing routes through lower-elevation forested approaches (Val Canali/Cant del Gal, Val di Zoldo valley-floor connectors, Agordino access roads to Passo Duran/Carestiato), check current trail-status bulletins — none of the specific trails researched above (Coldai, Tissi, Vazzoler, Pelmo ring, Rosetta/Pradidali/Velo, Val Venegia, Colbricon, Calaita, Treviso) turned up an active 2024–2026 closure notice in this research, but this is a "no news found" result, not a confirmed "all clear" — flag as UNVERIFIED and re-check closer to the trip.

## A) ALTA VIA 1 AND ALTA VIA 2 — 2-4 DAY SEGMENTS

### A.1 Recommended segment: AV1 Stages 1-3, Lago di Braies → Rifugio Biella → Rifugio Fanes → Rifugio Lagazuoi

This is the most feasible multi-day segment for a **late-June** window because it sits in the northernmost, lowest-snow-risk part of the range and its huts open earliest.

**Stage 1: Lago di Braies → Rifugio Biella**
- Distance: 14.4 km / 8.9 mi
- Elevation gain: 1,210 m / ~3,970 ft
- Difficulty/time: Moderate-hard, first day; hut sits at 2,327 m / 7,635 ft
- Source: [Alpine Exploratory — AV1 guide](https://www.alpineexploratory.com/walking-guides/alta-via-1.html), [Much Better Adventures — AV1 guide](https://www.muchbetteradventures.com/magazine/dolomites-alta-via-one-guide/)

**Stage 2: Rifugio Biella → Rifugio Fanes** (via Rifugio Sennes possible as a break/alternative, not obligatory)
- Distance: not separately confirmed this session; duration 4.5 h
- Elevation: Biella (2,327 m/7,635 ft) → Fanes (2,060 m/6,760 ft), net loss with rolling terrain, gain 565 m / 1,850 ft
- Difficulty: Moderate
- Source: [bookatrekking.com AV1 itinerary](https://bookatrekking.com/en/blog/alta-via-1/)

**Stage 3: Rifugio Fanes → Rifugio Lagazuoi**
- Duration: 5 h
- Elevation gain: 1,070 m / 3,510 ft, to Lagazuoi at 2,752 m / 9,029 ft

- Source: [bookatrekking.com AV1 itinerary](https://bookatrekking.com/en/blog/alta-via-1/)

**Total for the 3-stage segment:** roughly 3 hiking days, ~40+ km / ~25 mi, ~2,800 m / ~9,200 ft cumulative gain — realistic to slot into a 14-day trip as a 3-4 day block (add a rest/acclimatization day or a side trip from Fanes to Cascate di Fanes waterfall).

### A.3 Alta Via 2 — why it's much less realistic in late June, and the one exception

Full AV2 stage data (Wikivoyage, corroborated by Hut-to-Hut Hiking Dolomites and Trailhiking.com.au):

- **Stage 4 (Rifugio Puez → Rifugio Pisciadù):** via ferrata **mandatory** — Via Ferrata Brigata Tridentina, sustained cable-and-ladder ascent; harness/helmet/lanyards required. Source: [Wikivoyage AV2](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Alta_Via_2)
- **Stage 6 (Rifugio Castiglioni → Passo San Pellegrino):** crosses Sforcella de la Marmolada (2,885 m/9,465 ft) — **crampons and ice axe required**, retreating glacier. Explicitly flagged as risky in June: "snow likely lingers; consult rifugio staff." A non-glacier bypass via Malga Ciapela exists (28 km/17.4 mi, 7-8 hr).
- **Stage 8 (Rifugio Mulaz → Rifugio Pedrotti, Pale di San Martino plateau):** not formal via ferrata but fixed cables on steep exposed terrain, technically serious.
- **Stage 12 (Vette Feltrine, remote southern section):** old military via ferrata-grade route, no water sources between huts.
- **General AV2 June snow note (direct quote):** "Snow can linger on high passes — especially Forcella del Putia, Passo delle Farangole, and Marmolada — well into early July after a heavy winter." Source: [The Hiking Club — best time for AV2](https://www.thehiking.club/blog/when-is-the-best-time-to-hike-alta-via-2-dolomites), corroborated by [Wikivoyage AV2](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Alta_Via_2)

**Sections NOT realistic in late June 2027 (fit-but-not-expert, no glacier gear):**
- **Puez-Odle plateau (Stages 3-4):** high plateau above 2,400-2,500 m, snow patches and water scarcity typical in early season; Stage 4's mandatory via ferrata adds technical risk on top of possible snow/ice on rungs.
- **Passo Pordoi / Marmolada glacier crossing (Stage 6):** explicitly requires crampons/ice axe; not appropriate for non-expert hikers regardless of date, and snow cover in late June makes it worse. Use the Malga Ciapela bypass or skip entirely.
- **Pale di San Martino plateau, Passo delle Farangole (Stages 7-9):** named directly as a lingering-snow zone into early July; Farangole itself is a notoriously exposed, near-vertical via ferrata-grade descent even snow-free.
- **Forcella Marmolada:** see Stage 6 above.

**The one AV2 exception worth considering:** **Stage 1-2 only, Bressanone (or Plose cable car) → Rifugio Plose → Rifugio Genova/Schlüterhütte.** Distance ~15 km/9.3 mi each stage, ~5-6 h, moderate difficulty, no via ferrata required (cables assist but aren't mandatory), tops out at 2,447 m/8,028 ft (Plose) and 2,306 m/7,566 ft (Genova). Schlüterhütte's confirmed season is roughly mid-June to mid-October (specifically closed "12 Oct 2026 – 11 June 2027" per one source — **flag as needing reconfirmation since it implies the hut may not open until 11 June, which is fine for a late-June trip but cuts it close for booking-availability at the very start of season**). Source: [Wikivoyage AV2](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Alta_Via_2), [Schlüterhütte official](https://www.schlueterhuette.com/en/), [sentres.com hut listing](https://www.sentres.com/en/hut/schutzhuette-schlueter-huette/61267324/)

**Recommendation: do NOT attempt a multi-day AV2 segment in late June 2027 beyond Stages 1-2.** Route the 2-4 day hut-to-hut block onto **AV1 Stages 1-3 (Braies-Biella-Fanes-Lagazuoi)** instead — lower altitude corridor, huts open earliest, no mandatory via ferrata, well-trodden and rescue-accessible.

### B.2 Vaia storm (Oct 2018) and bark beetle (bostrico) — ongoing background damage, no specific 2026/2027 closure dates found

- Vaia (Oct 2018) felled trees across an area compared to "70,000 football fields," particularly severe in Alta Badia/Val d'Ega/Eggental. Triggered a subsequent bark beetle (bostrico) epidemic — 2021 was "the year of the bark beetle" across Trentino, Veneto, and other Alpine regions. Source: [Alta Badia — WOWnature](https://www.altabadia.org/en/alta-badia-dolomites/wownature), [vaia.eu bark beetle project](https://www.vaia.eu/en/project/bostrico/), [Eggental — Vaia's trail of destruction](https://eggental.com/en/eggental/blog/vaia-s-trail-of-destruction)
- Ongoing effect relevant to hikers: large salvage-logging areas remain barren with no natural regrowth years later; dead standing timber increases falling-branch risk directly on trails; cleared zones expose hikers to full sun and new erosion gullies where forest previously gave shade/stability. A forestry study found combined logging-trail length exceeding 700 km across just 29 surveyed sites (1,078 ha), indicating how extensively the terrain was reworked post-Vaia. Source: [MDPI Forests journal — salvage logging trails NE Italian Alps](https://doi.org/10.3390/f16040673), [Mountains For Everybody — Dolomites bark beetle crisis](https://mountainsforeverybody.com/the-dolomites-silent-crisis-forests-lost-to-bark-beetle/)
- **No specific named-trail closure notices tied to Vaia/bostrico for 2026 or 2027 were found this session.** This is a background condition (altered forest character, less shade, some erosion) rather than an active closure. **Explicitly UNVERIFIED for 2027 — re-check CAI/AVS sezione notices and dolomitipark.it directly in spring 2027**, since salvage logging and reforestation work can still trigger temporary trail diversions in affected valleys (Val di Fassa, Val Badia, Alta Badia, Eggental/Val d'Ega were named as heavily hit).

## C) FAMILY / EASY WALKS AND RAINY-DAY LOW-ALTITUDE OPTIONS

Twelve-plus options confirmed or reasonably sourced this session. Items without a directly fetched confirming source this session are marked UNVERIFIED (general regional knowledge, not independently re-verified today) — recommend a quick pre-trip re-check.

1. **Lago di Braies lakeside loop** — Base town: Braies/Prags. Distance: **3.7 km / 2.3 mi** loop. Elevation gain: **~100 m / ~330 ft**. Time: 1.5-2 hrs. Why it works: flat lakeside gravel path, minimal exposure, stroller/family friendly, scenic even in light rain (reflections). Parking: P1-P4 lots at the lake, reservation required 1 Jul-15 Sept 2026 9am-4pm (see Part B). Why it works: Europe's largest high alpine meadow, wide grassy paths, low technical difficulty, good even in marginal weather since it's rolling meadow not exposed ridge. Parking: use the Alpe di Siusi cable car park-and-ride at Compatsch/Siusi due to restricted vehicle access to the meadow itself (UNVERIFIED exact restriction rules for 2027 — reconfirm). Parking: village car park, €4/day; small free 8-12 car lot fills fast. Source: [Ulysse News — Val di Funes traffic changes](https://ulysse.com/news/en/travel/val-di-funes-without-the-traffic-what-changes-for-santa-maddalena-1581)

## Vallunga / Langental — the easy day in Val Gardena (added 30 Aug 2026)

**The gap-filler for a family day, and previously only 9 mentions across this research.**

- **Sub-region / base**: Val Gardena, trailhead 2.7 km (7 min drive) from Selva.
- **Distance**: ~4.5–5 km one way to the hut at the valley end; out-and-back.
- **Elevation gain**: 200–250 m across the whole valley — **the ascent is so gentle it is
  barely perceptible.** Wide gravel path the entire way from the car park.
- **Time**: about **1 h 15 min** each way to the hut at a walking pace. Families routinely take
  far longer because of the picnic meadows and the scrambling rocks along the way.
- **Difficulty**: easy. Explicitly recommended for small children and for grandparents —
  it is the one walk in the valley that asks nothing of anyone.
- **Setting**: a flat-floored glacial trough inside **Puez-Odle Nature Park**, walled on both
  sides by near-vertical rock. The valley is the attraction; there is no summit.
- **PARKING**: large **pay-and-display** car park at the trailhead by the La Cajota snack bar.
  Cards accepted. **Spaces are limited and it fills quickly in peak season — arrive early.**
  GAP: exact tariff.
- **Why it earns a slot**: the Val Gardena block as planned is Piz Boè (3,152 m, exposed,
  cable-protected sections), Alpe di Siusi and Seceda. **There is no low-effort day in it.**
  Vallunga is the weather bail-out, the recovery day after Piz Boè, and the obvious choice for
  the ten-year-old's legs.
