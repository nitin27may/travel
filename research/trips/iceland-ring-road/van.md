# Iceland — the camper van rules

This is the first van trip. These are the constraints that actually bite.

## You cannot sleep outside a campsite. Full stop.
Since the 2015 tightening of the Nature Conservation Act, **camping overnight in a campervan or
motorhome is legal only at a marked campsite.** The old "right to roam" exception does not extend to
vehicles. Pulling over in a remote spot for the night is not allowed, enforcement is active, and
fines are meaningful. Illegal parking can also compromise your insurance.

**Read this as a feature, not a restriction.** It removes the single biggest stressor of first-time
van travel — the evening scramble for somewhere legal. There are **170+ registered campsites** and
there is almost always one within reach.

**But it also cancels the usual van superpower.** You cannot sleep at a trailhead or a viewpoint.
Days must be planned around where the campsite is, not where the last waterfall is.

## F-roads are off-limits in a standard camper
- **2WD vehicles are not permitted on F-roads.** Driving one there **voids the entire insurance
  policy** and risks a fine under Icelandic traffic law.
- **No Icelandic insurer offers river-crossing cover at any price.** F-roads have unbridged rivers.
  Flood the van and you own it — they are worth **8–12 million ISK**.
- Consequence: **Landmannalaugar, Þórsmörk, Askja and Kerlingarfjöll are out.** Reachable only by
  renting an F-road-legal 4x4 camper, or by leaving the van and taking a highland bus.
- F-roads open mid-June to mid-July and begin closing mid-September anyway.

## Wind — the one that damages vehicles
Gusts over 50 km/h are routine and can exceed 80 km/h. **Never open a van door into the wind**; doors
bent backwards on their hinges are the classic Icelandic rental damage claim. Park nose-into-wind.
Check what the rental's insurance actually covers — sand-and-ash and gravel protection are usually
separate paid add-ons.
