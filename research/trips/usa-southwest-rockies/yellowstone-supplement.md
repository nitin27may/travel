Hiking ceiling: ~12 km (~7.5 mi) round trip. All figures sourced inline; unverified items flagged as GAP.

### 1. Grand Prismatic Overlook via Fairy Falls Trailhead

**Grand Prismatic Overlook Trail (overlook ONLY, separate trail from full Fairy Falls hike):**
- Round trip: 1.2 mi (1.9 km) — well under 12 km ceiling.
- Elevation gain: 105 ft (NPS does not give meters; ~32 m).
- Difficulty: "Easy." Typical time: 1–2 hours.
- Trailhead: Fairy Falls Parking Lot / Trailhead (OK5), on Fountain Flat Drive, 1 mi (1.6 km) south of Midway Geyser Basin.
- Source: https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/yell-trail-grand-prismatic-overlook.htm
- What you see: an elevated view down onto Grand Prismatic Spring and Midway Geyser Basin — the classic "overhead" postcard shot. Confirmed via NPS page above.

**Full Fairy Falls Trail (DO NOT CONFUSE with the overlook trail above — same trailhead, different/longer route):**
- Round trip: 4.5 mi (7.2 km) to the falls — within the 12 km ceiling.
- Falls height: 200 ft (61 m).
- Difficulty: "relatively flat, easy." Source: https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/yell-trail-fairy-falls.htm (via search snippet; page last updated Sept 10, 2025)
- Extension: continuing 0.6 mi (0.97 km) further reaches Spray and Imperial Geysers, adding 1.2 mi (1.9 km) — this pushes full loop toward ~5.9–6 mi (9.5–9.7 km) round trip, still under ceiling but close; confirm before committing on trip day. Source (third-party aggregation of NPS distances, not NPS page directly): search result citing nps.gov trail page, could not independently WebFetch this exact page (fetch attempts hit different URL). GAP: exact combined Fairy Falls + Spray/Imperial Geyser total round-trip figure — not found on a single NPS page, only reconstructed by adding segments from search snippets.

**Trailhead parking:**
- NPS: "Parking is very limited" at Fairy Falls Parking Lot. Source: https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/yell-trail-grand-prismatic-overlook.htm
- Capacity/fill time NOT stated by NPS. Third-party estimate (NOT an NPS source): "about 50 spaces," lot "fills before 9:00 AM" in peak summer (July–Aug); one visitor report of full lot by 8am. Source: aggregated trip-report content via search (earthtrekkers.com / campingkiddos.com style sites), not NPS. GAP: official NPS parking capacity number and fill-time — not published by NPS; late-September fill times likely later than peak summer since crowds are lighter, but unverified.
- Related construction note: search turned up an NPS news item "Construction Scheduled on Trails in the Grand Prismatic Area" (https://www.nps.gov/yell/learn/news/16025.htm) but the page returned HTTP 404 on direct fetch — GAP: could not verify current (2026) construction status/dates for Grand Prismatic-area trails/boardwalks. Also note a related NPS ParkPlanning project "Fairy Falls Trailhead Temporary Parking Area" surfaced in search (parkplanning.nps.gov/projectHome.cfm?projectID=67630) — suggests a temporary parking reconfiguration project exists/existed; GAP: current status/dates not verified.

AllTrails: https://www.alltrails.com/trail/us/wyoming/fairy-falls--4

Late-September conditions: no snow/ice issues typically reported for this low-elevation valley trail in late Sept (Fairy Falls elevation ~7,300 ft near the Firehole River). ### 2. Upper Geyser Basin Boardwalk Loop (Old Faithful area)

**Distance options** (from search of NPS/park sources, not a single authoritative NPS distance page — see GAP below):
- Flat boardwalk loop past many hot springs/geysers: ~2.8 mi.
- Fuller Upper Geyser Basin loop: ~3.5 mi.
- Loop extended to Old Faithful Observation Point: ~4.9 mi (AllTrails lists "Upper Geyser Basin and Old Faithful Observation Point Loop" at 4.9 mi, 357 ft elevation gain, 1.5–2 hr — https://www.alltrails.com/trail/us/wyoming/upper-geyser-basin-and-old-faithful-observation-point-loop — NOTE: this AllTrails listing is tagged "[CLOSED]" as of the search snippet; GAP — verify current open/closed status before relying on it).
- Old Faithful to Morning Glory Pool: 2 mi (3.2 km) one-way on flat trail (4 mi / 6.4 km round trip — still under ceiling). Also reachable via a 1.3-mi bike path from the visitor center. Source: aggregated search snippets citing NPS-style figures; GAP — could not pin to one exact NPS URL with this precise Morning Glory distance; treat as approximate.
- GAP: No single authoritative NPS "thingstodo" page with one canonical boardwalk-loop distance was found; NPS's own Old Faithful area page (https://www.nps.gov/yell/planyourvisit/exploreoldfaithful.htm) describes boardwalks weaving through "150 geysers in one square mile" but does not itself state loop distances.

**Geysers with predicted eruption times** (per NPS page https://www.nps.gov/yell/planyourvisit/exploreoldfaithful.htm): Old Faithful, Castle, Grand, Daisy, Riverside, and Great Fountain are listed with predictions; the same page notes "only five major geysers are predicted regularly" (Castle, Grand, Daisy, Riverside, Old Faithful) — a slight internal inconsistency in the source content itself (six named vs. "five" stated). Current-day predictions posted at Old Faithful Visitor Education Center and online at https://www.nps.gov/features/yell/live/Geysers.html.

**Old Faithful eruption interval:** every 90 minutes ± 10 min (44–125 min range cited elsewhere), ~20 times/day, viewable from a semicircular viewing area under 1/4 mile from the Visitor Education Center. Source: https://www.nps.gov/yell/planyourvisit/exploreoldfaithful.htm (via search synthesis).

**Morning Glory Pool:** noted above; it has a history of coin/trash-throwing damage from visitors (not independently re-verified this trip, general NPS knowledge — flagging as background, not a hard claim needing a fresh citation).

**Old Faithful Visitor Education Center seasonal closing:** GAP — could not extract an exact fall-2026 closing date via WebFetch of https://www.nps.gov/yell/planyourvisit/oldfaithfulvec.htm (page structure didn't expose hours/season text to the fetch). Search-derived general pattern (not 2026-specific, not NPS-dated): VEC and most park facilities close "early November" and reopen mid-December for winter season, per general secondary-source description. CRITICAL GAP: need the exact 2026 closing date for Old Faithful VEC and Old Faithful-area lodging (Old Faithful Inn / Snow Lodge) before finalizing a late-Sept/early-Oct itinerary — verify directly at https://www.nps.gov/yell/planyourvisit/conditions.htm or https://www.nps.gov/yell/planyourvisit/oldfaithfulvec.htm close to the trip.

AllTrails: https://www.alltrails.com/trail/us/wyoming/upper-geyser-basin

Late-September conditions: basin is low-elevation and boardwalk-based, generally hikeable into fall; overnight frost/ice on boardwalks possible in late Sept mornings (steam + cold = ice, general park hazard). GAP: no explicit NPS statement found for late-Sept icy-boardwalk conditions specific to this basin.

### 3. Artist Point and Brink of the Lower Falls (Grand Canyon of the Yellowstone)

**Artist Point:**
- Location: South Rim Drive, 2.3 mi south of Canyon Junction. Source: https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/yell-grand-canyon-of-the-yellowstone.htm
- Walk: paved sidewalk extends "out to the second viewing area at Artist Point" — essentially a short (~0.1 mi per one source) walk from parking. GAP: NPS page itself doesn't give a precise walk distance/elevation gain for the short paved spur; the "0.1-mile" figure came from a secondary aggregator (fullsuitcase.com/similar), not NPS directly.
- What you see: the classic view of the 308-ft Lower Falls and canyon — widely cited as the single best canyon view in the park.
- Extension option: South Rim Trail continues from Artist Point toward Point Sublime; AllTrails "Canyon Rim South Trail to Artist Point" is listed as 7.0 mi out-and-back (2 hr 54 min avg) — https://www.alltrails.com/trail/us/wyoming/canyon-rim-south-trail-to-artist-point — this is UNDER the 12 km (7.5 mi) ceiling if doing the full South Rim Trail, but the short direct walk to Artist Point itself is far shorter (well under a mile round trip) if you just want the headline view.

**Brink of the Lower Falls:**
- Location: North Rim Drive (one-way loop road), reached via a spur ~1.2 mi south of Canyon Junction. Source: https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/yell-grand-canyon-of-the-yellowstone.htm
- Trail: steep "Brink Trail" descends from the North Rim Drive parking area down to the very lip of the Lower Falls — a close, thunderous, spray-in-your-face view (very different payoff from Artist Point's wide vista). NPS explicitly cautions this trail is "not recommended" for visitors with heart, lung, or other health conditions due to steep grade/stairs.
- GAP: exact one-way/round-trip distance and elevation gain/loss for Brink of the Lower Falls Trail not stated on the fetched NPS page. (Widely cited elsewhere as roughly 0.5–0.7 mi round trip with ~600 ft elevation change, but that figure is NOT from the NPS page fetched here — flagging as unverified rather than stating it as fact.)

**North Rim Trail** (connects several North Rim viewpoints including Inspiration Point, Lookout Point, Red Rock Point, Grand View): AllTrails lists it as 2.2 mi, 403 ft gain, easy, 1–1.5 hr — https://www.alltrails.com/trail/us/wyoming/north-rim-trail-red-rock-point-lookout-point-grand-view

**South Rim vs North Rim access:** South Rim Drive reaches Artist Point (dead-end spur road, two-way); North Rim Drive is a one-way loop reaching Brink of the Lower Falls, Lookout Point, Grand View, and Inspiration Point. Both accessed from Canyon Village/Canyon Junction. Source: https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/yell-grand-canyon-of-the-yellowstone.htm

AllTrails (South Rim/Artist Point): https://www.alltrails.com/trail/us/wyoming/canyon-rim-south-trail-to-artist-point
AllTrails (North Rim): https://www.alltrails.com/trail/us/wyoming/north-rim-trail-red-rock-point-lookout-point-grand-view

Alerts: The fetched NPS Grand Canyon page made no mention of Uncle Tom's Trail or current closures — consistent with it having been fully removed from NPS materials (see item 4).

### 4. Uncle Tom's Trail — CLOSED (confirmed)

**Status: PERMANENTLY CLOSED.** This is a real, long-standing closure, not a rumor.
- Originally closed in 2019 for the staircase into the canyon.
- The Yellowstone Public Affairs Office confirmed via Cowboy State Daily (Dec 31, 2023) that NPS has "permanently closed Uncle Tom's Trail" and "plans to remove the staircase in the future" — the structure exceeded its design life (bent treads, missing handrails, cracked welds, rust) and NPS will not fund the repair. Source: https://cowboystatedaily.com/2023/12/31/yellowstones-uncle-toms-trail-is-steep-exhilarating-and-permanently-closed/
- NPS's own closure notice (via Facebook repost of official NPS content, referencing the official closure-map URL): "In order to prevent damage from off-trail travel around the Grand Canyon of the Yellowstone, the closure at Uncle Tom's Point has been extended to include Uncle Tom's Trail (the stairs into the canyon) and the South Rim Trail." Official map/info link cited by NPS: go.nps.gov/canyonclosures. Source: https://www.facebook.com/YellowstoneNPS/photos/in-order-to-prevent-damage-from-off-trail-travel-around-the-grand-canyon-of-the-/1768424673172879/
- The trail has been removed from current NPS publications entirely — it does not appear on the current NPS Grand Canyon of the Yellowstone thingstodo page (https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/yell-grand-canyon-of-the-yellowstone.htm), which is itself corroborating evidence of the closure.
- GAP: could not independently WebFetch a live nps.gov/yell alert page with the word "Uncle Tom's" in 2026-dated text (the general conditions page fetched, https://www.nps.gov/yell/planyourvisit/conditions.htm, is a rolling alert page and didn't surface trail-specific text in the fetch). The Cowboy State Daily + Facebook/NPS-repost sourcing above is the strongest confirmation obtained within budget; treat as reliable but not a raw nps.gov page snapshot.

**Substitute:** Use Artist Point (item 3) as the primary Grand Canyon viewpoint — it delivers the iconic wide Lower Falls/canyon view without stairs. For a closer, more visceral falls view in place of Uncle Tom's old stairway, use Brink of the Lower Falls Trail on the North Rim (steep but currently open, no elevation/distance figures confirmed from NPS — see GAP in item 3).

### 5. Mount Washburn — LIKELY OVER CEILING, substitute proposed

**Dunraven Pass Trailhead route:**
- NPS: 6 mi (9.6 km) round trip, "strenuous," 3–6 hr. Source: https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/yell-trail-dunraven-pass-mount-washburn.htm
- AllTrails gives a slightly longer figure: 7.1 mi out-and-back, ~3.5 hr — https://www.alltrails.com/trail/us/wyoming/dunraven-pass-to-mt-washburn-trail (note the NPS vs. AllTrails distance discrepancy: 6.0 mi vs 7.1 mi for what should be the same route — flagging, not resolving).
- Elevation gain: NOT stated on the NPS trail page itself. Summit elevation is 10,219 ft (3,115 m) per https://www.nps.gov/places/000/mount-washburn.htm. GAP: NPS does not publish a gain figure for this trail in the pages fetched.
- **Ceiling check:** at 6 mi (9.6 km) NPS figure, this is UNDER the 12 km ceiling. At the AllTrails 7.1 mi (11.4 km) figure, still under 12 km. Either way, Dunraven Pass route to Washburn is within the traveller's ~12 km ceiling — flagging this as a correction to the task's assumption that it's "likely over." It is strenuous and long (3–6 hr) but distance-wise fits.

**Chittenden Road Trailhead route (shorter option):**
- NPS: "5.8-mile (9.3-km) there-and-back," described as the shortest route up, moderate difficulty. Source: https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/yell-chittenden-loop-ski-trail.htm / corroborated at https://www.nps.gov/places/000/chittenden-road-mount-washburn-north-trailhead-2k6.htm
- Elevation gain: GAP — not stated on fetched NPS pages.
- Parking: "limited at this very popular trail," no numeric capacity given. Source: https://www.nps.gov/places/000/chittenden-road-mount-washburn-north-trailhead-2k6.htm
- **CRITICAL FALL WARNING (NPS, direct quote):** "CAUTION: Grizzly bears frequent this area especially in the fall. Hiking this trail is not recommended in September and October." Source: https://www.nps.gov/places/000/chittenden-road-mount-washburn-north-trailhead-2k6.htm — this directly affects a late-September trip. The Dunraven Pass NPS page carries a related but less absolute warning: grizzlies present in fall, groups of 3+ with bear spray required, not an explicit "not recommended" statement.

**Point-to-point option (through-hike, needs a shuttle/two cars):** Dunraven Pass to Chittenden Road via Mount Washburn — AllTrails: 8 mi, 1,417 ft gain, 4.5–5 hr, rated most popular/difficult in the area — https://www.alltrails.com/trail/us/wyoming/dunraven-pass-to-chittenden-road-via-mount-washburn (8 mi / 12.9 km — this ONE exceeds the 12 km ceiling; flag if considered).

**Summit payoff:** panoramic views for 20–50 mi (32–80 km), interpretive exhibits inside the base of an actively staffed fire lookout tower (staffed mid-June through fire season end). NPS does not explicitly confirm full 360-degree visibility — described generically as "panoramic." Source: https://www.nps.gov/places/000/mount-washburn.htm

**Recommendation given the September/October grizzly warning:** prefer the Dunraven Pass route (6 mi/9.6 km NPS figure, under ceiling) over Chittenden Road, since NPS explicitly advises against Chittenden Road in Sept/Oct. If a shorter option is wanted, GAP: no NPS-published sub-12-km alternate summit-view hike with comparable payoff was identified in this pass — Dunraven Pass route is itself already the shorter/safer-season choice, so no further substitute needed beyond avoiding Chittenden Road in fall.

- Tower Fall to Canyon road (which carries the Dunraven Pass trailhead access): "Open May 22 – October 12, 2026" for regular wheeled vehicles. Source: NPS park roads page, https://www.nps.gov/yell/planyourvisit/parkroads.htm
- This means: a **10 Sep 2028** trip start (leading option) is comfortably before typical closure timing (assuming similar seasonal pattern in 2028); a **20 Sep 2027** fallback start is also fine. Only a trip pushing into mid-October would risk the Oct 12-type closure. ### 6. Lamar Valley Wildlife Pullouts

**Named pullouts (from search synthesis of secondary wildlife-guide sources — NOT an NPS-published list of named pullouts; NPS's own page at https://www.nps.gov/places/000/lamar-valley-wildlife-watching.htm confirms the valley as a top wolf-watching area but does NOT itself name specific pullouts):**
- Slough Creek — gravel pullouts near the Slough Creek campground road; described as the most reliable wolf-watching real estate; Junction Butte pack has denned in this area.
- Hitching Post — pullout near the confluence area east of Lamar Canyon; good for valley-floor sightings and cottonwoods near the river.
- Footbridge — pullout where a wooden footbridge crosses Soda Butte Creek near its confluence with the Lamar River, a known wolf travel corridor.
- Dorothy's Knoll and "mid-valley" pullouts — informal names for wide-view pullouts across the central valley.
- Little America — rolling, boulder-strewn flats west of Lamar Canyon, a regular travel corridor for northern-range packs.
- GAP: none of the above pullout names were confirmed on an nps.gov page directly — they come from wildlife-guide sites (morethanjustparks.com, roamingmontana.com, etc.) surfaced via search, not NPS. Treat names as commonly-used/reliable in the guiding community but not NPS-official designations.

**Best hours:** NPS confirms the general safety distances (100 yd from wolves/bears, 25 yd from other wildlife) via https://www.nps.gov/places/000/lamar-valley-wildlife-watching.htm, and secondary sources consistently describe dawn (just after sunrise) as prime wolf-watching time with visitors gathering with spotting scopes/binoculars. GAP: NPS does not itself publish exact late-September dawn/dusk clock times; sunrise in the Lamar Valley in late September is roughly 6:50–7:10 AM MDT and sunset roughly 7:00–7:15 PM MDT (standard solar-calendar fact, not from an NPS page — verify locally/via a sunrise calculator for exact trip dates).

**Elk rut at Mammoth (bonus, confirmed via search of secondary sources, not a direct NPS page fetch):** Rut typically begins mid-August, peaks mid-September, and commonly continues into October with bugling heard as late as mid-October. Mammoth Hot Springs is the standout viewing location because elk concentrate directly among the historic buildings. Safety: stay ≥25 yd (two bus lengths) from elk; bulls can charge. GAP: not independently confirmed on a direct nps.gov page in this pass (search-engine synthesis only); general seasonal pattern is well-established but no exact 2026-specific NPS statement was fetched.

**Northeast Entrance road status:**
- The Beartooth Highway (US-212), which the Northeast Entrance connects toward, is "Open the Friday of Memorial Day weekend to mid-October." Source: NPS park roads page, https://www.nps.gov/yell/planyourvisit/parkroads.htm
- The Chief Joseph Scenic Byway (WY-296) stays open year-round for part of its route, with 9 miles switching to oversnow-only travel in winter. Source: same page.
- The core in-park Northeast Entrance road (Mammoth–Tower–Cooke City via Lamar Valley) is stated elsewhere on the conditions page as one of the few Yellowstone roads that stays open essentially year-round (with winter oversnow caveats on other roads) — GAP: the exact phrase/date confirmation for the Mammoth-to-Northeast-Entrance segment specifically was not cleanly extracted from the fetched conditions page; the fetch returned only that "most other park roads" close mid-Dec–mid-March, implying (but not explicitly stating) that the Mammoth–NE Entrance road is the exception. Re-verify at https://www.nps.gov/yell/planyourvisit/parkroads.htm before travel.

### Late September vs. Mid-October — differences noted
- Dunraven Pass / Tower–Canyon road: OPEN through Oct 12, 2026 (this-year figure) — late Sept is comfortably inside the window; a mid-October date is riskier and must be re-checked yearly.
- Beartooth Highway (NE Entrance approach from Cooke City side): open to "mid-October" — same caution as above.
- Chittenden Road Mount Washburn trail: NPS explicitly says NOT recommended in September or October due to grizzly activity — this applies to BOTH late-Sept and mid-Oct equally; use Dunraven Pass side instead.
- Elk rut (Mammoth): peaks mid-September, still active (bugling) into October — both a late-Sept and a fallback 20-Sep start should catch good rut activity; a mid-October visit would likely still catch tail-end bugling per secondary sources, unconfirmed via direct NPS page.
- Old Faithful VEC / lodging seasonal closing: GAP — exact date not confirmed (see item 2); this is the single most important date to nail down since it could differ meaningfully between a late-Sept and an early/mid-Oct visit and affects whether the Visitor Education Center and in-park lodging are even open.
- Grand Prismatic/Fairy Falls parking congestion: likely lighter in late Sept than the "fills by 9am / July-Aug" third-party figures cited above, but no NPS or dated source confirms a specific late-Sept fill time — treat as a GAP.
