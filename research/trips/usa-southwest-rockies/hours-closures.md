# Research lane 4 — Operating hours, fees, seasonal closures, crowd patterns

RAW DATA for synthesis. Not a human-facing report.
Compiled 2026-09-05. Trip window: late Sep – mid Oct 2027 (fallback ~10 Sep – 4 Oct 2028).

**Reading rule:** all published dates below are the *2026* season (or 2027 where the
operator publishes that far ahead, noted explicitly). NPS publishes road/facility dates
one year at a time. 2027 and 2028 dates are NOT yet published for most facilities. The
2026 dates are the best available proxy and the pattern is stable year to year — but every
date must be re-verified once the target year is fixed.

## 0. HEADLINE FINDINGS (the trip-ruining ones)

1. **Non-US resident $100/person surcharge.** From 1 Jan 2026, non-US residents 16+ pay
   **+$100 per person** on top of the normal entrance fee at 11 parks — including **Zion,
   Bryce Canyon, Grand Teton and Yellowstone**, four parks on this itinerary. It is
   **waived if you hold an Annual or America the Beautiful pass**. The non-resident
   America the Beautiful pass is **$250**. For a Canadian party of 2 this is $250 vs
   ~$800 in surcharges. Buy the pass. (see §9)
2. **Dunraven Pass and the Beartooth Highway close ~12 October** — three weeks before the
   rest of Yellowstone. The BRAINSTORM option (a) window puts Yellowstone at 10–13 Oct 2027,
   i.e. exactly on the closing date. (see §1)
3. **Yellowstone in-park lodging 2027 dates are already published.** Old Faithful Inn closes
   **11 Oct 2027**; Lake Yellowstone Hotel and Grant Village close **10 Oct 2027**. Only
   **Canyon Lodge (31 Oct 2027)** and **Mammoth (year-round)** survive past mid-October.
   The BRAINSTORM day-21–23 plan (Old Faithful/Grant → Canyon → Gardiner) partly collapses
   in option (a). (see §1)
4. **Zion Canyon Scenic Drive is shuttle-only in October.** The shuttle runs March–November,
   so you cannot drive your own vehicle up the canyon on any date in this window. (see §2)
5. **Monument Valley is on Navajo Nation time (observes DST); the rest of Arizona is not.**
   Driving Page → Monument Valley in Sep/Oct you **lose an hour**. This is the classic
   missed-tour error. (see §6)
6. **Mesa Verde cliff-dwelling tours are released only 14 days ahead** and the season ends
   **21 Oct**. Not a book-months-out item — a set-an-alarm item. (see §8)
7. **Capitol Reef's Fremont River ford was "Impassable" as of 2 Sep 2026**, closing the
   Cathedral Valley loop from the south. (see §5)
8. **1 October is the US federal fiscal-year boundary** — the classic government shutdown
   date — and it falls inside this trip window in both candidate years. **Two of the last
   three fiscal years opened with an NPS funding lapse**; the 2025 one ran 43 days and
   Mesa Verde closed its trails within four days of it. In 2013 state rescue money took
   **ten days** to arrive. (see §12)
9. **UT-95 Hanksville→Blanding is a hard 121-mile no-services fuel dead zone**, and the
   historic Hite Marina fallback is now permanently closed per NPS. This is on BRAINSTORM
   day 8. (see §10a)
10. **Columbus Day is Monday 11 October 2027** — day 22 of option (a), inside Yellowstone,
    in the last week its lodges are open. **Option (b) 2028 contains no federal holidays at
    all.** (see §11a)
11. **October is NOT the quiet month in southern Utah.** Zion and Arches October visitation
    *exceeds* September. The "go later to avoid crowds" instinct is wrong for the first half
    of this route and right only for the last third. (see §11d)
12. **Utah's fall break (UEA weekend) is 21–22 October 2027 — it misses both windows.** NPS
    names it a top-four annual crowd spike at Arches, so this is a real de-risking. (see §11g)
13. **A free Moab-to-Arches shuttle launched 1 Sep 2026** — but its last Delicate Arch
    departure is 4:05 pm, so it cannot serve a Delicate Arch sunset. (see §11f)
14. **Monday nights are the day-of-week risk.** Option (a) puts Mondays in **Hanksville and
    Ouray**, the two towns with the thinnest dining; option (b) puts one in **Gardiner**,
    where the chamber tags a third of restaurants "Open Summer". (see §11b, §10c)

### 1d. Grand Teton roads

Source: https://www.nps.gov/grte/planyourvisit/roads.htm

- **Teton Park Road: "closed November 1 to April 30 from Taggart Lake Trailhead to Signal
  Mountain Lodge."** This is the single hardest date in Grand Teton — Jenny Lake,
  Cathedral Group, Signal Mountain all sit on it.
- **Moose-Wilson Road: "closed November 1 until mid-May based on conditions."**
- Grassy Lake Road: "closes based on conditions."
- Antelope Flats, Deadmans Bar, Pilgrim Creek, Schwabacher, Two Ocean: "closed seasonally
  based on conditions" — no fixed dates.
- GAP: Signal Mountain Summit Road closing date — not stated on the NPS roads page.
  (Commonly reported as closing with the first snow / late October, but unsourced here.)

**CONFLICT TO RESOLVE — Moose-Wilson Road.** The NPS *roads* page says Moose-Wilson is
"closed November 1 until mid-May based on conditions", but the NPS *conditions* page
(https://www.nps.gov/grte/planyourvisit/conditions.htm) states it was **open only
19 June – 7 September 2026**. The most likely explanation is a construction/reconstruction
closure layered on top of the normal seasonal one — the hours page also warns "Anticipate
road closures from Laurance S. Rockefeller Preserve Junction to Moose." **Treat Moose-Wilson
Road as probably unavailable in late Sept/Oct and verify for the target year.** This matters
because Moose-Wilson is the Laurance S. Rockefeller Preserve / Death Canyon access and a
prime moose-viewing drive.

**Both Teton Park Road and Moose-Wilson Road are open for the whole of the candidate
window in both years.** Grand Teton is the *least* date-fragile of the two northern parks.

**Pattern: Grand Teton's in-park services cluster their closing on 3–4 October and again
11–12 October.** A 2028 trip (10 Sep – 4 Oct) catches almost all of them open. A 2027
option-(a) trip (Grand Teton ~day 19–20, i.e. ~8–9 Oct) misses Jackson Lake Lodge, Jenny
Lake Lodge, the Aerial Tram and the Jenny Lake boat shuttle, but keeps Signal Mountain,
Craig Thomas VC and Jenny Lake VC.

### 1f. How late can this trip run? — the answer

- **Hard wall:** Yellowstone interior roads close **early November** (2026 dates: 31 Oct
  for the main loop). Grand Teton's Teton Park Road closes **1 November**.
- **Soft wall (the real one):** **~12 October.** After that you lose Dunraven Pass, the
  Beartooth Highway, Old Faithful Inn, Lake Hotel, Grant Village, Jenny Lake VC, Signal
  Mountain Lodge and most of Grand Teton's food service.
- **Comfortable wall:** **~3–4 October**, after which Grand Teton's marquee lodges
  (Jackson Lake, Jenny Lake), the Aerial Tram and the Jenny Lake boat shuttle all shut.
- Verdict for the BRAINSTORM decision: option **(b) Sept 2028 (10 Sep – 4 Oct)** lands
  almost entirely inside the fully-open season. Option **(a) 2027 (20 Sep – 14 Oct)** puts
  Yellowstone on 10–13 Oct, i.e. *on* the Dunraven/Beartooth closing date and *past* most
  southern-loop lodging. It is survivable only by rebasing to Canyon + Mammoth/Gardiner.

### **THE DAYLIGHT SAVING TIME TRAP — CONFIRMED**

- **Arizona does not observe DST. The Navajo Nation does.** The Navajo Nation observes DST
  to stay aligned with its communities and services in Utah and New Mexico.
  (Navajo Nation Office of the President & Vice President:
  https://opvp.navajo-nsn.gov/260307-daylight-savings-times/)
- Independently confirmed by the park's own visitor site
  (https://www.monumentvalleytribalpark.com/pages/prepare), verbatim: "**Be aware that the
  Navajo Nation observes daylight savings time.** We are on the same time zone as Denver
  or Mountain Standard Time." (The phrasing is loose — the operative fact is that they
  shift with Denver and Utah, while Arizona does not.)
- Consequence: **from the second Sunday in March to the first Sunday in November, Monument
  Valley (MDT, UTC−6) is ONE HOUR AHEAD of the rest of Arizona (MST, UTC−7)** and is on
  the same clock as Utah.
- **Page, AZ is on MST and does NOT observe DST. Monument Valley does.** Therefore
  **Page is 1 hour behind Monument Valley** for the whole of the candidate trip window
  (both 2027 and 2028 — DST runs to the first Sunday of November in both).
  (https://www.travelinusa.us/time-zone-arizona-utah/ ;
  https://www.monument-valley.com/timezone.php)
- **Practical rule for this itinerary:** BRAINSTORM day 7 drives Page → Monument Valley.
  You **lose an hour**. A 2:30 pm "last entry" at Monument Valley is **1:30 pm Page time**.
  Set phones to auto-update and treat all Monument Valley times as Utah/Mountain *Daylight*
  time. The reverse leg (Monument Valley → Hanksville, day 8) stays in Utah so no change;
  but Antelope Canyon tours in Page are on MST and Monument Valley tours are on MDT.
- Note also: **Utah observes DST** and is the same clock as the Navajo Nation, so once you
  leave Page northbound there is no further discontinuity until the trip ends.
- GAP: whether individual Monument Valley tour operators quote times in Navajo (MDT) or
  Arizona (MST) time — practice varies and is a documented source of missed tours. Confirm
  with the specific operator at booking, in writing.

  (Capitol Reef) + $20 (Natural Bridges) + $30 (Glen Canyon) + Arches + Canyonlands +
  Mesa Verde.
- With one **$250 Non-Resident Annual ATB pass**: covers the vehicle and all passengers at
  every NPS unit on the route AND waives all four $100 surcharges.
- **Buy one $250 non-resident America the Beautiful pass.** It is the single highest-value
  admin action in the whole trip. Order well ahead or pick up in person — online orders
  ship a physical pass and "may take up to three weeks"; "Order receipts may not be used
  in place of a physical pass."
- **NOT covered by the ATB pass:** Monument Valley Navajo Tribal Park ($10pp + $15/vehicle),
  Horseshoe Bend parking (City of Page), Dead Horse Point State Park (Utah State Parks).
- **The $55 Southeast Utah annual pass is US-residents-only** and therefore unavailable.
- **Cashless parks on this route: Bryce Canyon and Glen Canyon** both state "This park does
  not accept cash." Natural Bridges accepts cash or card.

> "Fry's Canyon … contained the only gas station between Hanksville and Blanding, but it has

### 11a. US federal holidays in the window — computed, exact

Labor Day = first Monday in September. Columbus Day / Indigenous Peoples' Day = second
Monday in October. (Federal holiday definitions, 5 U.S.C. §6103.) Computed:

| | **2027** | **2028** |
|---|---|---|
| **Labor Day** | **Mon 6 Sep 2027** | **Mon 4 Sep 2028** |
| **Columbus Day / Indigenous Peoples' Day** | **Mon 11 Oct 2027** | **Mon 9 Oct 2028** |
| Veterans Day | Thu 11 Nov 2027 | Sat 11 Nov 2028 |
| DST ends (clocks back) | Sun 7 Nov 2027 | Sun 5 Nov 2028 |

Both are Monday holidays, i.e. **three-day weekends** (Sat–Mon).

**Verdict by option:**
- **Option (a) 20 Sep – 14 Oct 2027: Labor Day is safely before the trip (6 Sep), but
  Columbus Day Mon 11 Oct 2027 lands ON day 22, inside Yellowstone.** The 3-day weekend is
  Sat 9 – Mon 11 Oct = Jackson (day 20) + Old Faithful/Grant (day 21) + Canyon (day 22).
  This is the worst possible placement: a holiday long weekend in the Tetons/Yellowstone,
  in the *last week those lodges are open*, so the reduced remaining room inventory is
  competing with a holiday surge.
- **Option (b) 10 Sep – 4 Oct 2028 avoids BOTH federal holidays entirely.** Labor Day
  (Mon 4 Sep 2028) is six days before departure; Columbus Day (Mon 9 Oct 2028) is five
  days after return. **This is a clean win for option (b)** and should be weighed alongside
  the seasonal-closure argument in §1f.

## 12. GOVERNMENT SHUTDOWN RISK

**The US federal fiscal year begins 1 October.** A lapse in appropriations on that date is
the classic shutdown trigger, and **1 October falls inside the trip window in both
candidate years**:
- Option (a): **1 Oct 2027 = day 12**, arriving Durango. A shutdown would hit the back
  half — Mesa Verde, the San Juans, Grand Teton, Yellowstone.
- Option (b): **1 Oct 2028 = day 22**, at Canyon inside Yellowstone. A shutdown would hit
  only the last three days, but at the worst possible location.

### 12a. Shutdown history and duration

Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_shutdowns_in_the_United_States

| Shutdown | Dates | Duration | Interior/NPS affected? |
|---|---|---|---|
| FY2014 | **1 – 17 October 2013** | 17 days | **Yes — full closure** |
| FY2019 | 22 Dec 2018 – 25 Jan 2019 | 35 days | **Yes — open but unstaffed** |
| **FY2026** | **1 October – 12 November 2025** | **43 days** | **Yes — open but unstaffed** |
| FY2026 #2 | 31 Jan – 3 Feb 2026 | 4 days | ~half of federal departments |
| FY2026 #3 | 14 Feb – 30 Apr 2026 | **76 days — longest in US history** | **No — DHS only** |

Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_United_States_federal_government_shutdown ;
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_federal_government_shutdowns
The Feb–Apr 2026 shutdown was limited to the Department of Homeland Security because the
House had passed "the final six of twelve annual appropriations bills" on 22 January 2026 —
Interior received full-year FY2026 funding, which is why NPS was untouched by it.

**Two of the last three fiscal years began with an NPS funding lapse on 1 October**
(2013 and 2025). This is the fiscal-year boundary, not coincidence. The 2025 instance ran
43 days. **This is the single most important number in this lane for a trip designed to be
inside a national park on 1 October.**

GAP: the status of FY2027 appropriations as of September 2026, and therefore the near-term
trend — not established. Forward risk for FY2028 (1 Oct 2027) and FY2029 (1 Oct 2028) is
unknowable this far out; treat it as a live risk class given the recent base rate, not as a
forecast.

### 12b. What actually happened to the parks — and note the policy REVERSED between 2013 and 2025

**2013 — parks were fully CLOSED and gated.**
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_United_States_federal_government_shutdown
- "**All 401 units of the National Park System were closed to the public** during the
  shutdown, as Congress had not appropriated funding for their operations and maintenance."
- "The National Park Service responded that it is **legally mandated to protect national
  park lands** and, in the absence of available staff to patrol, maintain and administer
  the areas, **must close them to the public**."
- "**Various concession-operated visitor amenities were also closed** because the NPS staff
  who oversee concessionaires have been furloughed, preventing the agency from managing and
  directing concession operations." → **in-park lodging shut too.**
- Xanterra (the Yellowstone lodging concessioner) "reported that it was losing $1 million in
  revenue each day because the parks have closed."
- NPS estimated losses of "$76 million a day in tourism-related sales among local
  communities." A Moab cafe owner is quoted: "**Twenty percent of my yearly income comes
  from October and May.**"

**2025 — parks were left PARTIALLY OPEN but unstaffed.** The opposite approach.
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_United_States_federal_government_shutdown
- "According to a **September 30 contingency plan by the Department of Interior, all
  national parks were to be left partially open during the shutdown**, but **any buildings
  that required staffing, such as visitor centers … would be closed to the public, with
  only restrooms open and trash collected as normal.**"
- **Directly relevant to this itinerary: "Mesa Verde National Park trails closed on
  October 5, 2025."** Individual parks still closed individual assets.
- **Arches:** "a retired national park ranger discovered fresh white spray-painted graffiti
  and toilet paper on the park's sandstone features."
- "Conservationists, the National Parks Conservation Association, and **the National Park
  Service all issued warnings to the public to use caution or not to visit national parks
  during the shutdown** due to concern about skeleton staffing making it difficult to assist
  in an emergency."
- "over 35 former park superintendents urged the parks managed by the National Park Service
  to close to avoid damage by unsupervised visitors."
- NPS lost "about $1 million a day" in entrance revenue (National Parks Conservation
  Association analysis).

**2018-19 — the middle case, and the most encouraging one for this route.** Verbatim:
> "**Arizona and Utah** were able to keep **Grand Canyon National Park, Zion National Park,
> Arches National Park, and Bryce Canyon National Park open** and provided services including
> public restrooms, **shuttles** and trash collection. **Utah's funding included visitor
> centers.**"
(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%932019_United_States_federal_government_shutdown)
**Note the explicit inclusion of shuttles — the Zion canyon shuttle has previously been kept
running on Utah state money.** In the same shutdown, Bandelier NM, Valles Caldera, White
Sands, Petrified Forest and Casa Grande Ruins closed outright.

Also from 2018-19: overflowing trash and toilets system-wide by 1 January 2019; protected
Joshua trees cut down; a death at Yosemite unreported for a week.

**The fee-revenue workaround that made 2018-19 survivable has since been ruled ILLEGAL.**
In 2018-19 Interior spent FLREA recreation-fee revenue on custodial services normally paid
from the ONPS appropriation. **GAO Decision B-330776, 5 September 2019**, verbatim:
> "Interior **violated the purpose statute** when, during the shutdown, it obligated FLREA
> fees for expenses that it would normally charge to ONPS appropriations. Because NPS did not
> have an ONPS appropriation at the time that it incurred the obligations at issue, Interior
> **also violated the Antideficiency Act**."
(https://www.gao.gov/products/b-330776)
**Practical consequence: the fee-money trick that kept restrooms serviced in 2019 is now a
documented Antideficiency Act violation and cannot be assumed available in a future lapse.**

**State buy-outs are a real precedent — and Utah is the textbook case:**
- **2013:** "Utah governor Gary Herbert wired **$1.67 million** to the Department of Interior
  to reopen **eight national parks in Utah for at least 10 days**. **Arizona** agreed to
  reopen the Grand Canyon. New York reached a deal to reopen the Statue of Liberty.
  **Colorado funded Rocky Mountain National Park operations.**" South Dakota's partial
  Mount Rushmore offer was refused — NPS said only a full operation would be considered.
  (https://www.npr.org/blogs/thetwo-way/2013/10/11/232090272/utah-allowed-to-re-open-national-parks-and-foot-the-bill)
- **CRITICAL TIMING: the 2013 lapse began 1 October; Utah's deal landed 11 October — ten days
  of closure first.** The Statue of Liberty deal was announced 11 Oct and reopened 13 Oct;
  Mount Rushmore reopened 14 Oct. **Plan on roughly 10 days of closure before any state money
  reopens anything.** A 25-day trip that hits 1 October could lose its entire Utah leg inside
  that gap.
- **2018-19:** Utah and Arizona did it again (see above).
- **2025:** only **West Virginia** is documented, spending **$100,000**.
  (https://westvirginiawatch.com/2025/10/03/wv-spending-100k-to-keep-national-parks-open-during-federal-government-shutdown/)
- **Utah has done this twice and has the strongest incentive of any state** — Zion, Bryce,
  Capitol Reef, Arches and Canyonlands are all Utah. Arizona has done it once. Colorado has
  done it once (Mesa Verde is Colorado). For the southwest half of this trip the historical
  precedent is genuinely favourable.
- GAP: whether Utah or Arizona paid to keep their parks staffed during the Oct–Nov 2025
  shutdown — governor.utah.gov/newsroom returns 404 and no confirming source was retrieved.
  Plausible given precedent but **UNVERIFIED — do not assume it.**
- GAP: whether **Wyoming or Montana** have ever bought out a shutdown — no evidence found.
  Yellowstone and Grand Teton therefore have **no** state-rescue precedent, unlike Utah.

### 12c. Practical contingency for this trip

- **The policy is not stable between administrations** — 2013 closed everything, 2025 left
  parks open-but-unstaffed. **You cannot plan on either.** Assume the worst case (gated) for
  anything unrefundable, and treat an open-but-unstaffed park as the likely case.
- **What survives a shutdown regardless:** Monument Valley Navajo Tribal Park (tribal, not
  federal), Dead Horse Point State Park (Utah State Parks), Horseshoe Bend parking (City of
  Page), Goblin Valley and Kodachrome Basin (state parks), the Moki Dugway and UT-95/UT-24
  (state highways), the Million Dollar Highway (US-550), and all the gateway towns. **The
  Hanksville cluster — Factory Butte, Bentonite Hills, Moonscape Overlook, Blue Valley
  Spire — is BLM land, not NPS**, and in 2013 BLM land largely stayed accessible. That is a
  useful shutdown-proof reserve of content.
- **What dies immediately:** in-park lodging (2013 precedent), ranger-led tours (Mesa Verde
  cliff dwellings, Arches Fiery Furnace), the Zion and Bryce shuttles, all visitor centres,
  and permit issuance (Angels Landing).
- **Booking implication:** favour **refundable** rates for in-park Yellowstone/Grand Teton
  lodging and gateway hotels around the 1 October boundary, and buy travel insurance that
  covers a government-action cancellation. Xanterra's cancellation policy should be checked
  explicitly (GAP — not reviewed in this lane).
- **The Zion shuttle is the sharpest single point of failure**: with the shuttle down and
  private vehicles still barred, Zion Canyon becomes walk/bike-in only.
- GAP: the current NPS lapse-in-appropriations contingency plan document — doi.gov's
  contingency-plans page renders via JavaScript and returned no PDF links to curl, and the
  FY2026 NPS plan URL guessed at doi.gov returned 404. The plan is re-issued each fiscal
  year, so the 2027/2028 version does not exist yet in any case.
