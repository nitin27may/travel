## 0.1 The 2027 option (a) collides with Yellowstone's in-park closing dates

(BRAINSTORM.md days 21–23). Confirmed 2027 closing dates
(https://www.yellowstonenationalparklodges.com/stay/plan/opening-and-closing-dates/):

| Lake Lodge Cabins | 10 Jun | **3 Oct** | No |
| Grant Village | 28 May | **10 Oct** | No |
| Lake Yellowstone Hotel & Cabins | 14 May | **10 Oct** | No |

| **Canyon Lodge & Cabins** | 14 May | **31 Oct** | **Yes** |

**Consequence:** on option (a), the BRAINSTORM day-21 plan of "Old Faithful / Grant" is
half dead — Grant Village is closed, Old Faithful Inn is closed or on its last night.
The only in-park beds on 11–13 Oct 2027 are **Old Faithful Snow Lodge, Canyon Lodge and
Mammoth Hot Springs Hotel**. That is a survivable set (it happens to match a workable
3-night shape), but Grant Village must be struck from the plan and the Old Faithful night
must be Snow Lodge, not the Inn.

On option (b) (2028, Yellowstone landing ~30 Sep – 3 Oct) everything except Roosevelt is
still open — a materially better position. GAP: 2028 dates are not yet published; the 2027
list is the best available proxy and dates shift by a day or two year to year.

## 0.2 Yellowstone in-park booking opens on a rolling 13-month, day-of-month trigger

> "We accept reservations for stays up to 13 months in advance. Specifically, on the 5th of
> each month, we'll begin accepting reservations for the same entire month of the following
> year." (https://www.yellowstonenationalparklodges.com/frequently-asked-questions/)

So:
- To book **October 2027**, the phone/web opens **5 October 2026** — one month from today.
- To book **September 2027**, it opened **5 September 2026** — today.
- To book **October 2028** (fallback option b), it opens **5 October 2027**.

This is the single most time-critical item in the whole trip. In-park autumn rooms at
Canyon and Old Faithful go in the first hours.

## 0.3 Dunraven Pass (Canyon → Tower) closes ~12 October — this breaks BRAINSTORM day 23

NPS park-roads page, 2026 projected dates (https://www.nps.gov/yell/planyourvisit/parkroads.htm):

- Canyon Village ↔ Tower Fall (high elevation, Dunraven Pass): **22 May – 12 Oct**
- Beartooth Highway and Chief Joseph Scenic Byway: **22 May – 12 Oct**
- Everything else on the Grand Loop: opens Apr/May, **closes 31 Oct**
- "The only road generally open year-round to regular vehicles is from the North Entrance at
  Gardiner, Montana, through the park to Cooke City, Montana (via Tower Junction)."

Most roads then shut to regular vehicles on ~1 November
(https://www.nps.gov/yell/learn/news/24037.htm — 2024 release: West/South/East entrances and
nearly all roads closed to regular vehicle traffic Nov 1).

**Consequence:** BRAINSTORM day 23 is "Canyon → Lamar Valley → Mammoth". On option (a) that
day is ~13 Oct 2027 — **after Dunraven Pass has closed**. The direct Canyon→Tower→Lamar link
(50–60 min) is gone and you must reroute Canyon→Norris→Mammoth→Tower→Lamar, which is
30 + 45 + 40 = ~1h55m of driving before you even reach Lamar, versus ~1h50m to Lamar via
Dunraven. Roughly comparable in time but it forces you past Mammoth first, which actually
argues for **sleeping at Mammoth/Gardiner that night rather than Canyon**.

## 0.4 The exit leg to SLC punishes a Gardiner finish by 1h34m

- Gardiner MT → Salt Lake City: 601 km / 373 mi, **6h33m** (https://www.rome2rio.com/s/Gardiner-MT/Salt-Lake-City-UT)
- West Yellowstone MT → Salt Lake City: 514 km / 319 mi, **4h59m** (https://www.rome2rio.com/s/West-Yellowstone-MT/Salt-Lake-City-UT)

BRAINSTORM day 24 is "Yellowstone → SLC 5h30 + Stansbury Island sunset". That 5h30 estimate
only holds if you leave from the **west** side of the park. Leaving from Gardiner it is 6h33m
before stops, which kills the Stansbury Island sunset. See §11 for the fix.

## 0.5 Arches timed entry was dropped for 2026 — 2027 unknown

NPS announced 18 Feb 2026 that Arches will **not** require advance timed-entry reservations
in 2026 — no Recreation.gov booking, no $2 fee
(https://www.nps.gov/arch/learn/news/news02182026.htm,
https://www.moabtimes.com/articles/arches-national-park-drops-timed-entry-reservation-for-2026/).
Fiery Furnace (guided ranger tours and self-guided permits, bookable ~7 days ahead on

## 0.6 Zion shuttle runs through the whole window; private cars are banned in the canyon

- 2026 season: Zion Canyon Line and Springdale Line both run **7 Mar – 28 Nov 2026**
  (https://www.nps.gov/zion/learn/news/2026-02-06-shuttle-schedule.htm)
- 13 Sep – 24 Oct hours: first shuttle 07:00, last shuttle out of Temple of Sinawava **19:15**.
  25 Oct – 28 Nov: last shuttle **18:15** (same source).
- "Personal vehicles are not allowed on the Zion Canyon Scenic Drive during shuttle season"
  (https://www.nps.gov/zion/planyourvisit/traffic.htm)

Both target windows fall inside shuttle season. This is what makes the Springdale-vs-Hurricane
argument real (see §1).

## 0.7 Time zone trap: Page AZ vs Monument Valley

Verified and it is a genuine hazard for a sunrise/tour-time plan:

- Arizona outside the Navajo Nation does **not** observe DST — Page is MST (UTC-7) year round
  (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_in_Arizona)
- The **Navajo Nation does** observe DST, to stay aligned with Utah/New Mexico
  (https://opvp.navajo-nsn.gov/260307-daylight-savings-times/)
- Antelope Canyon, although on Navajo land, is a carve-out: tour operators there run on
  Page/Arizona time (MST year-round) to avoid confusing visitors
  (https://www.lowercanyon.com/timezone.php)
- Monument Valley **does** follow Navajo DST (https://www.monument-valley.com/timezone.php)

**Net for late Sep / Oct 2027 (US DST ends 7 Nov 2027):** Page and Antelope Canyon are on
MST (UTC-7). Monument Valley is on MDT (UTC-6) — **one hour ahead of Page**. Drive from Page
to Monument Valley and your phone jumps an hour forward. A sunrise alarm set in "Page time"
is an hour late at The View.

## VERDICT — Springdale vs Hurricane vs Kanab

**Springdale, and the premium is defensible for exactly this trip.** Reasoning:

- With private cars banned on the Scenic Drive, everyone rides the shuttle. The only variable
  is what you do before the shuttle: Springdale guests walk to the Pedestrian Entrance;
  Hurricane and Kanab guests drive 33–66 min and then compete for the visitor-center lot,
  which fills early. You pay ~$250/night extra to delete a parking gamble on three
  consecutive mornings, two of which are early starts (Angels Landing permit slot, Narrows).
- Springdale is genuinely dead after 19:15 and has only a boutique grocery. That is a real
  cost but it is a cost you were going to pay in half the towns on this trip anyway.
- **Hurricane is the right answer only if the budget is the binding constraint.** It saves
  roughly $250/night × 3 = ~$750 and gives you a real supermarket and a real town, at the
  price of ~70 min/day of driving and the parking risk. Note the tax load also drops from
  ~16% to ~13.3%, which compounds the saving.
- **Kanab is the wrong base for three dedicated Zion nights** — 66 min each way. Kanab earns
  its keep only if the itinerary later routes through it for Grand Staircase / Coral Pink /
  the Grand Canyon North Rim, which this one does not (it goes Zion → Kanarra → Bryce → Page).
- Zion Lodge is the only genuinely differentiated in-park option in this whole trip that is
  open year-round, so it carries no closure risk — but it books 13 months out and prime fall
  dates go in minutes. If a Zion Lodge night is wanted, that call had to be made ~1 Sep 2026.

**Booking urgency: MEDIUM-HIGH.** Springdale has tiny room inventory (pop. 514, one hotel
strip) against Zion's visitation. GAP: no source found stating an explicit "book a year out"
lead time for Springdale.

## VERDICT — Bryce Canyon City vs Tropic vs Panguitch

**Bryce Canyon City, for this one night specifically.** The night exists to serve a sunrise at
Sunset/Sunrise Point followed by the Navajo/Queens Garden loop, and then a 2h45 drive to Page.
Against a fixed sunrise time in sub-freezing dark:

- Bryce Canyon City puts you 5–10 min from the rim. That is the entire product.
- Tropic costs 25–30 min door-to-viewpoint each way, i.e. ~50 min of pre-dawn and post-hike
  driving, in exchange for a real (if tiny) town, ~6 °F warmer nights, and genuine price
  competition among ~a dozen properties. **Tropic is the right call if the Ruby's monopoly
  price comes back high or sold out** — it is the only one of the three with actual choice.
- **Panguitch is the wrong call and the reason is counter-intuitive:** it is the furthest
  (30–70 min, unresolved) *and* reportedly the most expensive in October (~$142 typical).
  It has the best town character of the three, which buys you nothing on a night whose only
  job is to end at 05:30.
- Bryce Canyon Lodge buys you almost nothing over Bryce Canyon City — you are already
  adjacent to the entrance — and carries an unresolved closing-date risk. Skip it.

**The cold is the real planning item, not the town.** NPS's own figure: rim temperatures fall
below freezing nearly every night from October, October snowfall normal is 3 in, and
"snowstorms in October are not unusual" (https://www.nps.gov/brca/planyourvisit/weather.htm).
The amphitheatre rim is 8,000–9,100 ft. Sunrise viewing means dressing for the 20s °F
regardless of which of the three towns you slept in. On option (a) (Bryce ~23–24 Sep 2027)
this is milder; on a mid-October date it is a genuine winter morning.

**Booking urgency: MEDIUM.** Driven by Ruby's dominance, not by demand alone.

## VERDICT — Page vs Big Water vs Kanab

**Page, and there is no real argument.** Everything the two nights exist for is hubbed there:
Horseshoe Bend is 5.5 mi, the Antelope Canyon tour operators are based in Page, and
Wahweap/Lake Powell access is at the town's edge.

- **Big Water UT** is a legitimate cheaper alternative — 8.7 mi to Lake Powell, 19 mi / ~30 min
  to Horseshoe Bend (https://www.hotels.com/ho713664992/rodeway-inn-suites-big-water-antelope-canyon-big-water-united-states-of-america/)
  — but Antelope Canyon tour check-in windows are strict and unforgiving, and adding 30 min of
  exposure to that is a bad trade for a modest saving.
- **Kanab** is 1h+ away and belongs to the Zion/Grand Staircase cluster, not this one.
- Page's "soulless" reputation is factually founded (planned 1957 dam-worker town, not an
  organic settlement). Accept it — you are buying a supermarket, chain hotels, and 10 minutes
  to Horseshoe Bend at golden hour.

**Booking urgency: LOW-MEDIUM.** Page has real chain inventory. Antelope Canyon tours, not the
hotel, are the thing that sells out.

  On-site restaurant Duke's, 17:00–21:00 (https://desertroseinn.com/dining/) — this is the
  "better restaurants" claim, and it holds.

## Navajo Nation constraints (apply to The View, Goulding's, Kayenta)

- **Alcohol is prohibited across the Navajo Nation**, including inside Monument Valley Tribal
  Park ("Consumption and/or possession of alcoholic beverages or illegal drugs are prohibited")
  (https://navajonationparks.org/navajo-tribal-parks/monument-valley/) and at Goulding's
  specifically (https://gouldings.com/faqs/). Mexican Hat and Bluff are unaffected.
- **Navajo Nation sales tax 6%**, in effect since 2018, on retail sales on the Nation
  (https://www.taxconnex.com/blog-/navajo-nation-sales-tax-increase).
  GAP: whether a separate, higher hotel-occupancy tax stacks on top of the 6% —
  tax.navajo-nsn.gov/sales_tax.htm 404'd. Assume 6% is the floor.
- **Gate hours constrain sunrise.** Valley Drive is officially 08:00–17:00, with last entry
  variously given as 14:30 or 16:30 summer / 14:30 winter (sources disagree on last entry;
  they agree on the 08:00 opening). **General admission does not open early enough for
  sunrise on the loop road.** The View's own reservations page says hotel guests retain
  "access to the hotel and the ability to access the Valley Drive via Locally Owned Navajo
  guided tours" during closures (https://monumentvalleyview.com/reservations/) — i.e. a
  guided tour, not general admission, is the mechanism for pre-08:00 valley access.

## VERDICT — The View vs Goulding's vs Kayenta vs Mexican Hat vs Bluff

**The View, for exactly this one night, and the reason is the gate hours — not the view.**

The plan is a sunset at The View plus a sunrise the next morning. Because Valley Drive does
not open until 08:00, a non-guest cannot legally be inside the park at sunrise. Staying at
The View is the only way to have the Mittens in front of you at both golden hours without
either buying a guided tour or standing at a roadside pullout. You are paying for gate-time
immunity, not for a balcony.

- **If the rate resolves at the high end (~$465–500 + $20 amenity fee),** Goulding's is a
  defensible downgrade — 10 min away, has a grocery, historic, roughly a third to a half the
  price — but it reintroduces the exact gate-timing problem you were paying to avoid, and
  the sunset view from Goulding's is of the buttes from the side, not head-on.
- **Kayenta and Mexican Hat** save more but cost 25–30 min each way in the dark, twice. Bad
  trade against a single sunset/sunrise pairing.
- **Mexican Hat's one real advantage** is that it is at the bottom of the Moki Dugway, which
  is the next morning's route to Natural Bridges. If the sunrise is sacrificed, Mexican Hat
  is the efficient sleep. It is also where you can buy a beer.
- **Bluff is wrong for a Monument-Valley-anchored night** (45–60 min each way) despite the
  best food of the five. It only makes sense if Monument Valley becomes a mid-day stop.

**Booking urgency: HIGHEST OF THE TRIP alongside in-park Yellowstone.** The View is small,
tribally owned, and described as perpetually sold out. Book it the moment dates are fixed.

- **Downtown Main Street** is the noise epicentre and buys nothing over a north-of-town position.
- **North of town on US-191**, toward the Arches turnoff, is the best compromise: shortest
  commute to both parks, off the Main Street ATV corridor, and inside the ordinary

the opposite of the Colorado bases where October is the cheapest. If budget flexes anywhere,
it flexes here.

### Ouray sun loss — the headline item for this base

**GAP: no source found that numerically states "Ouray loses direct sun at X:XX pm."**
What is sourced: Ouray's astronomical sunset window from sunrise-sunset.org
(https://sunrise-sunset.org/us/ouray-co), and separately, coverage of Box Cañon Falls noting
that "mid-day light may produce deep shadows within the gorge" because narrow canyon walls
limit natural light (https://visitfourcorners.com/box-canyon-falls-park/) — that describes the
falls slot, not the town.

**DERIVED (method stated, not sourced):** Ouray's floor is 7,790 ft; the walls rise to
11,000–13,000 ft within 1–2 miles. Taking a representative 3,000 ft rise over 1.2 mi
(6,340 ft horizontal) gives an apparent ridge angle of ~25°. At 38°N in early October the sun
descends ~10–11°/hour near the horizon. That puts direct sun off the town floor roughly
**2 to 2.5 hours before astronomical sunset** — so with sunset around 18:52 MDT in early
October, expect the town to be in full shadow from **~16:15–16:45**. Treat as an
order-of-magnitude estimate, not a measurement. The practical consequences are real: evening
photography in town is over by mid-afternoon, temperatures drop fast once the walls take the
sun, and any Yankee Boy Basin or Blue Lakes outing needs to be timed to come *down* into an
already-dark valley.

## Altitude — the real issue at this base

- **CDC Yellow Book 2026:** avoid going directly from low elevation to a sleeping elevation
  above 9,000 ft in one day; acclimatising 2–3 nights at 8,000–9,000 ft is "markedly
  protective" against AMS; full acclimatisation takes 3–5 days; avoid alcohol and heavy
  exertion in the first 48 h at altitude
  (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK620901/,
  https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK620901/box/altitude.box1/?report=objectonly)
- Local Summit County sources: ~50% of visitors get some altitude symptoms, ~20% meet the
  clinical AMS definition (headache, dizziness, nausea, fatigue, poor sleep), mostly in the
  first 24–48 h; recommend 3–4 days to acclimatise, ~100 oz / 3 L water per day, limit
  alcohol, consider acetazolamide after consulting a physician
  (https://www.thebrecklife.com/blog/breckenridge-altitude/,
  https://highcountryoxygen.com/altitude-sickness/,
  https://www.summitconcierge.com/breckenridge-colorado/breckenridge-colorado-high-alpine-health-atitude-sickness-acute-mountain-sickness_id-173.html)

**This itinerary's specific position:** you arrive from Ouray (~7,790 ft), which is itself
inside the CDC's protective 8,000–9,000 ft band, after 2 nights there and 1–2 in Durango
(6,512 ft). That is real partial acclimatisation, not a sea-level jump. But Breckenridge is
still ~1,800 ft higher than Ouray. Net: moderate, real risk concentrated on the first
Breckenridge night. The actionable items are hydration and no alcohol on night 1.

## Mud season — Breckenridge in early-to-mid October is substantially shut

Colorado mountain towns run "mud season" twice a year, and the autumn one runs roughly
**October into November**, with restaurants cutting hours or closing outright and the town
running discount "dining passport" promotions to survive the lull. Unlike Aspen or Vail,
Breckenridge publishes no list of what is open
(https://www.cbsnews.com/colorado/news/breckenridge-businesses-colorado-mountains-mud-season-deals/,
https://www.luckymountainhome.com/neighborhoods/breckenridge/activities/mud-season/).
Breckenridge Brewery, Gold Pan Saloon and Downstairs at Eric's are cited as reliably open
through it; others close for weeks.

**GAP: whether the resort runs any lift/gondola operation in early-mid October.** Summer scenic
operations typically end by early-mid September and ski season does not start until ~mid-November,
so **assume lifts are closed** unless breckenridge.com says otherwise. Verify directly.

On option (a) the Breckenridge nights land ~5–6 Oct 2027 — squarely in mud season. On option
(b) they land ~25–26 Sep 2028, which is *before* mud season and is materially better.

## VERDICT — the 3-night Yellowstone shape

**Do not do three different hotels in three nights.** Yellowstone's Grand Loop is a figure-8
and every base is 30–60 min from the next. Three moves means three pack/unpacks and three
check-ins in a park with no cell service to sort a problem.

**Recommended shape, given the constraints established above:**

**Night 1 — Old Faithful Snow Lodge** (7,349 ft). Not the Inn: the Inn closes 11 Oct 2027 and
Snow Lodge runs to 31 Oct 2027
(https://www.yellowstonenationalparklodges.com/stay/plan/opening-and-closing-dates/).
Being on site at Old Faithful is the whole point — you get the geyser basin at dawn and dusk
without the drive, and Grand Prismatic (Midway Geyser Basin) is 10 min north.

**Nights 2 and 3 — Canyon Lodge** (open to 31 Oct 2027; Wi-Fi in rooms; four dining outlets;
the most central base in the park). From Canyon:
- Grand Canyon of the Yellowstone and Artist Point: minutes
- Hayden Valley: ~10 min south — the best wildlife density in the park in the rut
- Lake Village: 35 min
- Old Faithful: ~1h40m
- Mammoth via Norris: ~1h15m
- Lamar Valley: ~1h50m via Dunraven, ~2h15m if Dunraven has closed

**Why not Grant Village:** it closes **10 Oct 2027**, so on option (a) it is simply unavailable.
Even when open it is the weakest base — 45 min from Lake, 40 min from Old Faithful, and it
serves West Thumb, which is a 30-minute stop, not a day.

**Why not Mammoth or Gardiner for one of the three:** two reasons, one of which is decisive.
1. It is the far north end of the figure-8. Mammoth → Old Faithful is ~1h30m and Mammoth →
   Canyon is ~1h15m, so a Mammoth night makes everything else further.
2. **The exit.** Gardiner → SLC is 6h33m versus West Yellowstone → SLC at 4h59m. BRAINSTORM
   day 24 budgets 5h30 *and* a Stansbury Island sunset. Ending in the north makes that day
   impossible.

**The honest counter-argument for Mammoth/Gardiner:** BRAINSTORM day 23 is Lamar Valley
wildlife plus the Mammoth terraces, and Lamar is genuinely 1 h from Mammoth versus ~2 h from
Canyon. If Lamar Valley wolves/bison at dawn are a must-do rather than a nice-to-have, the
answer is **Canyon ×2 + Mammoth ×1, then accept a 6h33m exit day and drop the Stansbury Island
sunset** — or, better, restructure so the Lamar day is a long out-and-back from Canyon and the
exit still runs west.

**In-park vs gateway town, honestly:**
- **In-park wins here, clearly.** The reasons are wildlife (Hayden and Lamar are dawn/dusk
  activities and you cannot be there at dawn from West Yellowstone — it is 30 min to Madison
  before you have even started), the geyser basins at first light, and avoiding two 30–60 min
  entrance-gate commutes per day.
- The costs are real: 13-month booking, no TV, no A/C, unreliable cell, slow Wi-Fi, and
  higher prices.
- **If in-park is sold out, take West Yellowstone over Gardiner** for this itinerary —
  it is closer to Old Faithful and Grand Prismatic, and it is 1h34m closer to SLC on the exit.
  Accept that it is 13 °F colder overnight and pay 12% tax instead of 11%.
- **Gardiner is the better town** (warmer, smaller, right at Mammoth, on the only year-round
  road) but it is on the wrong side of the park for this route.

**Booking urgency: HIGHEST, tied with The View.** Canyon Lodge and Old Faithful Snow Lodge in
autumn are the hardest rooms on this trip. **The booking date is 5 October 2026 for
October 2027, or 5 September 2026 for September 2027 (i.e. today).**
