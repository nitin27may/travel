## 1. IS 27 JULY – 10 AUGUST THE RIGHT WINDOW?

### 1.1 Snow line and lingering snowfields at 2,500–3,000 m

**Verified data points:**
- Jungfraujoch (3,580 m) 1991–2020 normals: July mean daily max **+3.4 °C**, mean daily min **−2.1 °C**; August max **+3.5 °C**, min **−1.7 °C**. Daily mean is barely above freezing at 3,500 m in high summer. [V] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jungfraujoch
- Implication: the freezing level in late July / early August typically sits around **3,500–4,000 m** in fair weather, which is why 2,500–3,000 m walking terrain is normally snow-free by then. Residual snow persists only in **north/north-east-facing gullies, couloirs and the lee side of cols**.

**Consensus (secondary, not primary):**
- "High passes typically clear from early July… by July most of the snow is gone, the huts are open, and the routes behave." [S] https://huttohuthikingswitzerland.com/blog/hiking-switzerland-summer
- Haute Route month-by-month guide places the reliable window as **mid-July to mid-September**, with June/early July still snow-affected on the high cols. [S] https://huttohuthikingswitzerland.com/haute-route-weather-guide

**Routes commonly still snow-affected in late July / early August:**
- **[GAP]** I could not verify a route-by-route, year-independent snow-persistence list from an authoritative Swiss source (SAC, Schweizer Wanderwege, cantonal). Treat the following as *categories to check live*, not as verified claims:
  - Walker's Haute Route: **Col de Prafleuri (2,987 m)**, **Col de Riedmatten (2,919 m)** / Pas de Chèvres, **Col de Torrent (2,916 m)**, **Augstbordpass (2,894 m)** — north-side descents hold snow patches longest.
  - Anything crossing a **glacier snout or lateral moraine** (Aletsch approaches, Trift, Zmutt, Findel, Gorner) — not "snow" but bare, unstable, retreating ice with rerouted paths.
  - **Hohtürli (2,778 m)** above Kandersteg (Blüemlisalphütte) — steep north side, snow-plastered into July in heavy years.
  - **Furggji / Höhbalmen** and the Europaweg high traverses — rockfall rather than snow is the governing hazard.
- **How to verify two days out:** SLF snow maps + individual hut condition reports (§4).

**Verdict on snow:** late July / early August is **objectively the safest week of the year for high-level walking**. Snow is not the argument against this window. Crowds and price are.

### 1.2 Alpine wildflowers — is early August past peak?

- Jungfrau Region tourism (official): at **1,500–2,500 m** "gentians, alpine asters and many other flowers unfold their full splendour"; the alpine bloom is a **July–August** phenomenon. [S/official-tourism] https://jungfrauregion.swiss/en/destination/magazine/detail/blooming-diversity-the-fascinating-flora-of-the-jungfrau-region.html
- The bloom **migrates uphill through the season**: mid-June ≈ up to 1,000 m; early–mid July ≈ 2,000 m+ for gentians and edelweiss; flowers persist to about early September at altitude. [S] https://www.tripadvisor.com/ShowTopic-g188082-i7944-k3417734-Wildflowers_in_the_Berner_Oberland_Is_June_or_July_best-Jungfrau_Region_Bernese_Oberland_Ca.html ; https://swissfamilyfun.com/wildflower-hikes-switzerland/

**Plain answer:** for **valley and mid-altitude hay meadows (600–1,500 m)** — yes, 27 July–10 Aug is **past peak**; those meadows are cut for hay by mid-July. For **2,000–2,600 m** — you are at or just past peak, still good: gentians, alpine aster, thistle, arnica, alpenrose finishing. For a *wildflower-led* trip you want **mid-June to mid-July**. For a *high-route-led* trip you want exactly this window. You cannot have both.
**[GAP]** No phenological dataset (e.g. MeteoSwiss PhenoNet flowering-date normals by altitude) verified. Worth pulling from https://www.meteoswiss.admin.ch/climate/ if precision matters.

### 1.3 SAC and private hut opening seasons

- **~120 SAC huts are staffed ("bewartet") from roughly mid-June to roughly mid-October.** Opening/closing dates vary hut by hut and can shift at short notice at the season edges depending on weather. [V] https://www.sac-cas.ch/de/huetten-und-touren/sac-huetten/huetten-informationen/
- Worked example: **Domhütte (Randa)** staffed 25 June – 13 September (2026 season). [V] https://domhuette.ch/huetteninformationen/oeffnungszeiten
- **Berghotel Faulhorn (2,681 m)**, Grindelwald–Schynige Platte ridge: "open continuously from Saturday 20 June"; rooms plus a Touristenlager dormitory. Closing date not stated on the page. [V] https://www.faulhorn.ch/
- Hut search / booking portal: **SAC Route Portal, filtered to huts** — https://www.sac-cas.ch/en/huts-and-tours/sac-route-portal/?type=hut [V, resolves]

**Verdict:** 27 July – 10 August is **inside the fully-staffed core** for every SAC and private hut. No opening-date risk at all. The risk is the opposite — **availability**. Popular huts (Blüemlisalp, Monte Rosa, Cabane du Mont Fort, Schönbiel, Rothorn) are booked out months ahead for this fortnight.
**[GAP]** Individual hut season dates for **2027** are not yet published (SAC huts publish in spring). Re-check ~March 2027.

**Verdict:** Swiss operators schedule revisions in **April–May and late October–November**, precisely to avoid this fortnight. **Nothing material is normally shut 27 Jul – 10 Aug.** That is a genuine, concrete argument *for* the window.

### 1.5 Head-to-head: this window vs. mid-June, September, early October

Scoring 1 (worst) – 5 (best). Ratings are my synthesis of the sourced facts above; the underlying facts are cited, the ratings are judgement.

| Criterion | Mid-June | **27 Jul – 10 Aug** | September | Early October |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| High-route snow clearance | 2 (cols still snowy) | **5** | 5 | 4 (new snow possible) |
| Hut operation | 3 (many just opening) | **5** | 5 | 2 (closing mid-Oct) |
| Lift/cogwheel operation | 3 (revisions still on; BLM shut to 10 Jul in 2026) | **5** | 5 | 3 (autumn revisions start) |
| Wildflowers | **5** at 1,000–1,800 m | 3 (past peak low, at peak 2,200 m+) | 2 | 1 |
| Weather stability | 3 | 3 (warm but convective) | **4** (most stable, driest anticyclonic spells) | 3 |
| Air clarity / haze | 2 | 2 (worst — heat haze, Saharan dust, convective murk) | 4 | **5** |
| Crowds | 3 | **1 — worst of the year** | 3 | 4 |
| Price | 3 | **1 — peak of peak** | 3 | 4 |
| Accommodation availability | 3 | **1** | 3 | 4 |
| Daylight length | **5** (15h20) | 4 (15h05 → 14h28) | 3 | 2 |
| Autumn larch colour | 1 | 1 | 2 | **5** |

**Objectively best overall: the first three weeks of September.** You keep full hut and lift operation, you keep snow-free high routes, you gain the most stable synoptic weather and better air clarity, and you lose the school-holiday load and the August pricing. You lose wildflowers (already largely gone) and ~1 h of daylight.

**Where 27 Jul – 10 Aug wins:**
1. Maximum daylight — 15:05 h on 27 July at Interlaken vs ~12:30 h in late September. [C]
2. Everything is open and running; zero revision risk (§1.4).
3. Warmest high-altitude temperatures — the only window where a 3,000 m bivouac/hut night is genuinely comfortable and glacier-adjacent terrain is at maximum accessibility.
4. Alpine flora at 2,200 m+ is still on.
5. Swiss National Day (1 Aug) — alphorns, bonfires, village fêtes, fireworks. A cultural asset, if you want it. https://www.myswitzerland.com/en-us/experiences/events/august-1-national-day/
6. Lake and river swimming (Caumasee, Verzasca, Lake Thun) is only pleasant now.

**Where it loses — and yes, this is the most crowded and most expensive window of the year:**
1. Every major European source market is simultaneously on holiday (§2.1). This is the only fortnight of the year where that is true.
2. Swiss National Day + the 31 Jul/1 Aug weekend lands **mid-trip**.
3. Peak accommodation pricing; "busiest and most expensive month". [S] https://www.tour-switzerland.ch/guides/best-time-to-visit/
4. Afternoon convective storms are at their annual maximum (§3).
5. Worst air clarity of the season for long-lens mountain photography.
6. Mandatory reservation systems (Jungfraujoch, Oeschinensee) are all in force precisely in this window.

**Honest recommendation to the synthesiser:** if the dates are movable, **shift to 1–15 September 2027** and you improve on almost every axis. If they are not movable (school-age travel, fixed leave), the window is workable but the itinerary must be built around *avoidance* — sleep high, move early, and treat 31 July – 2 August as a fixed-in-place, no-transit block.

### 2.2 Swiss National Day — Sunday 1 August 2027

- **1 August is Switzerland's national holiday**, a federal public holiday. [V] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_National_Day
- **In 2027 it falls on a Sunday**, creating a natural three-day weekend Fri 30 Jul – Sun 1 Aug, with Monday 2 August frequently taken as a bridge day. [C from calendar]
- Official events overview: https://www.myswitzerland.com/en-us/experiences/events/august-1-national-day/ [V]
- Interlaken's official 1 August programme page: https://www.interlaken.swiss/en/planning/events/top-events/1-august-in-interlaken [V]
- Grindelwald's: https://grindelwald.swiss/en/detail-events/swiss-national-day-6a6c2940-8c43-4ba8-8f3b-e60b927d6324.html [V]

**Operational consequences (secondary source, consistent across guides):** [S] https://www.tour-switzerland.ch/blog/swiss-national-day/
- **Trains run a Sunday/holiday timetable** on 1 August. Fewer services, and the ones that run are loaded.
- **Shops and many non-tourist restaurants close.** Supermarkets shut. Buy food on 31 July.
- Fireworks: municipal displays draw very large crowds; lakefront towns (Interlaken, Thun, Brienz, Lucerne, Zurich) are the worst.
- Accommodation: 31 Jul – 1 Aug sells out completely; guides advise booking **4–6 months ahead** for popular destinations, and note that the 1 August weekend and the Zurich Street Parade weekend are the two that "sell out completely". [S] https://www.tour-switzerland.ch/guides/best-time-to-visit/

**[GAP]** No verified quantitative price-index data (e.g. average ADR uplift for 31 Jul/1 Aug vs. a mid-July baseline). Would need STR/hotelleriesuisse data or a booking-site scrape.

**Actionable:** do **not** schedule a base change, a long transfer, or an airport day on 31 July, 1 August or 2 August 2027. Be stationary in one village. Book that block first, before anything else in the trip.

### 2.3 French and Italian August exodus

- **France:** the summer departure system is managed by **Bison Futé** (Ministry of Transport). The peak movements are the *chassé-croisé* weekends — the **last weekend of July** (juillettistes returning / aoûtiens departing) and the **first weekend of August**. In 2027 these are **Sat 31 July** and **Sat 7 August**, both inside the trip. Live forecast calendar: https://www.bison-fute.gouv.fr/ **[GAP — 2027 Bison Futé calendar not yet published; typically issued in June.]**
- **Italy:** the *esodo estivo* begins the **last weekend of July** and peaks in the **first ten days of August**, with **Ferragosto (15 August)** as the second peak. Italian traffic advisories come from Autostrade per l'Italia / Viabilità Italia: https://www.autostrade.it/en/ **[GAP — 2027 esodo calendar unpublished.]**
- **Practical impact on Switzerland:** north–south transit peaks. The **Gotthard road tunnel** queue northbound/southbound and the **Simplon** and **Grand-St-Bernard** approaches are the pinch points. Live queue data: https://www.tcs.ch/en/ and the Swiss road status https://www.tcs.ch/en/travel-camping/traffic-information/ **[GAP — did not verify these exact URLs; the authoritative source is https://www.astra.admin.ch/ and viasuisse.]**
- Rail equivalent: Milan–Zurich and Milan–Geneva EuroCity trains and the Gotthard Base Tunnel corridor run full on those weekends.

### 2.4 Site-by-site crowd patterns and mitigations

Hour-by-hour patterns below are the standard operator/tourism guidance plus inference from published lift hours. Where an hour is inferred from published first-lift times rather than from a crowd study, it is marked `[inferred]`. **[GAP]** — no site publishes real visitor-count-by-hour data; there is no Swiss equivalent of a footfall API.

**Jungfraujoch (3,454 m)**
- Peak load: **10:30–14:30**, driven by group/coach arrivals connecting off morning trains from Interlaken and Lucerne.
- **Seat reservation is mandatory 1 May – 31 Oct**, CHF 10/person; without one "the Jungfrau Railway may well be full at peak times and you will have to wait for the next train". [V] https://www.jungfrau.ch/en-gb/jungfraujoch-top-of-europe/
- Return ticket from **CHF 129.60**. A **"Good Morning Ticket" (May–Oct) gives 15% off** for the earliest departures. [V] same URL
- **Mitigation:** book the first available slot, take the Good Morning Ticket, be off the summit by 11:00. Priority boarding is sold separately: https://www.jungfrau.ch/en-gb/jungfraujoch-top-of-europe/book-priority-boarding/
- **Alternative that is 90% of the view for 10% of the crowd:** Schilthorn/Birg or Schynige Platte.

**Oeschinensee (1,578 m)**
- **Timed-slot gondola booking mandatory 20 June – 20 September.** Online sales for same-day travel close at **06:00**. On-site machines carry only a "very limited number of remaining tickets". If your slot is full, "transportation is not possible." [V] https://www.oeschinensee.ch/en/anreise/
- Gondola runs **08:30–18:00**. [V] https://www.oeschinensee.ch/en/
- **Car park fills by ~11:00**; premium reserved parking CHF 10 but **entry required by 09:30**. Standard parking: CHF 9 (up to 4 h, cable-car users) / CHF 20 (hikers); campers over 2 m CHF 60. [V] https://www.oeschinensee.ch/en/anreise/
- Public transport: train to Kandersteg; **June–October a local bus meets every train** between station and valley station; 10–15 min walk otherwise. [V] same URL
- **Mitigation:** book the 08:30 slot; or skip the gondola entirely and **walk up from Kandersteg (~1 h 20)** — no slot needed for walkers, and you arrive before the gondola crowd. Best light is morning anyway (§5).

**Bachalpsee (2,265 m), Grindelwald–First**
- First gondola season 13.04–25.10; the lake is a **~50 min walk** from First station. [V] https://www.jungfrau.ch/en-gb/grindelwaldfirst/
- **[GAP]** exact first/last gondola departure times for summer not retrieved; check https://www.jungfrau.ch/en-gb/live/operating-info/
- Load pattern `[inferred from lift hours]`: empty until the first gondola lands walkers at the lake ~09:00; solid procession **09:30–16:00**; the Cliff Walk and mountain carts keep First station itself busy all day.
- **Mitigation:** sleep at **Berghotel Faulhorn (2,681 m)** — open continuously from 20 June, rooms + dormitory [V] https://www.faulhorn.ch/ — and walk down to Bachalpsee for sunrise (~45 min). This is the single best crowd-and-light play in the Bernese Oberland.

**Five Lakes Walk / 5-Seenweg, Zermatt (Blauherd → Sunnegga)**
- **[GAP]** — the official Zermatt page (https://zermatt.swiss/en/Media/Attractions/5-Lakes-Walk) returned 404 on fetch; content not verified. Known structure: five lakes (Stellisee, Grindjisee, Grünsee, Moosjisee, Leisee), typically walked downhill from Blauherd (2,571 m) to Sunnegga (2,288 m), ~2–2.5 h. **Stellisee and Grindjisee** carry the Matterhorn reflection; Grünsee and Moosjisee do not.

### 3.2 What percentage of days are wet?

Derived directly from the precip-day normals above `[C]`:
- **Interlaken / Bernese Oberland: 12.8 precip days in a 31-day month ≈ 41% of days record measurable precipitation.**
- **Zermatt: 9.0–9.8 days ≈ 29–32% of days.** Zermatt sits in the driest inner-alpine valley in Switzerland (annual precipitation under 650 mm), which is a real argument for weighting the itinerary towards Valais.
- **Engadin/St. Moritz: 10.6–11.0 days ≈ 34–36%.**
- **Lugano: 8.2–9.6 days ≈ 26–31% — but the totals are the highest in the table (≈158 mm/month).** Ticino gets fewer, much heavier rain events: intense convective downpours, not drizzle.

**Important caveat:** a "precipitation day" is ≥1.0 mm in 24 h. In this window the typical wet day is a **dry morning followed by a 2–4 h afternoon thunderstorm**, not a washout. For planning purposes: expect roughly **4–6 of your 14 days to be affected by precipitation in the Oberland**, and **3–5 in Valais** — but expect **most of those days to have a usable morning**.

### 3.3 The afternoon thunderstorm pattern

- MeteoSwiss maintains a dedicated body of work on Swiss thunderstorm climatology and lightning frequency: https://www.meteoswiss.admin.ch/weather/weather-and-climate-from-a-to-z/thunderstorms/thunderstorm-and-lightning-frequency-in-switzerland.html and https://www.meteoswiss.admin.ch/weather/weather-and-climate-from-a-to-z/thunderstorms/how-thunderstorms-form.html and the warning methodology at https://www.meteoswiss.admin.ch/weather/weather-and-climate-from-a-to-z/thunderstorms/thunderstorm-warnings.html [V — pages exist]
- **[GAP]** The numerical content (thunderstorm days per summer month, diurnal peak hour by region) did not render for automated fetch. The pages are the right primary source; a human should open them and lift the figures.
- **Well-established pattern (state as pattern, not as a MeteoSwiss-sourced number):** July–August is the annual maximum for convective activity in the Alps. Storms are driven by daytime heating: cumulus builds from late morning, first cells fire between **13:00 and 15:00**, peak activity **15:00–19:00**, decaying after sunset. **Ticino and the southern Alpine slope (Lugano, Verzasca) are the most thunderstorm-prone region in Switzerland**, consistent with Lugano's 158 mm/month on only 8–10 precipitation days.
- **Operational rule for this trip: be off exposed ridges, summits, via ferrata and glaciers by 13:00.** Plan every high day as a 05:30–13:00 day. This dovetails perfectly with the crowd-avoidance rule and the photography rule — they all say the same thing: *start absurdly early*.
- Also note **MeteoSwiss forest-fire danger** in Valais and Ticino in a dry August: https://www.meteoswiss.admin.ch/weather/hazards/explanation-of-the-danger-levels/forest-fire.html [V] — open fires (including BBQ) get banned in dry spells, which affects 1 August bonfires.

### 3.4 Temperature at 3,000 m vs. valley floor

- **Jungfraujoch (3,580 m), August: mean daily max +3.5 °C, mean daily min −1.7 °C.** [V] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jungfraujoch
- **Interlaken (577 m), August: mean daily max 23.7 °C.** [V]
- **Difference ≈ 20 °C over 3,000 m of ascent ≈ 0.67 °C per 100 m** `[C]` — a slightly steeper-than-standard lapse rate, consistent with the free-atmosphere value.
- **Interpolating to 3,000 m: mean daily max ≈ +7 °C, mean daily min ≈ +1 °C.** At **2,500 m** (Bachalpsee, Stellisee, Fluhalp, Faulhorn): mean daily max ≈ **+10 °C**, min ≈ **+3 °C**. `[C]`
- **Wind chill and radiative cooling are the real story at dawn.** A pre-sunrise wait at Stellisee (2,537 m) or Riffelsee (2,757 m) in early August will feel like **0 to +3 °C with any breeze**. On the Jungfraujoch terrace at 08:00 it can be **−5 °C** with wind.

### 3.5 The heat question — how hot do the valleys and cities get?

- **Lugano August mean daily max 26.8 °C** — but that is the *mean*. Ticino regularly exceeds **32–34 °C** in a heat spell, with **17.8 °C** overnight minima and high humidity. This is genuinely unpleasant. [V] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lugano
- **Interlaken/Bernese Oberland valley floor: mean max 23.7 °C**, but Föhn and heatwave days push **30–33 °C**. [V]
- Swiss cities (Basel, Zurich, Geneva, Bern) run **hotter than the alpine valleys** and hold heat overnight. Swiss buildings are **largely un-air-conditioned**, including many hotels. This is the single most under-appreciated comfort risk of an early-August Switzerland trip.
- MeteoSwiss issues formal **heatwave warnings**: https://www.meteoswiss.admin.ch/weather/hazards/explanation-of-the-danger-levels/heatwave.html and https://www.meteoswiss.admin.ch/weather/weather-and-climate-from-a-to-z/heat-warnings.html [V]

**Answer: yes, staying high is the correct heat strategy.** Sleeping at **1,600–2,000 m** (Mürren 1,638 m, Wengen 1,274 m, Zermatt 1,608 m, Riederalp 1,925 m, Grindelwald 1,034 m) gives you overnight minima of **8–13 °C** and comfortable sleep without air conditioning, versus 17–20 °C nights in Lugano or Interlaken. It also puts you above the crowd-delivery infrastructure. Structure the trip as **high bases + day descents**, not the reverse. Put Ticino at the start or end and keep it short, or skip it and use the Engadin instead.

### 3.6 Packing list (driven by the numbers above)

- **Layers for a 25 °C swing in one day.** Valley 24 °C to 2,600 m dawn at +3 °C with wind.
- Hard shell (waterproof, hooded) + light insulated midlayer (synthetic or down sweater) — non-negotiable, for dawn waits and afternoon storms.
- **Gloves and a beanie.** Yes, in August. For 05:30 at Stellisee and for the Jungfraujoch terrace.
- Sun protection at extremes: **SPF 50, lip balm with SPF, category 3–4 sunglasses, brimmed hat.** UV at 3,000 m in August is the strongest you will encounter in Europe.
- **Microspikes: not required** for marked hiking trails in this window `[inferred from §1.1]`. Required only if you are doing glacier or high-col alpinism, in which case you need a full glacier kit and a guide.
- Poles — for the long descents (Faulhorn, Oeschinensee, Aletsch).
- **1.5–2 L water capacity** minimum; Swiss village fountains are potable and refill is easy.
- **Swimwear and a quick-dry towel** — Caumasee, Blausee, Verzasca, Lake Thun, Oeschinensee.
- Cash: CHF 5 coins for Iseltwald's turnstile, CHF 1 for pay toilets, cash for some huts.
- **Earplugs and a fan/eye mask** if any night is spent at low altitude in early August.
- Headtorch — mandatory for every pre-dawn approach in §5.

### 5.3 Spot-by-spot: light, position, approach

**1. Matterhorn from Stellisee (2,537 m)**
- **SUNRISE.** The classic. Matterhorn is roughly **W/WSW** of the lake; sun rises at **63° (ENE)** and rim-lights then floods the east face. Reflection needs still water — dawn is also the calmest hour.
- Exact spot: north-west shore of Stellisee, low to the water, ~30 m from the standard path.
- **Approach:** from **Fluhalp** ~15 min. From **Blauherd** (first lift ~08:30, **too late**) ~20 min. From **Sunnegga** ~1 h 15. From **Zermatt village on foot: 2 h 45 – 3 h**, so a **03:15 departure** for a 06:12 sunrise on 3 Aug.
- **Requires sleeping high** (Fluhalp) or a 3 h headtorch walk.

**2. Matterhorn from Riffelsee (2,757 m, at Rotenboden)**
- **SUNRISE.** Tightest, most graphic reflection; a small pond, two positions, both crowded by 09:00.
- Exact spot: the upper (northern) of the two pools, 5–10 min walk downhill/west of **Rotenboden** station.
- **Approach:** Gornergrat Bahn **first train 07:00** from Zermatt [V] → Rotenboden ~07:25 → lake ~07:35. **That is 1h23 after sunrise on 3 Aug.** You get good side light but not the alpenglow.
- **To actually get sunrise:** sleep at **Kulmhotel Gornergrat (3,100 m)** and walk down ~30 min, or **Riffelhaus 1853 (2,582 m)** and walk up ~45 min. [V] URLs in §5.2.
- Also works at **SUNSET** — the Matterhorn's east face goes into shadow but the summit holds warm light; fewer people.

**3. Zermatt Kirchbrücke (Bahnhofstrasse church bridge)**
- **SUNRISE** for the clean shot (empty street, Matterhorn catching first light at ~06:12 while the village is in blue shade), or **SUNSET/blue hour** for the lit-shopfronts-plus-alpenglow version.
- Exact spot: standing on the Kirchbrücke over the Matter Vispa, looking south down the river.
- **Approach: 5–10 min walk from anywhere in Zermatt.** No lift, no excuse. This is the one sunrise shot in the whole trip that costs you nothing but an alarm clock.
- Best window: **05:40–06:25**.

**4. Bachalpsee (2,265 m) with Schreckhorn and Wetterhorn**
- **SUNRISE.** The peaks are **SE/ESE** of the lake — sun rises at **63°**, so it comes up almost behind/beside them: you get rim-light and a warm sky rather than front-lit faces. **The stronger shot here is actually the last 20 minutes before sunrise (civil dawn, ~05:36)** when the sky glows and the water is glass.
- Alternative: **SUNSET** front-lights the Schreckhorn/Wetterhorn faces from the WNW (297°) — arguably the better light, and far easier logistically.
- Exact spot: the small promontory on the north-east shore of the **lower** of the two basins.
- **Approach:** from **First** ~50 min [V]. First gondola first departure ~08:00 **[GAP]** — too late for sunrise. From **Berghotel Faulhorn** ~45 min downhill. From **Grindelwald on foot** ~3 h 30.
- **Requires sleeping high** (Faulhorn) for sunrise. For sunset, take the last up-gondola, shoot, and **walk down to Bort/Grindelwald in the long twilight** (civil dusk 21:33 on 3 Aug gives you a huge margin).

**5. Oeschinensee (1,578 m)**
- **SUNRISE**, or **overcast**. The Blüemlisalp wall is **S/SSE** of the lake and is front-lit through the middle of the day but blown out; at dawn you get warm light on the summits with the lake in shade — that turquoise reads best under **soft/overcast** light or in shade, not in hard sun.
- Exact spot: the north-west shore near the boat hire, and the elevated path towards Heuberg for the classic elevated composition.
- **Approach:** gondola from Kandersteg **08:30–18:00** with a **mandatory timed slot 20 Jun–20 Sep** [V], then ~20 min walk. **For sunrise you must walk up from Kandersteg (~1 h 20) leaving ~04:40**, or sleep at **Berghotel Oeschinensee**.
- **The walk-up also bypasses the booking system entirely** — walkers do not need a gondola slot.

**6. Eiger north face**
- **SUNRISE** for the north face is a misnomer — a north face never gets direct sun. What you want is either (a) **first light on the Mittellegi ridge and the summit** at ~06:10 from **Kleine Scheidegg**, or (b) **SUNSET/late afternoon** when the west flank and the Rotstock catch warm light and the face itself goes deep blue.
- **Overcast/clearing storm is the best condition of all** for the north face — cloud tearing off the wall is the definitive image.
- Exact spots: **Kleine Scheidegg** (2,061 m) directly below the face; the **Eiger Trail** (Eigergletscher → Alpiglen) for the base-of-the-wall perspective; **Männlichen** for the Eiger–Mönch–Jungfrau trio.
- **Approach:** sleep at **Kleine Scheidegg** (Hotel Bellevue des Alpes) and walk out of the door. Otherwise the first Wengernalpbahn train **[GAP]** is far too late for dawn.
- Eiger Trail route info: https://www.jungfrau.ch/en-gb/kleine-scheidegg/eiger-trail/ [V]

**7. Lauterbrunnen valley and Staubbach Falls**
- **OVERCAST or late afternoon/evening.** In hard midday sun the valley is a contrast disaster — 300 m black cliffs against a white sky. The waterfall is backlit and blows out.
- **Staubbach specifically:** best in **soft light or in the shaded morning**; on a sunny **late afternoon** (roughly 16:00–18:00 in early August) the low sun can produce a **rainbow in the spray** at the base.
- Exact spots: the classic frame is from the **valley road just south of Lauterbrunnen village** looking south with Staubbach on the right; the elevated version is from the **Wengen side / the path to Wengen**, or from **Isenfluh–Sulwald**.
- **Approach:** trivial — walk from Lauterbrunnen station. The Staubbach tunnel gallery behind the falls is a short steep 15-min climb.
- **Note:** flow is snowmelt-fed and is **noticeably weaker in early August** than in June. **[GAP]** no discharge data source verified for Staubbach.

**8. Mürren and the Schilthorn panorama (2,970 m)**
- **SUNRISE** from Schilthorn for the Eiger–Mönch–Jungfrau being front-lit from the ENE across the valley — this is one of the few panoramas where sunrise front-lights rather than backlights the subject. **SUNSET** is also excellent (sun sets WNW behind the Blüemlisalp/Valais peaks).
- **Approach:** cable car from Mürren via Birg. **[GAP]** first departure time not retrieved; Schilthorn timetable at https://schilthorn.ch/en/Infos/Timetable__and__Tariff [V] carries revision dates and confirms **no summer 2027 closure**. Even the earliest cable car is well after sunrise → **Schilthorn sunrise requires the Piz Gloria overnight package if offered, or accept post-08:00 light.**
- **Mürren itself** (1,638 m) is a superb **evening** subject: the village on its shelf with the Jungfrau massif catching alpenglow, shot from the path towards Gimmelwald.

**9. Aletsch Glacier from Moosfluh (2,333 m) and Eggishorn (2,869 m)**
- **SUNRISE** for Moosfluh: the glacier runs roughly **N–S** below you and the Konkordiaplatz/Aletschhorn view faces **east-south-east** — dawn light rakes down the glacier surface and reveals the medial moraine stripes. Midday flattens it completely.
- **Eggishorn** gives the higher, wider, more distant view — better in **early morning** or with **broken cloud** for texture.
- Exact spots: Moosfluh station viewpoint and the ridge path 10–15 min south; Eggishorn summit platform.
- **Approach:** Riederalp–Moosfluh lift **first departure 08:30**; Fiescheralp–Eggishorn **08:55** [V]. Both are ~2.5 h after sunrise. **For dawn: sleep on Riederalp (1,925 m) or Bettmeralp and walk up to Moosfluh (~1 h 15–1 h 30), leaving ~04:30.**
- **Note:** ongoing **construction at Eggishorn** with noise during the operating season. [V]

**10. Creux du Van (Neuchâtel Jura, 1,463 m)**
- **SUNRISE.** The amphitheatre's 160 m limestone cliffs face roughly **north to north-west**, so the *cliff wall opposite you* catches early light while you stand on the rim. Also the classic **sea-of-fog** location — but the inversion fog season is **autumn/winter**, not early August. In August you get clear air and a green bowl.
- Exact spot: the rim path at the **Ferme du Soliat** end, on the fenced edge.
- **Approach:** ~1 h 15 – 1 h 30 walk up from **Noiraigue** (via the Ferme Robert / "Le Single" chain path), or ~20 min from the **Ferme du Soliat** car park (drivable). For sunrise: drive to Le Soliat and be on the rim by 05:30, or sleep at the Ferme du Soliat.
- Portal: https://www.creuxduvan.com/ [V 200]
- **Honest assessment:** in late July / early August this is a good walk but not a top-tier photograph. Save it for October.

**11. Lavaux terraces over Lake Geneva**
- **SUNSET.** The vineyard terraces face **south/south-west** across the lake to the Savoy Alps — golden hour (roughly 19:30–21:00 CEST) front-lights the vines and backlights the lake haze. Sunset azimuth ~297° (WNW) puts the sun down the lake, which is exactly the axis you want.
- Also strong at **blue hour** with the villages lit.
- Exact spots: the terrace path between **Rivaz and Epesses**; the **Dézaley** viewpoint above Épesses; the **Chexbres–Saint-Saphorin** "Corniche" path.
- **Approach:** trivial — train to **Rivaz**, **Epesses**, **Chexbres** or **Grandvaux**; the vineyard paths start at the station. No lift dependency. **This is the one major photo location in the trip with zero early-start problem** — it wants evening.
- **[GAP]** https://lavaux-unesco.ch/en/ returned 404; find the current UNESCO-site portal.

**12. Caumasee (997 m, Flims)**
- **MIDDAY / HIGH SUN or bright overcast.** Counter-intuitively this is *not* a golden-hour subject: the turquoise colour is produced by light penetrating and scattering in the water, and it needs the sun high. Overcast gives a flatter but very saturated teal.
- Exact spot: the elevated viewpoint on the **Conn / Il Spir** side of the path above the lake for the aerial-looking shot; shore level for the boathouse.
- **Approach:** short walk from Flims Waldhaus plus a small lift down to the shore. **[GAP]** — lift hours, bathing fee and parking unverified (https://www.flims.com/en/outdoor-activities/mountain-lakes/lake-cauma returned 404).
- **This is a rare "go at 12:00" site** — which is convenient, because you should be off the high ridges by 13:00 anyway (§3.3).

**13. Landwasser Viaduct (Filisur, 1,100 m)**
- **OVERCAST or soft morning.** The viaduct is in a wooded gorge; hard sun produces unusable dappled contrast. Light drizzle and mist are actively good here.
- Exact spot: the marked **Aussichtspunkt Landwasserviadukt** platform, ~20–25 min walk from **Filisur** station.
- **Approach:** train to Filisur, walk. **Timing is dictated by the train timetable, not the sun** — you need a train on the viaduct. Check https://www.rhb.ch/en/ [V].
- **Mitigation:** shoot a **regional RhB service** rather than the Glacier/Bernina Express — identical composition, no crowd at the viewpoint.
- **[GAP]** viewpoint opening/access and the specific crossing times not verified.

**14. Lavertezzo — Ponte dei Salti (536 m, Valle Verzasca)**
- **OVERCAST, or open shade in early morning / late afternoon.** The gorge is narrow; direct sun creates blown highlights on the pale granite and black water. The emerald water reads best with **no direct sun on the water**, so **before ~09:30** or **after ~17:30**.
- Exact spot: the boulders on the downstream (south) side of the double-arch bridge; also from the bridge itself looking down into the pools.
- **Approach:** PostBus from Locarno (included in the **Ticino Ticket**) [V] — this removes the parking problem entirely. Be there before 08:30.
- **Safety:** river levels can rise rapidly; a "Safe waters" campaign is published by the region. [V] https://www.ascona-locarno.com/en/explore/valle-verzasca
- **[GAP]** Verzasca Parking Card price/rules unverified.

**15. Iseltwald (Lake Brienz, 566 m)**
- **SUNSET / evening.** Lake Brienz runs roughly **WSW–ENE**; from Iseltwald on the south shore you look **north/north-west** across the lake, so evening light hits the far shore and the water goes turquoise-gold. The pier itself is best at **blue hour**.
- Exact spot: the fee-gated pier (**CHF 5** turnstile) [V]; the free alternative is the **Seeuferweg** path east of the village, and the **Giessbach Falls** side across the bay.
- **Approach:** BLS lake steamer from Interlaken Ost, or PostBus. Trivial.
- **Crowd note:** 1,000+ visitors/day at the dock [V] — but almost all are day-trippers gone by 18:00.

**16. Blausee (887 m)**
- **OVERCAST, or early morning shade.** This is the most light-sensitive subject on the list: the famous blue only renders when there is **no direct sun** on the water. A grey morning is ideal. Direct sun = white glare and you have wasted the trip.
- Exact spot: the wooden footbridge at the north end and the boat landing.
- **Approach:** train/bus to Blausee-Mitholz (BLS); **open 365 days/year**, ticketed admission, **parking explicitly limited, public transport recommended**. [V] https://www.blausee.ch/en/
- Be there at opening. **[GAP]** exact opening time and 2027 admission price.

**17. Seealpsee (1,143 m) and Berggasthaus Äscher (1,454 m)**
- **Seealpsee: SUNRISE.** The Säntis massif is **W/SW** of the lake — dawn light hits the cliffs while the lake is glass. Best reflection is in the first 30 min after ~06:00.
- **Äscher: OVERCAST or LATE AFTERNOON.** It is tucked under the Ebenalp cliff facing roughly **WSW**; it is in deep shade all morning and gets direct light only late. Overcast/mist is the atmospheric version and the one that made it famous.
- Exact spots: Seealpsee north-east shore near the Berggasthaus; Äscher from the path 100 m south, looking back at the building under the overhang.
- **Approach:** Wasserauen → Seealpsee on foot **~1 h 15** (leave ~04:40 for sunrise), or sleep at Berggasthaus Seealpsee [V 200] https://www.seealpsee.ch/. Äscher: Ebenalp cable car then ~15 min down; or on foot from Seealpsee ~1 h 15.
- **[GAP]** https://www.aescher-ai.ch/ blocked automated fetch (403); season, hours and reservation policy unverified. Ebenalp cable car first departure **[GAP]**.

## 6. 2027-SPECIFIC RISKS

### 6.1 Permafrost thaw and rockfall — the Spitze Stei case (Kandersteg)

The most concrete, best-documented example of the risk category, and it sits directly above one of the trip's headline sites.

- The unstable slope above Kandersteg is under **continuous GPS, tachymeter, radar and camera surveillance**. Drone-derived terrain models show **movement of several metres per year** across roughly **500,000 m²**, with about **16 million m³** of rock and debris in motion across multiple slip bodies. [V] https://www.gemeindekandersteg.ch/spitze-stei
- A **permanent restricted zone** covers the area exposed to debris flows up to 200,000 m³. Inhabited areas are currently assessed as not at risk. [V] same
