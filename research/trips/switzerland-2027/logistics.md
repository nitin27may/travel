# LANE 2 — TOP-LINE VERDICTS (read this first)

1. **RAIL, not car. Not close.** 15-day 2nd-class Swiss Travel Pass **CHF 499** vs an all-in mid-size car at **CHF 1,940–2,310**. Break-even is ~4 paying adults. And the best bases (Zermatt, Wengen, Mürren, Saas-Fee, Bettmeralp, Stoos, Braunwald) are **car-free** — a car would sit in a valley garage. Optional hybrid: pass + a 3–4 day rental (~CHF 400) for Appenzell / side valleys only. Two further nails: **the Swiss Travel Pass does NOT cover car-carrying trains** (confirmed by BLS), and **TCS forecasts "double-digit queue lengths with several hours of waiting" at the Gotthard north portal in July and the south portal in August** — this window straddles both. The rail alternative through the base tunnel is 20 minutes, unaffected. §8A-SUPPLEMENT S9, S11.
2. **⚠️ BUT the pass choice is genuinely open.** Half Fare Card is **CHF 150** for one month (covers the whole window). The STP only wins if you'd otherwise generate **CHF 700+** of full fares. A 2–3 base itinerary with guest cards may not. **Halbtax also beats the STP on Jungfraujoch by CHF 47–58.** Run the numbers once the route is fixed — §8B.9.
3. **⭐ Guest cards are the biggest lever, not passes.** **Engadin (2 nights) and Saas-Fee (1 night) give mountain lifts FREE** — worth ~CHF 230/adult over 5 nights. Engadin lifts **reject the STP, Half Fare and GA outright**, so the guest card is the only cheap route. Davos, Aletsch Arena and Flims/Laax are widely misreported as free — **they are not**. §8B.8.
4. **Only Rigi, Stanserhorn and Stoos are 100% free with the Swiss Travel Pass.** Everything else is 50% (Gornergrat, Schilthorn, Pilatus, Titlis, Männlichen, First, Glacier 3000), Jungfraujoch is **25%**, and Corvatsch / Bettmerhorn are **0%**. §8B.3.
5. **Skip the Glacier Express (CHF 54 reservation).** Regular trains run the same rails free with a pass, no reservation, with openable windows. Same for the Bernina Express (CHF 44 vs CHF 5 on the regional). **Do take the Gotthard Panorama Express** — it's the one route with no free alternative. **Centovalli is free with a pass and is the sleeper pick.** §8B.2.
6. **ZRH is the only Swiss nonstop from YYZ** (Air Canada + SWISS, ~11/wk, 8h). **The Dolomites chain makes an open-jaw obvious: YYZ→VCE nonstop … ZRH→YYZ nonstop.** Best overland entry on 27 Jul: **drop the Italian rental in Bolzano and go via Innsbruck (~5h30–6h30)** — kills the planned 280 km Bolzano→Venice backtrack. §1.6.
7. **1 August 2027 is Swiss National Day AND a Sunday** — maximum closures. Shop Saturday 31 July. Best place to be: **Lake Lucerne** (Rütli, bonfires, fireworks). §5D.
8. **Recommended base split: Lucerne 4 → Wengen 5 → Zermatt 5.** All transfers under 2.5h. Weather-hedged across three regions. **The Valais is the driest region in Switzerland** (Sion 603 mm/yr vs Locarno 1,897 mm) — Zermatt is rain insurance. §4B, §5F.
9. **ETIAS: assume you will need it.** EUR 20, 3 years, Switzerland included. No confirmed launch date as of Aug 2026 — 2027 is likely. **EES biometrics are already collected at the border with no pre-action needed.** §1.4.
10. **Diarise 25 April 2027** — Glacier Express reservations open exactly 93 days ahead. GoldenPass at 92. §7B.

### 1.4 Entry requirements — Canadian passport

Switzerland is **Schengen but not EU**. Practical consequence: Schengen rules apply (90/180, EES, ETIAS) but EU-specific consumer rules (e.g. EU roaming) do **not**.

**Government of Canada official (travel.gc.ca/locations/switzerland):**
- Passport must be valid **at least 3 months beyond your planned departure from the Schengen area**.
- "Tourist visa: **not required** for stays up to 90 days in any 180-day period."
- 90/180 is **cumulative across all Schengen countries** — a Dolomites trip in Italy immediately before counts against the same 90 days. A 14-night Swiss trip + ~10-night Italy trip = ~25 days, well inside 90. No issue.
- **EES (Entry/Exit System):** "the Entry/Exit System (EES) collects information about your trip as well as your facial image and fingerprints. **You don't need to take any action before you travel.**" Practical impact = slower first-entry border processing (biometric enrolment) at ZRH/VCE.
- **ETIAS:** "Applications are not yet open. Apply only through the official EU website once the system is launched."
- Source: https://travel.gc.ca/locations/switzerland

**ETIAS status specific to Switzerland:**
- Switzerland **is** an ETIAS country. ETIAS covers all EU states except Ireland, **plus Iceland, Norway, Liechtenstein and Switzerland** (30 countries total).
- **The launch date has slipped.** As of mid-2026 the official ETIAS site removed its late-2026 target; the EU says it will announce a start date several months before go-live, and 2027 is now the likely launch year.
- Fee **EUR 20**, validity **3 years** (or until passport expiry), applies to visa-exempt nationals including Canadians.
- If it launches, there is a **6-month transitional grace period** during which entry without ETIAS is permitted if all other conditions are met.

### 2.3 Half Fare Card (Swiss Half Fare Card) — 2026

- **CHF 150**, valid **one calendar month** from first day of validity (2025 price was CHF 120 — a 25% jump).
- Gives **50% off** trains, buses, boats, panoramic trains, **mountain railways and gondolas** — a broader and flatter discount than the STP's tiered structure.
- Also unlocks **half-price Saver Day Passes and Supersaver tickets**.
- Sources: https://www.myswissalps.com/travel-ticket/swiss-half-fare-card/ , https://www.myswissalps.com/details/buy-swiss-half-fare-card/ , https://www.sbb.ch/en/offers/swiss-half-fare-card , https://www.myswitzerland.com/en-us/planning/transport-accommodation/tickets-public-transport/swiss-half-fare-card/

**THE ARITHMETIC — STP 15-day vs Half Fare Card**

Break-even: the 15-day STP (CHF 499, 2nd cl.) vs Halbtax (CHF 150) means you must generate **CHF 349 of *net* extra value** from the pass over 14 nights. Under Halbtax you pay 50% of every fare; under STP you pay 0% of most fares. So the pass wins once **full-price rail/boat/bus fares consumed exceed ~CHF 700** across the trip (because the pass saves you the half you'd otherwise pay).

Real reference fares (full price, 2nd class, one way):
- Zurich HB → Interlaken Ost ≈ CHF 65
- Interlaken → Zermatt ≈ CHF 80
- Zermatt → St. Moritz (Glacier Express base fare) ≈ CHF 155–160
- Lucerne → Interlaken ≈ CHF 33
- Zurich Airport → Lucerne ≈ CHF 27
- (GAP: exact 2026 point-to-point fares not individually re-verified — pull from sbb.ch)

A 14-night, 3–4 base itinerary with day trips easily consumes **CHF 900–1,400** of full-price transport. **At that level the 15-day Swiss Travel Pass wins comfortably**, before counting the 500 free museums and the three free mountains (Rigi + Stanserhorn + Stoos alone are ~CHF 150–190 of value).

**BUT the counter-case is real:** if the itinerary is **base-heavy and lift-heavy** — e.g. 7 nights Zermatt + 7 nights Grindelwald, with most spend going on Gornergrat / Klein Matterhorn / Jungfraujoch / First rather than long inter-city trains — the Half Fare Card can win, because those lifts are 50% under *both* products (and Jungfraujoch is *better* under Halbtax). Two Halbtax cards would be needed if the trip spans two calendar months — **27 July–10 Aug 2027 spans July and August**, so **one Halbtax purchased with validity starting 27 July covers through 26 August. One card is enough.** Confirm the "one month from chosen start date" (not calendar month) rule at purchase — it is start-date based per SBB.

**Hybrid strategy worth costing:** Half Fare Card (CHF 150) + **Saver Day Passes** on the 5–6 heavy travel days.

  - **1st class: from CHF 49 with Halbtax; from CHF 88 without.**
  - Buy **1 day to 6 months in advance** — cannot be bought same-day. Valid until 05:00 the following day.
  - **Quota'd** — cheap tiers sell out, price rises as the date approaches. "only available while stocks last".

- **Supersaver tickets (Sparbillette)**: discounted point-to-point on specific trains, typically 20–70% off, non-refundable, train-specific. Released ~6 months out. Half-price again with Halbtax.
- **Buying early materially reduces cost — yes, decisively, for Saver Day Passes and Supersavers.** It does **not** reduce the Swiss Travel Pass price (fixed tariff), though buying an STP before a 1 Jan price increase locks the older price — **relevant: buy the 2026-priced STP in late 2026 if it can be dated into 2027. VERIFY this is permitted; passes are normally sold with a validity start date up to ~6 months ahead, so a 27 July 2027 start would only be purchasable from ~late Jan 2027, i.e. at 2027 prices. GAP.**

- **Zermatt Peak Pass** — Zermatt lifts only.
- **Engadin guest card / Bergbahnen inclusive** — in summer, guests staying 2+ nights in participating Engadin/St. Moritz accommodation get **most mountain lifts free**. Similar schemes exist in Saas-Fee (SaastalCard), Aletsch Arena, Braunwald, Flims/Laax. **These are extremely strong and often ignored — they can make a regional pass or even the STP redundant for lifts in those valleys.**
- **Key structural insight for a 14-night multi-region trip: regional passes fragment. Two regional passes usually cost more than one 15-day STP and cover nothing in between.** Regional passes win for single-region trips, not this one — with one exception: **the guest-card free-lift schemes stack on top of an STP for free and should be actively hunted when choosing hotels.**

### 2.6 Renting a car — costs and constraints

(Detail from parallel research appended in §8.)
- **Motorway vignette:** annual only, no short-term option, valid 1 Dec (prior year) – 31 Jan (following year). Available as sticker or e-vignette via via.admin.ch. **Swiss rental cars already have one.** Buying one only matters if you drive a car in from Italy/Austria.
- **Fuel:** Switzerland is *cheaper* than Italy for petrol — worth filling up before crossing back.
- **Parking:** the real killer. Swiss town/hotel parking is expensive, and the mountain villages you actually want to stay in are **car-free**.

**CAR-FREE VILLAGES — the decisive constraint**
Confirmed car-free (no private vehicle access at all):
**Zermatt, Wengen, Mürren, Gimmelwald, Saas-Fee, Bettmeralp, Riederalp, Fiescheralp, Braunwald, Stoos, Rigi Kaltbad/Rigi Kulm, Kleine Scheidegg** — plus smaller ones (Belalp, Niederrickenbach, Gspon, Rasa, Bosco Gurin (partially), Muottas Muragl, Wiesen).

Park-and-ride points:
- **Zermatt → Täsch** (Matterhorn Terminal Täsch, ~2,100 spaces, shuttle train every 20 min, 12 min)
- **Wengen → Lauterbrunnen** (station car park / multi-storey)
- **Mürren/Gimmelwald → Stechelberg** (Schilthornbahn valley station) or Lauterbrunnen (BLM funicular via Grütschalp)
- **Saas-Fee → village-entrance parking garages**
- **Bettmeralp/Riederalp/Fiescheralp → Betten Talstation / Mörel / Fiesch** valley garages
- **Braunwald → Linthal**
- **Stoos → Schwyz/Schlattli**
- **Rigi → Vitznau / Arth-Goldau**
(Exact 2026 daily parking rates in §8.)

### 2.7 VERDICT — rail vs car for 14 nights across multiple regions

**RAIL WINS, decisively, and it is not close.** The reasoning, sourced and structural:

1. **The best base towns are car-free.** Zermatt, Wengen, Mürren, Saas-Fee, Bettmeralp, Stoos, Braunwald — a car cannot reach any of them. You would pay for a rental AND pay CHF 15–25/day to park it in a valley garage AND still take the train up. You pay twice.
2. **The 15-day STP marginal cost is CHF 8.57/day** past day 8. A rental car in peak August plus fuel plus parking is comfortably CHF 90–150/day all-in. Rail is roughly one-tenth the marginal cost.
3. **The scenic railways ARE the attraction.** Glacier Express, Bernina Express, GoldenPass, the Brünig line — driving them means missing them.
4. **Mountain lifts are unavoidable and are priced separately regardless.** A car does not reduce lift spend by one franc; the pass does (50% or 100%).
5. **Swiss trains are punctual, frequent (usually half-hourly), and reach absurd places** — Kleine Scheidegg, Jungfraujoch, Gornergrat, Rigi Kulm. This is the exact inverse of the Dolomites, where a car is close to mandatory.
6. **Free museums + free boats + free city trams** are pure additional value the car cannot match.
7. **Alcohol, alpine roads, mountain passes and jetlag** — driving Furka/Grimsel/Susten after an 8-hour overnight flight is a bad plan.

**WHERE A CAR STILL WINS (be specific):**
- **Appenzell / Toggenburg back-country**, **Emmental**, **Jura**, **Gruyères–Moléson**, **Val d'Hérens / Val d'Anniviers**, **Val Verzasca / Val Bavona (Ticino side valleys)** — thin PostBus service, 1–2/hour or less, and last buses are early.
- **Early-morning trailheads** — a hike starting at 06:00 at a remote trailhead is car-only; the first PostBus is often 07:30–08:30.
- **Luggage-heavy / family travel** with multiple base changes.
- **Photography** — golden hour at Lauterbrunnen/Oeschinensee/Creux du Van requires being somewhere before the first bus.
- **Combined verdict: rent a car for a discrete 2–3 day sub-loop if the itinerary includes Appenzell or a Ticino/Valais side-valley — otherwise never.** Swiss rail even sells this cleanly: **Mobility car sharing** stations at most stations, and Hertz/Europcar at major stations, so you can hold a 15-day STP and add a 2-day rental without contradiction.

### The structural lesson from the matrix

- **Interlaken–Grindelwald–Lauterbrunnen–Wengen–Mürren are all within 35 minutes of each other.** They are ONE base, not four. Choosing between them is a lifestyle choice, not a logistics one.

## 4B. RECOMMENDED BASE SPLITS FOR 14 NIGHTS

**Testing the assumption "Switzerland's density means fewer bases work better".**

**The assumption is CORRECT but for a non-obvious reason.** It is not primarily about density of attractions — it is about **vertical transfers**. Every car-free base (Wengen, Mürren, Zermatt, Bettmeralp, Saas-Fee) requires a train/cable-car leg with luggage at both ends. A base change into or out of Mürren is a 4-leg operation (train → funicular → mountain train → walk). A base change in Tuscany is "put bags in car". So the *effective* cost of a base change in Switzerland is materially higher than the raw matrix time suggests, and 4 bases in 14 nights is more disruptive than the same count in Italy.

**Countervailing factor:** the Berner Oberland alone cannot fill 14 nights of weather-independent activity, and one long rain spell in a single base ruins a fortnight. Two regions is the minimum for weather-hedging. **Three is the sweet spot.**

### Option A — 2 BASES (7 + 7)
**Lauterbrunnen or Wengen (7 nights) → Zermatt (7 nights)**
- Transfer: **2:25 rail**, 1–2 changes, entirely scenic (Lauterbrunnen–Interlaken–Spiez–Visp–Zermatt). One travel morning, arrive by lunch.
- Reach from base 1 within 45 min: Jungfraujoch/Kleine Scheidegg, Männlichen, Schilthorn, Grindelwald First, Schynige Platte, Interlaken, Lake Thun & Brienz boats, Harder Kulm, Trümmelbach Falls.
- Reach from base 2 within 45 min: Gornergrat, Klein Matterhorn/Matterhorn Glacier Paradise, Rothorn, Sunnegga, Riffelsee, Täsch.
- **Pros:** minimum luggage friction, deep familiarity, best for hikers who want to *use* a region rather than sample it, easiest to book (2 reservations).
- **Cons:** misses Graubünden entirely, misses Ticino, no lake/city variety, and both bases are expensive-and-crowded in August. Two consecutive rain days in a narrow valley is grim.
- **Best for:** hiking-focused, low-tolerance-for-packing travellers.

### Option B — 3 BASES (5 + 5 + 4) — **RECOMMENDED DEFAULT**
**Lucerne (4) → Wengen/Lauterbrunnen (5) → Zermatt (5)**
- Lucerne → Interlaken: **1:50** (the Brünig line, scenic, or the Luzern–Interlaken Express).
- Interlaken area → Zermatt: **2:05–2:25**.
- Both transfers under 2.5h. Total transfer overhead across 14 nights: **~5 hours**.
- **Why Lucerne first:** it is 1:05 from ZRH airport, at low altitude (good for jetlag), has the three 100%-free STP mountains within reach (**Rigi, Stanserhorn, Stoos** — plus Pilatus and Titlis at 50%), the lake boats are free with the pass, the Verkehrshaus is free with the pass, and it is a city so it works in bad weather. It is the correct **landing** base.
- **Rationale:** covers Central Switzerland + Berner Oberland + Valais. Three genuinely different landscapes (lake/city, glacier-valley/waterfall, high-alpine/Matterhorn). Weather-hedged. Every transfer is short and scenic.

### Option B-alt — 3 BASES, Dolomites-chained
**St. Moritz or Pontresina (4) → Lucerne or Interlaken (5) → Zermatt (5)**
- Enter Switzerland from Tirano on the **Bernina Express** (~2:20 from Tirano), so the arrival is itself a highlight.
- Then **Glacier Express St. Moritz → Zermatt** later, OR St. Moritz → Lucerne (4:00) → Interlaken → Zermatt.
- **The elegant version:** St. Moritz (4) → **Glacier Express all the way to Zermatt (~8h, one travel day)** (5) → Interlaken/Wengen (5), exiting via ZRH. This runs the country west-to-east-to-west but makes both long transfers into flagship experiences.
- **Cleaner version:** St. Moritz (4) → Lucerne (4) → Wengen (6), fly out ZRH. Zermatt dropped. Transfers 4:00 and 1:50.

### Option C — 4 BASES (4 + 4 + 3 + 3)
**Lucerne (3) → Wengen/Lauterbrunnen (4) → Zermatt (4) → Lugano or Montreux (3)**
- Lucerne→Interlaken 1:50; Interlaken→Zermatt 2:05; Zermatt→Lugano 4:45 (or Zermatt→Montreux 2:55).
- **The Montreux variant is much better than the Lugano variant** — 2:55 vs 4:45, it adds Lavaux vineyards + Lake Geneva + Chaplin's World (free with STP), and it sets up a **GVA departure**, enabling the ZRH-in/GVA-out open-jaw.
- **Pros:** maximum variety — city/lake, alpine valley, high alpine, Mediterranean-ish lakeside. Good for a first-time-in-Switzerland "see it all" trip.
- **Cons:** four check-ins, four packing days, ~9h of transfer time, and 3–4 nights is not enough to absorb a bad weather day in the mountain bases. Also four sets of August accommodation to secure.
- **Only do this if the traveller genuinely prefers variety over depth.**

### Bases to actively AVOID as a primary base for this trip
- **Zurich / Geneva** — fine for one arrival/departure night, poor as a base; every mountain is 2h+ each way, which burns 4h/day.
- **Interlaken** — a transit town with a tourist-strip character; you sleep 20 min from the actual valleys and pay similar money. Prefer Lauterbrunnen/Wengen/Grindelwald unless you want nightlife and supermarkets.
- **Grindelwald** vs **Lauterbrunnen/Wengen** — Grindelwald has become very built-up and busy; the Eiger Express makes Jungfraujoch fastest from there, but Wengen is quieter, car-free and equally connected.
- **Scuol, Appenzell, Sion/Sierre, Saas-Fee** — all excellent, none is a good *primary* base for a 14-night multi-region trip; they are 2–3 night specialist detours.

### The strongest single recommendation
**Lucerne 4 → Wengen 5 → Zermatt 5, fly ZRH in and ZRH out (or GVA out via a Montreux night).**
With a **15-day 2nd-class Swiss Travel Pass (CHF 499)** covering all three transfers, all lake boats, all city transport, 500 museums, and Rigi + Stanserhorn + Stoos free — plus 50% off Pilatus, Titlis, Schilthorn, First, Gornergrat, Klein Matterhorn, Glacier 3000.

**CRITICAL CAVEAT — the astronomical sunset is not the useful number in the Alps.** Deep valleys lose *direct* sun hours before the horizon sunset:
- **Lauterbrunnen** sits in a 300m-walled trough; in midsummer the valley floor typically loses direct sun by roughly **19:00–19:30** and does not get it until well after 07:00. In winter it famously gets almost none.
- **Zermatt** village (1,608 m) is walled by the Mischabel and is in shadow noticeably before the stated 21:05.
- **Practical implication:** golden-hour photography of the Matterhorn or the Lauterbrunnen walls must be done **from height** (Gornergrat, Sunnegga, Riffelsee, Männlichen, Kleine Scheidegg, Mürren) — the last lifts down are typically **17:00–18:00**, so evening light usually requires staying up top (Riffelberg, Kulm Hotel Gornergrat, Berggasthaus) or hiking down.
- **GAP: exact valley-floor sun-loss times not sourced — verify per-village with a sun-path tool (e.g. suncalc.org) at planning time.**

**Consequence for itinerary design:** with sunrise ~06:00–06:20 and lifts opening ~08:00–08:30, the mountain morning is short. To get on a summit at sunrise you must sleep at altitude. Conversely the long evenings (light until ~21:40 civil twilight) mean **dinner-then-walk** is very viable, and the last valley trains run late.

For a 3-base national itinerary it loses to the 15-day STP. The only realistic scenario where it wins is **Option A (2 bases) with a long Berner Oberland stay**, and even then you'd need separate tickets for the Zermatt half.

Alternative per swissasap: **Jungfrau Travel Pass** is the narrower choice "if you're staying mainly around Grindelwald, Wengen and Mürren"; **Berner Oberland Pass** if you want to range wider (Kandersteg, Gstaad, Meiringen, the lakes).

**REGA explained**
- Swiss Air-Rescue Rega is a **non-profit foundation**, not a state service and **not an insurer**. It runs rescue helicopters, ambulance jets and coordinates search-and-rescue in Switzerland and abroad.
- **Call 1414** for a mountain/helicopter emergency. **Install the free Rega app (iOS/Android) before the trip** — it transmits your GPS position with the alert, which in a valley with no landmarks is the difference between a 10-minute and a 2-hour response.
- Source: https://www.rega.ch/en

**REGA patronage (Gönnerschaft) — the numbers**
- **CHF 40.00 per year for an adult.** Children and young people: **CHF 0.00**.
- Validity: **from date of payment until 15 May of the following year** — so a payment made in spring/summer 2027 covers the whole 27 Jul–10 Aug 2027 window and beyond.
- Benefit: "Rega can waive rescue mission costs for patrons when insurance companies don't cover expenses… last year, Rega waived over 14 million Swiss francs in costs of missions for its patrons that insurance companies did not cover."
- **Foreigners / non-Swiss residents CAN become patrons**, and receive **air rescue benefits within Switzerland**. They do **NOT** get repatriation to a country outside Switzerland (that benefit is for Swiss nationals living abroad).
- **Critical caveat:** "Patronage is **not insurance**. Rega is a non-profit foundation where contributions function as donations, giving the organization **discretionary authority** to waive costs rather than a legal obligation."
- Source: https://www.rega.ch/en/rega-patron/become-a-patron

**VERDICT for a Canadian tourist:** **Yes, buy it — CHF 40 is trivially cheap** relative to a Swiss helicopter evacuation (commonly quoted at CHF 3,000–5,000+, **GAP: not price-verified**). But understand what you are buying: a discretionary cost waiver, not a contract. It is a **supplement to**, not a replacement for, proper travel medical insurance. **Separately confirm in writing that the Canadian policy covers helicopter mountain rescue and hiking at altitude** — many Canadian credit-card and basic travel policies exclude "mountaineering" and some exclude hiking above a stated altitude. That confirmation is on the re-verify list.

## 3A. SCENIC TRAINS — verified structural facts

(2026 reservation fees are pending from a parallel research stream — see §8B. What is confirmed:)

### Glacier Express
- **Route:** Zermatt (1,616 m) ↔ Chur ↔ St. Moritz. **291 km, 8 hours end to end.** Major intermediate point **Andermatt**.
- **291 bridges, 91 tunnels**, including the **15.4 km Furka Base Tunnel**. Highest point **Oberalp Pass, 2,033 m**.
- Operated by **Glacier Express AG** (HQ Andermatt), a partnership of **Matterhorn Gotthard Bahn (MGB)** and **Rhaetian Railway (RhB)** since 2017.
- **Summer (June–October): multiple daily departures each direction.** Winter: one daily train.
- Nickname: **"the slowest express train in the world."**
- Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glacier_Express
- **GAP: 2026 reservation fee in CHF, Excellence Class supplement, full fare, booking window.** Check https://www.glacierexpress.ch

### Bernina Express
- **Route:** Chur ↔ Tirano (Italy), **~4 hours**. **196 bridges, 55 tunnels.**
- Operator: **Rhaetian Railway**, as a Panorama Express with enlarged windows and multilingual audio guides.
- **UNESCO World Heritage** — the Albula and Bernina lines were jointly inscribed in **2008**.
- **Highest point: Ospizio Bernina, 2,253 m (7,392 ft)** — the highest transalpine railway crossing without rack or tunnel.
- Daily year-round; summer has a dedicated express train, winter integrates regional trains.
- **SEAT RESERVATIONS ARE MANDATORY**, either bought with the ticket or as a **supplement on top of a rail pass**.
- **Bernina Express Bus** connects Tirano via **Lake Como to Lugano**, linking with the Gotthard Panorama Express.
- Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernina_Express

**Structural point for the synthesizer:** the St. Moritz↔Tirano segment (~2h20) is the *scenic core* of the Bernina Express and is the segment that matters. The Chur↔St. Moritz portion runs on the Albula line, which the **regular RhB regional trains also traverse — free with a Swiss Travel Pass, no reservation, same views, and you can get off**. The premium is paid for panoramic glass and a guaranteed seat, not for a different route.

The same logic applies to the Glacier Express: the whole 8h route is covered by **ordinary MGB and RhB trains** that are free with the pass and need no reservation. **Doing Zermatt→St. Moritz on regular trains with 2–3 changes costs a pass-holder CHF 0 and takes roughly the same time.** The counter-argument is real though — 8 hours of table service, no luggage shuffling at Andermatt/Disentis/Chur, and the panoramic roof glass genuinely changes the Oberalp and Rhine Gorge sections.

**Best single segments if not doing the full route:**
- Glacier Express: **Andermatt → Disentis → Chur** (Oberalp Pass + the Rhine Gorge / "Swiss Grand Canyon") — the best scenery-per-hour on the line.
- Bernina Express: **St. Moritz → Tirano** (~2h20) — Morteratsch glacier, Ospizio Bernina, Alp Grüm, the Brusio circular viaduct. This is the segment worth paying for.
- **Both of these also happen to be the natural transfer legs of a Graubünden-inclusive itinerary — so the scenic train can be the transfer rather than an extra day.**

### Gotthard Panorama Express
- **Route:** **Lucerne → Lugano**, combining a **boat on Lake Lucerne** (past the **Rütli meadow**, birthplace of the Swiss Confederacy, and the **Tell Chapel**) to **Flüelen**, then a panoramic train on the **original 1882 Gotthard line** with its famous **spiral loops** and the historic Gotthard summit tunnel, down through Bellinzona to Lugano.
- **Duration: ~5h 30m**, split roughly evenly between boat and train.
- **Season: almost daily EXCEPT MONDAYS, mid-April to mid-October.** (In window for 27 Jul–10 Aug — but **avoid Mondays**.)
- **Premium fare / supplement required**; 1st-class panoramic coaches, air-conditioned, onboard dining.
- Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gotthard_Panorama_Express
- **GAP: 2026 supplement in CHF on top of a Swiss Travel Pass.**

**IMPORTANT distinction the synthesizer should not miss:** unlike the Glacier and Bernina Expresses, the Gotthard Panorama Express runs on **different track from the fast trains**. The Milan EuroCity services use the **Gotthard Base Tunnel** (20 min, no scenery). The panorama route uses the **old 1882 mountain line over the spiral loops** — a genuinely different and far more scenic journey. **However**, a regular hourly service (the *Treno Gottardo*, operated by SOB, Basel/Zurich ↔ Locarno via Göschenen–Airolo) also runs the old line, is **free with a Swiss Travel Pass and needs no reservation**. **GAP: Treno Gottardo details not independently re-verified in this research** — confirm on sbb.ch. If it runs as described, it is the best value on this list: the old Gotthard scenery for free.

- MVR also runs the **Montreux–Rochers-de-Naye** line and several funiculars.

**Swiss National Day, 1 August 2027, falls on a SUNDAY. This compounds.**
- It has been an **official public holiday since 1994** (federal popular initiative approved 1993). It commemorates the **Federal Charter of 1291**, when Schwyz, Uri and Unterwalden swore the oath of confederation.
- Celebrations: **paper lantern parades, bonfires on hilltops, strings of Swiss flags, fireworks** — organised mostly at municipal level.
- Notable: the **Rhine Falls at Schaffhausen has been illuminated since 1920**; the **Rütli Meadow** hosts a representational ceremony at the site of the legendary pledge; **Basel** fires its display along the Rhine on the **evening of 31 July**.
- "As an official public holiday, many businesses and services may have modified hours or closures on August 1st."
- Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_National_Day

**Planning consequences:**
1. **Sunday + national holiday = maximum closure.** Supermarkets, most shops and many restaurants shut. **Stock food on Saturday 31 July.** The reliable exceptions are **station and airport shops** (Coop Pronto / Migrolino at major stations trade on Sundays and holidays) — that is the fallback.
2. **Do not schedule a base change on 1 August.** Public transport runs a Sunday/holiday timetable — thinner, and packed with domestic day-trippers.
3. **Positively:** it is a genuinely good day to be in Switzerland. **Lake Lucerne is the single best place to be** — the Rütli Meadow ceremony is the symbolic heart of the day, it is reachable by a pass-covered lake boat from Lucerne, and there are evening bonfires on the surrounding ridges plus fireworks over the lake. If the itinerary uses Lucerne as the landing base (Option B), **1 August lands naturally in the Lucerne window if you arrive 27 July** — that is a strong argument for the Lucerne-first ordering.
4. Mountain-top restaurants and many resorts run brunches/bonfires — worth booking ahead.

## 5F. WEATHER AND CLIMATE — hard data

Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_of_Switzerland

**Average JULY maximum temperature by region and altitude**

| Station | Region | Altitude | July max |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Locarno** | Southern Switzerland / Ticino | ~200 m | **27.1 °C** |
| **Sion** | Inner Alpine valley (Valais) | 482 m | **27.0 °C** |
| **Bern** | Swiss Plateau | ~540 m | **24.3 °C** |
| **La Chaux-de-Fonds** | Jura | ~1,000 m | **20.7 °C** |
| **Arosa** | Alps | 1,878 m | **15.4 °C** |
| **Jungfraujoch** | High Alps | 3,571 m | **3.1 °C** |

**THE MOST USEFUL DERIVED NUMBER — the lapse rate.**
Bern (540 m, 24.3 °C) → Arosa (1,878 m, 15.4 °C) = **0.66 °C per 100 m**.
Bern → Jungfraujoch (3,571 m, 3.1 °C) = **0.70 °C per 100 m**.
**Working rule: subtract ~0.65–0.7 °C for every 100 m of ascent.**

Applied to this trip:
- Interlaken (566 m) on a 26 °C day → **Kleine Scheidegg (2,061 m) ≈ 16 °C**, **Jungfraujoch (3,454 m) ≈ 6 °C**.
- Zermatt (1,608 m) on a 22 °C day → **Gornergrat (3,089 m) ≈ 12 °C**, **Klein Matterhorn (3,883 m) ≈ 6 °C**.
- **Add wind chill and the Jungfraujoch/Klein Matterhorn figure goes to near or below freezing.** Even in the first week of August, a proper insulating layer, a windproof shell, gloves and a hat are non-optional for the high excursions. This is the single most commonly under-packed thing on a Swiss summer trip.
- **Jungfraujoch's July mean max is 3.1 °C.** That is the *average high*, in the warmest month.

**Precipitation — the weather-hedging insight**

| Place | Annual rainfall |
|---|---|
| **Locarno** | **1,897 mm — the wettest** |
| **Lugano** | **1,559 mm** |
| **Sion (Valais)** | **603 mm — "semi-arid"** |

- **Ticino (Locarno/Lugano) is both the WARMEST and the WETTEST part of Switzerland.** It gets Mediterranean-style convective downpours — big, intense, often short.
- **The inner Alpine valleys of the Valais — Sion, and by extension Zermatt and Saas-Fee — are the DRIEST region in Switzerland**, shielded on all sides by high mountains.
- **Ticino is 2–4 °C warmer than the Plateau** because of its lower, southern Alpine position.

**Strategic consequence for a 14-night itinerary:** **the Valais (Zermatt/Saas-Fee) is the best weather hedge in the country.** If a wet spell sets in over the Berner Oberland, the Valais is statistically the most likely place to still be dry. Building Zermatt into the itinerary is a rain-insurance decision as much as a scenery one. Conversely, do not assume Ticino is the "sunny escape" — it is warm but it is the rainiest region.

**"Altweibersommer" — answering the question directly.** Altweibersommer ("old women's summer") is the **German-language term for an autumn phenomenon** — a spell of warm, calm, hazy weather in **September/October**, the equivalent of North American "Indian summer", named for the gossamer spider silk that drifts in the air at that time of year. **It has no bearing whatsoever on a 27 July – 10 August trip.** The relevant late-July/early-August pattern is the opposite: peak convective instability, i.e. warm mornings and afternoon thunderstorms. **GAP: not source-verified in this research, but the seasonal definition is not in dispute.**

## 4A. BASE TOWNS — verified structural facts

(Nightly price ranges and booking lead times were assigned to a parallel research stream — see §8C for whatever it returned. What is independently verified:)

### Zermatt — 1,620 m, Valais
- **Entire municipality is a combustion-engine-free zone.** "Almost all vehicles in Zermatt are battery driven and almost completely silent." Electric shuttles, "electro" taxis run by local families, two electric bus routes, horse-drawn carriages. Emergency services may use combustion engines.
- **Access: rack-assisted railway from Täsch** (every 20 min, 5.61 km). No road access for visitors.
- Population **~5,847** (2024) year-round; "tourist numbers frequently exceed permanent residents several times over"; **about half of all jobs in town are in hotels or restaurants.**
- **Gornergratbahn reaches 3,089 m — "the highest open-air railway in Europe."** **Klein Matterhorn cable car reaches 3,883 m**, with cross-border access to Italian ski areas via Testa Grigia.
- Glacier Express terminus.
- Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zermatt
- **Within ~45 min:** Gornergrat, Sunnegga–Blauherd–Rothorn, Klein Matterhorn/Matterhorn Glacier Paradise, Riffelberg/Riffelsee, Täsch, Randa.
- **Weather advantage:** sits in the Valais, the driest region in Switzerland (see §5F).

### Lauterbrunnen — 802 m, Bern
- Valley village. **"One of the deepest [valleys] in the Alpine chain when compared with the height of the mountains that rise directly on either side"**, cliff walls up to **1,000 m** high.
- Waterfalls: **Staubbach 297 m — the highest free-falling waterfall in Switzerland**; **Trümmelbach** 3 km from the village by bus; **Mürrenbachfall 417 m — the highest waterfall in Switzerland**.
- The municipality includes **Wengen, Mürren, Gimmelwald, Stechelberg and Isenfluh** — i.e. the whole valley system is one commune.
- Access up-valley: **Wengernalp Railway** to Wengen and Kleine Scheidegg; **cable car to Mürren**; **Jungfrau Railway from Kleine Scheidegg — Europe's highest railway.**
- **NOT car-free** — it is the park-and-ride point for Wengen and Mürren.
- **⚠️ OVERTOURISM RISK FOR 2027:** "In May 2024, it was reported the local authority had initiated a working group to explore solutions for over-tourism, including **potential entry fees for day-trippers similar to Venice's approach**." **RE-VERIFY whether a day-tripper fee is in force by summer 2027** — note that staying overnight would exempt you.
- Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lauterbrunnen

### Wengen — 1,274 m, Bern
- **"One of very few car-free resort villages in Europe."** Some service and electric vehicles only.
- **Access: Wengernalpbahn rack railway from Lauterbrunnen, 14 minutes uphill.** **~40 daily services**, with extra trains inserted at peak times. Also reachable by train via Kleine Scheidegg from Grindelwald, and by the seasonal **Luftseilbahn Wengen–Männlichen** cable car.
- Population **~1,150** year-round → **5,000 in summer**, 10,000 in winter.
- Hosts the **Lauberhorn** races in January (irrelevant to this trip).
- Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wengen,_Switzerland
- **Assessment: the strongest single base in the Jungfrau region.** Car-free, 14 min and ~40 services/day from the valley (so it is car-free without being isolated), sun-facing terrace position above the Lauterbrunnen shadow, direct to Kleine Scheidegg/Jungfraujoch and Männlichen.

### Mürren — 1,638 m, Bern
- **Entirely car-free, pedestrian-only.**
- **Two access systems:** (1) **Bergbahn Lauterbrunnen–Mürren** — cable car to Grütschalp plus narrow-gauge railway along the terrace (opened 1891); (2) **Stechelberg–Mürren–Schilthorn** cable cars, and **a NEW DIRECT cable car from Stechelberg opened December 2024.** Also the **Allmendhubelbahn** funicular.
- Population **~430** year-round, **2,000 hotel beds.**
- **Schilthorn / Piz Gloria** — revolving restaurant, *On Her Majesty's Secret Service* (1969).
- Views of Eiger, Mönch and Jungfrau directly across the valley.
- Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%BCrren
- **Assessment: the most beautiful and quietest of the three, and the hardest to reach.** Two transfers from the valley with luggage. Best for someone prioritising atmosphere over convenience. The new 2024 Stechelberg cable car materially improves access — **verify its 2027 operating pattern and fares.**

### Grindelwald — 1,034 m, Bern
- **NOT car-free** — road-accessible with vehicle traffic.
- Population **3,800** (2020). **"Almost the entire economy of Grindelwald is based on tourism."**
- **Grindelwald Terminal** is the modern hub for the **Eiger Express** (direct to Eigergletscher on the Jungfraubahn) and the **Gondelbahn Grindelwald–Männlichen**. Plus the **First** cable car and the **Wengernalpbahn** to Kleine Scheidegg. Bernese Oberland Railway to Interlaken.
- Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grindelwald
- **Assessment: the fastest access to Jungfraujoch (Eiger Express) and the widest lift choice, but the busiest and most developed. Road access is a double-edged sword — convenient, but it means tour-bus volume.**

### Saas-Fee — 1,800 m, Valais
- **Car-free village centre since 1951**, implemented to eliminate air pollution. "No cars are allowed to enter the city (they have to be parked in **special car parks outside**); only small electric vehicles operate on the streets."
- Population **~1,559** (2020).
- **Access: PostBus from Brig and Visp, half-hourly for much of the day.** No rail.
- **Metro Alpin — the highest underground funicular railway in the world**; **the highest revolving restaurant in the world at 3,500 m.**
- **"The Pearl of the Alps"** — ringed by **13 peaks over 4,000 m, the highest concentration in the Alps.**
- Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saas-Fee
- **Assessment: the quiet alternative to Zermatt** — higher, arguably a more dramatic amphitheatre, far fewer crowds, and in the same dry Valais rain-shadow. **Downside: bus-only access (no rail), so it is the one car-free village where the Swiss Travel Pass journey is a PostBus rather than a train.** A 2–3 night specialist stop rather than a primary base.

**GAP for all base towns: nightly rates in peak August, booking lead times, and the within-45-min lists for the towns not covered above** (Interlaken, Kandersteg, Bettmeralp/Fiesch, Sion/Sierre, Lucerne, Engelberg, St. Moritz, Pontresina, Scuol, Appenzell, Locarno/Ascona, Lugano, Montreux/Lavaux, Zurich, Geneva).

## 8B.5 ⭐ DOES BUYING EARLY SAVE MONEY? — the direct answer

- **Regular point-to-point tickets: NO.** Swiss domestic fares are **fixed and published — no dynamic pricing, and they never sell out.** *"The price will be the same regardless of when you buy."* (https://www.myswissalps.com/travel-ticket/regular-ticket/)
- **Supersaver: YES** — quota-limited, up to 50% off, 6-month window.
- **Saver Day Pass: MOSTLY** — demand-driven; the reliable lever is the Half Fare Card (CHF 52 → 29).
- **Swiss Travel Pass / Half Fare Card: NO** — fixed price 11 months out or 5 minutes before. **2027 prices already published and unchanged.**
- **Scenic-train reservations: NO price benefit, but a CRITICAL availability benefit** — Glacier Express opens at exactly **93 days**, GoldenPass at **92**, and peak-summer Prestige/Excellence go fast.

**STRATEGIC CONCLUSION: in Switzerland you don't save by booking rail early — you save by (a) choosing bases whose GUEST CARD gives free lifts (lift tickets are the biggest line item and are never discounted by early booking), and (b) holding a Half Fare Card to halve everything else and unlock the CHF 29 Saver Day Pass.**

## 8B.9 ⭐ WHEN A REGIONAL PASS BEATS THE NATIONAL PASS — the verdict

**Structural point: a 15-day STP at CHF 499 = CHF 33/day for the entire national network plus 500 museums. No regional pass beats that standalone on a two-week multi-region trip. Regional passes are SUPPLEMENTS you stack, and the "reduced" column for STP holders is what makes stacking viable.**

- **Berner Oberland Pass — STRONGEST stacking case.** At the reduced rate (**6 days CHF 254** for an STP holder) it converts **Schilthorn, Männlichen, Schynige Platte, Niesen, Brienzer Rothorn and Niederhorn from 50%-off to FREE**, and Jungfraujoch from 25% to a further 25%. Official worked example: six excursions = **CHF 590 individually, CHF 0 with the pass**. **BUY if 5+ nights based in Interlaken/Grindelwald/Lauterbrunnen with lifts most days. Skip at ≤2 mountain days.**
- **Jungfrau Travel Pass vs Berner Oberland Pass — pick ONE.** JTP is fractionally cheaper (3d reduced 165 vs 168) and includes **Eiger Express free**, but **excludes the Schilthorn entirely** and covers a much smaller footprint. **BOP wins for a two-week trip** unless Schilthorn is off the list. ⚠️ **OPEN CONFLICT: Eiger Express is listed as 50% on the BOP but FREE on the JTP — that single fact could flip the decision. Verify.**
- **Lucerne Travel Pass (ex Tell-Pass) — WEAKEST stacking case by design.** **Zero discount for STP holders**, so it's a full CHF 240+ on top. The STP already gives Rigi/Stanserhorn/Stoos free and 50% on Pilatus and Titlis, so incremental value is thin. **Only wins if you are NOT buying an STP** — for a Lucerne-only trip, CHF 240 for 3 days of Pilatus + Rigi + Titlis + boats is excellent. **On a 15-day national itinerary: SKIP.**
- **Zermatt Peak Pass — usually SKIP; buy Peak2Peak instead.** One Peak2Peak at **CHF 106 reduced** buys Matterhorn Glacier Paradise *and* Gornergrat in a day vs ~CHF 176 for a reduced 1-day Peak Pass. Peak Pass only pays from **3+ full mountain days**.
- **⭐ Engadin "Bergbahnen inclusive" — BEST DEAL ON THE LIST AND IT COSTS NOTHING.** ~**CHF 230/adult** of lifts on a 5-night stay, plus RhB and local buses, on top of your STP — and it is the **ONLY** way to get Engadin lifts cheaply since they reject the STP outright. **Only cost: constraining your hotel choice.**
- **Graubünden Pass — redundant with an STP.** Same rail/bus territory the STP covers 100%, only 50% on cableways. Buy only if skipping the STP (5 days all zones 2nd = CHF 175 is genuinely cheap for Bernina + Albula).
- **Lake Geneva–Alps — cheapest pass here (CHF 89 reduced / 5 days) but marginal alongside an STP**, since the STP already covers CGN boats, regional rail and the Geneva Airport link. **Default skip** unless Glacier 3000 turns out to be included at better than 50%.

### ⚠️ THE BIGGER STRATEGIC CALL — Half Fare Card may beat the 15-day pass
The Half Fare Card at **CHF 150** covers the whole window and halves everything; the delta to a 15-day STP is **CHF 349/adult**. The STP only wins once you'd otherwise generate roughly **CHF 700+** of full-fare travel.
Calibration — full 2026 fares: **Zurich–Zermatt CHF 130** 2nd / 222 1st · **Geneva–Zurich CHF 92** · **Basel–Interlaken CHF 64** (https://www.myswissalps.com/travel-ticket/regular-ticket/).
**An itinerary built around 2–3 mountain bases with guest cards generates a handful of long transfers, not CHF 700 of them.** So **Half Fare Card + Saver Day Passes on the 2–3 big transfer days is plausibly CHEAPER than a 15-day STP — and it stacks with guest cards the STP can't use, and it beats the STP on Jungfraujoch by CHF 47–58.**
**Run the real numbers once the route is fixed. This is the single most important open decision in this lane.**

### 4A-2. Additional base towns — verified structural facts

**Engelberg — ~1,000 m, Obwalden**
- **Hourly InterRegio trains from Lucerne** on the Luzern–Stans–Engelberg line (two stations serve the municipality). **~45 min from Lucerne.**
- **Mount Titlis, 3,238 m.** The **"Rotair" — the world's first rotating cable car** — to Kleintitlis at 3,028 m, with glacier access and dining. **Trübsee** alpine lake.
- Benedictine **abbey**, first mentioned 1122.
- Population **4,194** (2020). **354,960 hotel overnights (2015), 67.1% international.**
- Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engelberg
- **Assessment: a day trip from Lucerne, not a base.** Hourly rail from Lucerne in ~45 min means there is little reason to sleep there unless you want an early Titlis. Titlis is 50% with the STP (from CHF 60) and **FREE with the Lucerne Travel Pass**.

**Appenzell — Appenzell Innerrhoden**
- Northeast Switzerland. **Säntis, 2,504 m** is the high point; alpine terrain in the south, **green hills and valleys** in the north.
- Known for pastoral farming, embroidery, and **Landsgemeinde** open-air assemblies — one of the last places practising direct democracy by show of hands.
- Split from Appenzell Ausserrhoden in **1597** over the Reformation; Innerrhoden is the Catholic, mountainous half.
- Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appenzell
- **Assessment: the most culturally distinctive region on the list and the most *car-favouring*.** Served by the narrow-gauge **Appenzeller Bahnen** (pass-covered), but the back roads, the Ebenalp/Aescher cliff guesthouse and the Alpstein trailheads are where a car genuinely wins (see §8A.8). **A 2-night detour, not a base** — and 2:20 by rail from Lucerne, 4h from Interlaken, so it does not fit a Berner-Oberland-and-Valais itinerary without cost.
- **GAP: Ebenalp cable car / Aescher access and STP treatment; Säntis Schwebebahn STP treatment.**

**Bettmeralp — 1,970 m, Valais**
- **"A car-free village which can be reached by cable car from the Bettmeralp train station."** Population ~452 (2020).
- Overlooks the **Aletsch Glacier** (UNESCO); **Bettmerhorn** directly above; **Bettmersee** mountain lake at 2,006 m. Views to the Matterhorn and Weisshorn.
- Municipality formed 1 Jan 2014 from the merger of Betten and Martisberg.
- Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bettmeralp
- **⚠️ CRITICAL COST FINDING: Bettmeralp–Bettmerhorn is 0% — NOT discounted at all by the Swiss Travel Pass or the Half Fare Card** (§8B.3). And the Aletsch Arena guest card gives only **20% off**, not free lifts (§8B.8). **However, the valley cable car Betten–Bettmeralp IS free** on a Saver Day Pass and on the STP.
- **Assessment: the Aletsch Glacier is arguably the single greatest sight in the Alps and Bettmeralp is car-free and quiet — but it is the WORST-value lift region in Switzerland for a pass holder.** Budget the **paid Aletsch Explorer at CHF 27.50/day (reduced rate with STP/Half Fare)** if doing it properly.

**Kandersteg — 1,174 m, Bern**
- In the Kander valley, west of the Jungfrau massif, under the **Balmhorn (3,698 m)**. Population **~1,288** (2020); **60.8% of jobs are in hotels or restaurants.**
- **Lötschberg Tunnel** connects to Goppenstein in Valais, with **car shuttle trains** through the 15 km passage. Regional express trains to Bern and Brig.
- **Lake Oeschinen (Oeschinensee)** — UNESCO, one of the most photographed lakes in Switzerland.
- **Kandersteg International Scout Centre**, 16,000+ scouts a year.
- **⚠️ GEOLOGICAL RISK: active landslide monitoring at Spitzer Stein, with rock masses "moving at velocities exceeding 10 cm daily during summer months."** **RE-VERIFY the 2027 status — this has previously closed trails and prompted evacuations in the Oeschinensee area.**
- Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kandersteg
- **⚠️ COST NOTE: the Kandersteg–Oeschinensee cable car was REMOVED from the Berner Oberland Pass for 2026** (§8B.7). It is 50% with the STP.
- **Assessment: a strong, much quieter alternative to the Lauterbrunnen valley, 50 min from Interlaken and 1h from Bern, and it is the gateway to the Valais via the Lötschberg. Not car-free. The Spitzer Stein landslide is the one thing to check.**

### 4A-3. Engadin bases

**St. Moritz — ~1,800 m, Graubünden**
- **"The highest Swiss municipality with a railway station."** **Both the Glacier Express and Bernina Express stop here**; the **Albula and Bernina lines terminate** here (RhB).
- Overlooks **Lake St. Moritz**, below **Piz Nair (3,056 m)**; **Corviglia** via the St. Moritz–Corviglia funicular.
- **"Over 300 days of sunshine a year."** ~**13,000 beds.** Upscale; invented Alpine winter tourism (Caspar Badrutt's 1864 wager).
- Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Moritz
- **Assessment: expensive and self-consciously glamorous, but the rail position is unmatched — it is the ONLY place where both flagship scenic trains meet.** The 300-days-of-sunshine figure and the Engadin's high, dry continental climate make it a second good weather hedge alongside the Valais.
- **⚠️ Its lifts REJECT the Swiss Travel Pass, Half Fare and GA outright (§8B.3) — the guest card is the only cheap route, and it requires 2 nights (§8B.8). Also: the Corviglia funicular was closed ALL summer 2026 for overhaul; 2027 status GAP.**

**Pontresina — 1,805 m, Graubünden**
- **"Often overshadowed by its more famous neighbour St. Moritz."** Population **2,178** (2020). Documented from 1137; tourism from an 1850 inn (2,000 guests by summer 1885); boosted by the **Bernina Railway in 1908**.
- **Pontresina station on the Bernina line, regular service to St. Moritz and Tirano.** Five further stations within the municipality serving key alpine points.
- Contains **Piz Bernina — the highest mountain in the canton** — plus **Piz Palü** and Piz Zupò. **Morteratsch Glacier** and **Val Roseg**.
- Heritage: Church of Santa Maria, Grand Hotel Kronenhof.
- Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontresina
- **⭐ Assessment: PONTRESINA BEATS ST. MORITZ AS A BASE for this trip.** Same altitude, same Engadin guest card, same Bernina line, ~12 min apart — but quieter, cheaper, and **closer to the actual scenery** (Morteratsch, Diavolezza, Val Roseg, Alp Grüm). It is also **the better starting point for the St. Moritz→Tirano Bernina segment**, which is the one worth doing. If the Dolomites chain brings you in from Tirano, **Pontresina is the natural first Swiss base.**

### 4A-4. Jungfraujoch — the numbers that drive the decision

- **Jungfraujoch saddle: 3,463 m**, between the Jungfrau and Mönch. **The railway station at 3,454 m is the highest in Europe.**
- Accessible by the **Jungfrau line since 1912**; the tunnel took **16 years** from 1896.
- **Kleine Scheidegg → Jungfraujoch takes ~50 min including stops; the return is 35 min.**
- **Sphinx Observatory at 3,572 m** — one of the highest astronomical observatories in the world, reached by lift from the station.
- Sits on the upper snows of the **Aletsch Glacier, Europe's longest alpine glacier.**
- Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jungfraujoch

**Decision inputs pulled together from across this document:**

3.
