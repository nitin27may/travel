# 🪨 Rockwood Conservation Area — Day Trip

> The everything-in-one-spot pick. Limestone cliffs, caves to poke through, some of the world's largest **glacial potholes**, the photogenic ruins of an old woollen mill, cliff-top trails over a reservoir, and an on-site **sandy beach for swimming** — plus canoe rentals to paddle among the cliffs. All in one gated park, ~1h45 from Whitby. Low-stress, high-variety.

## Trip profile

- **Outing type:** Explore (caves, ruins, potholes) + beach swim
- **Drive radius:** ~155 km / ~1h45 each way
- **Time:** Full day
- **Group:** Family with school-age kids
- **Transport:** Car

## The plan

> [!map] **Route:** [Open the day in Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/dir/?api=1&origin=Whitby%2C%20ON%2C%20Canada&destination=Rockwood%20Conservation%20Area%2C%20161%20Fall%20St%20S%2C%20Rockwood%2C%20ON&travelmode=driving)

**Leave Whitby by 7:30 am.** Day-use opens 8 am; an early arrival means an easy parking spot and a cooler morning for the trails.

**~9:30 am — Trails, caves, potholes & mill ruins** ⭐
- [Rockwood Conservation Area](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Rockwood%20Conservation%20Area%2C%20161%20Fall%20St%20S%2C%20Rockwood%2C%20ON) — hike the **Pothole Trail** and **Cedar Ridge Trail** (both moderate) along cliff tops, across wetland boardwalks, and past the glacial potholes. Explore the **caves** with a flashlight, and climb around the ruins of the old **Harris Woollen Mill** by the Eramosa River. ~2–3 hrs at an easy pace.

**~12:30 pm — Picnic lunch** by the reservoir.

**~1:30 pm — Beach swim**
- The park's **sandy beach** has summer swimming with a life-jacket loaner program. Cool off and let the kids play.

**~2:30 pm — Canoe or kayak the reservoir** (rentals on site)
- Paddle the flooded quarry/reservoir among the limestone cliffs — the most memorable way to see the park, and tweens love steering. ~1 hr.

**~3:45 pm — Wind-down: ice cream** in Rockwood village or nearby Acton/Guelph. 🍦

**~4:30 pm — Drive home** (~1h45).

> [!tip] **Interactive add-on / rainy-day swap:** Guelph is ~15 min away for indoor options if weather turns (caves and potholes get slick when wet). For a bigger-ticket alternative, [African Lion Safari](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=African%20Lion%20Safari%2C%201386%20Cooper%20Rd%2C%20Hamilton%2C%20ON) is ~30 min south — a drive-through safari kids love (book ahead; pricier).

## Booking & gotchas

- **No reservation needed** — pay day-use at the gate (or self-register/ePass when the gatehouse is unstaffed). Open all year, 8 am–sunset.
- **Fees:** ~$7.75/adult incl. tax; child/senior rates lower. Canoe rental is extra.
- **Caves:** bring a flashlight; passages are tight and dark. Wear shoes with grip.
- **Beach:** life jackets available to borrow — use them for weaker swimmers.

## Packing

- Hiking shoes with grip + a flashlight each for the caves
- Swimsuits + towels + change of clothes
- Sunscreen, hats, water, picnic lunch
- Quick-dry clothes if you're canoeing
