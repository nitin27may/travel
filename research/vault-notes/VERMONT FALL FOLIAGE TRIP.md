**Why Stowe as base:** Stowe is widely considered the Fall Foliage Capital of Vermont. It sits at the foot of Mount Mansfield (Vermont's tallest peak) surrounded by the Green Mountains. Everything you need — scenic drives, world-class hiking, waterfalls, craft beer, covered bridges, and family activities — is within a 15–30 minute drive. For 3 families, basing here for all 3 nights avoids the friction of moving lodging mid-trip, while still allowing a day trip south to Woodstock/Quechee.

## Day-by-Day Itinerary

### DAY 1: THE DRIVE + EVENING IN STOWE

**Wednesday, October 1 | Whitby, ON → Stowe, VT**

**6:00–7:00 AM — Depart Whitby** Head east on 401. Pack snacks, car chargers, passports/NEXUS cards. Kids can sleep in the car for the first stretch.

> _TIP: Fill up gas in Ontario — it's cheaper than Quebec/Vermont._

**~10:00 AM — Border Crossing at Highgate Springs** I-89 crossing into Vermont. With NEXUS, expect 5–15 min. Without, budget 20–45 min during weekday morning.

> _TIP: Smaller crossings like Noyan/Alburgh can avoid peak-season backups at Highgate._

**~1:00–2:00 PM — Arrive in Stowe** Check into accommodation. The drive from the border through Vermont on I-89 is already stunning in early October — rolling hills, farms, and color everywhere.

**3:00–5:00 PM — Stowe Village Walk + Stowe Recreation Path** Stretch your legs after the long drive. Walk the charming village center, browse shops. The 5.3-mile paved Recreation Path along the West Branch River is perfect for families — rent bikes or just walk a section.

**5:30–7:30 PM — Dinner + Trapp Family Bierhalle** Head up to the von Trapp Bierhalle for Austrian-style lagers brewed on-site with mountain views. Alternatively, try Harrison's Restaurant or Piecasso Pizzeria in the village for family-friendly dining.

> _TIP: The Bierhalle has a casual, family-friendly vibe. Great intro to the Stowe scene._

---

### DAY 2: STOWE AREA — HIKES, DRIVES & WATERFALLS

**Thursday, October 2 | Full Day in Stowe**

**8:00–8:30 AM — Early Breakfast** Cook at the rental or grab coffee/pastries at Black Cap Coffee & Beer or PK Coffee.

> _TIP: Get an early start — trails and Smugglers' Notch get crowded by 10 AM during peak foliage._

**9:00–11:30 AM — Morning Hike (Pick One)** See the trail guide below. Stowe Pinnacle is the top pick for views. Bingham Falls is easiest for young kids. Sterling Pond for a mountaintop reward.

**12:00 PM — Lunch in Stowe** The Bench for upscale casual, Doc Ponds for burgers and craft beer, or pick up sandwiches from Harvest Market for a picnic.

**1:30–4:00 PM — Smugglers' Notch Scenic Drive (Route 108)** The most dramatic drive in Vermont. The winding road through a narrow mountain pass with 1,000-foot cliffs and blazing fall color is unforgettable. Stop at the boulders near the top for photos. Kids will love climbing the massive rocks.

> _TIP: The road is narrow and winding — drive slowly. Park at pulloffs, not on the road._

**2:30 PM — Stop: Moss Glen Falls (Stowe)** Just 3 miles north of Stowe off Route 100. A 68-foot waterfall with a short, moderate trail. Gorgeous with fall color framing the cascade.

**4:00–5:00 PM — Cold Hollow Cider Mill (Waterbury)** Famous for fresh apple cider donuts — a Vermont fall tradition. Free cider samples, local products. 15 min from Stowe.

> _TIP: If you're beer fans, The Alchemist Brewery (home of Heady Topper) is right nearby._

**5:30 PM — Ben & Jerry's Factory Tour (Waterbury)** Optional — great for kids. 30-minute guided tour of the ice cream factory with samples. Book online in advance.

**Evening — Dinner & Hot Tub** Cook a group dinner at the rental, fire up the grill, and end the day in the hot tub under the stars. Or try Plate for a farm-to-table experience.

---

### DAY 3: DAY TRIP — WOODSTOCK, QUECHEE & ROUTE 100

**Friday, October 3 | Southern Vermont Loop**

**7:30 AM — Early Departure South on Route 100** Vermont's most scenic highway — 216 miles through the Green Mountains. You'll drive the middle section south from Stowe through Warren and into central Vermont. The road follows rivers, passes covered bridges, and runs through tiny villages.

> _TIP: Pack snacks and lunch supplies from your rental kitchen._

**9:00 AM — Stop: Moss Glen Falls (Granville)** Different from the Stowe one — this is a roadside waterfall right off Route 100 with a short boardwalk. Quick 10-minute stop for photos.

**9:30 AM — Stop: Warren Store** A beloved Vermont general store with great coffee, baked goods, and deli sandwiches. Grab provisions for the day.

**10:30 AM — Arrive Woodstock Village** Walk the picture-perfect village green, browse shops, see the Middle Covered Bridge. Everything is within 500 feet. The town decorates beautifully for fall with pumpkins and harvest displays on every storefront.

**11:00 AM–12:30 PM — Hike: Mount Tom or Mount Peg** Mt. Tom via Faulkner Trail offers panoramic views of Woodstock and rolling hills in fall color. Moderate difficulty, great for mixed-age families. Mt. Peg is shorter/easier with views of the village and valleys.

**12:30 PM — Lunch at Simon Pearce (Quechee)** 15 min east of Woodstock. Restaurant overlooks a waterfall on the Ottauquechee River. Watch artisans blow glass in the basement studio. Pricier but a unique Vermont experience.

> _TIP: Reserve ahead — this place fills up during foliage season._

**2:00 PM — Quechee Gorge** Vermont's Little Grand Canyon — a 165-foot deep gorge. View it from the Route 4 bridge, or hike the 2.7-mile Quechee Gorge Trail loop along the rim and down to the river.

**3:00 PM — VINS Nature Center (Optional)** Forest canopy walk through the treetops — surprisingly uncrowded even during peak foliage. Great for kids. Adults $19.50, kids $16.50.

**3:30 PM — Pomfret Backroads Loop** Drive the 18-mile loop through rolling hills, past historic farms, and along Silver Lake. Some of the most photographed Vermont countryside. Note: Cloudland Road near Sleepy Hollow Farm is closed to non-residents during foliage, but the surrounding Pomfret backroads are open and just as beautiful.

**5:00–6:30 PM — Drive Back to Stowe** Take Route 100 North back. Different light, different views. Stop at Sugarbush Farm on the way if time allows — free tastings of maple syrup and cheese.

**Evening — Final Night Dinner** Splurge on a nice dinner at Harrison's Restaurant, Hen of the Wood (Waterbury), or The Dining Room at Trapp Family Lodge for Austrian-inspired cuisine.

---

### DAY 4: MORNING HIKE + DEPARTURE

**Saturday, October 4 | Stowe → Whitby**

**7:30–9:00 AM — Sunrise/Morning Hike: Sunset Rock** Just 1.2 miles, takes about 37 minutes. Short trail through woodland to a rocky outcrop with views of the surrounding mountains and valley. Perfect send-off.

**9:30 AM — Check Out + Pack Up** Load up the cars. Grab coffee and cider donuts from the village for the road.

**10:00 AM — Optional: Burlington Detour** If not in a rush, swing through Burlington (45 min west). Walk the Church Street Marketplace, grab lunch on the Lake Champlain waterfront. Adds about 1 hour to the overall trip.

**~4:00–5:00 PM — Arrive Whitby** Total drive 6–6.5 hours. The Quebec stretch on Autoroute 10 can have construction — budget extra time.

## Trail Guide: Best Fall Hikes

### Must-Do Hikes in Stowe

#### Stowe Pinnacle

|Difficulty|Distance|Elevation|
|---|---|---|
|Moderate|3.8 mi roundtrip|+1,520 ft|

THE quintessential Stowe foliage hike. Steady climb through woods to an open rocky summit with 360-degree views. You feel completely immersed in fall color. One of the most photographed viewpoints in Vermont. Go early or on weekdays to avoid crowds.

#### Bingham Falls

|Difficulty|Distance|Elevation|
|---|---|---|
|Easy–Moderate|1.2 mi roundtrip|+200 ft|

A stunning 40-foot waterfall in a narrow gorge, accessible via a short trail from Route 108. The cascade is framed by fall foliage and carved rock formations. Best option for families with younger kids. Trail can be slippery near the bottom — sturdy shoes recommended.

#### Sterling Pond Trail

|Difficulty|Distance|Elevation|
|---|---|---|
|Moderate|2.4 mi roundtrip|+1,000 ft|

Climbs from Smugglers' Notch to Vermont's highest-elevation trout pond at 3,000 feet. The mountaintop pond surrounded by fall color is the reward. Can combine with a longer loop to Spruce Peak. Great for older kids who want an adventure.

#### Mt. Elmore (Hidden Gem)

|Difficulty|Distance|Elevation|
|---|---|---|
|Moderate|4.4 mi roundtrip|+1,438 ft|

Fire tower at the summit provides panoramic views of the Worcester Range in full fall color. Much less crowded than Stowe Pinnacle. Loop option via Ridge Trail (steeper) and Fire Tower Trail (gradual). 20 min from Stowe.

#### Kirchner Woods (Easy Family Walk)

|Difficulty|Distance|Elevation|
|---|---|---|
|Easy|~2 mi loop|Minimal|

Casual trail system under magnificent sugar maple canopy. The perfect leaf-peeping walk — especially beautiful in early October when the maples are peak color. Flat, well-maintained paths ideal for strollers and young children.

### Hikes in Woodstock/Quechee Area

#### Mt. Tom (Faulkner Trail)

|Difficulty|Distance|Elevation|
|---|---|---|
|Moderate|2.6 mi roundtrip|+600 ft|

Switchback trail to the summit with sweeping views of Woodstock village and surrounding hills in fiery fall colors. The Faulkner Trail zigzags gently making it family-friendly. Can extend to The Pogue, a scenic pond in Marsh-Billings-Rockefeller National Park.

#### Quechee Gorge Trail

|Difficulty|Distance|Elevation|
|---|---|---|
|Easy–Moderate|2.7 mi loop|+300 ft|

Walk along the rim of Vermont's deepest gorge (165 ft). Trail descends to the river with swimming holes. The gorge walls draped in fall foliage are spectacular from the Route 4 bridge. You can shorten to just the bridge viewpoint for a 5-minute stop.

## Practical Tips

### What to Pack

**Layers are essential.** Early October in Vermont can range from 5–18C (40–65F). Mornings are crisp, afternoons can be warm in the sun, and mountain summits are significantly colder.

- Fleece/light jacket for mornings
- T-shirt for afternoon sun
- Warm jacket for summit hikes (Mt. Mansfield summit can be near freezing)
- Waterproof layer for unpredictable rain
- Sturdy hiking shoes/boots (trails can be muddy and rocky)
- Binoculars for scenic overlooks
- Camera/phone chargers
- Reusable water bottles

### Border Crossing Tips

Bring valid passports for all travelers (NEXUS cards if you have them). Declare any food items — citrus and some produce cannot be brought across. Keep vehicle registration and insurance docs handy. If renting a car, confirm the rental agreement covers cross-border travel.

### Driving Tips

Vermont gas stations can be sparse on Route 100 — fill up when you can. Cell service can be spotty in mountain areas (download offline maps). Speed limits on Route 100 range from 25 mph in villages to 50 mph between towns. Watch for frost heaves (bumps in the road) especially on back roads. Moose are active at dawn and dusk — drive carefully.

### Money & Currency

Vermont uses USD. Most places accept credit cards, but some farm stands and small stores are cash-only. Withdraw USD from ATMs in Vermont for best rates.
