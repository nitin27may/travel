# Place Discovery Playbook

How to find the full range of places for a destination — must-sees, hidden gems, nature, relaxation, adventure, food — using live web research. Load this in Phase 2.

## Categories to cover

Sweep every category the user's vibe touches. Don't stop at the obvious landmarks — the value of this skill is breadth plus the non-obvious.

- **Must-see** — the iconic, can't-miss landmarks. Cover them, but don't let them eat the whole trip.
- **Hidden gems** — local favourites, lesser-known spots, places guidebooks skip. See the dedicated section below.
- **Nature / scenic** — viewpoints, parks, waterfalls, scenic drives, lakes, beaches, gardens, sunrise/sunset spots.
- **Relaxing / slow** — cafes, spas, easy strolls, picnic spots, quiet corners, somewhere to do nothing well.
- **Adventure** — hikes, water sports, climbing, zip lines, caves, cycling, tubing, anything with a pulse. Note difficulty and any height/age/fitness requirements.
- **Food** — signature local dishes, standout restaurants, markets, street food, dessert/ice-cream stops, a special-occasion dinner.
- **Culture / history** — museums, heritage sites, old towns, religious/architectural sites, local crafts.
- **Family-friendly** — when kids are along, flag what works for their ages: splash pads, easy trails, interactive museums, beaches, animal encounters.
- **Seasonal / events** — what's actually happening during the dates: festivals, foliage, blooms, markets, harvests, migrations.

## Search query patterns

Run multiple targeted searches rather than one broad one. Useful patterns (substitute the destination, season, and year):

- `<place> hidden gems local favourites`
- `<place> things to do <season> <year>`
- `best <category> near <place>` — viewpoints, hikes, beaches, restaurants, etc.
- `<place> off the beaten path` / `<place> what locals do`
- `<place> with kids` / `<place> family <season>` (when applicable)
- `<place> events <month> <year>` / `<place> festivals <season>`
- `<region> scenic drive` / `<region> day trips from <base>`
- `<specific attraction> hours tickets <year>` — to confirm current logistics
- `<place> itinerary <N> days` — to sanity-check pacing against how others route it

Fetch the promising pages (travel blogs, official park/tourism sites, recent Reddit threads) for specifics — hours, prices, the actual hidden-gem names — rather than trusting search snippets. Prioritize recent and official sources for hours and prices, which go stale fast.

## Finding genuine hidden gems

The differentiator. To get past the recycled top-10 lists:

- Search local subreddits and forums (`r/<city>`, `<place> reddit hidden gems`) — locals name spots tourists miss.
- Look for "where locals eat / locals' favourite", neighbourhood guides, and small-town side trips near the main destination.
- Check what's one valley/town/exit over from the famous spot — the quieter alternative to the crowded icon.
- Note free or low-cost spots with outsized payoff (a short walk to a great view, a free lookout, a town swimming hole).
- Verify each gem is currently open and accessible before committing it to the plan.

## What to capture per place

For every place that makes the shortlist, record:

| Field | Why it matters |
|-------|----------------|
| Name | Identity, and feeds the map pin |
| Category | Drives variety and map color-coding |
| Why (one line) | The reason it's worth the stop |
| Time needed | Drives realistic day pacing |
| Cost | Feeds the budget and sets expectations |
| Best time | Crowds, light, tides, opening hours |
| Area / cluster | Lets you group stops geographically |
| Address / coords | Feeds the maps CSV and route links |
| Logistics note | Booking, closed days, height/age limits, weather-dependent, cell-dead zone |

## Practical intel to always pull

The existing vault travel notes are valued for hard logistics, not just lists. Always surface:

- **Crowd timing** — when lots fill, when to arrive, when crowds thin.
- **Advance-booking deadlines** — timed-entry tickets, campsite release dates/times, popular restaurants.
- **Money-saving passes** — see `budget.md`; for Ontario/Canada day trips the Ontario Parks pass and Parks Canada pass are recurring wins.
- **Parking and access** — where to park, lot-fill risk, transit alternatives (e.g. GO Train from Whitby), offline-map needs.
- **Wind-down spots** — an ice-cream stop, waterfront bench, or patio to end the day rather than sitting in traffic.
- **Backup options** — an indoor or rain alternative for weather-dependent days.
