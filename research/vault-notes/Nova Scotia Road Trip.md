# Nova Scotia Road Trip

11-day road trip from home through Quebec, New Brunswick, Cape Breton, and Nova Scotia.

**Total distance:** ~5,500 km return | **Travel dates:** 13 August – 23 August 2026

> [!important] Book Before You Leave
> - **Skyline Trail parking** — reservations open May 6, 2026 at reservation.pc.gc.ca ($13/slot). Books out fast for August.
> - **Harbour Hopper Halifax** — summer slots sell out, book ahead at harbourhopper.com
> - **Parks Canada Discovery Pass** — buy before departure. Note: Parks Canada has a free admission program (June 19–Sept 7) — your trip (Aug 13–23) falls within this window, so **entry may be free**. Verify at parks.canada.ca closer to departure.
> - **Auberge Gisele's Inn** — small inn, books out in August peak season. Confirm reservation at giseles.com

## Itinerary

### Day 1 — Home → Rivière-du-Loup (900 km)

**Stay:** Hôtel Levesque (13 August) · 171 rue Fraser, Rivière-du-Loup

**Drive notes:** ~5 hr from Montreal via A-20 East. Rest stops roughly every 50–80 km. Fill gas before leaving — prices rise past Quebec City.

**On arrival:**
- Kids wind-down: the hotel has an **indoor pool** — great after a long drive day
- Evening walk: **Parc des Chutes de la Rivière-du-Loup** is 5 min from the hotel. Free municipal park with a 33-metre waterfall, gorge lookouts, and a pedestrian bridge. Budget 45–60 minutes.

**Hotel highlights:** Restaurant on-site, free parking, elevator access, indoor pool, EV charging.

---

### Day 2 — Rivière-du-Loup → Moncton (556 km)

**Stay:** Chateau Moncton Trademark Collection by Wyndham (14 August)

> [!tip] Route Option — Scenic Detour via Lac-Saint-Jean (+1.5 hr)
> The direct route to Moncton via Trans-Canada takes ~5 hours. If you want to add **Parc le Trou de la Fée** and **whale watching at Pointe-Noire/Tadoussac**, route via Lac-Saint-Jean (Route 169) and Chicoutimi instead. Add ~1.5–2 hours to the day but worth it if kids are up for it.

**Activities (if taking scenic route):**

**Parc le Trou de la Fée** (Desbiens, QC) — ~3.5 hr from RDL via Lac-Saint-Jean
- 7 suspended pedestrian bridges over a narrow canyon gorge, 35 m high rock walls
- Best for kids 6+. Bridges sway — kids must be comfortable with heights.
- Wear proper shoes (no sandals). Not stroller-accessible.
- ~$40–45 CAD family admission. Open ~9 AM – 5 PM (6 PM peak summer).
- Snack bar at entrance. Nearest full meal: Alma (~35 km).

**Whale Watching — Pointe-Noire, Baie-Sainte-Catherine**
- Parks Canada interpretation centre at the mouth of the Saguenay Fjord
- One of the most reliable spots in the world to see **belugas from shore**. Naturalist guides explain what you're seeing.
- Family pass: $18.50 CAD. Youth 17 and under: free.
- Open from late June through mid-October. Arrive and scan the water — belugas are frequently visible within 20–30 minutes.
- **Free Tadoussac ferry** (10-min crossing) often produces sightings from the deck. Queue can be 30–90 min in summer — worth it.

**Parc National du Fjord-du-Saguenay** (if time allows)
- Entry: $9.50/adult, $4.75/child (SEPAQ), under 6 free
- [Activities](https://www.quebecmaritime.ca/en/company/parc-national-du-fjord-du-saguenay/activities) | [AllTrails](https://www.alltrails.com/parks/canada/quebec/saguenay-fjords-national-park--3)

**If taking direct Route (no Lac-Saint-Jean):**

**Parc des Chutes** trails en route
- [Boucle de Monte-à-Peine](https://www.alltrails.com/trail/canada/quebec/boucle-de-monte-a-peine) — 5.1 km, ~1h 30m
- [Desjardins le Plateau](https://www.alltrails.com/trail/canada/quebec/desjardins-le-plateau-et-sentier-d-interpretation) — 2.7 km, ~45 min

### Day 3 — Moncton → Baddeck (418 km)

**Stay:** Auberge Gisele's Inn, Baddeck (15 August)

**Drive notes:** ~4 hr. Fill up in Moncton — gas is cheaper in NB than NS.

**Activities in Moncton (morning, then drive):**

**Hopewell Rocks** ⭐ — must do with kids
- Walk on the **ocean floor** between 15-metre sea stack rock formations. At high tide the same floor is 15 m underwater.
- Admission: Adult $15.85, Child (5–18) $9.06, Under 5 free. **Tickets valid 2 consecutive days** — consider if you want low tide one day, high tide the next.
- Admission includes both ocean floor access (low tide) and upper viewing decks.
- **Tide timing is critical:** Ocean floor access is open 3 hrs before to 3 hrs after low tide. Check the NB Parks tide table at [nbparks.ca](https://www.parcsnbparks.ca) before you go. The tide window rotates through the day — plan your entire morning around it.
- Wear water shoes or old sneakers on the ocean floor — not bare feet.
- Facilities: café, interpretive centre, picnic areas, playground, good washrooms.
- [Hopewell Rocks](https://www.tripadvisor.ca/AttractionProductReview-g499179-d11991515-Hopewell_Rocks_Admission-Hopewell_Cape_Atlantic_County_New_Brunswick.html)

**Magnetic Hill**
- Optical illusion where your car appears to roll uphill. ~$5/vehicle. Takes 5 minutes but kids love it.
- **Magnetic Hill Zoo** adjacent: Adult $14.99, Child (4–11) $10.99, Under 3 free. Atlantic Canada's largest zoo, ~2 km walking route, toddler carts available.
- Also nearby: Magic Mountain Water Park (big draw for kids if weather is warm, separate admission).
- [Magnetic Hill info](https://www.tripadvisor.ca/Attraction_Review-g154958-d183715-Reviews-Magnetic_Hill_Park-Moncton_New_Brunswick.html)

**Moncton Tidal Bore** — free
- Watch the incoming Bay of Fundy tide reverse the Petitcodiac River flow. The bore (wave) arrives twice daily.
- Best viewing: **Bore Park**, 10 Bendview Court, downtown Moncton.
- Check exact times at [tidalboretimes.ca](https://tidalboretimes.ca) — times shift daily. Arrive 30 min early, bore can be 20 min early or late.
- Stay 30–45 min after the bore to watch the river fill dramatically.
- [Tidal Bore info](https://www.tripadvisor.ca/Attraction_Review-g154958-d183715-Reviews-Tidal_Bore-Moncton_New_Brunswick.html)

**Centennial Park** (if kids need energy outlet)
- Free park with miniature train (~$3–4/ride), splash pad, playgrounds, duck pond with paddle boats.
- Best for kids 2–10. Budget 1.5 hours. Seasonal attractions run through late August — fully operational during your visit.

### Day 4 — Baddeck / Cabot Trail East

**Stay:** Auberge Gisele's Inn (16 August)

**Drive notes:** Ingonish is 1.5 hr from Baddeck. Plan an early start to get time at the beach before Middle Head hike.

> [!note] Auberge Gisele's Inn — Baddeck Base
> Central village location, walking distance to the Alexander Graham Bell National Historic Site and evening ceilidhs (traditional Cape Breton music — kids enjoy these). The inn has a restaurant, garden grounds, and fireplaces in many rooms. Book well in advance for August peak season.

**Activities:**

**Ingonish Beach** ⭐ (inside Cape Breton Highlands NP)
- Unique beach with a **natural barachois** — a sandbar separating the Atlantic Ocean from a freshwater lake. Two supervised swimming options: cold salt water (waves) or warm, calm **freshwater lake**. Kids strongly prefer the lake.
- Lifeguards on duty July–August. Your August visit will have full lifeguard coverage on both the ocean and freshwater lake sides.
- Facilities: picnic tables, washrooms, changing areas, playground, tennis courts.
- Park entry required (see [[#Parks Canada Entry & Costs]]).

**Middle Head Trail** — 4.5 km, ~2 hr
- Peninsula trail with the **Atlantic Ocean on both sides** — stunning. Rocky headland with views of Cape Smokey, Ingonish Island, seals, and bald eagles.
- Easy to moderate. Some rocky sections and exposed roots. Fine for school-age kids.
- Trailhead starts at the **Keltic Lodge** grounds — one of Canada's grand historic resort hotels. Beautiful setting.
- Combine with Ingonish Beach for a full afternoon.
- [Parks Canada](https://www.pc.gc.ca/en/pn-np/ns/cbreton/activ/randonnee-hiking/middlehead)

**Cabot Trail Drive — Half Day (clockwise toward Baddeck)**
Drive the eastern section back via Cabot Trail, stopping at Cape Smokey.

### Day 5 — Baddeck / Cabot Trail West

**Stay:** Auberge Gisele's Inn (17 August)

**Drive notes:** Drive counterclockwise today — Baddeck → Margaree → Chéticamp → Skyline Trail → Pleasant Bay → back to Baddeck. Full loop is 298 km, ~4.5 hr non-stop. With stops and the Skyline hike, budget 10–11 hours.

> [!important] Cabot Trail Direction
> **Drive counterclockwise** (north from Baddeck along the west coast). You stay on the outside lane relative to the ocean — better views. The west coast (Chéticamp → Pleasant Bay) in morning light, then Skyline Trail at late afternoon for the sunset.

**Activities:**

**Uisge Ban Waterfall Hike** — 1.5 km, easy stroll
- Easy 1.5 km walk to a beautiful waterfall. A gentle start for the day.
- Good warm-up before the Skyline Trail in the evening.

**Cabot Trail Stops Counterclockwise:**
- **Margaree Valley** — scenic river valley, salmon fishing culture
- **Chéticamp** — Acadian fishing village, good lunch stop. La Bella Bella or Restaurant Acadian for traditional cuisine.
- **Lone Shieling** — 15-min loop walk through old-growth maple forest. Peaceful. A reconstructed Scottish stone shieling (shepherd's hut). Perfect for a leg-stretch with kids.
- **Pleasant Bay** — whale watching and zodiac tours. Mid-point fuel and food stop (Rusty Anchor restaurant).
- **Clucking Hen Cafe & Bakery** — quirky bakery between North River Kayaking and Ingonish Beach. Good coffee and pastries, fun photo stop.

**Skyline Trail** ⭐⭐ — MUST DO — 9.5 km loop, ~2.5–3 hr
- The most iconic hike in Nova Scotia. Forested plateau trail ending on a **dramatic headland boardwalk** with the Cabot Trail winding 300 m below and the Gulf of St. Lawrence stretching to the horizon.
- **Start at 5:30–6:00 PM** to hit the headland at golden hour — sunset is around 8:00–8:15 PM in mid-August on Cape Breton. This is a genuine once-in-a-life experience at sunset.
- Families with toddlers have done this trail. The boardwalk at the headland is safe. Keep close watch on young children near cliff edges.
- **No dogs permitted** (they disturb wildlife).
- **Moose sightings common** in the forested section — do not approach.
- Elevation gain: modest. Mostly level with one notable climb near the start.
- Bring headlamps — you'll finish in low light if timing for sunset.
- Pack snacks and water. Nothing at the trailhead.
- [AllTrails](https://www.alltrails.com/trail/canada/nova-scotia/skyline-trail-loop)

> [!warning] Skyline Trail Parking Reservation — Required 2026
> From June 26 to October 25, 2026, paid parking reservation required ($13 online). Reservations open **May 6, 2026** at reservation.pc.gc.ca — book immediately when they open. 4-hour timeslots, last entry 5:00 PM. For sunset hiking, book the last available slot.

### Day 6 — Baddeck → Halifax (350 km)

**Stay:** Chateau Bedford, Trademark Collection by Wyndham (18 August)

**Leave Baddeck at 2 PM.** Drive via Trans-Canada/Highway 105 to Highway 104 to Halifax.

> [!note] Chateau Bedford Location
> The hotel is in **Bedford**, a suburb ~20–25 km from downtown Halifax (25–35 min by car). Not walkable to the waterfront. Free parking. Good suburban location — easy highway access for day trips. For the next two days, you'll be driving in to Halifax centre (~30 min each way).

**En route stop — Cape Smokey Provincial Park** (if leaving earlier)
- Short hike with panoramic ocean views. Or take the gondola to the summit (Cape Smokey ski area, operating in summer).

### Day 7 — Halifax → Hall's Harbour day trip

**Stay:** Chateau Bedford (19 August)

**Activities:**

**Hall's Harbour** ⭐ — Bay of Fundy tidal spectacle
- A tiny fishing village with one of the most dramatic tidal swings you'll see. Boats sit on dry ocean floor at low tide; the same boats float 14–16 metres higher at high tide.
- **Time your visit around tides** — check [tide-forecast.com](https://tide-forecast.com) for Hall's Harbour station before driving out. Best: arrive at low tide, watch the boats on the floor, then wait 1–2 hours for the tide to rush in.
- **Hall's Harbour Lobster Pound** — pick a live lobster, they cook it, eat it overlooking the water. Quintessentially Nova Scotian. Seasonal (May–Oct), confirm at [hallsharbourlobster.com](https://hallsharbourlobster.com).
- Other things: walk the rocky beach, watch fishing boats from the wharf, browse small artist studios.
- ~1.5 hr from Halifax (Bedford). No washrooms beyond the restaurant facilities.

**Drive back via Annapolis Valley** (scenic) or direct via Hwy 101.

### Day 8 — Halifax (local day)

**Stay:** Chateau Bedford (20 August)

**Activities — Halifax Waterfront** ⭐

Park once downtown (~$15–25/day in a parkade) and walk everything. The waterfront is 4.4 km of connected boardwalk.

**Free:**
- **Submarine playground** on the waterfront boardwalk — one of the best playgrounds in the city
- **Wave monument** — kids slide down it
- Walk the entire Harbourfront boardwalk
- Window shopping along Historic Properties

**Paid:**
- **Discovery Centre** (1215 Lower Water St) — 4 floors of hands-on science, dome theatre. Youth (3–18): $7.00, Under 2 free. Budget 2–3 hours. Very good for kids.
- **Maritime Museum of the Atlantic** — ship models, Titanic artifacts, HMCS Sackville corvette. Child (6–17): $5.10, Under 6 free, Adult: $10.95. One of the best-value museums on the trip.
- **Harbour Ferry to Dartmouth** — ~$3–4/person, 10-min crossing. Kids love it. Good harbour view.
- **Halifax Citadel** (on the hill above downtown) — worth 1.5–2 hr for older kids interested in history/fortifications. Changing of the Guard ceremonies.

**Harbour Hopper** — amphibious bus, land tour + drives into harbour
- Adult: ~$53.72, Youth (6–15): ~$29.90, Child (2–5): ~$18.55, Under 2 free
- Duration: 1 hour. Kid-friendly — the moment it drives into the water is the highlight.
- Book ahead: [harbourhopper.com](https://www.harbourhopper.com)

**Food on the waterfront:** Murphy's on the Water (casual, boardwalk), Bicycle Thief (nicer dinner). Fish and chips at multiple spots.

### Day 9 — Halifax → Fredericton (436 km)

**Stay:** Fredericton Inn (21 August)

**Drive notes:** ~4 hr via Trans-Canada. Mostly four-lane divided highway.

**If arriving with energy — Fredericton quick stops:**
- **Odell Park** — 400 acres of old-growth forest in the city. Arboretum, duck pond, walking trails. Free.
- **Science East** — hands-on science museum downtown. Adult ~$9, Child ~$7, Family ~$25. Good for kids 5–12. Budget 2 hours.
- **The Green** — riverfront promenade on the Saint John River. Good post-dinner walk.
- **Officers' Square** — free public space. Changing of the Guard ceremony (weekday mornings ~11 AM in summer, free).

**Dinner:** The Palate or Isaac's Way (vegetarian-friendly) downtown.

## Parks Canada Entry & Costs

**Discovery Pass (family/group — covers up to 7 people in a vehicle):** $136.40 CAD — covers all Parks Canada sites for 12 months including Cape Breton Highlands NP, Alexander Graham Bell NHS, Fortress of Louisbourg, and all others on the route.

**Daily family/group pass:** ~$21.00/day for 2 adults (youth 17 and under always free).

> [!important] Free Parks Canada Admission — August 2026
> Parks Canada announced free admission June 19–September 7, 2026 (Canada Strong program). Your trip (Aug 13–23) falls within this window. **Verify this program is still active for 2026** at parks.canada.ca closer to departure. If it is, the Discovery Pass may not be needed for entry — but the Skyline Trail parking reservation ($13) is a separate fee regardless.

**What park entry covers:** All trails, Ingonish Beach, park roads and viewpoints. Does not cover camping.

**Skyline Trail parking reservation (separate fee):** $13 online, $15 by phone. Book at reservation.pc.gc.ca starting May 6, 2026.

**Fortress of Louisbourg** (if adding a day trip from Baddeck area — 1.5 hr each way):
- The largest reconstructed 18th-century French fortress in North America. Excellent for kids with actors in period costume. Worth a half-day if you have a spare day.
- Adult ~$18.70, Child (6–16) ~$9.55, Family ~$47.40.
- [Parks Canada — Louisbourg](https://parks.canada.ca/lhn-nhs/ns/louisbourg)

## Driving & Family Tips

**Cabot Trail direction:** Drive **counterclockwise** from Baddeck (north on west coast first). Outside-lane ocean views, Skyline Trail in evening light.

**Gas up in Moncton** before entering Nova Scotia — NB fuel is consistently cheaper than NS.

**Cell service:** Non-existent through much of Cape Breton Highlands interior. Download offline maps (Google Maps or maps.me) before entering the park. Same for Route 138 through Charlevoix.

**Moose warning:** Real risk on Route 138 through Charlevoix, rural NB, and Cape Breton after dark. Avoid driving rural highways after 9 PM.

**Driving with kids:** Segment days to 4–5 hr max with a half-day stop. The A-20 rest areas at Kamouraska (near RDL) have good St. Lawrence river views — good stretch break.

**A-20 Quebec City traffic:** Avoid the Lévis/QC City A-20/A-40 interchange on Friday 3–6 PM in August. Use A-73 bypass on the south side.

**Tadoussac ferry** (if taking scenic route Day 2): Free 10-min crossing. Queue can be 30–90 min in peak summer. Worth it for deck-level beluga sightings.
