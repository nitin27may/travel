# Ontario Family Cycling Guide: 20 Trails Near Whitby

Ontario families based near Whitby have access to 20 outstanding cycling trails — most built on converted railways — offering flat, car-free routes ideal for school-age kids. This guide covers each trail with surface type, difficulty, bike rental options, cool-down spots, and nearby hike recommendations to round out a full day.

See also: [[Day Trips Local]] for destination guides to 40 Ontario attractions within 500 km.

---

## Before You Ride

### Gear and legal requirements

Helmets are mandatory by Ontario law for riders under 18. Adults are strongly encouraged. Bring helmets from home — trail-side rentals often have limited stock in kids' sizes and fit quality varies.

Bike type is the single most important planning decision. Paved trails (Niagara River, Ottawa pathways, Whitby–Oshawa Waterfront, Kingston, Ajax) work with any bicycle. Crushed limestone and gravel trails (Millennium Trail, Kawartha Trans Canada, Victoria Rail Trail, Algonquin Old Railway) require hybrid or mountain bikes — road bikes with thin tires will struggle and puncture. Check the surface type before renting or loading up the car.

For school-age kids: saddle height should let the child touch the ground with the ball of the foot (not flat-footed). Gears help significantly on any ride over 10 km, even flat ones. Padded shorts eliminate seat soreness on rides over 8 km — worth buying before a longer trail day.

### What to pack

- Helmets — bring your own, don't rely on rental stock
- Water — minimum 750 mL per person per 10 km; top up at trailheads
- Snacks and lunch for trails without nearby food (many rail trails pass through nothing for long stretches)
- Sunscreen (SPF 50+) and UV lip balm — paved trails in open sun are relentless
- Bug spray (DEET or picaridin-based) — shaded rail trails have mosquitoes and blackflies from May through July
- Basic repair kit: spare inner tube matched to your tire size, tire levers, mini pump — most rental shops lend these but don't count on it
- Small first aid kit: bandages, antiseptic wipes, blister pads
- Phone mount or handlebar bag for maps, music, emergencies
- Cash for trail-side ice cream stops — many rural vendors are cash-only
- Light rain jacket — weather turns fast near Georgian Bay and the Great Lakes
- Dry bag or zip-lock for phones and valuables near any water access

### Safety on the trail

- Ride single-file on busy multi-use trails; give pedestrians the right of way
- Call out "on your left" before passing — don't assume people hear you coming
- Stop completely at road crossings — rail trails cross active roads, often without traffic lights
- Keep kids in front of you on unfamiliar sections so you can see them at all times
- Many rural trail sections have no cell service — download the route offline before leaving, and tell someone where you are going
- In Algonquin and other wilderness trails: make noise around blind corners and carry bear spray

### GO Train bike access

GO Transit allows bikes on off-peak trains at no extra cost and no reservation required. Rush-hour restrictions (6–9 AM outbound, 3:30–6:30 PM inbound on weekdays) prohibit non-folding bikes. This means families can ride the GO to the trailhead car-free.

| Route | GO Station | Trail Access |
|---|---|---|
| Lakeshore East | Whitby GO | Whitby–Oshawa Waterfront Trail |
| Lakeshore East | Oshawa GO | Waterfront Trail east extension |
| Lakeshore East | Ajax GO | Ajax Waterfront Trail |
| Lakeshore West (seasonal) | Niagara Falls GO | Niagara River Recreational Trail |

Check GO Transit's bike policy page before heading out — occasional service disruptions or special event restrictions apply.

### Seasonal notes

Most trails are best from May through October. Key exceptions:

- Algonquin Outfitters bike rentals open spring through Thanksgiving weekend
- Elora Cataract Trailway section between Hillsburgh and Erin closed until early 2027 (sewer construction)
- Cobourg Marina bike rentals operate June–October only
- NCC Weekend Bikedays in Ottawa (May–October) close select parkways to cars — plan Ottawa rides around these dates for the best experience

## Local rides from Whitby

Before tackling trails further afield, these are the best options right outside your door — all accessible without loading bikes into a car.

### Whitby–Oshawa Waterfront Trail (local segment)

The home trail. The Heydenshore Kiwanis Park to Lakeview Park segment (8–10 km) is the best family ride: entirely off-road on 3-metre paved asphalt, dead flat, and lined with wetlands, old-growth white pines at Thickson's Woods, beaches, and playgrounds. GO Train accessible from Whitby station.

**Cool-down:** Lakeview Park in Oshawa has a splash pad and beach access. In summer, the Oshawa waterfront has concession stands. Lynde Shores Conservation Area on the west end has sheltered picnic areas and benches overlooking the wetlands.

**Nearby hike:** Thickson's Woods Nature Reserve has a short loop through genuinely ancient old-growth pines — one of the last stands of its kind in Durham Region, right beside the trail. Lynde Shores Conservation Area boardwalk trails are flat, birdwatcher-friendly, and stroller-accessible.

### Ajax Waterfront Trail

A 7 km paved path — the first fully off-road community section of the entire 3,600 km Great Lakes Waterfront Trail — connecting Whitby and Pickering, passing Rotary Park, Duffin's Creek bridge, and wetland crossings. Short, flat, and entirely car-free. GO Train accessible from Ajax station.

**Cool-down:** Rotary Park has benches with lake views and a small beach area. Ajax downtown is a 5-minute ride for food and coffee.

**Nearby hike:** Duffin's Creek Wetland Trail (flat boardwalk through a provincially significant wetland, excellent birdwatching year-round).

### Oshawa Creek Trail / Joseph Kolodzie Bike Path

A 7 km paved path winding from Lakeview Park through ravines to the Oshawa Valley Botanical Gardens. Free, scenic, and connects to the regional Waterfront Trail. The botanical gardens have 300+ peony varieties at peak bloom late May through June.

**Cool-down:** The gardens themselves provide shade and benches. Lakeview Park splash pad is at the south end. Nearby Parkwood Estate has a café.

## The 20 best family trails ranked

### 1. Niagara River Recreational Trail — Niagara-on-the-Lake to Queenston

The gold standard for Ontario family cycling. This 56 km paved trail runs along the Niagara River, but the prime family segment is the 12 km from Niagara-on-the-Lake to Queenston — flat, fully separated from traffic, river views, wineries, fruit stands, and NOTL Old Town as the reward destination. Distance markings every 500 m help kids track progress. Zoom Leisure Bikes in Old Town rents children's bikes with helmets. Avoid the narrow urban section under Rainbow Bridge with young riders.

- **Surface:** paved asphalt, 2.5 m wide
- **Difficulty:** easy
- **Bike rental:** Zoom Leisure Bikes, NOTL
- **Rating:** TripAdvisor 4.5/5

**Cool-down:** Old Town Niagara-on-the-Lake has several ice cream options on the main strip. The waterfront at Fort George provides a small beach with river views. Queenston Heights Park at the north end has a picnic area, interpretive trail, and views across the Escarpment.

**Nearby hike:** Queenston Heights Trail (Bruce Trail access point, short loop along the Niagara Escarpment with river and gorge views). Fort George grounds are free to walk around the exterior.

**Watch out for:** Strong winds off the river in spring and fall — dress in layers. NOTL gets extremely crowded on summer weekends; ride early morning or after 4 PM to avoid pedestrian congestion on the shared path. The segment under the Rainbow Bridge at Niagara Falls is narrow and busy — skip it with kids and stick to the NOTL-to-Queenston stretch.

### 2. Georgian Trail — Collingwood to Meaford

A 34 km converted railway along Georgian Bay's south shore with virtually zero elevation change (0.012% grade). The Collingwood-to-Thornbury segment (21 km) passes trestle bridges, apple orchards, and beach access points, ending in Thornbury with ice cream shops and restaurants. Hard-packed crushed limestone rides almost like pavement with over 30 access points.

- **Surface:** crushed limestone
- **Difficulty:** easy
- **Bike rental:** several shops in Collingwood
- **Rating:** AllTrails 4.5/5 (500+ reviews)

**Cool-down:** Thornbury's bakeries and ice cream shops at the far end. Georgian Bay beach access at several mid-trail points for a swim. Craigleith Provincial Day Use Area has picnic tables and bay access.

**Nearby hike:** Nottawasaga Lookout Provincial Nature Reserve (short but rewarding climb with panoramic Georgian Bay views). Blue Mountain trails connect from the Collingwood end and offer easy escarpment-edge walking.

**Watch out for:** The crushed limestone surface tires out kids on mountain bikes with aggressive tread — hybrid or gravel bikes perform best here. Very exposed to weather with few sheltered spots; check the forecast before heading out.

### 3. Whitby–Oshawa Waterfront Trail

The closest world-class trail to home. This 19 km paved path hugs the Lake Ontario shoreline from Lynde Shores Conservation Area through to Second Marsh — entirely off-road on 3-metre-wide asphalt. The recommended family segment runs 8–10 km from Heydenshore Kiwanis Park to Lakeview Park.

- **Surface:** paved asphalt, 3 m wide
- **Difficulty:** easy, dead flat
- **Bike rental:** no trail-side rentals; bike shops nearby in Oshawa and Whitby
- **GO Train:** Whitby and Oshawa stations

**Cool-down:** Multiple beach access points along the full trail. Lakeview Park (Oshawa end) has a splash pad and playground. Rotary Park (Whitby end) has benches and picnic tables overlooking the lake.

**Nearby hike:** Thickson's Woods Nature Reserve (loop through old-growth white pines — 10 minutes off the trail). Lynde Shores Conservation Area boardwalk trails are flat, family-friendly, and excellent for birdwatching.

**Watch out for:** Heavy pedestrian and dog traffic on summer weekends. Start before 9 AM or ride on weekday mornings for the best experience.

### 4. Algonquin Park Old Railway Bike Trail

The best wilderness cycling experience in Ontario. This 16 km one-way trail follows the 1896 Ottawa, Arnprior & Parry Sound Railway through pristine Algonquin wilderness between Rock Lake and Cache Lake. Families routinely spot moose, beavers, and loons.

- **Surface:** hard-packed gravel with some rocky patches
- **Difficulty:** easy (park designation); hybrid or mountain bike essential
- **Bike rental:** Algonquin Outfitters at Lake of Two Rivers (~$50/day for mountain bikes, tandems, and kids' bikes)
- **Rating:** TripAdvisor and AllTrails 4.5/5

**Cool-down:** Lake of Two Rivers Café at the rental base does excellent post-ride ice cream — plan for this. Cache Lake has a picnic area with lake access at the far end.

**Nearby hike:** Beaver Pond Trail (2 km loop, prime moose sighting territory — best very early morning or at dusk). Track & Tower Trail (7.7 km, follows an old railway bed through varied terrain — great for fit older kids).

**Watch out for:** This is a wilderness trail. Cell service is essentially zero for the entire length. Bring a paper map, make noise around blind corners, and carry bear spray. Algonquin Outfitters opens spring through Thanksgiving weekend only — rental availability drops off after Labour Day. The gravel can be loose in spots; flat tires happen.

### 5. Ottawa Rideau Canal Pathway

Cycling alongside a UNESCO World Heritage Site through the heart of Canada's capital. The 7.8 km paved pathway from Parliament Hill to Hog's Back Falls is flat, immaculate, and packed with sights: Château Laurier views, Dow's Lake Pavilion, Commissioners Park, and the Corktown Footbridge.

- **Surface:** paved asphalt
- **Difficulty:** easy, entirely flat
- **Bike rental:** RentABike under the Plaza Bridge beside the Château Laurier; VeloGO bikeshare (600+ bikes, 80+ stations)

**Cool-down:** Dow's Lake Pavilion at the midpoint offers a restaurant, pedal boat rentals, and canal views — excellent lunch or ice cream stop. Multiple shaded benches and park areas along the full canal.

**Nearby hike:** Rideau River Nature Trail connects near Hog's Back Falls (flat, pleasant, follows the river). Commissioners Park tulip garden (peak mid-May, free, one of Ottawa's best spring experiences) is a short walk off the path.

**Watch out for:** NCC Weekend Bikedays (May–October) close select parkways to car traffic — plan visits around these dates for the best riding experience. Check the NCC website for the current season's schedule before booking accommodations.

### 6. Ottawa River Pathway

Extending 31 km west from Parliament Hill along the Ottawa River. The best family segment is the 10 km round trip to Remic Rapids — home to famous balanced-rock sculptures with Parliament Hill views across the water.

- **Surface:** paved asphalt
- **Difficulty:** easy, flat
- **Bike rental:** same as Rideau Canal — RentABike and VeloGO bikeshare

**Cool-down:** Westboro Beach has a sandy beach directly off the pathway — a perfect mid-ride swim stop. Andrew Haydon Park (near the turnaround point) has a splash pad, picnic facilities, and great river views.

**Nearby hike:** Remic Rapids itself is worth a walking exploration once you lock up bikes — the balanced-rock formations are a genuine curiosity. The Ottawa River Pathway connects to Gatineau Park trails via the Portage Bridge for those wanting longer wilderness walks.

**Watch out for:** The pathway gets very busy near Parliament Hill on weekday lunch hours and summer weekends. The NCC Weekend Bikedays schedule applies here too — same dates as the Rideau Canal.

### 7. 1000 Islands Parkway Trail — Gananoque to Butternut Bay

A 37 km fully paved, fully separated path along the St. Lawrence River through the 1000 Islands region. The standout family segment is Mallorytown Landing to Rockport (16 km one-way) — Thousand Islands National Park playground, stunning island views, Canadian Shield rock outcroppings, and restaurants at Rockport village. Recently repaved.

- **Surface:** paved asphalt, recently resurfaced
- **Difficulty:** easy
- **Bike rental:** Ahoy Rentals, Gananoque waterfront (bikes and kayaks)

**Cool-down:** Rockport village has restaurants and a waterfront patio at the end. Brown's Bay Day-Use Park (mid-route) has picnic areas and St. Lawrence River access. Mallorytown Landing has a playground and small sandy beach.

**Nearby hike:** Thousand Islands National Park has short interpretive trails at Mallorytown Landing (flat, family-friendly). Jones Creek Trail (2.5 km loop, flat riverside walking, good for spotting river wildlife).

**Watch out for:** Combine this trail with a [[Day Trips Local|1000 Islands boat cruise]] the same day for a full adventure. Brown's Bay Day-Use Park is free for cyclists — one of the few spots along the trail with guaranteed shade.

### 8. Prince Edward County Millennium Trail

A 46 km rail trail on a former CN Railway bed from Carrying Place to Picton through Ontario's wine country. Firm crushed limestone surface, dead flat (207 ft total elevation gain over the full length). Well-appointed trailheads with bike repair stations.

- **Surface:** crushed limestone
- **Difficulty:** easy
- **Bike rental:** Bloomfield Bicycle Company, County Bike Rentals (multiple options in PEC)
- **Best segments:** Wellington to Bloomfield (10 km) or Bloomfield to Picton (12 km)

**Cool-down:** Bloomfield has several food stops mid-trail — a natural lunch break town. Picton main street at the end has good ice cream and cafés. The trail sits 10 km from [[Day Trips Local|Sandbanks Provincial Park]] — combine a morning ride with an afternoon beach swim.

**Nearby hike:** Macaulay Mountain Conservation Area in Picton (easy trails through mixed forest, great fall colour). Lake on the Mountain (free, short walk to a mysterious lake perched 60 m above the Bay of Quinte — a genuinely curious geological feature).

**Watch out for:** ATVs are permitted on some sections of the trail — watch for them, especially on weekends. Not suitable for road bikes or bikes with narrow tires. Hybrid or mountain bike essential.

### 9. Victoria Rail Trail — Lindsay to Fenelon Falls

The 85 km trail from Lindsay to Kinmount follows a former CN rail line built in 1874, but the 22 km Lindsay-to-Fenelon Falls segment is the family sweet spot. Resurfaced crushed limestone, flat grades, shaded cedar corridors, and Sturgeon Lake views.

- **Surface:** crushed limestone
- **Difficulty:** easy
- **Bike rental:** limited; check Fenelon Falls bike shops in-season

**Cool-down:** Fenelon Falls town at the end is the reward — Kawartha Dairy ice cream (a regional institution and a genuinely excellent stop), Murphy's Lockside Pub for patio lunch overlooking Lock 34. The falls themselves are a natural cool-down photo spot.

**Nearby hike:** The Trent-Severn Waterway channel at Lock 34 has a pleasant walking path along the water. The Victoria Rail Trail continues north to Kinmount for families wanting more distance.

**Watch out for:** Services are sparse between Lindsay and Fenelon Falls — pack all food and water before starting. The trail can be muddy for several days after significant rain.

### 10. Welland Canal Trail — St. Catharines to Port Colborne

A 45 km mostly paved trail (99% asphalt) following the Welland Canal with the unique experience of watching 226-metre ocean freighters navigate eight locks. The Lock 7 Viewing Complex at Thorold — where ships are raised 42.5 metres — is the highlight.

- **Surface:** paved asphalt (99%)
- **Difficulty:** easy to moderate (one significant Escarpment climb at Thorold)
- **Bike rental:** Lock 8 Gateway Park
- **Best segment:** St. Catharines to Thorold (12 km) to see the flight locks

**Cool-down:** Lock 7 Viewing Complex at Thorold has a café and viewing terrace — plan a food stop here. Port Robinson has a free seasonal ferry crossing for cyclists — a fun novelty that kids enjoy.

**Nearby hike:** Short walking paths around Lock 1 at Port Colborne waterfront. The canal-side path between locks is pleasant as a walking trail for non-cyclists in the group.

**Watch out for:** The Escarpment climb at Thorold is the only real hill on an otherwise flat canal trail — warn kids before you reach it. Lock schedules are unpredictable; you may arrive at a lock mid-operation or between ships. The Port Robinson ferry is seasonal only.

### 11. Kawartha Trans Canada Trail — Lindsay to Peterborough

A 53.8 km crushed limestone rail trail — 3 metres wide, flat, and well-maintained with no motorized vehicles allowed on the full length.

- **Surface:** crushed limestone
- **Difficulty:** easy
- **Best segment:** Lindsay to Omemee (15 km one-way)

**Cool-down:** Omemee is a small town with basic services — a good turnaround point. Lindsay has a full range of options for a post-ride meal; downtown Lindsay is 10 minutes from the trailhead.

**Nearby hike:** The trail passes through significant wetland habitat with good birdwatching opportunities. Doube's Trestle Bridge (undergoing fire damage repairs as of 2025 — confirm current status before visiting) was a trail highlight; check whether repairs are complete.

**Watch out for:** Services are sparse between towns — pack all food and water for the full segment. Hybrid bikes essential; narrow tires will struggle on crushed limestone.

### 12. Elora Cataract Trailway

A 47 km rail trail on a former CP Rail corridor connecting Elora to the village of Cataract near Forks of the Credit Provincial Park.

- **Surface:** crushed stone, 3 m wide
- **Difficulty:** easy to moderate
- **Best segment:** Elora to Belwood Lake (15 km)
- **Important:** section between Hillsburgh and Erin is closed until early 2027 (sewer construction) — plan your segment around the open portions

**Cool-down:** After the ride, combine with [[Day Trips Local|Elora Gorge]] for swimming or tubing in the river. The village of Elora has excellent restaurants and the famous Shepherd's Pub for a post-ride meal.

**Nearby hike:** Elora Gorge Conservation Area trails follow the limestone gorge rim above the river — striking views, easy walking. Grand River trail connections at the Elora end for extended walking.

**Watch out for:** Verify the exact open sections before visiting — the multi-year Hillsburgh–Erin closure significantly shortens the usable trail. The gorge trails are separate from the bike trail; leave bikes locked at the Elora trailhead to walk the gorge.

### 13. Presqu'ile Provincial Park Loop

An 8 km paved road loop with a dedicated cycling lane through this birding paradise on Lake Ontario. Flat terrain, lighthouse destination, sandy beaches, and a marsh boardwalk as a walking side trip.

- **Surface:** paved road with dedicated cycling lane
- **Difficulty:** easy, flat
- **Bike rental:** none on-site — bring your own
- **Entry:** no vehicle entry fee for cyclists (one of Ontario's few parks with this policy)

**Cool-down:** Outlet Beach at the end — sandy, family-friendly Lake Ontario swimming with a gentle slope. The park store sells ice cream in summer. Presqu'ile Point Lighthouse has a picnic area with lake views and interpretive signage.

**Nearby hike:** Marsh Boardwalk Trail (1.2 km, elevated boardwalk through extensive wetland — rated 4.7 stars, accessible, and excellent for birdwatching — 338+ recorded species including significant spring and fall migrations).

**Watch out for:** No on-site bike rentals — this trail requires bringing your own bikes. Book day-use parking online up to 5 days in advance for summer weekend visits; this park fills up.

### 14. Iron Horse Trail — Kitchener-Waterloo

A 5.5 km fully paved urban rail trail connecting Waterloo Park (playgrounds, splash pad, and animal enclosures) to Victoria Park (lake, gardens, and food vendors). Extendable to 8 km via the Laurel Trail connection.

- **Surface:** paved asphalt
- **Difficulty:** easy, flat, year-round snow-cleared maintenance
- **Google rating:** AllTrails 4+/5 (575 reviews), 250,000+ annual users

**Cool-down:** Victoria Park has a lake, benches, and summer food vendors. Waterloo Park splash pad at the north end makes a perfect start or end-point for younger riders. Nearby downtown Waterloo has coffee shops and ice cream.

**Nearby hike:** Waterloo Park has short walking paths through the animal enclosures (free). St. Jacobs trails are a 15-minute drive north for a more rural walking experience with a farmers' market nearby.

**Watch out for:** The trail's popularity means it gets very busy on summer weekends — go on weekday mornings. This is a good urban training trail for kids before tackling longer rural rail trails.

### 15. Kingston Waterfront Pathway

An 8.3 km paved waterfront ride from Lake Ontario Park to Emma Martin Park, passing Portsmouth Olympic Harbour, Gord Edgar Downie Pier, Fort Henry, and Breakwater Park Beach.

- **Surface:** paved asphalt
- **Difficulty:** easy (one steep hill at Fort Henry is the only challenge)
- **Bike rental:** Ahoy Rentals and I-Cycle Electric Bike Co. on the waterfront (children's bikes available)

**Cool-down:** Breakwater Park Beach mid-route for a Lake Ontario swim. Gord Edgar Downie Pier has a splash pad. Kingston waterfront has excellent restaurants and ice cream directly off the path.

**Nearby hike:** Fort Henry grounds are free to walk around the exterior (paid admission for the guided interior tours). The Kingston Waterfront Trail extends to Portsmouth Olympic Harbour for a pleasant flat walk.

**Watch out for:** The hill at Fort Henry is the one significant grade on an otherwise flat route — worth warning kids before you reach it. Pair this trail with [[Day Trips Local|Fort Henry]] and a 1000 Islands cruise for a full Kingston day.

### 16. Ajax Waterfront Trail

The first fully off-road community section of the entire 3,600 km Great Lakes Waterfront Trail. This 7 km paved path connects Whitby and Pickering sections, passing Rotary Park, Duffin's Creek bridge, and wetland crossings. Short, flat, entirely car-free.

- **Surface:** paved asphalt
- **Difficulty:** easy, flat
- **GO Train:** Ajax station

**Cool-down:** Rotary Park has lake views, benches, and a small sandy area. Ajax waterfront has a park area suitable for a picnic.

**Nearby hike:** Duffin's Creek Wetland Trail (flat boardwalk through a provincially significant wetland — excellent birdwatching, short and easy).

**Watch out for:** At 7 km, this trail is excellent for introducing beginner riders to off-road cycling. Too short for experienced riders as a standalone trip — combine with the Whitby–Oshawa Waterfront Trail for a longer day.

### 17. Cobourg Waterfront Trail

A 5–8 km paved waterfront ride with new cycling boardwalk sections through one of Ontario's most charming Lake Ontario towns.

- **Surface:** paved asphalt and new boardwalk sections
- **Difficulty:** easy, flat
- **Bike rental:** Cobourg Marina — Experience Cobourg program (June–October only; helmets, locks, and maps included)

**Cool-down:** Victoria Beach is right on the trail — natural white sand, splash pad, and playground, perfect as a post-ride reward. The heritage main street is 5 minutes from the waterfront for ice cream and lunch. The Mill Restaurant in a historic 1830 building overlooks the creek.

**Nearby hike:** Cobourg Ecology Garden walking paths (short, quiet, good for a wind-down walk). Hibernia Road wetland area (informal walking).

**Watch out for:** Bike rental is June–October only — no trail-side rental option in shoulder seasons. The trail is short; pair it with a beach afternoon for a full day.

### 18. Lynn Valley Trail — Port Dover to Simcoe

A 10 km one-way rail trail (20 km round trip) through deep shaded woods along the Lynn River, featuring four original trestle bridges with decking and railings. The canopy provides 95% shade — ideal for hot summer days when other trails are exposed.

- **Surface:** hard-packed gravel
- **Difficulty:** easy
- **Bike rental:** Long Point Eco-Adventures near Turkey Point
- **Rating:** AllTrails 4.7/5 (connected rail trail)

**Cool-down:** Port Dover beach town at the south end — fish and chips are a local institution (Erie Beach Hotel Fish & Chips is the classic stop). The Lynn River itself is suitable for wading at several points along the trail.

**Nearby hike:** Norfolk County rail trail connections at the Simcoe end for extended riding or walking. Port Dover waterfront walk along the harbour.

**Watch out for:** Hard-packed gravel works fine for mountain and hybrid bikes but can be unpleasant after rain when the surface softens in spots. The shade makes this trail one of the best hot-weather options on the list — save it for July and August days when other trails are punishing.

---

### 19. Wasaga Beach Cycling Paths

A network of trails including the Schoonertown Trail, beachside paths, and the 15 km Wasaga-to-Collingwood connector linking to the Georgian Trail.

- **Surface:** mix of paved paths, stone dust, and some on-road sections
- **Difficulty:** easy to moderate
- **Bike rental:** Wasaga Beach Nordic Centre (fat bikes available)

**Cool-down:** Wasaga Beach is the obvious reward — the world's longest freshwater beach is right there. Beach swimming after any ride on this network. Beach Areas 4–5 have the best facilities closest to the trails.

**Nearby hike:** Nancy Island Historic Site (short walk, War of 1812 museum and lighthouse — free in summer). Wasaga Beach Provincial Park has nature trails through the dune ecosystem behind the beach.

**Watch out for:** The Wasaga-to-Collingwood connector uses some on-road segments — be aware of traffic, especially on summer weekends. The beach path itself gets extremely crowded in July and August; go early or on weekdays. Fat bikes available at the Nordic Centre for off-season riding.

---

### 20. Long Sault Parkway — near Upper Canada Village

A unique 20 km route hopping across 11 islands connected by bridges and causeways on the St. Lawrence River. The 9.4 km paved Waterfront Trail section from Upper Canada Village to Ingleside passes through the Upper Canada Migratory Bird Sanctuary.

- **Surface:** mixed (paved waterfront section; Parkway road shared with light car traffic)
- **Difficulty:** easy
- **Bike rental:** none nearby — bring your own bikes

**Cool-down:** Brown's Bay Beach on the Long Sault Parkway — St. Lawrence River swimming with a gravel/sand beach and picnic area. Upper Canada Village (10-minute drive) has a restaurant for a post-ride lunch.

**Nearby hike:** Upper Canada Migratory Bird Sanctuary trails (200+ bird species, flat paths along the river). Lost Villages Museum grounds include a walk along the shoreline of the original village sites flooded for the St. Lawrence Seaway in 1958.

**Watch out for:** No bike rental options anywhere nearby — this trail requires bringing your own bikes. The Parkway road section is shared with cars but traffic is typically very light on weekdays. Combine with [[Day Trips Local|Upper Canada Village]] the same day for a history-and-cycling combo.

## Planning summary

**Nature wins on ratings, rail trails win on wheels.** Ontario's converted railway corridors deliver the flattest, safest, most scenic family rides because they follow century-old rail grades engineered for gentle slopes. For a family based near Whitby, a set of hybrid bikes unlocks every trail on this list and connects to a network spanning thousands of kilometres across the province.

**GO Train access** removes the need to drive for Whitby–Oshawa, Ajax Waterfront, and seasonal Niagara access. Check the GO Transit bike policy and plan departure times around the rush-hour restriction window.

**Provincial parks reward cyclists** — Presqu'ile waives the vehicle entry fee for cyclists entirely. Most other parks charge $7.50–$21 per vehicle for day use.

**Closures and seasonality matter.** The Elora Cataract Trailway section between Hillsburgh and Erin is confirmed closed until early 2027. Cobourg bike rentals run June–October only. NCC Weekend Bikedays in Ottawa close select parkways to cars on designated dates from May through October — the best cycling weekend experience in the province.
