# The ultimate Whitby family trip guide: 40 destinations for 2026

**Whitby, Ontario sits at a geographic sweet spot for family adventures.** Within a 500 km radius, families with school-age kids can access world-class beaches, provincial parks, adventure parks, living history sites, and amusement destinations spanning three seasons of fun from April through October. This guide covers 40 researched destinations organized by distance, with practical details on costs, timing, and itineraries to make planning effortless.

Two money-saving programs deserve attention before diving in. The **Ontario Parks Annual Vehicle Pass** ($112) pays for itself after 6–7 park visits and covers every provincial park in this guide. The **Parks Canada Strong Pass** (June 19–September 7, 2026) provides **free admission to all national parks and historic sites** plus 25% off camping — a massive savings for families hitting Rouge, Bruce Peninsula, Fort Henry, Upper Canada Village, and the Trent-Severn locks.

## Essential tips for day trips from Whitby

### Timing to avoid crowds

Summer weekends at beach destinations and provincial parks follow a predictable pattern: parking lots fill by 10 AM and reach capacity before noon. Go on weekdays whenever possible. If the weekend is the only option, arrive by 8–8:30 AM. Cobourg Beach, Sandbanks, and Wasaga Beach see peak congestion between 11 AM and 3 PM — build your day around arriving early and leaving before the afternoon rush, or staying through until after 5 PM when crowds thin.

### What to pack by destination type

**Beach and waterpark days:** sunscreen SPF 50+ (reapply every 90 minutes — bring more than you think you need), UV lip balm, rash guard for kids, waterproof bag for valuables, dry change of clothes per person, towels, water shoes for rocky lake entries, packed snacks and drinks to avoid on-site food prices.

**Provincial park hikes:** bug spray (DEET or picaridin-based — essential on shaded trails from May through July), trail snacks, water (minimum 500 mL per person per hour of hiking), proper footwear (sneakers minimum — no flip-flops on rocky trails), offline maps downloaded before leaving home, small first aid kit with blister treatment.

**Amusement and adventure parks:** closed-toe shoes (mandatory at most aerial and water attractions — check requirements before packing sandals), sun hat, portable phone charger, lock for cubby storage, lunch packed in a cooler or plan to eat before noon before lines build.

**Heritage and museum days:** comfortable walking shoes, camera, and modest budget for admission. Most destinations have on-site dining or nearby restaurants within walking distance.

### What to watch out for

**Cell service:** Bon Echo, Algonquin, Silent Lake, and several Muskoka destinations have poor to no cell coverage. Download Google Maps offline for your route before leaving home. Tell someone where you're going.

**Parking:** Provincial park day-use lots fill faster than campgrounds. Book day-use parking online through Ontario Parks up to 5 days in advance — worth doing for summer weekend visits to Sandbanks, Presqu'ile, and Forks of the Credit.

**Food costs:** On-site food at amusement parks (Canada's Wonderland, African Lion Safari, Great Wolf Lodge) runs 2–3x restaurant prices. Pack a cooler lunch for any destination without a restaurant you actually want to eat at.

**Height and age requirements:** Adventure parks (Treetop Trekking, Elora Gorge, Scenic Caves zip line) enforce strict minimums. Confirm your kids' heights before buying tickets — several attractions require 55″ (140 cm) or 48″ (122 cm) minimums that can catch you off-guard.

**Weather-dependent activities:** Elora Gorge tubing, the Bruce Peninsula Grotto, and boat cruises (Niagara, Thousand Islands, Muskoka) are significantly better or worse depending on conditions. Have a backup plan on days with uncertain forecasts.

**Advance booking deadlines:** Ontario Parks releases camping reservations daily at 7:00 AM for the date exactly 5 months out. Sandbanks, Algonquin, Bon Echo, and Killbear fill within minutes. Have your account logged in, payment saved, and preferred sites selected before the clock strikes 7. Parks Canada (Bruce Peninsula, Rouge) reservations for Ontario opened February 2, 2026.

### Cool-down and wind-down spots by region

End the day properly rather than sitting in traffic. These spots make excellent final stops before the drive home.

**Oshawa / Whitby area:** Lakeview Park splash pad (free, open summer), Whitby waterfront Rotary Park benches with lake view, Oshawa Valley Botanical Gardens for a quiet evening stroll.

**Kawartha Lakes:** Kawartha Dairy in Bobcaygeon — the original location, genuinely excellent ice cream. The Lock 34 patio in Fenelon Falls is one of the nicest places in Ontario to eat lunch overlooking the Trent-Severn.

**Niagara Region:** NOTL main street ice cream (Ed Sulkowski's Homemade Ice Cream). Queenston Heights Park picnic area with Escarpment views.

**Collingwood / Blue Mountain:** Thornbury ice cream and bakeries after any Georgian Trail ride. Craigleith Provincial Day Use Area for a Georgian Bay swim.

**Peterborough:** Riverview Park splash pad (free, right in town). The Canoe Museum café overlooks the water and is open to non-museum visitors.

**Prince Edward County:** Slickers Ice Cream in Bloomfield. Lake on the Mountain — a free, beautiful 10-minute walk with a view that's completely out of proportion to the effort required.

**Kingston / Thousand Islands:** Breakwater Park Beach on the Kingston waterfront. Gord Edgar Downie Pier splash pad (free). Downtown Kingston's Princess Street has excellent ice cream options.

**Muskoka:** Bracebridge Falls (walk right in town, free, genuinely beautiful). Henrietta's Pine Bakery in Dwight on the way back from Algonquin.

**Ottawa:** BeaverTails at ByWard Market is mandatory. Dow's Lake Pavilion on the canal has pedal boats and a patio.

## Within a 30-minute drive: your backyard adventures

### 1. Darlington Provincial Park

**Bowmanville | ~20 km | 15 min** [Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/dir/Whitby,+Ontario/Darlington+Provincial+Park)

The closest provincial park to Whitby delivers a sandy Lake Ontario beach, sheltered McLaughlin Bay for beginner canoeing, and three solid hiking trails. The **Burk Trail** (1.25 km) climbs to a Lake Ontario lookout through a pioneer cemetery, while the McLaughlin Bay Trail offers marsh boardwalk walking with Great Blue Heron sightings. The free **TackleShare program** lets kids borrow fishing rods at no cost. Summer Discovery Programs run drop-in children's activities. Day use costs **$12–$21/vehicle**; camping runs $35–$55/night across 300+ sites. Pack a picnic — the park store sells ice cream but Bowmanville restaurants are just 10 minutes away. Best from May through October.

### 2. Treetop Eco-Adventure Park

**Oshawa | ~15 km | 15 min** [Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/dir/Whitby,+Ontario/Treetop+Eco-Adventure+Park,+53+Snow+Ridge+Court,+Oshawa)

The closest adrenaline fix to Whitby features 8 aerial courses, 10 zip lines, and 96 challenge elements in the forest canopy, crowned by the **734-foot Big Zip** finale. The Children's Course suits ages 6–10 (minimum 48″ tall), while youth/adult courses require reaching 5′11″ with hand raised — a key detail for this age group. The outdoor **iBOUNCE** trampoline nets add variety. Budget approximately **$100–$150 per person** for season passes; single-visit pricing varies by course. Open spring through fall. A perfect half-day paired with lunch in Oshawa.

### 3. Jungle Cat World Wildlife Park

**Orono | ~35 km | 25 min** [Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/dir/Whitby,+Ontario/Jungle+Cat+World,+Orono,+Ontario)

This intimate wildlife park houses **150+ animals** including lions, tigers, snow leopards, Arctic wolves, and jaguars, plus free-roaming alpacas, peacocks, and goats. The **daily guided feeding tour at 1:30 PM** is unmissable — keepers narrate while predators eat. The **Behind the Scenes program** ($125/person, ages 9+) lets kids meet three animals face-to-face; children under 12 get 50% off when accompanied by a paying adult. Admission runs $20/adult, **$10/child (3–12)**, with free parking. Open year-round, 10am–5pm. Allow 2–3 hours.

### 4. Oshawa Valley Botanical Gardens & Creek Trails

**Oshawa | ~10 km | 12 min** [Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/dir/Whitby,+Ontario/Oshawa+Valley+Botanical+Gardens)

**Completely free**, this network of paved trails along Oshawa Creek connects to the 1,400 km Waterfront Trail. The 7 km Joseph Kolodzie bike path winds from Lakeview Park through lush ravines to the gardens, which house **300+ peony varieties** (peak bloom late May–June during the Oshawa Peony Festival). Kids enjoy cycling the creek path, spotting spawning salmon in fall, and exploring the waterfall garden. No public washrooms at the gardens themselves. Best paired with a visit to nearby Parkwood Estate.

### 5. Rouge National Urban Park

**Toronto/Scarborough | ~40 km | 35 min** [Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/dir/Whitby,+Ontario/Rouge+National+Urban+Park)

Canada's first national urban park offers **free admission year-round** as a Parks Canada site. The **Vista Trail** delivers panoramic Rouge Valley views with adventurous steep sections that challenge active kids, while the easier **Harvest Trail** meanders through crop fields and marshland. **Rouge Beach Park** provides sandy Lake Ontario swimming at the river mouth. Parking costs $4/hour or $13/day (free with Canada Strong Pass summer 2026). Download the free Rouge App for GPS-guided trail adventures with kid-friendly reward games. Pack food — no restaurants inside the park. Best spring through fall; spectacular autumn colour.

### 6. Treetop Trekking Stouffville

**Whitchurch-Stouffville | ~50 km | 40 min** [Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/dir/Whitby,+Ontario/Treetop+Trekking+Stouffville)

The largest Treetop Trekking location in Ontario and the best aerial adventure option for older kids. The signature **Zipline & Aerial Game Trek** (3 hours, ages 9+, minimum 55″/140 cm tall) runs $49 for ages 9–11 and $57 for ages 12–15 — use promo code **TTT_Family_Rate** for family discounts. Also home to **Uplå**, North America's largest trampoline net park: 9,000 sq ft of interconnected nets, slides, and ramps suspended 30 feet high ($35/person, ages 7+, 2 hours). The **Treewalk Village** ($10–$27) provides a gentler option. Open March through November; Uplå runs year-round. Arrive 30 minutes early; show your booking confirmation at the gate to waive conservation area parking fees.

## 30 to 60 minutes: easy day trips

### 7. Toronto Zoo

**Scarborough | ~55 km | 40 min** [Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/dir/Whitby,+Ontario/Toronto+Zoo)

With **3,000+ animals across 10 km of walking trails**, the Zoo demands a full day. The award-winning **Tundra Trek** puts kids face-to-face with polar bears and Arctic wolves across 10 acres, while the African Rainforest Pavilion houses the world's largest indoor gorilla habitat. **Splash Island** (summer only) provides water play relief. Dynamic pricing means tickets range from **$23–$46/child** and **$34–$46/adult** — book online early on off-peak weekdays for the lowest prices. Parking is $20. The CityPASS bundle ($137 adult/$91 child) covers the Zoo plus four other Toronto attractions. Arrive at opening; start from the farthest pavilion and work back to avoid crowds. Download the Zoo Experience App for real-time keeper talk schedules.

### 8. Port Hope

**Port Hope | ~58 km | 39 min** [Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/dir/Whitby,+Ontario/Port+Hope,+Ontario)

This charming heritage town packs surprising variety into a half-day or full-day trip. The **Capitol Theatre** (a restored 1930 atmospheric cinema, National Historic Site) still shows movies. **Corbett's Dam Fish Ladder** explodes with action during the salmon run (mid-August to early October) when **up to 10,000 Chinook and Coho salmon** fight upstream. **Haute Goat Farm** offers alpaca walks, pig races, and butter tart fuel at the Screaming Goat Café (~$10+ per person). The sandy Port Hope Beach is uncrowded compared to Cobourg. Eat at **Trattoria Gusto** overlooking the river, or hunt down Port Hope's famous butter tarts at local bakeries. Combine with Treetop Trekking Ganaraska (30 minutes north) for a full adventure day. Most town activities are free; beaches are free.

### 9. Treetop Trekking Ganaraska

**Campbellcroft | ~65 km | 50 min** [Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/dir/Whitby,+Ontario/Treetop+Trekking+Ganaraska)

Set inside southern Ontario's largest forest, this location features the province's most challenging aerial course (the **Timberwolf**) alongside beginner-friendly options. Same pricing and age requirements as Stouffville: **ages 9+, 55″ tall, $49–$67/person**. The Ganaraska Forest itself offers extensive hiking and mountain biking trails. Night Treks add headlamp-only adventure after dark. Combine with Port Hope (30 min south) or Peterborough (30 min north) for a packed day. Free parking.

### 10. Cobourg Beach & Victoria Park

**Cobourg | ~69 km | 47 min** [Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/dir/Whitby,+Ontario/Victoria+Park+Beach,+Cobourg,+Ontario)

Routinely rated among Ontario's best town beaches, Cobourg's groomed sand, splash pad, and playground make it a reliable summer day trip. The walkable downtown along King Street serves ice cream and family dining. Waterfront cycling connects to the Cobourg East Pierhead Lighthouse. The **annual Sandcastle Competition** (first weekend of August) is a highlight. Parking runs **$5/hour or $40 for a day pass** (non-resident) — arrive early on summer weekends as lots fill fast. Eat at **The Mill Restaurant** in a historic 1830 building overlooking the creek. Best June through September.

### 11. Wet'n'Wild Toronto

**Brampton | ~75 km | 1 hr** [Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/dir/Whitby,+Ontario/Wet'n'Wild+Toronto,+Brampton)

Formerly Wild Water Kingdom, this **45-acre outdoor waterpark** features 30 slides, a half-million-gallon wave pool, lazy river, and zip line. Thrill rides like **Oh! Canada** (trapdoor freefall) and **Caribbean Chaos** suit adventurous older kids. **Gold Season Passes run ~$60 each** when buying 2+ (includes unlimited visits, free parking, and 2 friend tickets) — exceptional value versus daily gate prices. Open May through September. Go on weekdays to avoid long lines. Some visitor reviews note aging facilities, but the price-to-fun ratio beats many competitors.

### 12. Canada's Wonderland

**Vaughan | ~85 km | 1 hr** [Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/dir/Whitby,+Ontario/Canada's+Wonderland,+Vaughan)

Ontario's premier amusement park opens **May 3, 2026** with 200+ attractions and **18 roller coasters**, headlined by **AlpenFury** — Canada's tallest, longest, and fastest launching coaster in its first full season. Older kids who meet the 48″ height requirement can ride Flight Deck, Backlot Stunt Coaster, and Wonder Mountain's Guardian (3D dark ride). Taller kids (54″+) unlock Behemoth, Leviathan, and Yukon Striker. The **20-acre Splash Works** waterpark is included with admission (summer). A **Gold Pass at $99/person** delivers unlimited visits, free parking, and access to 40+ Six Flags parks — extraordinary value versus single-day tickets ($45+ each plus $30 parking). Weekdays in June or September offer the shortest lines. Budget **$190–$250 for a single-day family of three** (tickets + parking + meals).

### 13. Kawartha Lakes: Fenelon Falls & Bobcaygeon

**Fenelon Falls ~90 km / Bobcaygeon ~95 km | 1 hr 15 min** [Google Maps – Fenelon Falls](https://www.google.com/maps/dir/Whitby,+Ontario/Fenelon+Falls,+Ontario) | [Google Maps – Bobcaygeon](https://www.google.com/maps/dir/Whitby,+Ontario/Bobcaygeon,+Ontario)

The Kawartha Lakes corridor delivers a classic small-town Ontario summer day. Watch boats lock through **Lock 34 at Fenelon Falls** on the Trent-Severn Waterway (free to view; lockage free during Canada Strong Pass period June 19–Sept 7), then walk the swing bridges and admire the dramatic 7-metre falls. Rent kayaks or SUPs at **Garnet Graham Beach Park** (~$20–$40/hour). Drive 20 minutes to Bobcaygeon for the obligatory stop at **Kawartha Dairy** — the original home of Canada's beloved ice cream (~$5–$8/cone). Charming Colborne Street in Fenelon Falls offers **The Little Pie Shack** for butter tarts and **Murphy's Lockside Pub** for patio lunch overlooking the lock. Parking is free everywhere. A laid-back, affordable summer day trip best from May through October.

## 1 to 2.5 hours: solid day trips and easy overnights

### 14. Balsam Lake Provincial Park

**Kirkfield | ~100 km | 1 hr 20 min** [Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/dir/Whitby,+Ontario/Balsam+Lake+Provincial+Park)

A family camping favourite with a large sandy beach, free PFD lending, and the excellent **Lookout Trail** (2.6 km) through 10,000-year-old glacial channels to a panoramic kame viewpoint that fascinates curious kids. Canoe, kayak, and paddle boat rentals operate from the beach. The **Junior Naturalists passport program** lets kids earn stamps and certificates through guided activities. Fishing is strong (walleye, muskie, bass) with free TackleShare rod lending. Day use **$12–$21/vehicle**; camping $35–$55/night across 515 sites. **Coboconk** (5 km away) has go-karts and mini-putt; Fenelon Falls (20 min) adds dining options. Open mid-May to end of October. Best as a summer overnight.

### 15. Presqu'ile Provincial Park

**Brighton | ~100 km | 1 hr 15 min** [Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/dir/Whitby,+Ontario/Presqu'ile+Provincial+Park)

A 2.5 km sandy beach with a gentle slope makes this park a top family swimming destination. The **Marsh Boardwalk Trail** (1.2 km, rated 4.7 stars) delivers sweeping wetland views on elevated walkways — accessible and fascinating for kids. The 1840s **Presqu'ile Point Lighthouse** (Ontario's second-oldest on the north shore) has an interpretive centre open summer weekends. An 8 km paved cycling loop circles the park. This is one of Ontario's premier birding locations with **338+ recorded species**. Day use $12–$21/vehicle; camping $35–$55/night across 394 sites. Book day-use permits up to 5 days in advance for guaranteed summer weekend entry. Best May through September.

### 16. Forks of the Credit Provincial Park

**Caledon | ~110 km | 1 hr 15 min** [Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/dir/Whitby,+Ontario/Forks+of+the+Credit+Provincial+Park)

The crown jewel of fall-colour hiking within day-trip range. The main **Forks of the Credit Trail** (9.8 km loop) passes Cataract Falls and Niagara Escarpment vistas through what visitors describe as "a Tom Thompson painting." One 164-step wooden staircase challenges kids but rewards with stunning views. Shorter alternatives include the Ruins Trail Loop and Kettle Trail Loop (featuring a glacial kettle lake). **Advance online reservations are mandatory** — the park hits capacity regularly during October peak colour. Day use $13–$21/vehicle. No drinking water on-site; bring supplies. Best in **late September through late October** for fall colour, though lovely year-round. A half-day commitment; pair with lunch in Caledon village.

### 17. Peterborough & area

**Peterborough | ~115 km | 1 hr 15 min** [Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/dir/Whitby,+Ontario/Peterborough,+Ontario)

A packed day trip combining engineering wonder, world-class museum, free zoo, and underground adventure. Start at the **Canadian Canoe Museum** — a brand-new $45M facility opened May 2024, named one of National Geographic's Best Cultural Spots in the World. The 65,000 sq ft lakefront museum features 100+ watercraft, interactive birchbark canoe building, kayak simulators, and on-water programming. Admission: **$12 adult / $9.50 youth / $30 family**. Next, the **Peterborough Lift Lock** raises boats 65 feet on the Trent-Severn Waterway; Liftlock Cruises offers 2-hour boat tours (~$30/person) through the lock with entertaining commentary. **Riverview Park & Zoo** is completely free (donations welcome) with Canadian and exotic animals, a splash pad, playground, and mini train. For adventure, drive 30 minutes to **Warsaw Caves** for self-guided spelunking through 7 caves with headlamps (conservation area fee ~$8–$12). Total family budget: **~$100–$130** for museum, cruise, and food.

**Suggested itinerary:** Canoe Museum (10am–noon) → museum café lunch → Liftlock Cruise (1–3pm) → Riverview Park & Zoo (free) or Warsaw Caves.

### 18. Niagara Falls & Niagara Region

**Niagara Falls | ~160 km | 1.5 hrs** [Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/dir/Whitby,+Ontario/Niagara+Falls,+Ontario)

The most expensive destination on this list but undeniably bucket-list worthy. The **Voyage to the Falls boat ride** ($37/adult) drenches you in Horseshoe Falls mist; **Journey Behind the Falls** ($30/adult) puts you inside the curtain of water through tunnels; the new **Niagara Takes Flight** immersive flying ride ($33 adult/$21.50 child) is a 2025 addition. Clifton Hill delivers SkyWheel rides, arcades, and haunted houses via a **Fun Pass (~$30 for 6 attractions)**.

The **Niagara Parks Adventure Pass Classic** ($69 adult/$45 youth ages 3–12) bundles Journey Behind the Falls + White Water Walk + 2-day WEGO transit. The **Falls Pass** ($89/$58) adds the Power Station, Whirlpool Aero Car, and Incline Railway. Parking is $22/day. A 2-night stay is ideal; budget hotels 2–3 km from the falls run **$120–$200/night** (use the WEGO bus). For a quieter afternoon, drive 20 minutes to **Niagara-on-the-Lake** for ice cream and Fort George. **Total 2-night family budget: $700–$1,200.** Free highlights include Queen Victoria Park, evening falls illumination, and the Floral Clock. Best May through October.

### 19. African Lion Safari

**Hamilton/Flamborough | ~165 km | 1 hr 45 min** [Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/dir/Whitby,+Ontario/African+Lion+Safari,+Hamilton)

Drive your own car through **7 game reserves spanning 9 km** to see lions, giraffes, rhinos, baboons (they will climb on your vehicle!), and cheetahs in a 1–1.5 hour safari loop. Admission includes the African Queen boat cruise (spider monkeys, lemurs), scenic railway, three daily animal shows (Birds of Prey, parrots, elephant bathing), and the **Misumu Bay water play** area. Approximate admission: **$38–$43/adult, $30–$35/child (3–12)**, plus ~$10–$12 parking. Pack a lunch — on-site food gets mixed reviews. Arrive at opening for peak animal activity. Season runs early May through mid-October. **Family of 3 budget: ~$120–$150.** Early-bird 2026 season passes (available before March 31) include unlimited visits and 20% off food.

### 20. Elora Gorge

**Elora | ~165 km | 1 hr 45 min** [Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/dir/Whitby,+Ontario/Elora+Gorge+Conservation+Area)

Floating through Class I–II rapids beneath **22-metre limestone cliffs** makes Elora the most thrilling day trip on this list. Kids must be over **48″ (122 cm) tall** and able to swim — most older kids qualify. The 2 km tube run operates late June through Labour Day. Complete equipment rental (tube + helmet + PFD) costs **$54/person**; day-use admission adds $9/adult and $4/child — so a family of three pays roughly **$185 all-in** plus a $75 equipment deposit. **All tickets must be purchased online in advance** (available 3 days before visit). After tubing, explore the gorgeous 19th-century stone village of Elora with its shops, cafés, and the **Tooth of Time** gorge lookout. Eat at **Shepherd's Pub** or grab ice cream in the village core. Arrive by 11:30am to hike the gorge trails before your afternoon tubing slot.

### 21. Great Wolf Lodge

**Niagara Falls | ~175 km | 1 hr 50 min** [Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/dir/Whitby,+Ontario/Great+Wolf+Lodge,+Niagara+Falls)

A **100,000 sq ft indoor waterpark** kept at 84°F year-round — the ultimate rainy-weekend backup or shoulder-season escape. Access is exclusive to hotel guests (no day passes), keeping crowds manageable. Beyond slides, the wave pool, and lazy river, older kids love **MagiQuest** (interactive wand adventure), laser tag, bowling, and the arcade. Room rates range **$300–$500+/night** depending on season and suite type; midweek stays (Sunday–Thursday) in September or June offer the best value. All waterpark access is included with your room from check-in day through checkout day. Budget **$450–$700 for a 1-night family stay** including meals and some extras. Book directly on greatwolf.com and watch for 48-hour flash sales. Best for: spring breaks, rainy weekends, or shoulder-season treats.

### 22. Midland & Penetanguishene

**Midland | ~175 km | 1 hr 50 min** [Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/dir/Whitby,+Ontario/Midland,+Ontario)

A history-rich day trip combining three attractions in a tight cluster. **Sainte-Marie among the Hurons** (open May 3–Thanksgiving) recreates the 1639 French Jesuit mission with costumed interpreters demonstrating fire-starting, Indigenous games, and longhouse life — admission ~$10–$15/adult with your ticket also granting **free same-day entry to Martyrs' Shrine** across the road. A season pass ($35) adds **Discovery Harbour** (reconstructed British naval base, 15 min away). Right next door, **Wye Marsh Wildlife Centre** ($9/person) offers floating boardwalk walks, birds of prey viewing (eagles, owls, hawks), and canoe or kayak rentals through marsh channels ($8–$10/person). The **Discovery Tour combo** covers 4 attractions for $34.95. Eat at **Restaurant Sainte-Marie** on the patio overlooking the historic site. Budget **$80–$110 for a family of three.** Best June through September.

### 23. Wasaga Beach

**Wasaga Beach | ~175 km | 2 hrs** [Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/dir/Whitby,+Ontario/Wasaga+Beach,+Ontario)

The **world's longest freshwater beach** (14 km) spreads across numbered beach areas. **Beach Areas 4–5** offer the best balance of space and nearby amenities for families; **Areas 6 and Allenwood** are quieter and more natural. Beyond swimming, **Skull Island Mini Golf** ($12–$15/person) features pirate caves and waterfalls; **Wasaga 500 Go-Karts** ($15–$20/ride, must be 10+ and 54″ to drive solo) offers a GPS-verified 42 km/h track. Nancy Island Historic Site adds a War of 1812 museum and lighthouse. Parking runs **$20–$25/day** at municipal lots. A long but doable day trip; combining with nearby Blue Mountain or Scenic Caves makes an overnight worthwhile. Best July through August. Budget $80–$120 for a family beach day.

### 24. Silent Lake Provincial Park

**Near Bancroft | ~180 km | 2 hrs** [Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/dir/Whitby,+Ontario/Silent+Lake+Provincial+Park)

The **motor-free policy** on Silent Lake creates an exceptionally peaceful paddling and swimming experience — no jet skis, no motorboats, just loons calling across glassy water. Two sandy beaches (the day-use beach has a gradual slope; Pincer Bay has rock jumping spots), canoe and kayak rentals, and well-marked trails make this ideal for a quiet family camping weekend. The **Lakehead Loop** (1.5 km) suits all ages; the **Lakeshore Hiking Trail** (15 km) offers sections for ambitious older kids. Nearby Bancroft (20 min) is the **Mineral Capital of Canada** — gem and mineral collecting thrills rock-hound kids. Camping $34–$47/night; heated cabins ~$125/night (sleep 5). Best as a **2-night summer or fall overnight.** The Highlands East region is also Ontario's Geocaching Capital.

### 25. Blue Mountain Resort

**Collingwood | ~190 km | 2 hrs** [Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/dir/Whitby,+Ontario/Blue+Mountain+Resort,+Ontario)

Summer transforms this ski resort into a **mountain adventure playground.** The **Ridge Runner Mountain Coaster** (gravity-powered descent) is the headliner for older kids, complemented by the Wind Rider Triple Zip Line, Timber Challenge High Ropes, open-air gondola with Georgian Bay views, and 25+ km of hiking trails along the escarpment. The **Full-Access Ticket** costs ~$77/person (booked 7 days in advance online) for unlimited one-day access to all attractions — family of 3 runs **$200–$230/day**. Multi-day tickets for overnight guests save up to 65%. The pedestrian village offers restaurants and shopping. Day trip works well; overnight unlocks better ticket value. **Fall colour season** (September–October) adds gondola-ride leaf peeping. Pair with Scenic Caves (10 min away) for a packed weekend.

### 26. Scenic Caves Nature Adventures

**Collingwood | ~190 km | 2 hrs** [Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/dir/Whitby,+Ontario/Scenic+Caves+Nature+Adventures)

A geological adventure through **17 glacial cave and crevasse features** — including "Fat Man's Misery" (36 cm wide!) and the "Refrigerator Cave" (icy even in July). The self-guided trail emerges onto Southern Ontario's longest **suspension bridge** (128 feet, 25m above the valley floor) with panoramic Georgian Bay views. The **Thunderbird Twin Zip Line** (ages 10+, half-mile long, up to 70 km/h) is perfect for older kids. Additional activities include gemstone mining, 18-hole mini golf, and a children's adventure playground. Admission: **$35/adult, $32/youth (3–17)**; zip line and mini golf cost extra. Free parking. Open May through October. **Allow 3–4 hours minimum.** Hiking shoes mandatory — no strollers (terrain too rugged). Combine with Blue Mountain Village (10 min) for dinner.

### 27. Muskoka: Bracebridge & Gravenhurst

**Gravenhurst ~190 km / Bracebridge ~210 km | 2–2.5 hrs** [Google Maps – Bracebridge](https://www.google.com/maps/dir/Whitby,+Ontario/Bracebridge,+Ontario)

The Muskoka region anchors around two family highlights. **Santa's Village** in Bracebridge is a 60-acre theme park with 30+ rides including the new **Yeti Trek family roller coaster**, jet boat rides, paddle boats, and a water park — all included in admission (**$50–$56/person**, children under 36″ free). The park runs May through Labour Day, plus the fall **Spooktacular** (weekends through October). In Gravenhurst, **Muskoka Steamships** operates cruises on North America's oldest steamship (RMS Segwun, launched 1887). **Pirate Cruises** on Sundays in July/August feature interactive kid adventures; the Millionaire's Row cruise shows grand historic cottages (~$30–$45/adult, $15–$25/child). Don't miss **Bracebridge Falls** (free, right in town). Budget for accommodation at ~$200–$400/night for Muskoka lodging. Best as a **2-night summer overnight** to experience both towns. The adjacent **Muskoka Zip Lines & Aerial Park** near Santa's Village adds more adventure.

### 28. Prince Edward County & Sandbanks Provincial Park

**Picton ~200 km / Sandbanks ~210 km | 2.25–2.5 hrs** [Google Maps – Sandbanks](https://www.google.com/maps/dir/Whitby,+Ontario/Sandbanks+Provincial+Park)

Sandbanks delivers the **world's largest bay-mouth barrier sand dunes** and three distinct beaches. **Outlet Beach** (2 km, shallow entry) is most popular; **Dunes Beach** on West Lake offers warmer water. The **Sandbanks Dunes Trail** (1.4 km) lets kids climb and explore massive dune formations. Day use costs $12–$21/vehicle but **sells out on summer weekends — book online in advance.** Camping ($40–$54/night) is among Ontario's most competitive; book exactly 5 months ahead at 7:00 AM.

Beyond Sandbanks, Prince Edward County charms with **Lake on the Mountain** (a mysterious lake perched 60m above the Bay of Quinte, free), the county's **Millennium Trail** for family cycling, and the free **Glenora Ferry** ride (kids love it). Essential food stops: **Slickers Ice Cream** in Bloomfield and **Acoustic Grill** in Picton. Best as a **2–3 night summer overnight.** Budget $250–$400 for a camping weekend.

### 29. Bon Echo Provincial Park

**Near Cloyne | ~230 km | 2.5–3 hrs** [Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/dir/Whitby,+Ontario/Bon+Echo+Provincial+Park)

The showstopper here is **Mazinaw Rock** — a 100-metre cliff stretching 1.5 km along crystal-clear Mazinaw Lake, bearing **over 260 Indigenous pictographs** (the largest such collection in North America). The only way to see them up close is by canoe or kayak, making this a must-paddle destination. Rentals inside the park: canoe $30/4 hours, $40/8 hours; kayak $25/$30. The **Cliff Top Trail** climbs to panoramic views (strenuous but manageable for fit kids). Three sandy beaches offer warm swimming. The Visitor Centre features live snakes and wildlife exhibits. Camping runs $34–$53/night; heated camp cabins (~$125–$150/night, sleep 5) are popular. Cell service is very poor — embrace the disconnection. Best as a **2–3 night summer or early fall overnight.** Family of 3 budget: ~$250–$400.

**Suggested itinerary:** Day 1 — set up camp, beach, campfire. Day 2 — morning canoe to pictographs (2–3 hours), afternoon swim, evening Cliff Top Trail hike. Day 3 — explore secondary trails, pack up.

### 30. Arrowhead Provincial Park

**Near Huntsville | ~240 km | 2.5 hrs** [Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/dir/Whitby,+Ontario/Arrowhead+Provincial+Park)

Set in the heart of Muskoka with motor-free lakes, four sandy beaches, and the beautiful **Stubb's Falls** accessible via an easy 2.6 km trail — the signature family hike. Canoe or kayak rentals at the park store let you paddle to the falls for a dramatic approach. The **Beaver Meadow Trail** (4 km) rewards with the Big Bend Lookout. Nearby Huntsville (10 min) adds Treetop Trekking, restaurants, bowling, and escape rooms. Camping runs $34–$53/night across 378 sites; heated cabins ~$100–$150/night. **Fall colour here is exceptional** — September and October visits rival Algonquin without the crowds. Best as a 2-night overnight.

### 31. Turkey Point & Long Point (Lake Erie)

**Turkey Point ~240 km | 2.5 hrs** [Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/dir/Whitby,+Ontario/Turkey+Point+Provincial+Park)

Lake Erie is Ontario's shallowest Great Lake, producing the **warmest swimming water** in the province — a big draw for families tired of frigid Lake Ontario. Turkey Point Provincial Park features a 2+ km sandy beach with shallow sandbars ideal for kids, mountain biking trails, and the only provincial park golf course in Ontario. **Long Point** (UNESCO World Biosphere Reserve) extends 40 km into the lake with 1.5+ km of sand beach and world-class birding (300+ species, including fall monarch butterfly migration). **Long Point Eco-Adventures** (20 min away) offers zipline canopy tours for adventure seekers. Camping $40–$65/night. Best as a **2-night July or August camping weekend.** Budget $300–$480 for the trip.

### 32. Bingemans Big Splash

**Kitchener | ~160 km | 1 hr 40 min** [Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/dir/Whitby,+Ontario/Bingemans,+Kitchener)

A budget-friendly outdoor waterpark with a heated wave pool, 11 water slides (including the new **Typhoon Twister** for 2026), and the indoor FunworX play structure included with admission. Gate prices run **$44/person (over 48″) or $32 (under 48″)**, but discount sites like WagJag frequently offer Monday–Thursday passes at **$25–$32** — check before booking. Free parking, and you can bring your own food (no fast-food packaging). Most older kids clear the 48″ requirement for all slides. Open late June through Labour Day. A solid budget day trip alternative to Canada's Wonderland's Splash Works. Family of 3 budget: **$60–$130** depending on discounts.

## 2.5 to 4+ hours: weekend overnight adventures

### 33. Kingston & Fort Henry

**Kingston | ~250 km | 2.5 hrs** [Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/dir/Whitby,+Ontario/Kingston,+Ontario)

Ontario's limestone city delivers serious historical adventure. **Fort Henry National Historic Site** (open May 16–Sept 7) runs 50-minute guided tours with **artillery firing demonstrations** at 11:05am, 1:05pm, 3:05pm, and 4:05pm. Under the Canada Strong Pass, **kids 17 and under enter free** (June 19–Sept 7); adult admission is ~$20. The **Kingston Penitentiary Tour** walks families through Canada's oldest maximum-security prison (closed 2013) — the Express Tour (45 min, ~$42/person) suits this age group best. The **K-Pass** bundles Fort Henry, Pen tour, Trolley Tours, and a 1000 Islands cruise for significant savings.

Free and low-cost options include Lemoine Point trails, the waterfront splash pad, and the Saturday Kingston Public Market. Eat at **Woodenheads Pizza** or **Dianne's Fish Shack**. Stay downtown ($120–$300/night) for walkable access to Princess Street. **Budget $500–$800 for a 2-night stay.** Best May through September.

### 34. Thousand Islands

**Gananoque | ~270 km | 2.5 hrs** [Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/dir/Whitby,+Ontario/Gananoque,+Ontario)

The 1000 Islands boat cruise is one of Ontario's most iconic family experiences. Options range from 1-hour sightseeing ($35/person) to the 5-hour **Boldt Castle Stopover** ($80, includes castle admission — **passport required** for the US island). Kids love the 3-hour cruise past Millionaire's Row and under the International Bridge. The **1000 Islands Tower** at Hill Island offers panoramic views (~$15 adult/$10 child). Kayak rentals ($30–$60/half day) let adventurous families paddle among the islands. The **Thousand Islands National Park** Mallorytown Landing Visitor Centre is free with excellent trails. Base yourself in Gananoque at the Gananoque Inn (~$200–$350/night) or budget motels ($130–$200). Eat at **Dockside Restaurant** at the cruise port. Best June through September. **Budget $500–$800 for 2 nights.** Combine with Kingston (1 hour west) for a packed long weekend.

### 35. Algonquin Provincial Park

**West Gate ~290 km | 3 hrs** [Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/dir/Whitby,+Ontario/Algonquin+Provincial+Park)

Ontario's most famous park is unbeatable for **fall colour** (late September–mid-October) and summer wilderness immersion. Along the Highway 60 corridor, the **Lookout Trail** (1 km, easy) delivers a stunning cliff-edge panorama — the park's most accessible wow moment. **Beaver Pond Trail** (2 km) is prime wildlife spotting territory. The ambitious **Track & Tower Trail** (7.7 km) follows an old railway bed through varied terrain that engages active kids. The **Visitor Centre at KM 43** houses interactive exhibits and a panoramic viewing deck.

Canoe rentals along Highway 60 run **$50–$70/day**; paddling on Canoe Lake or Lake of Two Rivers is quintessential Algonquin. Summer Discovery Programs run by park naturalists teach kids about wildlife. Camping at Lake of Two Rivers or Mew Lake costs **$42–$55/night**; heated yurts ($85–$130/night) at Mew Lake offer comfort with a 2-night minimum. Camp cabins sleep 5 with queen bed, bunk bed, and propane heat. Book **exactly 5 months in advance at 7:00 AM** — popular sites sell out in minutes. Eat at **Henrietta's Pine Bakery** in Dwight or **Mad Musher** in Whitney. **Family of 3 budget: $350–$550 for 2 nights camping plus canoe rental.** Best in fall for colour; summer for full activities.

### 36. Sauble Beach

**Sauble Beach | ~295 km | 3.5 hrs** [Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/dir/Whitby,+Ontario/Sauble+Beach,+Ontario)

Ranked Canada's #1 freshwater beach, this **11 km stretch of white sand** faces west for spectacular sunsets. The **Ascent Aerial Park** ($30–$50/person) delivers rope courses, climbing walls, and a zip coaster ideal for older kids. **Putterama Mini Golf** is a bargain at $6–$7/person. Nearby **Sauble Falls Provincial Park** adds a scenic waterfall, adventure playground, and picnic grounds. The huge sand dunes along Lakeshore Road provide free climbing and sliding fun. Beach parking costs **$30/day or $7/hour** (free after 5pm). The trendy **June Motel** ($200–$350/night) features an on-site restaurant (Heydays); budget motels run $100–$160. Eat at **Beach Burger** or **Two Chicks Café**. Best as a 2-night July/August weekend. Combine with Tobermory (1.5 hrs north) for an extended trip. **Budget $400–$800 for 2 nights.**

### 37. Upper Canada Village

**Morrisburg | ~350 km | 3.5 hrs** [Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/dir/Whitby,+Ontario/Upper+Canada+Village,+Morrisburg)

This immersive living history village recreates **1866 rural English Canada** across 40+ historic buildings. Costumed interpreters demonstrate blacksmithing, weaving, broom-making, and bread baking. Kids ride horse-drawn carriages, take tow scow barge rides on the canal, visit the village schoolhouse, and interact with heritage farm animals. Admission ~$22–$25/adult; **kids 17 and under free with Canada Strong Pass** (June 19–Sept 7). The fall **PumpkInferno** features thousands of hand-carved illuminated pumpkins. Plan 3–4 hours for the village. Best combined with Kingston (1 hr west) for an overnight — drive to Kingston on day 1 for Fort Henry, then day 2 to Upper Canada Village on the way home. The **SLPC ONePass** covers both Fort Henry and Upper Canada Village at a discount. Budget **$200–$350** for the Upper Canada Village portion alone.

### 38. Tobermory & Bruce Peninsula National Park

**Tobermory | ~370 km | 4 hrs** [Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/dir/Whitby,+Ontario/Tobermory,+Ontario)

The most spectacular natural destination on this list. The **Grotto** — a sea cave with otherworldly turquoise water — requires a **Parks Canada parking reservation** (4-hour time slots, book at reservation.pc.gc.ca well in advance for summer). A 30-minute gravel path from the Cyprus Lake parking lot leads to the cave; the final descent involves a 12-metre cliff scramble (views from above are equally stunning). **Glass-bottom boat tours** ($40–$55 adult/$25–$35 child) reveal shipwrecks in Big Tub Harbour. The **Flowerpot Island** boat trip lets you hike among iconic sea stack formations. **Singing Sands Beach at Dorcas Bay** is the family-friendly alternative — shallow, warmer water, perfect for younger visitors.

Accommodation in Tobermory books months ahead for summer: motels run $150–$300/night; camping at Cyprus Lake (Parks Canada) costs $28–$36/night. Eat at **The Fish & Chip Place** or **Tobermory Brewing Co.** Budget **$500–$900 for 2 nights.** Book the earliest Grotto parking slot on Day 2 for the best experience. The **Chi-Cheemaun ferry** to Manitoulin Island adds an optional side adventure. Best July through September.

### 39. Ottawa

**Ottawa | ~390 km | 4 hrs** [Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/dir/Whitby,+Ontario/Ottawa,+Ontario)

The capital delivers exceptional museum value, especially under the **Canada Strong Pass** when national museums offer free or reduced admission. The **Canadian Museum of Nature** houses dinosaur fossils, whale skeletons, and an Arctic gallery — children 3–17 enter **free year-round**; adults pay $24. The **Canada Science & Technology Museum** features interactive exhibits and a Crazy Kitchen (free daily 4–5pm; regular ~$18–$22 adult). The **Canadian Museum of History** in Gatineau includes the extraordinary Canadian Children's Museum with interactive world cultures — the highlight for this age group (~$23 adult, family passes available). Parliament Hill's Changing of the Guard runs daily in summer. **ByWard Market** is mandatory for BeaverTails pastry.

Stay downtown near ByWard Market ($150–$300/night) for walkable access. Budget **$500–$900 for 2 nights**, significantly less if visiting during the Canada Strong Pass period. Best combined with Calypso Waterpark for a packed long weekend. Best June through August.

### 40. Calypso Waterpark

**Limoges (near Ottawa) | ~400 km | 4 hrs** [Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/dir/Whitby,+Ontario/Calypso+Waterpark,+Limoges,+Ontario)

**Canada's largest waterpark** with 35 slides, 100 water games, the country's largest wave pool (the size of 3 NHL rinks), and all heated pools. New for 2026: a revamped entrance, Main Street with new shops and dining, plus live Caribbean music. Three dedicated children's areas ensure variety. Opens **June 13, 2026**. Daily tickets run **~$57–$70** depending on age and online booking; **Gold Season Passes at $100** include unlimited visits plus access to Wet'n'Wild Toronto and Valcartier — remarkable value. Parking ~$22.50. You can bring your own food — a significant money saver. Best combined with an Ottawa weekend. Open mid-June through Labour Day.

## Seasonal planning at a glance

**Spring (April–May)** is ideal for uncrowded hiking, spring birding, and shoulder-season savings. Top picks: Rouge National Urban Park (free, migratory birds), Forks of the Credit (spring wildflowers), Presqu'ile (world-class spring migration with 338+ species), Peterborough (Canoe Museum year-round), and Treetop Trekking locations (open from early April). Canada's Wonderland opens May 3.

**Summer (June–August)** unlocks every destination on this list. Priority bookings: Sandbanks camping (5 months ahead at 7 AM), Algonquin yurts and campgrounds (same), and the Bruce Peninsula Grotto parking reservation. The Canada Strong Pass (June 19–Sept 7) makes national sites free and camping 25% off. Top summer picks: Sandbanks, Wasaga Beach, Elora Gorge tubing, Cobourg Beach, Blue Mountain, Santa's Village, and Canada's Wonderland.

**Fall (September–October)** delivers Ontario's most dramatic landscapes. Essential picks: **Algonquin Park** (peak colour late Sept–mid-Oct), **Forks of the Credit** (mandatory advance booking during peak), **Arrowhead Provincial Park** (Muskoka colour without Algonquin crowds), **Scenic Caves** (suspension bridge views), and **Upper Canada Village PumpkInferno**. Regional fall fairs, the Brooklin Harvest Festival, and Pingle's Harvest Fest round out the season close to home.

## Key booking dates and money-saving strategies

The single most important booking habit for Ontario families is this: **mark your calendar 5 months before your desired camping date and set an alarm for 6:55 AM.** Ontario Parks releases reservations daily at 7:00 AM for the date exactly 5 months out. Sandbanks, Algonquin, Bon Echo, and Killbear fill within minutes. Have your account created, payment method saved, and preferred sites selected before the clock strikes 7.

For Parks Canada sites (Bruce Peninsula, Rouge, Fort Henry, Upper Canada Village, Trent-Severn Waterway), the **Canada Strong Pass** eliminates admission fees and cuts camping by 25% from June 19 through September 7 — this alone can save a family **$200+ over a summer** of visiting national sites. Parks Canada reservations for Ontario opened February 2, 2026.

Season passes transform expensive attractions into bargains. A **Canada's Wonderland Gold Pass** ($99/person) includes unlimited visits, free parking, and access to 40+ Six Flags parks — it pays for itself in two visits versus single-day tickets. The **Wet'n'Wild/Calypso Gold Pass** ($100) covers two waterparks all summer. **African Lion Safari early-bird season passes** (available before March 31) include unlimited entry plus food discounts.

## Conclusion

Whitby families have an embarrassment of riches within reach. The best-value trips pair **free or low-cost natural destinations** (Rouge, Forks of the Credit, Kawartha Lakes, Presqu'ile) with **one or two splurge weekends** (Niagara Falls, Tobermory, Muskoka). The Canada Strong Pass makes summer 2026 unusually generous for families willing to target national sites during its June 19–September 7 window. For maximum variety across the season, build a rhythm: spring hiking at Rouge and Forks of the Credit, early summer amusement parks and waterparks, peak summer beach camping at Sandbanks or Bon Echo, and fall colour chasing at Algonquin or Arrowhead. The 40 destinations above can fill every weekend from April through October — and then some.
