**Verdict for a 14-day Dolomites road trip:** **Venice VCE is the clear winner.** It is the only *nonstop* from YYZ into the region, it is the shortest drive to the eastern Dolomites (~2h to Cortina), and it has the deepest rental-car inventory including automatics. **Innsbruck note:** INN has the shortest drive to Val Gardena (1h30) but from Toronto it is always 1-stop, has thin summer schedules, and forces an Austria→Italy cross-border rental. Only worth it if a specific 1-stop itinerary is unusually cheap.

### 1.2 Entry requirements for Canadians — ETIAS

- Canadians currently need **only a valid Canadian passport** for Schengen stays up to 90 days in any 180. No visa. https://travel-europe.europa.eu/etias_en
- **ETIAS is NOT yet in operation as of mid-2026.** The official ETIAS site states the system is "currently not in operation" and no applications are being collected. The previously announced Q4-2026 launch has been pushed; **2027 is now the likely launch**, and the EU has committed to announcing the new date **several months before** it goes live. Sources: https://dailyhive.com/canada/europe-union-etias-delayed · https://www.paxnews.com/news/airline/etias-no-longer-has-confirmed-launch-date
- **Cost when live: €20** (~CAD 32). **Free** for applicants under 18 and over 70. Validity: **3 years**, or until passport expiry, whichever is first. Sources: https://europe-visa.eu/etias-for-canadian-citizens/ · https://eurotripcheck.com/etias/canadians/
- **Action for a June-2027 trip:** ETIAS is a live risk for this window. Re-check https://travel-europe.europa.eu/etias_en at **6 months out (Dec 2026)** and again at **3 months out (Mar 2027)**. If in force, apply as soon as the portal opens for the trip — most applications approve in minutes but a small share go to manual review taking up to 30 days.
- Separately, the **EU Entry/Exit System (EES)** biometric registration is being rolled out and is distinct from ETIAS — expect fingerprint + facial capture at first Schengen entry. Verify status at the same URL.
- **Passport rule:** must be valid at least 3 months beyond intended departure from Schengen and issued within the last 10 years.

### 1.3 Open-jaw (fly into Venice, out of Munich) and cross-border one-way rental

- **Open-jaw flights:** YYZ→VCE / MUC→YYZ is bookable as a multi-city on Air Canada/Star Alliance since both are nonstop AC routes. Removes the ~5h backtrack drive to Venice at trip end.
- **Cross-border one-way rental IS allowed** by the major brands (Sixt, Avis, Hertz, Europcar) between Italy, Austria and Germany — but it is **never free**. https://www.sixt.com/rental-services/one-way-car-rental/germany/ · https://www.sixt.com/rental-services/europe-car-rental/one-way-europe/
- **Cross-border drop-off fee: typically €220–€1,100**, varying wildly by brand, vehicle class and route. Real-world reports for Munich↔Italy cluster around **USD ~390 to EUR ~1,000**. Sources: https://gemut.com/car-rental-germany/one-way-fees/ · https://www.tripadvisor.com/ShowTopic-g187309-i118-k14284729-Driving_rental_car_from_Munich_to_Italy-Munich_Upper_Bavaria_Bavaria.html · https://www.foxnews.com/travel/6-foreign-car-rental-fees-to-watch-for-on-vacation
- You must also **declare cross-border travel at booking** — many Italian rentals ban Austria/Germany entry by default and driving there uninsured voids CDW.
- **Recommendation:** price the open-jaw as: (VCE-in / MUC-out flights) + (VCE pickup / MUC drop-off car + cross-border fee) versus (VCE round-trip flights + VCE round-trip car). Given fees can hit €1,000, the round-trip VCE option usually wins unless the fee quote comes in under ~€350. Get the fee quoted in writing before committing.

## 2. CAR RENTAL AND DRIVING

### 2.1 International Driving Permit (IDP)

- **Legally required.** Government of Canada: holders of a valid Canadian licence may drive in Italy for up to 1 year **provided they also carry an IDP or an official sworn translation (traduzione giurata)**. https://www.international.gc.ca/country-pays/italy-italie/drivers_licenses-permis_de_conduire.aspx?lang=eng
- **Issued in Canada only by CAA**, must be obtained **before leaving Canada** — cannot be issued or renewed abroad. Cost is nominal (~CAD 25) and it is valid 1 year. Application: https://ww2.aaa.com/CAA/277/static/travel/IDPPrintableApplicaition.htm
- **The IDP alone is not valid** — you must carry the Canadian licence with it at all times.
- **Enforcement/insurance consequence — this is the real reason to get one:**
  - Rental desks **do ask for it** at pickup; some agents refuse the vehicle without it (inconsistent, but it happens, and there is no recourse).
  - Police can demand it at roadside checks; driving without it draws a fine.
  - **Driving without a valid IDP can nullify your rental insurance/CDW.** If you have an accident on a mountain pass without an IDP, the insurer has a clean route to deny the claim and you are exposed to the full vehicle value.
  - Sources: https://www.hertz.ca/p/hertz-together/where-do-canadians-need-international-driving-permits · https://wise.com/ca/blog/drive-italy-canadian-guide · https://carrentallab.com/italy/can-you-rent-a-car-in-italy-with-canadian-license/
- **Verdict: get it. CAD ~25 against a possible five-figure liability.** Apply ~1 month before travel.

### 2.2 ZTL (Zone a Traffico Limitato)

- ZTLs are camera-enforced restricted zones in historic town centres; only residents and registered vehicles may enter. **Bolzano operates a ZTL** in its historic centre. Sources: https://urbanaccessregulations.eu/countries-mainmenu-147/italy-mainmenu-81/bolzano-province/bolzano-ar · https://www.locautorent.com/en/blog/bolzanos-traffic-guide-for-getting-around-by-car/
- **Fine: €100–€120 per entry**, plus a rental-company admin fee of **typically €30–€50** on top. https://www.vipcars.com/guide/car-rental-tips/ztl-zones-in-italy
- **Multiple camera points on one journey = multiple separate fines.** Driving in and out and back in can produce three notices for a single afternoon.
- **How the fines arrive months later:** the camera reads the plate and generates the ticket automatically. Because the plate is registered to the rental company, the notice goes to them first. The rental company cannot pay on your behalf; it forwards your personal details to the police, charges you its admin fee immediately on your card, and the police then post the fine to your **home address in Canada**. The chain takes **months — commonly 3 to 12** — so you can be billed in spring 2028 for a June 2027 mistake. Sources: https://www.evz.de/en/topics/transport/car/fines-abroad/ztl-italy/ · https://www.europe-consommateurs.eu/en/topics/vehicles/fines/ztl-italy/ · https://www.autoeurope.eu/travel-blog/what-are-ztl-zones-in-italy/
- **Practical rules for this trip:**
  - **Bolzano:** never drive into the centro storico. Park in a signed garage on the ring (Parcheggio Bolzano Centro / Walther / Città) and walk.
  - **Bressanone/Brixen, Trento:** both have restricted historic cores; same rule — park on the periphery.
  - **Cortina d'Ampezzo:** the main Corso Italia is a **pedestrian zone** rather than a classic ZTL; the practical risk is lower but parking is paid and scarce. Use the Piazza Roma / multi-storey lots.
  - Set your nav to avoid restricted zones, and **treat any round white sign with a red border marked "ZTL" as an absolute stop.**
  - Being lost is not a defence. Hotel-guest exemption exists but **only if the hotel registers your plate in advance** — confirm by email before arrival, and get the confirmation in writing.

### 2.3 Tolls, Telepass and fuel

**Italian autostrada tolls**
- Italy has **no vignette** — distance-based tolls, roughly **€0.07/km** for a car. Verona→Brenner is about **€15–20**. https://vignette.express/blog/brenner-toll-from-italy-to-austria/
- Pay by **credit card (blue "Carte" lanes)** or **cash (white "Contanti" lanes)**. Card is easiest for visitors.
- **Telepass is effectively unavailable to tourists.** Rental companies no longer rent Telepass devices, and Telepass subscriptions require a **SEPA-country IBAN**, which a Canadian resident will not have. Do not plan around it. Sources: https://www.locautorent.com/en/blog/telepass-with-rental-cars-useful-tips/ · https://italyplanner.ai/italy-toll-road-telepass-guide.html · https://rental24h.com/blog/how-to-pay-tolls-in-italy-with-a-rental-car
- **Backup:** a prepaid **Viacard** (€25/€50 denominations) from any autostrada service station or tabacchi works in the card lanes.
- **Never enter a yellow Telepass-only lane.** You will trigger an unpaid-toll violation that follows the same months-later billing path as a ZTL fine.

**Austrian tolls (Brenner corridor)**
- Austria uses a **vignette** for general motorways: 2026 car prices **€9.60 (1 day), €12.80 (10 days), €32.00 (2 months), €106.80 (annual)**. https://tollguru.com/austria-motorways-and-expressways-tolls-vignette-rates · https://www.tolls.eu/austria
- **Key exception:** the **A13 Brenner Autobahn is a *Sondermautstrecke* (special toll section)** — it is **NOT covered by, and does not require, the vignette.** You pay a fixed section toll of **~€10.50–€11 each way** for cars under 3.5 t at the barrier. Sources: https://vignette.express/blog/brenner-toll-from-italy-to-austria/ · https://travelinformation.eu/austria/toll-roads/
- **Therefore:** if your only Austrian driving is a straight Brennero→Innsbruck→Germany transit on the A13, **you do not need a vignette** — just pay the ~€11 section toll. If you leave the A13 onto any other Austrian motorway (e.g. A12 Inntal toward Innsbruck city or onward to Munich), **you DO need the vignette** — buy the **€9.60 one-day** or **€12.80 ten-day** digital vignette online at least 18 days ahead, or instantly at a border service station.
- Fines for a missing vignette start at €120 on the spot.

**Fuel prices (Aug 2026 reference)**
- **Austria petrol E10: €1.694/L** — **Germany petrol E10: €2.091/L** — Italy diesel: €1.944/L, with Italy levying the **highest diesel excise in Europe at €0.632/L**. Sources: https://www.fuel-prices.eu/ · https://finorum.com/fuel-prices-europe/ · https://taxfoundation.org/data/all/eu/diesel-gas-taxes-europe/
- **Is it worth filling up in Austria? Yes, if you are crossing anyway.** Austria is materially cheaper than both Germany (~€0.40/L on petrol) and Italy. A 50 L fill saves roughly **€15–20 versus Italy** and **~€20 versus Germany**.
- **But:** do **not** make a dedicated 2-hour round trip over the Brenner to save €18 — the ~€21 in section tolls plus fuel burned cancels it. This is only worth doing on a leg you are already driving (e.g. an open-jaw exit to Munich: fill at Innsbruck/Matrei, not at Vipiteno and not in Bavaria).
- **Avoid Austrian motorway service-station pumps** — they carry a large premium; exit to a village station.
- In the mountains: **fill up in the valleys (Bolzano, Bressanone, Brunico, Cortina, Belluno).** Stations on the passes are rare and expensive; several close by 19:00 and many are unstaffed self-service requiring a chip-and-PIN card.

### 2.4 Automatic transmission and mountain-driving realities

- **Book an automatic. Book it early.** Automatics are genuinely scarce in Italy — the fleet is predominantly manual and supply has not kept up with foreign demand. **Book 3–4 months ahead for summer**, and earlier still for Venice Marco Polo which is called out specifically as a high-demand pickup point. https://untolditaly.com/renting-a-car-in-italy/ · https://mominitaly.com/renting-a-car-in-italy/
- **Surcharge: ~10–15% per day** over an equivalent manual; some sources report up to **50%** more. https://travelandoo.com/manual-vs-automatic-in-italy-tips-this-year/
- **Verdict: worth it here specifically.** On steep, winding Dolomite pass roads the premium is explicitly described as "worth every euro," and the advice is unambiguous: if you are not fully comfortable with a manual, **do not learn on a Dolomite pass**. Constant hairpin hill-starts with a queue of motorcycles behind you is the worst possible place to be rusty.
- **Size matters more than power.** Book a compact/small SUV, not a large estate. Pass roads are narrow, hairpins are tight, village streets are tighter, and garage parking is sized for European compacts. A powerful small car beats a big underpowered one.
- **Mountain-pass driving realities:**
  - **Hairpins:** Passo Giau (29 turns), Passo Sella, Passo Pordoi (33 turns), Passo Falzarego, Passo Fedaia. Expect sustained 1st/2nd gear, 20–40 km/h averages. **Budget roughly double what a mapping app predicts** for pass roads, and more on July weekends.
  - **Motorcycles:** the Sella Ronda passes are one of Europe's premier motorcycling circuits. In summer expect **heavy, fast, frequently overtaking motorcycle traffic**, including riders crossing the centre line on blind hairpins. Hold your line, do not drift wide, and check mirrors before every turn-in.
  - **Cyclists:** the same passes are a road-cycling mecca. Expect slow-moving groups in both directions all day.
  - **Descending:** use engine braking (manual mode / L on an automatic). Riding the brakes down Pordoi or Giau will fade them.
  - **Parking scarcity is the defining daily constraint.** Pass-summit lots (Sella, Pordoi, Giau, Falzarego) are small and **roadside parking is explicitly prohibited** at Sella and Gardena. https://www.valgardena.it/en/summer-holidays-dolomites/mobility-val-gardena/dolomites-passes/ Trailhead lots at every popular site fill **before 09:00 in July**. The single highest-leverage habit for this trip: **be parked by 08:00**, every day.
  - **Afternoon thunderstorms** (see §6) make wet hairpins a real hazard from ~14:00.

**How to actually get there for sunrise:**
- **26–30 June 2027 (before the ban starts): trivially easy.** Drive in at any hour, no booking, no permit. ### 3.4 Passo Sella, Passo Gardena, Passo Giau — car-free days

**Passo Sella / "Dolomites Vives"**
- The DolomitesVives scheme closed **Passo Sella every Wednesday 09:00–16:00** to all motorised vehicles, from the Miramonti junction (Val Gardena side) to the Passo Pordoi/Passo Sella junction (Val di Fassa side). https://www.val-gardena.net/en/events.asp?id=220
- **IMPORTANT:** that page is a **2017** event listing and is explicitly flagged "Information relating to this event no longer up to date." The Wednesday closure was a pilot; **there is no confirmed 2026 or 2027 Passo Sella Wednesday closure.** The current Val Gardena passes page mentions **no closures or booking requirements**, only that parking is severely limited and roadside parking is prohibited. https://www.valgardena.it/en/summer-holidays-dolomites/mobility-val-gardena/dolomites-passes/
- **Status: UNRESOLVED — must be verified for 2027** via https://verkehr.provinz.bz.it/ or Val Gardena tourist office **+39 0471 777777**.

**Passo Gardena — the 2027 signal**

- The 2026 run is explicitly described as a **"virtual"/digital test — no physical roadblocks and no penalties** for non-compliance.

- Bus **line 473** runs over the pass from both valleys. **Dantercëpies gondola** from Selva reaches within ~20 min walk of the pass.

### 3.6 Other 2026–2027 access limits

**Prato Piazza / Plätzwiese**
- **6 June – 8 November 2026:** access **only by paying a toll**, with a hard cap of **100 cars on the road at any one time**.
- **10:00 – 15:00:** the wider **Braies Valley** is accessible **only** by public transport, on foot, by bike, or with a **valid online booking or transit permit**.
- **Shuttle bus runs from the Ponticello/Brückele parking area up to Prato Piazza** in peak season — the low-stress option.

**Val Fiscalina / Fischleintal**
- **Car traffic restricted from mid-June to early October** — so **the entire trip window is affected**.
- Alternatives: hike in from Sesto/Moos, or take **bus 446 from Dobbiaco/Toblach**, or the **Fischleintal Shuttle from Sexten/Sesto**.
- Source: https://throneandvine.com/val-fiscalina-fischleintal/

**Lago di Carezza / Karersee**
- **The easiest major site in the region — no timed entry, no vehicle cap, no ZTL.** SS241 open year-round.
- **P1 paid lot** beside the visitor centre: **€3/hour, €30 per 24 hours**. **Fills before 09:00 on summer weekends in July/August.**
- Bus **180** runs hourly Bolzano ↔ Vigo di Fassa and stops at the lake. A **free Eggental valley summer shuttle** ran **30 May – 11 Oct 2026** linking Welschnofen, the lake and Karerpass.
- Source: https://www.wilderwithyou.com/blog/lago-di-carezza-karersee-dolomites

**Val Venegia / Passo Rolle (Paneveggio – Pale di San Martino Natural Park)**
- **Private vehicle traffic restricted on sections of the park road from June through September.**
- **Access to Baita Segantini by private vehicle is not permitted at all** (disabled access guaranteed with documentation).
- Shuttle costs quoted: **€40 per trip Passo Rolle → Pian dei Casoni → Val Venegia**; **€80 Baita Segantini → Pian dei Casoni → Val Venegia**. *(These look like per-vehicle/charter rates rather than per-person — verify directly.)*
- Sources: https://www.sanmartino.com/de/planen-sie-ihren-bergurlaub/mobilitaet/mobilitaet-im-naturpark · https://www.sanmartino.com/en/territory/villages/other-villages/passo-rolle-and-val-venegia

**Seceda (not a road restriction, but a hard access constraint)**
- Cable car **Ortisei–Furnes–Seceda**: **pre-booked timeslots required starting summer 2026**. Hours ~**08:30–17:30**.
- **Single uphill €46.50 · return €70.50** online (~5% discount); €74 standard walk-up quoted elsewhere.
- **NOT covered by the DolomitiSuperSummer card** — Ortisei–Furnes, Furnes–Seceda and Fermeda are all excluded.
- **€5 per person ridge access fee** at a turnstile on the main ridgeline trail. Meadow walking prohibited, designated footpaths only, **drones banned**, rangers enforce.
- Alternative: **Col Raiser gondola from Santa Cristina** + ~1 hour uphill hike.
- Sources: https://elinetasma.com/dolomites-access-guide/ · https://wildconnectionsphotography.com/new-2026-access-restrictions-in-the-dolomites/

**Region-wide rules**
- **Drones illegal** in National/Nature Parks without special permit — **fines €3,000+**.
- **Swimming prohibited** in most alpine lakes inside protected areas.
- **Wild camping illegal** outside official campsites — **€100–€500+**.
- **Parking colour codes: white = free/disc · blue = paid · yellow = reserved, do not park.**
- Source: https://wildconnectionsphotography.com/new-2026-access-restrictions-in-the-dolomites/

## 4. BASE TOWNS

> Nightly price ranges below are **directional estimates for late June** based on typical 3–4★ double-room rates in each area, **NOT individually source-verified** — treat as planning bands only and confirm against live inventory. Everything else in this section is sourced.

### 4.1 Val Gardena cluster (South Tyrol / Alto Adige, German-Ladin speaking)

**Ortisei / St. Ulrich** — *€180–€350/night*
- **Pros:** the easiest base for a first-time visitor. **Direct cable-car access to both Seceda and Alpe di Siusi**, two of the most iconic areas, without a long or difficult hike. Walkable, lively pedestrian centre, excellent restaurants, good supermarkets, the most infrastructure of any Dolomite village. Free **Val Gardena Mobil Card** with most accommodation.
- **Cons:** **the busiest of the three Val Gardena towns** — described as a "circus atmosphere with loads of Instagrammers" in peak season. Priciest in the valley. Seceda lift is expensive (€70.50 return) and excluded from the SuperSummer card.
- **Within 45 min:** Selva, Santa Cristina, Castelrotto, Alpe di Siusi (via lift), Val di Funes, Bressanone, Passo Sella, Passo Gardena, Bolzano (~50 min), Corvara (~45 min).
- Source: https://www.dolomitesjourney.com/dolomites-travel-tips/ortisei-vs-corvara-vs-cortina

**Selva di Val Gardena / Wolkenstein** — *€160–€300/night*
- **Pros:** "**fab for hikers who prefer to earn their views**," **slightly less polished and more budget-friendly** than Ortisei, and you **feel more in the mountains** here than in the other two towns. Closest of the three to Passo Sella and Passo Gardena — best base for the Sella Ronda pass loop. Dantercëpies and Ciampinoi lifts on the doorstep.
- **Cons:** smaller, quieter evenings; further from Bolzano and the motorway.
- **Within 45 min:** everything Ortisei reaches, plus better access to Corvara/Alta Badia and Canazei over the passes.
- Source: https://www.catsninelives.com/where-to-stay-in-the-dolomites/

**Santa Cristina** — *€150–€280/night*
- **Pros:** the quiet middle option; cheapest of the three Gardena villages; **Col Raiser gondola** gives a cheaper back-door route to Seceda than the Ortisei lift; free valley bus to both neighbours.
- **Cons:** least amenity, fewest restaurants, feels like a dormitory village.

**Castelrotto / Kastelruth** — *€140–€260/night*
- **Pros:** the prettiest classic Tyrolean village of the group, frescoed houses and a landmark bell tower; **noticeably cheaper** than Val Gardena proper; the natural base for **Alpe di Siusi via the Siusi/Seis cable car** (free base parking); closest of this cluster to Bolzano and the A22.
- **Cons:** **not actually in the high mountains** — it sits on a plateau below them, so every hike starts with a drive or lift. Less dramatic immediate scenery.

### 4.2 Alta Badia

**Corvara** — *€180–€330/night*
- **Pros:** **feels more open and less busy than Ortisei**, surrounded by wide mountain views, with **Passo Gardena, Campolongo, Pordoi and Valparola all easy to reach by car** — the best base in the region for **scenic pass driving**. Food is exceptional (Alta Badia has multiple Michelin stars). Sits at the centre of the Sella Ronda.
- **Cons:** **public transport is patchy — "a car makes life 100x easier."** Ground zero for the **Maratona on 4 July 2027** — expect the village to be overwhelmed, hotels booked out a year ahead and roads shut. Also the area targeted by the **Passo Gardena 2027 restriction pilot**.
- **Within 45 min:** Selva, Ortisei, Canazei, Arabba, San Cassiano, Badia, La Villa, Passo Falzarego, Cortina (~1h), Brunico.
- Source: https://www.dolomitesjourney.com/dolomites-travel-tips/ortisei-vs-corvara-vs-cortina

**San Cassiano / Badia** — *€170–€320/night*
- **Pros:** quieter and more refined than Corvara, strong hotel and dining quality, excellent for Fanes-Sennes-Braies park access and the Lagazuoi/Falzarego side.
- **Cons:** smallest amenity base; same Maratona and Passo Gardena exposure as Corvara.

**Arabba** — *€140–€250/night*
- **Pros:** cheapest Sella Ronda base, dramatic setting under the Sella massif, superb for Pordoi/Campolongo/Fedaia and Marmolada. Excellent value.
- **Cons:** tiny, very limited dining, dead in the evenings, transit-town feel.

### 4.3 Ampezzo / Cadore (Veneto, Italian speaking)

**Cortina d'Ampezzo** — *€250–€500+/night*
- **Pros:** access to **the most dramatic and well-known locations** — **Tre Cime, Cinque Torri, Lago di Braies and Passo Giau all within reach**. Genuine town amenities: hospital, pharmacies open late, real supermarkets, guides, gear shops. The best single base for the eastern Dolomites. Summer bus lines 51/52/53 from town to Misurina, Falzarego and Giau.
- **Cons:** **larger and busier than Ortisei or Corvara with more of a resort feel**, and bluntly characterised as **"the Aspen of Italy: very expensive and full of pretentious rich people."** Most expensive base in the region by a wide margin. Also hosting Milano-Cortina 2026 Olympic legacy construction — check for works in 2027.
- **Within 45 min:** Misurina, Tre Cime, Passo Tre Croci, Passo Falzarego, Passo Giau, Cinque Torri, San Vito di Cadore, Dobbiaco, Auronzo, Lago di Sorapis trailhead, Lago di Braies (~50 min).
- Sources: https://cortinaitaly.com/blog/cortina-vs-ortisei-which-dolomites-base-is-better · https://dolomitibus.it/it/news/linee-estive-2026-in-bus-tra-le-valli-bellunesi/272

**San Vito di Cadore** — *€120–€220/night*
- **Pros:** **the value play for the Cortina area** — roughly 15 min south of Cortina at a materially lower price, under the Antelao and Pelmo. Real Italian village feel, not a resort.
- **Cons:** 15–20 min further from everything north of Cortina; limited dining; you will drive through Cortina traffic daily.

**Alleghe** — *€110–€200/night*
- **Pros:** lakeside setting under Monte Civetta, cheapest base in the Veneto Dolomites, good for the Civetta group, Passo Giau and Passo Staulanza.
- **Cons:** peripheral — long drives to Val Gardena, Braies and Tre Cime. Better as a 1–2 night stop than a main base.

### 4.4 Puster Valley / Val Pusteria (South Tyrol)

**Dobbiaco / Toblach** — *€130–€240/night*
- **Pros:** **the single best base for Lago di Braies and Tre Cime.** Braies is ~20 min, Misurina ~30 min, Cortina ~35 min. Direct shuttle line 444 to Rifugio Auronzo and line 442 to Braies. Rail station on the Pustertal line. Good value versus Cortina. Flat, easy driving with no pass required to reach the marquee sights.
- **Cons:** the town itself is unremarkable — a transit hub, not a scenic village. Far from Val Gardena (~1h20).
- **Within 45 min:** Lago di Braies, Prato Piazza, Misurina, Tre Cime, San Candido, Sesto, Val Fiscalina, Cortina, Brunico, Plan de Corones/Kronplatz.

**San Candido / Innichen** — *€130–€240/night*
- **Pros:** **much prettier than Dobbiaco** — a genuinely charming Tyrolean town with a fine collegiate church, good restaurants and a lively centre. Same access profile as Dobbiaco plus 10 min. Best gateway to the Sesto Dolomites and Val Fiscalina.
- **Cons:** ~10 min further from Braies and Tre Cime than Dobbiaco; on the Austrian border, so furthest from everything west.

### 4.5 Isarco Valley / Bolzano

**Bressanone / Brixen** — *€130–€250/night*
- **Pros:** a **real historic city** — cathedral, cloisters, arcades, proper restaurants and bars, and prices well below the resorts. Directly on the A22 and the mainline railway, so the best "arrival/departure night" base. **Bus 330 into Val di Funes departs from here.** Excellent bad-weather backup with museums and a walkable centre.
- **Cons:** **not in the mountains** — every hike is a 30–60 min drive. Hot in the valley in July.
- **Within 45 min:** Val di Funes, Val Gardena, Plose, Chiusa, Bolzano, Alpe di Siusi base, Brunico.

**Bolzano / Bozen** — *€130–€250/night*
- **Pros:** the regional capital — Ötzi Museum, best food market in the region, funiculars to Renon/Ritten and San Genesio, full transport hub, wide hotel choice. Best wet-weather and arrival/departure base. **Mobilcard sold here.**
- **Cons:** **furthest from the mountains of any base listed**, and **hot** — valley temperatures can exceed 32°C in July while the peaks are at 15°C. **Active ZTL** in the historic centre. Not a hiking base.

### 4.6 Val di Fassa / Trentino

**Canazei** — *€140–€260/night*
- **Pros:** superb central position for **Passo Sella, Pordoi, Fedaia and the Marmolada**; the Pordoi cable car to Sass Pordoi ("terrace of the Dolomites") is 10 min away. Lively, good value, well-serviced, Italian-speaking Trentino side.
- **Cons:** can feel like a busy through-road town; heavy motorcycle traffic all summer; Trentino cards do not cover South Tyrol transport.

**Moena** — *€120–€220/night*
- **Pros:** cheapest Val di Fassa base, pleasant town, gateway to Val di Fiemme and Lago di Carezza; good for the Latemar and Catinaccio.
- **Cons:** at the low end of the valley — 20–25 min further from the big passes than Canazei.

**San Martino di Castrozza** — *€130–€250/night*
- **Pros:** the most spectacular immediate skyline of any village listed — the Pale di San Martino wall rises directly above the town. **Dramatically less crowded** than anything north. Passo Rolle, Val Venegia and Baita Segantini on the doorstep.
- **Cons:** **geographically isolated from the rest of the Dolomites** — 2h+ to Val Gardena, 2h30 to Cortina, 2h+ to Braies. Only worth including as a deliberate 2–3 night southern extension, not as a main base. Also subject to the Paneveggio park vehicle restrictions (§3.6).

### 4.7 Recommended base splits for 14 days

**Drive times between the recommended pairs are in §7.**

#### **2-BASE (9 nights + 4 nights) — lowest friction, most recommended**

| Nights | Base | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| 1–7 (26 Jun – 3 Jul) | **Dobbiaco/Toblach or San Candido** | Puts you on the doorstep of **Lago di Braies during the 26–30 June window when there is NO car ban and NO booking requirement** — the single biggest scheduling win available. Also covers Tre Cime, Prato Piazza, Val Fiscalina, Misurina, and Cortina day trips. |
| 8–14 (3–9 Jul) | **Ortisei or Selva (Val Gardena)** | Seceda, Alpe di Siusi, Val di Funes, the Sella passes, Bolzano/Bressanone. **On 4 July (Maratona) stay west — Val Gardena's own lifts and Alpe di Siusi are unaffected while Corvara/Cortina are gridlocked.** |

**Transfer:** Dobbiaco → Ortisei ~1h20 / 95 km via Brunico + Val Badia, or ~1h35 via the Pustertal–Bressanone motorway route.
**Why this wins:** only one hotel change in 14 days, both bases have real amenities, and it neatly sidesteps both the Braies ban and the Maratona.

#### **3-BASE (5 + 5 + 4) — best coverage-to-effort ratio**

| Nights | Base | Covers |
|---|---|---|
| 1–5 (26–30 Jun) | **Dobbiaco/Toblach** | **Braies with zero restrictions**, Tre Cime, Prato Piazza, Val Fiscalina, Misurina, San Candido |
| 6–9 (1–4 Jul) | **Cortina d'Ampezzo** (or **San Vito di Cadore** to save ~40%) | Cinque Torri, Lagazuoi/Falzarego, Passo Giau, Lago di Sorapis, Cadini. **On 4 July the Maratona shuts Giau/Falzarego — use it as a Cortina town day, Faloria/Tofana lifts, or Sorapis from the Tre Croci side.** |
| 10–14 (4/5–9 Jul) | **Ortisei or Selva** | Seceda, Alpe di Siusi, Val di Funes, Passo Sella/Gardena, Bolzano, Bressanone |

**Transfers:** Dobbiaco → Cortina ~35 min / 32 km · Cortina → Ortisei ~1h30 / 65 km (via Falzarego + Passo Gardena — **check the Passo Gardena 2027 restriction**; alternative via Dobbiaco/Brunico ~2h).

#### **4-BASE (4 + 3 + 4 + 3) — maximum coverage, most packing**

| Nights | Base | Covers |
|---|---|---|
| 1–4 (26–29 Jun) | **Dobbiaco/Toblach** | Braies unrestricted, Tre Cime, Prato Piazza, Sesto |
| 5–7 (30 Jun – 2 Jul) | **Cortina** or **San Vito di Cadore** | Cinque Torri, Lagazuoi, Giau, Sorapis |
| 8–10 (3–5 Jul) | **Corvara or Arabba** | Sella Ronda, Pordoi, Marmolada/Fedaia, Alta Badia. **⚠ Maratona 4 July hits this base hardest — either swap this block to Canazei, or accept a deliberate no-drive day and use the Boè/Vallon lifts from Corvara on foot.** |
| 11–14 (5/6–9 Jul) | **Ortisei or Selva** | Seceda, Alpe di Siusi, Val di Funes, Bolzano/Bressanone, easy return |

**Transfers:** Dobbiaco → Cortina ~35 min · Cortina → Corvara ~1h / 45 km via Falzarego · Corvara → Ortisei ~45 min / 30 km via Passo Gardena (or ~1h via Passo Campolongo–Pordoi–Sella if Gardena is restricted).

**Alternative 4-base if you want Trentino instead of Alta Badia:** substitute **Canazei** for Corvara — it is off the Maratona's worst congestion, closer to Marmolada/Fedaia and Lago di Carezza, and cheaper.

**Which to pick:** with a **VCE round-trip and 14 days, the 3-base split is the sweet spot.** The 2-base is better if the traveller values unpacking once; the 4-base only pays off if pass-driving and Alta Badia dining are priorities, and it collides badly with 4 July.

**Note on trip direction:** if flying **into and out of VCE**, run the itinerary **Dobbiaco/Cortina first → Val Gardena last** only if you accept a ~3h final drive back to Venice. Running it **Val Gardena first → Cortina last** puts you 2h from VCE on the final morning, which is better for an early flight — **but it sacrifices the unrestricted-Braies window of 26–30 June.** Given how much easier restriction-free Braies is, **prioritise the Braies window and accept the longer final drive**, or take the last night in **Bressanone/Belluno** as a positioning stop.

## 5. GUEST CARDS AND PASSES

### 5.1 Südtirol Guest Pass (South Tyrol) — **best value in the region, and it's free**
- **Not purchasable.** It is **included free in the room rate at participating accommodation** across South Tyrol and issued digitally before arrival.
- Includes **free use of (almost) all public transport in South Tyrol** — regional trains, all urban and suburban buses, and **selected cable cars**.
- Destination-dependent extras: activities, guided tours, admission fees or discounts.
- Sources: https://www.suedtirol.info/en/en/information/suedtirol-guest-pass · https://guestpass.suedtirol.info/en · https://www.south-tirol.com/useful-information/advantage-cards/suedtirol-guest-pass
- **Action: when booking any South Tyrol hotel (Val Gardena, Alta Badia, Puster Valley, Bressanone, Bolzano, Castelrotto), explicitly confirm the Südtirol Guest Pass is included.** Most participate; it is worth choosing a participating property over a non-participating one.
- **It also gets you the 10 reserved Braies shuttle seats without a reservation** (as a Südtirolmobil Pass holder) — see §3.1.

### 5.2 Mobilcard South Tyrol — buy only if your hotel doesn't give you a Guest Pass
- **2026 adult prices: 1 day €22.00 · 3 days €32.00 · 7 days €52.00.** Children 6–13: **€11 / €16 / €26**.
- Unlimited travel on **all urban and suburban buses, regional trains, cable cars**, the Mendola funicular, the historic Renon/Ritten train, and the Malles–Müstair bus into Switzerland.
- Sources: https://www.suedtirol.info/en/en/information/mobility/mobilcard · https://www.south-tirol.com/useful-information/advantage-cards/mobilcard
- **Verdict: redundant if you have the Guest Pass.** Only buy for the days you are outside participating accommodation.

### 5.3 DolomitiSuperSummer Card — **the big lift pass**
- **2026: 1 day €67 · 3-of-4 days €145 · 5-of-7 days €185.** Valid **14 May – 8 November 2026**.
- Covers **over 100 lifts across 12 regions**; **bike transport included**.
- **CRITICAL EXCLUSION: it does NOT cover the Seceda lifts** — Ortisei–Furnes, Furnes–Seceda and Fermeda are all excluded in 2026.
- Sources: https://www.dolomitisuperski.com/en/plan-and-book/lift-pass · https://www.altabadia.org/en/dolomites/summer-in-motion/dolomiti-supersummer-card · https://www.in-moments.com/dolomites/dolomites-lift-pass-comparison-guide
- **Verdict:** the **3-of-4 day card at €145** is the recommended sweet spot, reckoned to save ~**€47 per person** versus individual tickets. Worth it only if you will ride 2+ significant lifts on each of 3 days. For a hiking-led trip with a handful of lift rides, **pay-as-you-go is usually cheaper** — most single return rides are €20–€45.

### 5.4 Val Gardena cards
- **Val Gardena Mobil Card:** **free with most Val Gardena accommodation** (issued by the local tourist association); unlimited free travel on all public buses between Ortisei, Santa Cristina and Selva. https://www.valgardena.it/en/summer-holidays-dolomites/mobility-val-gardena/val-gardena-mobil-card/
- **Val Gardena Guest Pass:** free local bus travel including **line 473 over Passo Gardena**. https://www.valgardena.it/en/val-gardena-guest-pass/
- **Gardena Card (lift pass):** unlimited use of **17 Val Gardena lifts**, valid **6 June – 11 October 2026**. **⚠ SOURCES CONFLICT ON PRICE:** valgardena.it quotes **€124 for 6 days / €87 for 3 days**, while the in-moments comparison quotes **€124 for 3 days / €160 for 6 days**. Junior (8–18) quoted at **€112 / 6 days**. **Verify directly at https://www.gardena-card.com/en/ before purchase.** Other sources: https://www.valgardena.it/en/summer-holidays-dolomites/mobility-val-gardena/gardena-card/ · https://www.in-moments.com/dolomites/dolomites-lift-pass-comparison-guide
- **The Gardena Card is the only pass that includes Seceda.** If Seceda is a priority (€70.50 return on its own) plus Alpe di Siusi plus Ciampinoi/Dantercëpies, the Gardena Card can pay for itself in **two days**.

### 5.5 Alta Badia Summer Card
- **2026: 1 day €47 adult / €33 junior (<18) · 3-of-4 days €72 / €50 · 5-of-7 days €93 / €65 · full season €228 / €176.** **Children under 8 free** (born after 01.01.2018).
- Sources: https://altabadia.org/en/summer-holidays/lifts/dolomiti-supersummer-card.html · https://www.altabadia.org/en/dolomites/summer-in-motion/dolomiti-supersummer-card
- **Verdict:** at **€72 for 3-of-4 days** this is far better value than the €145 SuperSummer card **if you are only riding lifts in Alta Badia**. Buy the regional card, not the mega-pass, unless you are genuinely lift-hopping across multiple valleys.

### 5.6 Cortina summer lifts
- **Cortina Vertical Pass:** available as **1, 3, 5 or 7-day** tickets, usable across the summer until the purchased days expire. Buy online or at any lift ticket office including **Lagazuoi, Faloria and the Tofana facilities**. https://skipasscortina.com/EN/page3-choose-your-pass
- **Tofana / Freccia nel Cielo return fares:** **Col Druscié €25 · Ra Valles €35 · Cima Tofana €45.** https://www.freccianelcielo.com/en/what-to-do-in-summer/summer-hours-and-prices/
- **Operating dates 2026:** Falzarego–Lagazuoi cable car **30 May – 18 Oct, 09:00–17:00**; Cortina–Col Drusciè gondola **13 June – 29 Sept**. https://cortinadampezzo.it/en/news-en/summer-lift-openings/ · https://lagazuoi.it/EN/Information-Prices-and-useful-information-page20-Lagazuoi-cable-car
- **5% discount and queue-skip for paying by credit card** at the ticket office.
- **Verdict:** Cortina's lifts are not on the Dolomiti Superski summer card in a useful way — **buy individual returns or the Vertical Pass**. The Lagazuoi cable car is the one non-negotiable (it is also the start of the Lagazuoi tunnels and the Alta Via 1).

### 5.7 Bus networks
- **South Tyrol (SAD / Südtirolmobil):** covered free by the **Südtirol Guest Pass** or **Mobilcard**. Relevant lines: **330** (Bressanone–Val di Funes), **439/442** (Braies), **444** (Dobbiaco–Rifugio Auronzo, reservation required), **446** (Dobbiaco–Val Fiscalina), **473** (Passo Gardena), **179 / Seiser Alm Express line 10** (Alpe di Siusi), **180** (Bolzano–Carezza–Vigo di Fassa). https://www.suedtirolmobil.info/en/news/details/destinations-in-the-dolomites
- **DolomitiBus (Veneto / Belluno province, incl. Cortina):** summer network **8 June – 9 September 2026**. Three dedicated Cortina lines: **51** Cortina–Passo Tre Croci–Misurina (7 days/wk, hourly 08:00–18:00) · **52** Cortina–Passo Falzarego (7 days/wk, hourly mornings, 2-hourly afternoons) · **53** Cortina–Passo Giau–Pescul (new). Also **line 31** Misurina–Rifugio Auronzo. https://dolomitibus.it/it/news/linee-estive-2026-in-bus-tra-le-valli-bellunesi/272 · https://cortinadelicious.it/EN/p97-Bus-timetables
- **Trentino Trasporti (Val di Fassa, Fiemme, San Martino):** separate network, **not** covered by the Südtirol Guest Pass. Local guest cards (e.g. Garda Guest Card, Fassa Card) cover it. https://www.trentinotrasporti.it/
- **Cortina Express** runs a fly-bus Venice airport ↔ Cortina if you ever need a car-free leg. https://www.cortinaexpress.it/en/timetable/
- **Key gotcha: the three networks do not interoperate.** A Südtirol Guest Pass is worthless on a DolomitiBus in Cortina and vice versa. Plan bus use base-by-base.

### 5.8 Card verdict summary
1. **Südtirol Guest Pass — take it, free, best value.** Filter hotels for it.
2. **Alta Badia Summer Card / Gardena Card — buy the regional card for the valley you're in**, not the mega-pass.
3. **DolomitiSuperSummer — only if lift-hopping across 3+ valleys.**
4. **Cortina — pay per ride or Vertical Pass; separate ecosystem.**
5. **Mobilcard — skip unless your hotel gives no Guest Pass.**

### 6.2 Mobile coverage, roaming and eSIM

- **Canadian carrier roaming is the expensive option:** the Big 3 average **~CAD 16.50/day** for roaming. Over 14 days that is **~CAD 230 per line**. https://www.vancouverisawesome.com/travel/arialo-esim-canada-travel-roaming-charges-12051335
- **eSIM is dramatically cheaper:**
  - **Airalo:** a 7-day 5 GB Italy plan is **~USD 6**; a **30-day 20 GB plan is USD 33**. Uses **Vodafone, TIM, Iliad and WindTre**, auto-selecting on signal strength. https://www.airalo.com/italy-esim · https://saily.com/blog/airalo-esim-pricing/ · https://esimdb.com/italy/airalo
  - **Holafly:** **USD 1.55–6.90/day** with unlimited data, price per day falling with duration; described as the only provider reaching **all four Italian networks**. https://esim.holafly.com/esim-italy/ · https://esim.holafly.com/data-plans/holafly-esim-price-comparison/
  - Reported savings: **over CAD 100 on a one-week trip**, up to **92% versus roaming**.
- **Network choice matters in the mountains:** **TIM has the widest geographic reach in mountains and low-density areas**, so pick an eSIM that leans on TIM. https://www.esim-now.com/en/guide/italy-network-coverage
- **Where coverage dies:** **above the tree line and in deep valleys.** Realistically you have signal **~80% of the time while hiking**, less the more remote you go. Cortina town itself has surprisingly strong 4G. Sources: https://yohomobile.com/yoho-mobile-esim-italy-dolomites-hiking · https://travelexperta.com/best-esim-italy-remote-alpine-villages/ · https://singletrackworld.com/forum/off-topic/mobile-phone-coverage-and-sims-for-the-dolomites/

Screenshot your rifugio bookings and parking reservations. Note that **112 works even with no SIM registration and roams onto any available network** — an emergency call will connect where data will not.

### 6.3 Weather pattern, late June / early July

- **Valley temperatures (1,200–1,500 m, incl. Cortina): 20–28°C by day, 10–15°C at night.**
- **Above 2,000 m: 12–18°C by day, 4–8°C overnight.**
- **The defining feature is the afternoon thunderstorm cycle, and it is a daily pattern, not an occasional risk:** clear mornings → cloud build-up from late morning → **thunderstorms between roughly 14:00 and 17:00 on most days**.
- By mid-to-late June the weather typically settles into this reliable pattern; **mornings are almost always clear**.
- Sources: https://huttohuthikingdolomites.com/blog/dolomites-weather-in-july · https://huttohuthikingdolomites.com/blog/dolomites-in-june · https://huttohuthikingdolomites.com/blog/best-time-to-visit-the-dolomites-guide
- **The operating rule that follows from this:** **start hiking at 07:00–08:00, be at your high point by early afternoon, and be descending or in a rifugio by 14:00.** Experienced hikers build the entire schedule around this. It also aligns perfectly with the parking problem (§2.4) and the access-restriction cut-offs (§3) — **the early start solves three problems at once.**
- **Late-June specific risks:** **residual snow on north-facing high routes and above ~2,400 m**, especially on the Alta Via traverses and the Sorapis approach; some via ferrata and high rifugi may not have opened. Check rifugio opening dates individually.
- **Lightning on exposed ridges and via ferrata is the primary objective hazard** — steel cables are the last place to be at 15:00.

### 6.4 Altitude

- **Altitude sickness is essentially a non-issue for standard Dolomites travel.** Base towns sit at **1,000–1,600 m** and the great majority of hiking tops out **below 2,700 m** — well under the ~2,500 m threshold where AMS typically begins and far below where it becomes common.
- **The two places it can register:** the **Sass Pordoi cable car (2,950 m)** from Passo Pordoi and the **Marmolada / Punta Rocca cable car (~3,265 m)** — both take you from valley floor to over 3,000 m in minutes with **zero acclimatisation**. Expect breathlessness, mild headache and reduced exercise capacity for the first 20–30 minutes at the top. This is normal and resolves on descent.
- **Practical guidance:** on lift days to 3,000 m+, **move slowly for the first 15 minutes, hydrate, and dress warmly** — it can be near freezing with wind at Punta Rocca in July while the valley is 28°C. Anyone with cardiac or pulmonary conditions should consult a physician before the Marmolada lift.
- **Bigger real risks than altitude:** UV exposure (much stronger at altitude — high-SPF, hat, sunglasses), dehydration, and exposure/hypothermia when a thunderstorm catches you on a ridge in a t-shirt.

### 6.5 Emergency and medical

- **112 — single European emergency number.** Works for **all emergencies on Italian territory including all mountain rescue**. Works from any phone, on any network, with or without a SIM. As of 2026 it has **improved multilingual support and real-time translation for tourists**. Sources: https://aiut-alpin-dolomites.com/en/emergency-call-112/ · https://aiut-alpin-dolomites.com/en/
- **118** — medical emergency (still routes correctly, but **112 is the correct default**).
- **When calling mountain rescue, state clearly:** your **name**, your **position (altitude and GPS coordinates if possible)**, and a **callback number**. https://www.guidedolomiti.com/en/dolomites-mountain-rescue/
- **Aiut Alpin Dolomites** is the region's dedicated helicopter mountain-rescue service. **Mountain rescue in Italy can be charged to the patient** — confirm your travel insurance explicitly covers **helicopter mountain rescue and repatriation**, and that it covers **via ferrata** if that is on the plan. Many standard policies exclude both.
- **Pharmacies (farmacia):** green cross sign. **Farmacia di turno** is the rotating out-of-hours duty roster giving **24/7 access 7 days a week** — the rota is **posted on the door of every closed pharmacy**, and there is a **"Farmacia di Turno" app**. Sources: https://mominitaly.com/pharmacies-in-italy-farmacie/ · https://www.expatfocus.com/italy/guide/italy-prescriptions-and-medications
- Italian pharmacists have wide latitude to advise and dispense for minor issues — the first stop for anything non-urgent.
- **Local numbers:** Val Gardena doctors and pharmacies https://www.valgardena.it/en/addresses/doctors-pharmacies/ · Alpe di Siusi important numbers https://www.seiser-alm.it/en/service/important-phone-numbers/
- **Hospitals:** Cortina (Ospedale Codivilla-Putti), Bolzano (main regional hospital), Brunico, Bressanone, Belluno.
- **Canadians:** no reciprocal healthcare agreement with Italy. **Travel medical insurance is mandatory in practice.** Carry the policy number and the insurer's 24h number offline.

### 6.6 Shop hours

- **Weekdays:** typically **09:00–19:00**, but **many smaller shops close for lunch, roughly 13:00–15:00** (the *pausa pranzo*, quoted as anywhere from **13:00–16:00** for smaller stores).
- **Saturday:** typically **10:00–18:00**.
- **Sunday: this is the one that will catch you out.** South Tyrol's autonomous provinces of **Bolzano and Trento hold their own jurisdiction over retail and take a deliberately more restrictive line on Sunday opening than the rest of Italy.** In **smaller South Tyrolean towns, virtually all stores are closed on Sundays.** Large shopping centres in Bolzano do open.
- Sources: https://www.bolzano-bozen.it/en/shopping-bolzano.htm · https://vivereinitalien.de/en/is-it-possible-to-go-shopping-in-italy-on-sundays · https://www.reidsitaly.com/planning/comm/open_hours.html · https://www.south-tirol.com/useful-information/public-holidays
- **Practical rules for this trip:**
  - **Buy hiking food and supplies on Saturday.** Sunday in Ortisei, Corvara, Castelrotto or Dobbiaco means a closed supermarket.
  - **Restaurants and rifugi are open on Sundays** — it is retail that shuts.
  - Sundays in the trip window: **27 June and 4 July 2027**. Note that **4 July is also the Maratona** — a genuinely bad day to need anything from a shop in Alta Badia.
  - Many mountain restaurants and some hotels also take a **weekly closing day (*Ruhetag*)**, often Monday or Tuesday. Check before driving to a specific restaurant.
  - **Public holiday in window:** **29 June (Santi Pietro e Paolo)** is a patronal feast in some comuni — not a national holiday, but can close local businesses. **15 August (Ferragosto)** is outside the window.
