# Dolomites — Tue 13 July to Mon 26 July 2027

13 nights, 14 days. Toronto (YYZ) → Venice (VCE) nonstop, rental car for all 14 days.
Hotel every night. Hut-to-hut was considered and set aside — see the note at the end
if that ever changes.

Dates moved from 26 June at Nitin's request. The June draft is kept as
`PLAN-superseded-june.md` — it still holds the reasoning for the earlier window.

---

## Why mid-July, and what it costs

**What opens up.** Everything is running from day one, including the Fedare chairlift
at Passo Giau (13 July) and Faloria (11 July). North-facing snow above 2,300 m is gone,
which is the real prize: **Croda del Becco, the Puez plateau, the summit push on Piz Boè
and the Alta Via 1 huts all become realistic.** The Maratona is past, so no pass closures,
and no car-free bike day falls inside the window either — mid-July is clean.

**What it costs.**

| Lost | Detail |
|---|---|
| Free Braies | Car ban 09:00–16:00 from 1 July, €44 prepaid booking. Arriving before 09:00 is still legal but parking is no longer guaranteed. |
| Malga Ra Stua by car | Road shut to private cars 1 July–1 Sep. Walk or shuttle from Fiames. |
| Peak wildflowers | Peak is mid-June to mid-July. The 13th catches the tail; by the 26th it is over. |
| Crowd advantage | Italian schools out since 17 June, Austrian Tirol from 10 July, most German states from 1 July. Bavaria not until 2 August, so it is busy but not peak. |
| Weather odds | Late July carries the highest chance of rain in the season. Morning starts matter more, not less. |
| Price | Low season into high season across flights, hotels and cars. |

**Watch for:** the Coppa d'Oro delle Dolomiti regularity rally ran 16–19 July in 2026 and is
Cortina-based. It does not close roads, but it fills the town. Verify the 2027 dates.

# The plan

Four bases. The extra snow-free terrain that mid-July buys goes in as day hikes.

| Base | Nights | Dates | Cost/night |
|---|---|---|---|
| San Candido / Innichen | 4 | 13–16 Jul | €130–240 |
| Cortina d'Ampezzo | 4 | 17–20 Jul | €250–500 (San Vito di Cadore ~40% less) |
| Ortisei or Selva | 4 | 21–24 Jul | €160–350 |
| Bolzano | 1 | 25 Jul | €130–250 |

### Day 1 — Tue 13 Jul. Venice to the Alta Pusteria
VCE → San Candido, 195 km / 2h30 via A27 then SS51. No pass driving on arrival day.
Roadside stop at **Lago di Landro** for the Cristallo head-on. Arrive ~16:00, buy groceries.
Evening: Lago di Dobbiaco boardwalk, 3.5 km flat, or San Candido's collegiate church.

### Day 2 — Wed 14 Jul. Lago di Braies, early
**The rules changed on 1 July.** Either book P4 online in advance (€44 for a car, including
a €22 voucher, no same-day payment at the gate) or arrive well before the 09:00 cut-off
knowing parking is not guaranteed. Either way, be there at first light: dawn 04:54,
sunrise 05:32, alpenglow on the Seekofel 15–20 min before the sun. Boats are moored in
their photogenic row only before 08:00; rental €20 shared or €55 private per 45 min.
Lake loop 3.7 km flat.
Afternoon: **Val Fiscalina** — flat 20–30 min to the Talschlusshütte under the Croda dei
Toni. Cars restricted; shuttle 440/446, tickets on board.

### Day 3 — Thu 15 Jul. Tre Cime di Lavaredo
Book the toll slot 30 days out at pass.auronzo.info, €40 per car, 12 hours from the booked
slot, no same-day sales. Loop 9.5–10.5 km, 400–480 m, 3.5–4.5 h, wide gravel, no exposure.
**Rifugio Locatelli is certainly open now** — the June date risk is gone.
Decision rule: settled forecast, take a midday slot and walk clockwise so the north faces
are lit when you reach Locatelli, after the 09:00–12:00 crush. Unsettled, earliest slot and
off the loop by 14:00.
Side trip: **Cadini di Misurina viewpoint**, 30–40 min on trail 117. An exposed spur ending
at a drop-off. Do not continue onto the Bonacossa — it becomes via ferrata.

### Day 4 — Fri 16 Jul. Croda del Becco, 2,810 m — *or* the Drava bike ride
**The hard option, newly possible.** Croda del Becco / Seekofel from Lago di Braies:
14.6 km, 1,300–1,400 m, 7–8.5 h, strenuous. Steep gorge to Forcella Sora Forno, then
sustained ascent with short cable-protected steps near the top. In late June this gorge
holds snow and ice; in mid-July it does not. The summit panorama takes in the whole
Fanes-Sennes-Braies plateau and the Tre Cime. Braies parking booking applies again.

**The easy option.** San Candido → Lienz on the Drava path: 43.4 km, +15 m, −517 m, ~2h50.
Rent at Papin Sport by the station, drop the bike at their Lienz branch for €7, train back
as a foot passenger. Note the cheap tariff band ends 26 July, so you are inside it.
Buy the combined bike-and-person ticket at Lienz — südtirolmobil fares stop at the border.

Take Croda del Becco if the group is fit and the forecast is clean. Take the bike if the
previous two days have been long.

### Day 5 — Sat 17 Jul. To Cortina, via Monte Piana
45-minute transfer, so the content is the hike inside it. **Monte Piana** from Lago
d'Antorno: 9.2 km, ~600 m, 4–5 h, or the jeep shuttle from Misurina. The whole summit
plateau is a WWI open-air museum — trenches, tunnels and emplacements from a battle that
killed around 14,000 men. Small headlamp for the tunnel remnants.

### Day 6 — Sun 18 Jul. Lago di Sorapis
**05:30 departure.** The Passo Tre Croci lot holds about 20 cars and fills by 06:00–06:30 —
worse in July than June. 11–12.6 km, 460–630 m, 4.5–6 h, but only 114 m of net gain: this
is a traverse, and the difficulty is exposure. Ladders, boards and cabled ledges, with the
Cengia del Banco a genuine drop. Do not use the 216 as a return loop.
Water saturates turquoise 09:00–11:00, which is also peak crowd. Rifugio Vandelli serves
lunch 11:30–14:30, cash only.

### Day 7 — Mon 19 Jul. Lagazuoi and Scotoni
Cable car from Passo Falzarego, 2min50 to 2,752 m, first up 09:00, **parking free all summer**.
**The tunnel descent (Percorso 6):** 647 m over ~3 km, 1–1.5 h inside the mountain plus
30–45 min out. **Headlamp mandatory** — it is pitch black. Helmet recommended. Cold, damp,
steep rock-cut stairs with a cable handrail. Not for claustrophobia or serious vertigo.
The payoff is the tunnel interior, mid-descent, not the summit view.
Afternoon: **Rifugio Scotoni** — from Capanna Alpina it is 2 km and about 50 minutes up,
and the open-grill lunch is what the place is known for. Or Passo Valparola, Forte Tre
Sassi and Lago di Valparola, five minutes from the road.

### Day 8 — Tue 20 Jul. Nuvolau and Averau — by chairlift at last
**This is the day the date change pays for itself.** The Fedare chairlift at Passo Giau
opened on 13 July, so the short approach to Rifugio Averau and the Nuvolau summit is
available instead of the long walk from Bai de Dones.
Rifugio Nuvolau sits on a 2,575 m pinnacle with a 360° panorama; Averau is the better
lunch. Book Averau, +39 0436 4660. **Nuvolau's rooms open 20 June and sell out in
February** if you want the night.
Cinque Torri and its free open-air WWI trench museum sit at the top of the 5 Torri
chairlift on the same road — the best history-for-no-effort site in the range.
**Sunset at Lago di Limides**, 20–30 min off the Falzarego road, mirroring Tofana di Rozes.
It shrinks as summer dries it, so mid-July is the last reliable window. Needs dead calm.
Sunset 20:59, colour to ~21:30 — headlamp for the walk out.

### Day 9 — Wed 21 Jul. Cortina to Val Gardena, the scenic way
65 km / 1h30 via Falzarego, Valparola, San Cassiano, Corvara, Passo Gardena.
**Verify Passo Gardena before committing.** A pilot from 1 September 2026 capped
non-resident day-trippers at 150 pre-booked slots and was explicitly a trial for a full
May–October closure planned for 2027. Backups: Campolongo–Pordoi–Sella, ~2h, or north via
Brunico and Bressanone, ~2h.
Expect roughly twice the mapping-app time: sustained low gear, 33 turns on Pordoi, and
heavy fast motorcycle traffic crossing the centre line on blind hairpins.
Stop: **La Val** — European Hiking Village, centuries-old *viles* hamlets, and the
Armentara meadows beneath Sas dla Crusc. Optional lunch at **Ütia de Bioch**, 30 min on
foot from Piz Sorega, 08:30–17:30, Josper grill and 1,700 wine labels.

### Day 10 — Thu 22 Jul. Sass Pordoi and Piz Boè, 3,152 m — *newly safe*
Cable car from Passo Pordoi to 2,950 m in four minutes, €18 one-way / €32 return,
09:00–17:00. Then Forcella Pordoi to **Piz Boè, 3,152 m**, the high point of the Sella.
Moderate-to-difficult: exposed sections and short cable-protected *ferratine*, with a steep
exposed final stretch. **In late June this holds ice and wants crampons. In mid-July it
does not** — which is precisely why the dates moved.
**Rifugio Capanna Piz Fassa sits on the summit** — the highest staffed rifugio in the
Dolomites, bar and restaurant always open.
Easier alternative on the same lift: the **Viel dal Pan / Bindelweg** balcony traverse,
7.5–9.2 km, 250–513 m, 2.5–4 h. The Marmolada glacier and Lago di Fedaia unfold
continuously along the route — the view is not at an endpoint, so do not rush to the hut.

### Day 11 — Fri 23 Jul. Alpe di Siusi
**Cable car from Siusi, not the car.** €30 return, 08:00–19:00, free parking at the base,
15 min up — sidesteps both the 09:00–17:00 driving ban and the €30 pre-booked P2 slot.
Flowers are past their peak now but the plateau still works.
By appetite: easy 6.8 km rolling loop · **Bullaccia/Puflatsch and the Witches' Benches**,
4.8 km, 152 m, 1.5–2.5 h · **Forcella Denti di Terrarossa**, 2,499 m under the Rosszähne
spires, 6–8 h and ~1,000 m.
Do not confuse the Terrarossa walk with Via Ferrata Maximilian on the ridge above it —
long unprotected scrambling despite a beginner billing.

### Day 12 — Sat 24 Jul. Seceda, then Val di Funes at sunset
**Pre-booked 30-minute slots, non-refundable. €74 return or €49 one-way, plus €5 per
person at the ridge turnstile. Excluded from DolomitiSuperSummer** — only the Gardena Card
covers it. Cheaper door: **Col Raiser from Santa Cristina, €34 return**, plus ~1 h uphill.
**The famous photograph is not at the top station.** Walk the ridge toward the Fermeda
towers. 100–400 mm telephoto is the lens that matters. Meadows are off-limits and rangers
enforce; drones are illegal in the nature park, fines from €3,000.
Lift runs 08:30–17:30, so the ridge is empty at sunrise and sunset by definition — but only
on foot.
Late afternoon: **Val di Funes**, ~40 min. Barriers block non-residents from driving to
Santa Maddalena; park in the village and walk 20–30 min to the panorama. **Sunset light**,
70–200 mm to compress church against the Odle wall. **San Giovanni in Ranui** is on private
Ranuihof land with a paid viewing point — the exact rule could not be confirmed from any
published source. Call +39 339 604 46 85. Do not enter the meadow.

### Day 13 — Sun 25 Jul. Adolf Munkel Weg, then Bolzano
**Adolf Munkel Weg** under the Odle wall: 7.5–9 km, ~420 m, ~3 h, zero exposure. The payoff
is the whole trail — a continuous close-up of Sass Rigais and the Furchetta above meadow
and larch. Lunch at Geisler Alm / Rifugio Odle at the midpoint, walk-in.
Zanser Alm parking runs on a **traffic light**: green means drive up and pay on arrival,
red means pre-booked only. If red, park at Ranui and take **Bus 330**.
Afternoon: **Bolzano**, ~50 min. **Ötzi** at the South Tyrol Museum of Archaeology, €13,
10:00–18:00, last entry 17:30, open daily in July. Reservations recommended.
Bolzano has an active ZTL — garage and walk, or the fine reaches Canada months later.

### Day 14 — Mon 26 Jul. Bolzano to Venice
280 km / 2h40, A22 then A4, tolls ~€20. Fill up before the airport. Blue "Carte" lanes for
card payment; **never a yellow Telepass lane**.

### ⚠ Travelling with a 10-year-old — and the good news is the Dolomites suit him better

| Activity | Limit | A 10-year-old? |
|---|---|---|
| **Guided via ferrata** | **~10 is the accepted minimum** on easy routes; under 18 case-by-case and must be with a parent | **Yes, on the right route** |
| **Cinque Torri beginner ferrata** | Grade **A/B — the easiest Italian grade**, explicitly suitable for **10+ comfortable with heights** | **Yes — and it is already in this plan** |
| Funbob, San Candido | rides alone from 8 | Yes |
| Paragliding, Seceda | age 4+, no upper limit | Yes |
| Rafting, Rienza | min age 6 | Yes |
| Rafting, Isarco advanced | min age 14 | No |
| Alpine Coaster Gardonè | 9–14 accompanied by someone 14+ | Yes, but off-route |

**The constraint on via ferrata at this age is psychological, not physical** — guides describe it
as the ability to follow safety instructions under stress at height, not strength. That is worth
knowing when judging whether he is ready.

**The Cinque Torri find is the one to act on.** `research/trails.md` already has Cinque Torri in
the itinerary for the rock towers and the Nuvolau panorama. There is a **beginner A/B-graded via
ferrata at the base of the towers**, and it is the route most often named as the family
introduction in this region. That turns an existing stop into the trip's adventure centrepiece
without adding a day.

Junior gear rents for **€15–20/day** in Cortina — cheaper than the €25–35 adult figure below.

### The pricing model to understand first

**A Dolomites mountain guide charges per GROUP, not per person, and the fee is split.**
Via ferrata is **€380–500 for the group**, max 3–4 people on a medium or difficult route,
5–6 on a very easy one. So a pair pays ~€200 each and a four pays ~€110 each — the group size
changes the per-head cost more than the route does.

- **Harness, helmet and via ferrata set are included in the guide fee.**
- **Lift tickets are not.** Several ferrata approaches start from a paid cable car.
- Prices exclude 5% VAT.
- **Self-guided:** full kit rents for **€25–35/day** in Cortina. Only for people already solid on
  cables — `research/trails.md` is clear that several routes here are EEA-graded and the
  Bonacossa traverse is explicitly not for non-expert hikers.
- ⚠ **GAP: no guide service publishes a minimum age.** If anyone under ~14 is coming, ask before
  building a day around it.

### Funbob, Monte Baranci — at your first base, and the easy win

**The longest rail toboggan in South Tyrol, in San Candido itself.** 1,739 m of track, **314 m
drop, 40 km/h**, on new Mountain Coaster 2.0 sleds. Chairlift from the village to 1,500 m.

| | Adult | Junior (<18) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 ride | **€21.50** | €15.00 |
| 3 rides | €48.50 | €34.00 |
| Unlimited, mornings | €60.00 | €43.50 |

Open **09:00–18:30** across 13–16 July. **Under 3 not permitted; under 8 must ride with an
adult; from 8 they ride alone with ID.** No booking needed.

### Paragliding over Seceda — base 3

Gardenafly fly from **Seceda**, which is the Val Gardena view. Panoramic, Thermal and Superior
flights, **age 4 upwards with no upper limit**. **+39 392 1286600.**
⚠ **GAP: price, weight limit and cancellation terms.** Call before the trip, not on the day.

### Rafting, Val Pusteria — from San Candido

**Rienza river: minimum age 6. Isarco advanced: minimum age 14.** Rafting Club Aktiv,
Valle Aurina. ⚠ **GAP: prices.**

### Cortina — and one thing that does not exist

Tofana lifts run **13 Jun – 29 Sep, 09:00–17:00**, covering 17–20 July. **Cortina Bike Park
Dolomiti** is 31 km of track and 4,000 m of vertical over the same window.

**There is no zipline in Cortina.** Searched repeatedly and found none — the adrenaline offering
here is via ferrata, bike park and paragliding. Worth stating plainly so nobody goes looking.

### Ruled out

**Alpine Coaster Gardonè** (€6, min height 105 cm) is genuinely good but sits at Obereggen south
of Bolzano — off every base bar the single Bolzano night, and a detour even then. The Funbob is
longer, cheaper per thrill and already at base 1.

### The gap that runs through everything

**No Dolomites operator publishes weather-cancellation or refund terms** — the same hole the
Switzerland research hit. Given the afternoon-storm pattern these dates sit in, ask at booking
every single time.

## Val Gardena — which village, and is the lift card worth it

### The three villages

| | Elevation | Character | Pick it if |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ortisei** | 1,236 m | Best town atmosphere, best pedestrian centre, most shops | You want a proper village in the evenings. ⚠ **Strict camera-enforced ZTL in the centre — the hotel must issue a permit on arrival.** |
| **Santa Cristina** | 1,428 m | About a third the size of Selva, **bypassed by the main road**, quietest of the three | You want quiet and a central position. Also the **Col Raiser** door to Seceda at €34. |
| **Selva** | 1,563 m | The sport hub — highest, liveliest, Sellaronda access | You want to be closest to Vallunga, Passo Gardena and Dantercepies. |

**The choice matters less than it looks.** Every guest gets the **Val Gardena Mobil Card free**
from any affiliated accommodation — unlimited public buses between all three villages, running
every 10–15 minutes at peak, plus most South Tyrol public transport. Pick on price and quiet, not
on logistics.

### Gardena Card — do the arithmetic before buying

All 18 Val Gardena lifts, valid 6 Jun – 11 Oct. **Seceda is included but still needs its own
pre-booked slot at seceda.it.**

| | Adult | Junior (u18) | Under 8 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 consecutive days | **€124** | **€87** | €5 |
| 6 consecutive days | €160 | €112 | €5 |

**As the itinerary currently stands it does not pay.** Only one of the three Val Gardena days uses
a Gardena Card lift: Sass Pordoi is a different valley, and Alpe di Siusi is planned from Siusi
rather than the Ortisei cable car. That leaves Seceda — €79 all-in direct, or €34 via Col Raiser.
**Buy Seceda separately and skip the card**, unless you restructure the block around Val Gardena's
own lifts, in which case the 3-day card wins quickly.

### The missing day — Vallunga

The Val Gardena block is Piz Boè (3,152 m, exposed, cable-protected sections), Alpe di Siusi and
Seceda. **There is no low-effort day in it**, which is a problem with a ten-year-old and with
afternoon storms.

**Vallunga fixes that.** A flat-floored glacial trough inside Puez-Odle Nature Park, 2.7 km from
Selva. Wide gravel path, **~4.5 km each way and only 200–250 m of gain — the ascent is barely
perceptible**, about 1 h 15 to the hut at the valley end. Explicitly recommended for small
children and grandparents. Pay-and-display car park at the trailhead, **fills early in peak
season**. It is the weather bail-out, the recovery day after Piz Boè, and the obvious choice for
his legs.

## Not doing: hut-to-hut

Set aside at Nitin's request, kept here in case it comes back. The candidate was Alta Via 1
stages 1–3 — **Lago di Braies → Rifugio Biella (14.4 km, 1,210 m) → Rifugio Fanes
(~4.5 h, 565 m) → Rifugio Lagazuoi (~5 h, 1,070 m)** — roughly 40 km and 2,800 m over three
days, with no mandatory via ferrata and a cable car at the far end. The draw is a night at
2,752 m on Lagazuoi and descending the WWI tunnels next morning instead of riding up to them.

If it is ever revived: leave the car in **Cortina**, not Braies (Braies parking is sold by
the day and cannot be left for three nights). Cortina Express 445 to Dobbiaco, then 442 to
the lake; DolomitiBus 52 back from Falzarego. **Rifugio Biella opens bookings 1 November
2026 with a 50% non-refundable deposit**, and Lagazuoi takes the same — so the decision
belongs to autumn 2026, not spring. It would cost Croda del Becco, Nuvolau, Limides,
Piz Boè and the Drava ride.

## Car — needed all 14 days

Rent at Venice, return at Venice. Do not split the rental; mid-trip drops in the mountains
are expensive and the branch network is thin.

Genuinely load-bearing on 12 of the 14 days. It sits idle only if you take the Drava bike
option on day 4, where you rent a bike at the station and train back.

The reason buses cannot replace it is timing, not distance. Public transport runs roughly
08:00–18:00; this itinerary lives outside that on purpose — Braies at 05:00, Sorapis at
05:30, Limides at 21:30, Val di Funes at sunset.

**Deliberately do not drive:** up to Alpe di Siusi (take the cable car), into Bolzano's ZTL
(garage and walk), or to Prato Piazza and Val Fiscalina when the shuttles are running.

Book an **automatic 3–4 months ahead** — scarce in Italy, and Marco Polo is a high-demand
branch. Compact, not large. **IDP from CAA before leaving Canada** — driving without one
can void the collision cover.

## Where to stay

| Town | Nights | Why | Alternative |
|---|---|---|---|
| **San Candido** | 4 | Braies, Tre Cime, Prato Piazza, Val Fiscalina, and the Drava ride starts at the station | **Dobbiaco** is 10 min closer to Braies and Tre Cime but a plainer town |
| **Cortina** | 4 | Sorapis, the Falzarego road, Nuvolau, and real town amenities including a hospital | **San Vito di Cadore**, 15 min south, ~40% cheaper |
| **Ortisei / Selva** | 4 | Alpe di Siusi, Seceda, Val di Funes, Sella passes | **Selva** is the hiker's town and closer to the passes; **Santa Cristina** is cheapest and has the Col Raiser back door to Seceda |
| **Bolzano** | 1 | Cuts an hour off the airport drive, adds Ötzi | Skip it for a 4th Gardena night and accept a 2h50 final drive |

**Filter South Tyrol hotels for the Südtirol Guest Pass** — free, included in the room rate,
and it covers nearly all South Tyrol public transport plus some cable cars. San Candido,
Val Gardena and Bolzano qualify. Cortina is in Veneto and does not.

## Packing

Boots with a heel welt · headlamp, mandatory in the Lagazuoi tunnels and for 21:30 sunsets ·
waterproof jacket **and** pants · hat and gloves for dawn at 2,500 m · polarizer ·
100–400 mm telephoto for Seceda · **cash for rifugi** ·
a TIM-leaning Italian eSIM, ~USD 33 for 30 days against ~CAD 230 roaming.
Microspikes are **no longer needed** — that is a June problem.
Emergency **112**, works with no SIM. Confirm insurance explicitly covers helicopter
mountain rescue and repatriation; many policies exclude both.
