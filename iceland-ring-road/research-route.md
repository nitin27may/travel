# Iceland — route, stops and activity limits

## The Ring Road
**Route 1 is 1,332 km** and could be driven without stopping in about 17–20 hours. Most people take
7–10 days. This plan takes **12 days and covers ~2,190 km**, because it adds Snæfellsnes, Seyðisfjörður,
Dettifoss/Ásbyrgi and Stokksnes — none of which are on Route 1 itself.

**Direction: counter-clockwise** (south coast first). Two reasons. The south coast is the most
weather-exposed stretch, so it gets the earliest and statistically driest days of the window. And the
north and east — where the trip ends up on nights 6–9 — is where the astronomical dark arrives during
the trip, so the aurora chances land in the right half.

## Activity limits — the facts that decide who can do what

### Silfra snorkelling, Þingvellir
Drifting between the North American and Eurasian plates in 2 °C glacial meltwater, in a dry suit.
- **Minimum age 12.** Under-18s need a parent or guardian booked on the same tour.
- **Height 150–200 cm. Weight 45–120 kg.** These are dry-suit fit limits, not policy.
- **Must be a competent swimmer and comfortable in water.**
- **Medical form is mandatory before booking.** A "yes" to anything in Section 1 — heart or
  blood-vessel disease, heart attack, cardiac or vascular surgery, angina, **pregnancy**, lung
  disease, collapsed lung, chest surgery, epilepsy or seizures — **disqualifies you outright**.
  A "yes" in Section 2 — including **being 60 or older**, high blood pressure, fainting, cancer or
  cancer treatment within two years, high cholesterol, family history of heart disease — requires
  **written doctor's clearance** in advance.

### Glacier walks — Sólheimajökull and Skaftafell (Falljökull)
- **Easy 3-hour hike: minimum age 8**, roughly 90 minutes on the ice, about **US$105**.
- **Advanced 5-hour hike: minimum age 12**, roughly 3 hours on the ice, about **US$162**.
- **Ice climbing: about US$251.**
- Falljökull tours run from the Tröll Expeditions base at Skaftafell; **groups capped at ten,
  minimum age 12**.
- Prices are 2026 GetYourGuide figures. GAP: no 2027 prices.

### Whale watching, Húsavík
Humpback and minke are the September species; blue whales are early-season only.
**Puffins are gone** — operators stop selling whale-and-puffin combinations from 15 August.
GAP: September 2027 sailing times and prices not obtained.

## Site-specific conditions checked

**Reynisfjara** — a **landslide and severe coastal erosion in February 2026** narrowed the beach; one
viewing platform was largely destroyed by wave surge and needs assessment. Access is managed by
safety zone and can be restricted or closed depending on surf, tide and wind. **Since August 2025
entering Hálsanefshellir cave is prohibited whenever the red warning light flashes.** Sneaker waves
here have killed people. Stay high on the beach; never turn your back on the water.

**Fjaðrárgljúfur** — no entrance fee. **Lower lot parking 1,000 ISK**, upper lot free. Seasonal
closures are possible in May and early June to protect vegetation; check **ust.is** or
**safetravel.is**. Not expected to affect September.

**Reykjanes / Svartsengi** — no eruption underway. The Sundhnúkur sequence that began December 2023
has slowed; the ninth eruption ran **16 July – 5 August 2025**. **Uplift continues at Svartsengi and
magma volume is the highest since the sequence began**, so a further eruption or dike intrusion is
possible. Blue Lagoon operating normally as of June 2026 (3.5 km from Svartsengi, evacuates on
instruction). Grindavík is open but has crevasses — stay on paths.

**Walk distances** — Svartifoss: sources disagree, **5.4 km return / ~120 m gain** on the direct
route versus **7.1 km / 310 m** for the full Sjónarnípa circuit. Report the range, pick on the day.
Hverfjall: the crater rim is **~3 km in circumference**; 30–45 min up from the car park, ~1 h around,
1.5–2 h total.

## Flights
**YYZ → KEF is a 5 h 35 m overnight**, roughly 19 blocks weekly on Icelandair and Air Canada
combined. Departures 19:15–23:25, arrivals **06:00–09:00**. Day 1 therefore begins at Keflavík at
dawn with a night of aeroplane sleep behind you — which is why Day 1 is deliberately light.
