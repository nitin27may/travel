Before you add this day

The one thing that changes this plan: the tram is down

The White Mountains are a different range from the Green Mountains you're driving on to. Vermont is worn-down, ice-scraped Appalachian rock, rounded and forested to the summit. New Hampshire's Whites are younger and harder granite, cut through by deep glacial notches — Franconia Notch, Crawford Notch, Pinkham Notch — and they top out much higher: Mount Washington is 6,288 ft, the highest peak in the northeastern US, against Vermont's 4,393 ft.

What the region is actually famous for is two specific drives, not a single view: the Kancamagus Highway ("the Kanc"), a 34.5-mile National Scenic Byway through the national forest with almost no commercial development, and Franconia Notch, a narrow pass with Flume Gorge (a granite gorge walk) at its south end.

The plan has to change because of a real closure, not a preference. Cannon Mountain's Aerial Tramway — the no-hike way up for a summit view — retired its 45-year-old tram on October 26, 2025, and New Hampshire is building a third-generation replacement. The earliest realistic reopening is the 2028–29 season. This page substitutes Artist's Bluff & Bald Mountain, a short trail at the same trailhead that looks across at Cannon Mountain instead of riding up it.

Artist's Bluff & Bald Mountain

A short, steep loop to a rocky ledge over Echo Lake, with Cannon Mountain rising straight across the notch — the closest thing to the tramway's payoff without the tramway. Free, no timed entry, dirt lot right on Route 18 across from the Cannon Mountain base area.

Why this instead of the tramway

Cannon Mountain's Aerial Tramway retired its second-generation tram on October 26, 2025, after 45 years of service. New Hampshire is building a third-generation system, targeted for the 2028–29 season at the earliest — a $27.2M project, bids due mid-2026. It will not be running for this trip. Artist's Bluff is the nearest substitute view and needs no ticket.

9:15 – 10:30 AM

Flume Gorge

A boardwalk loop through a granite gorge with an 800-foot-long chasm, plus a covered bridge and Avalanche Falls — about two miles of easy, flat walking, family-friendly. Opens 9:00 AM through October 12 (9–5), so this is right after the gates open.

~2 mi loop75–90 minAdult $18 online / $21 windowChild 6–12 $16 / $19, under 6 free

Book online first

Tickets are $3 cheaper per person bought online in advance, and it likely means less time in a ticket-window line. Hours are weather-dependent and can change without notice — call 603-745-8391 to confirm on the day if it matters.

11:25 – 11:40 AM

C.L. Graham Wangan Ground overlook

The high point of the Kancamagus, just beyond Kancamagus Pass, with a wide view over the Swift River watershed and the mountains to the north and east. A paved pull-off with interpretive signage — no hiking needed, and a good stretch after the drive up from Lincoln.

11:55 AM – 12:35 PM

Sabbaday Falls — and lunch

A tiered waterfall reached by a genuinely short, easy walk with a mini loop of wooden rails above the falls — one of the most popular stops on the Kanc for a reason. Packed lunch at the picnic area either before or after the walk.

0.7 mi RT~20 min walkingParking $3, cash, no change given

Swap option: Rocky Gorge

Rocky Gorge Scenic Area is closer to Lincoln than Sabbaday Falls and has its own short paved walkway to a footbridge over the Swift River, plus a pond loop if anyone wants to stretch further. Similar $3 fee. Map · AllTrails. Swimming in the gorge itself is prohibited and enforced.
