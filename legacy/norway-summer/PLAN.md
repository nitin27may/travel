# Norway — 14 nights, summer 2028. Dates open.

Research lives in the five `research/*.md` files beside this; the light figures come from
`sun-times.py`, which is reproducible. The deliverable is `index.html`.

---

## Constraints now fixed

- **Year: summer 2028.** Exact dates open.
- **Hiking ceiling: 12 km round trip.** Every hike is now measured against it — two things
  failed and are cut.
- **The day-4 drive to Andenes stays.** The whale safari and the Gryllefjord crossing are in.

## What the 12 km ceiling changed

| Hike | Distance | Gain | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reinebringen | **2 km** trail RT (~5 km from the lot) | 448 m, **1,978 steps** | fits easily |
| Hesten — the Segla view | **5.3 km** RT | 510–550 m | fits easily |
| Segla summit | **5 km** RT | 610 m, strenuous | fits — separate trailhead |
| Ryten, viewpoint only | **8.7 km** RT | 680 m | fits |
| Romsdalseggen | **10–11 km** point-to-point | **970 m up / 1,277 m down** | fits — the biggest day |
| **Ryten + down to Kvalvika beach** | **12–13 km** | ~930 m, 5–7 h | **OVER — beach descent cut** |
| **Vidden, Ulriken → Fløyen** | **13–15 km** | 4–6 h, often 7–8 | **OVER — cut entirely** |

**Two cuts.** Ryten is now the **viewpoint only**; going down to the beach and back is what
pushes it past the ceiling. And **Vidden is gone** — the Bergen substitute is the Ulriken cable
car up and back, same view, no distance.

**One upgrade the numbers unlocked.** Hesten and Segla are both about 5 km round trip and their
trailheads sit **600 m apart in Fjordgård**, so *both* fit in one day — about 10.3 km total.
The catch: **they cannot be linked**, each is its own out-and-back from its own start.

**On Romsdalseggen, note which way the numbers point.** It climbs 970 m and **descends 1,277 m**
into Åndalsnes. The descent is the longer half, and it is what will be felt the next day.

## What changed once the year was fixed to 2028

**The recommended window flipped.** The first pass defaulted to late July using 2026's
*fellesferie* dates. *Fellesferie* is ISO weeks 28–30 every year, and in **2028 that is
10–30 July** — a full week later than 2026's 6–24 July.

That creates a window 2026 did not have: **~24 June to 8 July 2028 is entirely ahead of the
Norwegian domestic surge *and* entirely inside the midnight sun.** Svolvær runs 25 May –
18 July, Reine 27 May – 16 July, Senja to 24 July, Tromsø to 25 July.

The usual objection to late June is snow on the high ridges — and it does not apply here,
because no high inland ridge is in this plan. What *is* in it opens earlier: the
Romsdalseggen bus runs **from 12 June**, the Nibbevegen up Dalsnibba opens **mid-to-late
May**, and Loen and Nigardsbreen run from spring.

**So the trip is now built Arctic-first**, to reach Lofoten and Senja while the sun does
not set, and to finish in the fjords.

## The other four findings that shaped the plan

1. **Trollstigen is not a plan, it is a bonus.** Shut for all of 2024, opened only 11 July
   in 2025, opened unusually early in 2026 — and a landslide on 27 August 2026 shut it
   again, "for the rest of the year… to all motorized vehicles, cyclists and pedestrians."
   A three-season securing programme is approved that closes it **from 15 August every
   year of the works**, and it is closed preemptively in heavy rain by design. Nothing here
   depends on it. Geiranger is reached by **Ørnevegen, the only year-round road in**.

2. **Do not enter Lofoten by the Bodø–Moskenes ferry.** Only part of its capacity is
   reservable; the rest is a queue that genuinely packs out in summer. **Fly to Evenes
   (EVE)** — road-connected via the E10, no ferry, and it sets up the Senja loop.

3. **The best light is 22:00–02:00 and almost nobody uses it.** In Window A there is no
   true night anywhere north of Ålesund. Loen Skylift runs to 22:00. Reinebringen at 23:00
   is empty and better lit than at noon. The plan runs a deliberate **second shift**.

4. **Sleep in Aurland, not Flåm.** Flåm is a cruise port with a railway attached. Aurland
   is ten minutes away and quiet after 18:00; you drive into Flåm for the 08:30 ferry.

**Also verified, against common assumptions:** Preikestolen no longer needs the Tau ferry
(Ryfast tunnel — pre-2020 guides are wrong). Nordkapp charges **no** entrance or parking
fee since a 2021 court ruling; skip it for distance, not money. And there is **no summer
aurora** anywhere in Norway, in either window.

## The shape

Bergen to Lofoten is **1,300–1,500 km, about 24 hours of driving**. No 14-night trip drives
it. Three honest options existed; this is the one chosen:

| | **Chosen** | Alternative B | Alternative C |
|---|---|---|---|
| | **Arctic 6 + fjords 7, one internal flight** | Fjords only, 14 | Arctic only, 14 |
| Cost | ~1.5 days transit, two rentals | No transit, no Arctic | No fjords |
| Verdict | **Two weeks is exactly where this stops being greedy** | Runs out of new material by night 11 | Extraordinary, but you flew to Norway and skipped the fjords |

**Open-jaw: in to Evenes, out of Bergen.** The awkward two-stop leg lands on the way out,
when you have a whole day to lose; the way home is Icelandair's single-stop Bergen–Keflavík–
Toronto. There is no nonstop from Toronto in either direction. Icelandair's Tromsø route is
**winter-only** and does not help.

**Ten bases across fourteen nights.** Svolvær 1 · Reine 2 · Andenes 1 · Senja 2 · Ålesund 1 ·
Åndalsnes 2 · Geiranger 1 · Loen 2 · Aurland 1 · Bergen 1. Six of them are single nights, which
is the cost of a two-region trip — every one of those singles is placed where a single night is
the *point* (Geiranger for the empty hours, Andenes for the whales, Ålesund for the evening).

**Two cars, and the fjord leg is driven north to south.** Car A: Evenes → Tromsø.
Car B: Ålesund → Bergen. Driving the fjords southbound means the trip ends in a city with an
airport instead of a dawn drive to one.

# Days 1–7: the Arctic, under the midnight sun

**Car A: Evenes (EVE) → Tromsø (TOS).**

### Day 1 — arrive · **N1 Svolvær / Henningsvær**

YYZ → hub → OSL → EVE. Drive **EVE → Svolvær on the E10, ~2h45**. No ferry, no queue.
It will be broad daylight whatever the clock says; do not treat the arrival hour as a
constraint.

### Day 2 · **N2 Reine / Hamnøy**

- **Trollfjord sea-eagle RIB** from Svolvær — from ~USD 122. A fjord 100 m wide with walls
  to 1,000 m, and one of Europe's densest white-tailed eagle populations. `Book ahead.`
- **Henningsvær** on the skerries, then **Nusfjord**, then west to Reine (~2h15 with stops).

### Day 3 · **N3 Reine / Hamnøy**

- **Ryten by day — the viewpoint, not the beach.** 8.7 km round trip, 680 m gain, 3–5 hours,
  for the overhead view down onto Kvalvika. **Do not descend to the beach**: that turns it into
  a 12–13 km loop with ~930 m of gain, over the ceiling.
  Parking: the main lot at the end of Fv806, Innersand paid at NOK 100 — and the **Torsfjorden
  trailhead lot holds about sixteen cars**, so treat it as full from mid-morning.
- **Reinebringen at 22:00.** **1,978 Sherpa-built steps — 2 km round trip on the trail, about
  5 km from the nearest paid lot, 448 m of gain, 1.5–3.5 hours.** **The parking is harder than
  the hike**: all of Reine is now a no-parking zone outside the paid lots — Steffenakken free
  but tiny and gone by breakfast, Reine Kulturhus **NOK 150 for 3–5 h**, harbour
  **NOK 25–40/hour** on EasyPark. At 22:00 the stairs are empty and the light is better.

### Day 4 — the long leg · **N4 Andenes**

**Å i Lofoten** in the morning — literally the end of the E10 — then **Reine → Andenes,
roughly 4–5 hours** by E10 and Rv85/82. **Confirmed in:** the whales and the Gryllefjord
crossing are worth the day.

**The Fiskebøl–Melbu ferry — GAP CLOSED, with a caveat.** It is **free** (cars and passengers
alike, no AutoPASS registration), takes **25 minutes**, runs **every two hours** from 06:20, and
takes no reservations. It cuts the corner across Hadselfjorden. But the widely-repeated claim
that it "saves at least two hours" **does not apply to this leg** — the all-road route already
runs via Sortland, not Lødingen, so the real saving here is plausibly 45–60 minutes. **And on a
two-hourly service, arriving five minutes late costs more than the shortcut saves.** Check the
sailing before choosing it; otherwise just drive.

### Day 5 · **N5 Senja (Fjordgård / Hamn)**

- **Whale safari from Andenes** in the morning — catamaran from ~USD 199 (discounted ~159),
  speedboat with puffins ~194. Season late May to mid-September; **puffins to late August**.
  Rough water on the shelf edge; take that seriously. `Book ahead.`
- **Andenes → Gryllefjord ferry**, summer only, **13 May – 27 Sep**, 2–3 weekday sailings,
  first 08:45 and last **17:00**, ~100 minutes, **~NOK 925** car and driver, pay on board.
  Take the 17:00 after the whales and arrive on Senja in bright evening light.

### Day 6 · **N6 Senja**

**Hesten from Fjordgård — 5.3 km round trip, 510–550 m, 2–3 hours.** Hesten is where the famous
photograph *of* Segla is taken from; the summit of Segla is not. It is graded hard despite the
modest numbers, and **is not for anyone uneasy with exposure**.

**Both peaks fit in the day if you want them.** Segla's own summit is **5 km round trip, 610 m,
3–4 hours, strenuous** — about 10.3 km for the pair, inside the ceiling. The trailheads are
**600 m apart in Fjordgård, but the two hikes cannot be linked**; each is its own out-and-back.
Large paid lot at Fjordgård, EasyPark.

Then **Tungeneset** and **Bergsbotn** on the National Tourist Route. Senja is the
deliberate anti-Lofoten: the same landscape with a fraction of the traffic. The trade is
thin accommodation, thin restaurants, and narrow roads full of campervans — so drive the
route early or late, which the light makes easy.

`GAP CLOSED 2026-08-31` for Hesten and Segla — numbers above, and both fit the ceiling.
`GAP:` **Husfjellet** and **Barden** still have no verified numbers; only worth closing if
either gets added to the day.

### Day 7 — the transfer · **N7 Ålesund**

Senja → Tromsø **~3h**. Drop Car A. Fly **TOS → AES**. Pick up Car B.
**418 steps up Aksla** in the evening for the Art Nouveau town, the archipelago and the
Sunnmøre Alps — the best cheap view in Norway, and the right way to meet the fjords.

# Days 8–15: Fjord Norway, north to south

**Car B: Ålesund (AES) → Bergen (BGO).**

### Day 8 · **N8 Åndalsnes**

Ålesund → Åndalsnes **~2h**. **Rampestreken** on foot or the **Romsdalen gondola**
(NOK **300** one way / **450** return) for the first look down Romsdalen at Trollveggen.

> Optional, adds ~2h: the **Atlantic Road** — 8.3 km, 8 bridges, Storseisundet, and the
> suspended walkway at Eldhusøya. **Better in weather than in sun**; it is built over a
> shipwreck coast and should look like one.

### Day 9 — the big one · **N9 Åndalsnes**

**Romsdalseggen** — ~10 km, **970 m of gain**, 7–8 hours along a ridge with Trollveggen on
one side and Romsdalsfjord on the other. **The logistics are already solved**: a dedicated
bus leaves **platform 5 at Norsk Tindesenter**, **daily from 12 June**, 20 minutes to the
Vengedalen start. You walk back into town — no car shuttle, no second driver. `Book ahead.`

Weather backup from the same base: the gondola, Rampestreken, or **Via Ferrata
Romsdalsstigen**, reputedly the hardest in Norway.

### Day 10 · **N10 Geiranger**

Åndalsnes → Geiranger via Valldal, the **Eidsdal–Linge ferry** and **Ørnevegen** — 11
hairpins, 620 m of climb, ~2h15. **Drive Trollstigen only if it is open that day.**

> `GAP:` the detour when Trollstigen is shut is most likely E136 to Sjøholt, Rv650 to
> Liabygda, the Liabygda–Stranda ferry, then Stranda–Hellesylt. **Unverified** — check
> vegvesen.no and Entur before committing a day to it.

**One night, and the whole point is the hours nobody else is there.** Ships berth
mid-morning and the tour-bus system runs to late afternoon; the lot at Flydalsjuvet is
small and fills instantly. So: **Ørnesvingen at 21:00 tonight, Flydalsjuvet at 06:30
tomorrow. Not in between.**

> **Before booking this night, pull Geiranger's cruise-call list** for the date from
> cruisetimetables.com. Shifting to a zero-ship or one-ship day is worth more than every
> other crowd tactic on this trip combined.

### Day 11 · **N11 Loen**

**Flydalsjuvet at 06:30.** Then **Dalsnibba** — 1,500 m, the Geiranger Skywalk, up the
Nibbevegen toll road — and over Grotli and Videseter to Loen, **~1h45**.

**Loen Skylift** in the evening: return **NOK 650 adult / 345 child**, family (2+2)
**1,665**. Summer season 1 May – 11 Oct; it runs to **22:00, last down 22:30**. Ride it
after the day-trippers have gone.

### Day 12 — pick one · **N12 Loen**

- **Via Ferrata Loen — NOK 2,380**, including instructor, kit and the Skylift descent.
  Departures from Loen Active in the village at **09:00, 10:00, 11:00**.
  **GAP CLOSED — recommended from age 12, minimum weight 40 kg, maximum 120 kg.** Both weights
  are set by the harness and lanyard system rather than by policy, so they are not negotiable.
  No height limit is published. Bring 1.5 L of water each, gloves, long trousers and a
  windproof; shoes and gloves are rentable on site.
- **or Nigardsbreen blue-ice walk** on an arm of **Jostedalsbreen, mainland Europe's
  largest glacier** (~1h20 from Loen). Four grades: 4.5 h with ~1.5 h on ice (age 8+),
  5.5 h with 2.5 h on ice, 6 h with 4 h on ice, and a max-6 ice-climbing variant. Book by
  email; starts **09:45–12:30**; +6% booking fee.
- Weather fallback: **Gamle Strynefjellsvegen**, summer-only gravel — **not for motorhomes**.

### Day 13 · **N13 Aurland**

Loen → Aurland **~3h**. **Stegastein** by the shuttle from Flåm — ~90 min round trip,
running 1 Jun–31 Aug. The road up is narrow with vehicle-size limits; the shuttle is the
right call, not a cop-out.

### Day 14 · **N14 Bergen**

**Nærøyfjord on the 08:30 electric ferry, Flåm → Gudvangen**, back on the 11:00. UNESCO,
250 m wide at the narrowest, walls to 1,400 m. **NOK 550 adult one way, 815 return.** The
boat is silent — you hear the waterfalls instead of an engine, and that is the whole
difference from every diesel fjord cruise. The 08:30 is the quiet one.

> Swap, if the group would rather ride than sail: **Rallarvegen** — the Flåm Railway up to
> Myrdal, then **57 km downhill from Finse at 1,222 m to the fjord**, dropping the bike in
> Flåm. One-way is built into the rental system. *Added — not asked for; veto freely.*

Then Aurland → Bergen **~2h45**.

### Day 15 — out

**Bryggen** and **Fløibanen** in the morning — the funicular is six minutes. Drop Car B at
BGO. Fly **BGO → KEF → YYZ**.

Bergen is deliberately last: it sees rain on **15–20 days a month** in summer, and it is the
one base where a wet day costs nothing.

> **Vidden is cut.** The Ulriken → Fløyen plateau traverse is **13–15 km** — one source says 18
> — and the quoted 4–6 hours becomes 7–8 for most people. Over the ceiling.
>
> **The substitute, if the morning is clear:** ride the **Ulriken cable car to 643 m and come
> back down the same way.** Same view, no distance, and it does not put the flight at risk.

## Book ahead, in this order

1. **Open-jaw flights** (YYZ→EVE, BGO→YYZ) — routing matters more than price.
2. **Reine / Hamnøy rorbu** — the scarcest thing on the itinerary. **NOK 2,000–4,000/night**,
   plus the **3% visitor levy** now that Lofoten has adopted it.
3. **Romsdalseggen bus** — sale opens in advance; daily from 12 June.
4. **Whale safari, Andenes**, and the **Trollfjord RIB**.
5. **Via Ferrata Loen or the Nigardsbreen ice walk** — small groups, fixed start times.
6. **The two internal flights (TOS→AES) and both car rentals.**

Flåm sells out weeks ahead in summer on the popular departures. The Andenes–Gryllefjord
ferry does not normally need booking.

## Money

Mid-range, two people, 14 nights on the ground: **~NOK 52,000–69,000 (CAD 6,900–9,100)**
before flights and cars. Petrol **NOK 22/L** (~CAD 3). Ferries alone can run
**NOK 4,000–6,000**. Three levers actually move it:

1. **Self-cater breakfast and lunch** — groceries NOK 112–195/day per person against
   restaurant mains at NOK 190–350 each. Book kitchens. Beats any pass or card.
2. **Vinmonopolet, not bars** — NOK 35–60 against 90–130. Anything above 4.7% ABV is
   Vinmonopolet-only, and it is **closed Sundays and every public holiday**, Saturdays only
   to 16:00. Buy Saturday morning or go without.
3. **The best hours are free** — Ørnesvingen, Flydalsjuvet, Aksla, every hike above, and the
   whole National Tourist Route viewpoint network. Transport and food are what bleed.

## Driving

Speed cameras run a **~3 km/h tolerance** and **section control is standard in tunnels**.
NOK 3,350 for 6–10 km/h over; ~NOK 10,100 at 20 over. Tolls are automatic via AutoPASS and
billed back with a **NOK 30–80/day handling fee**. Ferries bill the same way — the full 50%
ferry discount needs a prepaid agreement a visitor in a rental generally cannot get, so
assume full fare. EVs are the default rental, charging is NOK 4–8/kWh across 10,000+ fast
points, and EVs pay materially less on ferries.
