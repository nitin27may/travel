## The three decisions, settled

### 1. Rail, decisively — and it is not close

| | Cost, 14 nights |
|---|---|
| **15-day 2nd-class Swiss Travel Pass** | **CHF 499** |
| All-in mid-size rental car | **CHF 1,940–2,310** |

Car components that make it lose: ZRH rental ~USD 89–97/day in July · **vignette CHF 40, annual only, no short option** · petrol **CHF 1.99/L** · Zurich parking CHF 37–45/day.

Three things kill the car outright:

- **The best bases are car-free.** Zermatt, Wengen, Mürren, Gimmelwald, Saas-Fee, Bettmeralp, Riederalp, Braunwald, Stoos. You would pay to park a car you cannot use.
- **The pass does not cover car-carrying trains.** BLS states plainly that GA and Half-Fare are not valid for car transport. The Täsch–Zermatt shuttle *is* covered.
- **⚠ TCS forecasts "double-digit queue lengths with several hours waiting" at the Gotthard north portal in July and the south portal in August.** This window straddles both. The base tunnel is unaffected — and trains use it.

A car only wins for Appenzell back roads, the Jura, Val d'Hérens, Emmental, Ticino side valleys, pre-dawn trailheads, or four-plus adults. **Break-even against the pass is roughly four paying adults.** If pass-road driving is itself the attraction — Furka, Grimsel, Susten, Nufenen, Klausen, all confirmed open in this window — the answer is a **3–4 day rental for about CHF 400**, not fourteen days.

### 2. The pass choice is genuinely open — and the answer may be the cheap one

**2027 prices are already published and unchanged from 2026.** Swiss Travel Pass 15-day 2nd class **CHF 499**. **Half Fare Card CHF 150**, valid one month from any start date, so one card covers the whole trip.

**The Travel Pass only wins if you would otherwise generate CHF 700+ of full fares.** Calibration: Zurich–Zermatt is CHF 130, Basel–Interlaken CHF 64. A three-base itinerary with only two long transfers may not get there.

**What the Travel Pass actually makes free on mountains — the entire list is three:** **Rigi, Stanserhorn, Stoos.** Everything else is a discount:

| Coverage | Mountains |
|---|---|
| **100% free** | Rigi · Stanserhorn · Stoos funicular |
| **50%** | Gornergrat · Matterhorn Glacier Paradise · Titlis · Pilatus · Schilthorn (Mürren–Schilthorn section) · Schynige Platte · Männlichen · First · Harder Kulm · Brienzer Rothorn · Glacier 3000 · Monte Generoso |
| **25%** | Jungfraujoch |
| **Nothing** | Corvatsch · Bettmeralp–Bettmerhorn · **every Engadin St. Moritz lift rejects STP, Halbtax and GA outright** |

It also gives free lake boats, free Ballenberg, Olympic Museum, Chillon and the Glacier Garden.

**On Jungfraujoch the Half Fare Card beats the Travel Pass by CHF 47–58.** Buying early does not reduce Swiss fares — domestic tickets are fixed-price and never sell out. Only Supersavers and the **Saver Day Pass (CHF 29 with Halbtax vs CHF 52 without)** reward booking ahead.

**Decision rule:** price the actual transfers for the final itinerary. If they clear CHF 700, take the Travel Pass. If not, take the Half Fare Card and buy Saver Day Passes for the two transfer days.

### 3. The biggest lever is guest cards, not the national pass

This is the finding that most changes the shape of a trip, and it is widely misreported.

| Destination | Free lifts with an overnight? | Min nights |
|---|---|---|
| **Engadin / St. Moritz** | **Yes — 10 lifts, plus RhB 2nd class and local buses** | 2 |
| **Saas-Fee** | **Yes — all nine cable cars** (Metro Alpin CHF 50 extra) | 1 |
| **Arosa** | **Yes — 3 lifts plus the Lüen-Castiel train** | 1 |
| Davos / Klosters | No — flat rates CHF 20–30/day | — |
| **Aletsch Arena** | **No — 20% off only.** The "free lifts" claim refers to the *paid* Aletsch Explorer | — |
| Flims / Laax | No — 5% off | — |

The Engadin card is worth roughly **CHF 230 per adult over five nights** and is the only affordable route to those lifts, because they refuse every national pass. **Saas-Fee gives you all nine cable cars from the first night.**

## The plan — three bases, 14 nights

| Base | Nights | Dates | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Lucerne** | 4 | 27–30 Jul | 1h05 from Zurich airport, low altitude for jet lag, and **Rigi, Stanserhorn and Stoos are all free** with a Travel Pass. Lake boats free. Works in bad weather. |
| **Wengen** | 5 | 31 Jul – 4 Aug | The strongest Jungfrau base — **car-free**, 14 minutes and around 40 services a day from Lauterbrunnen. Sits above the valley haze and the day-tripper crush. |
| **Zermatt** | 5 | 5–9 Aug | **Rain insurance.** The Valais is the driest region in Switzerland — Sion gets 603 mm a year against Locarno's 1,897. Also car-free. |

Transfers: Lucerne→Wengen **1h50**, Wengen→Zermatt **2h05–2h25**. About five hours of transfer overhead across a fortnight.

**Ordering logic.** Lucerne first because it is closest to the airport and lowest — you arrive off an overnight flight and do not need to be sharp. Zermatt last because it is the payoff and because a buffer day before an international departure matters on a single-track line with a weather-closure history.

### Fixed points and things not to schedule

**Sat 31 July** — transfer day to Wengen, and **the shopping day**. Buy food today.

**Sun 1 August — Swiss National Day, and it is a Sunday.** The only federal holiday, so maximum closures: banks, post offices, most retail and most supermarkets shut. Station and petrol-station shops stay open. Trains run a **Sunday/holiday timetable**. Restaurants are open and packed. **Do not move bases, take a long transfer, or fly on 31 July, 1 or 2 August.** When the 1st falls on a weekend it is the busiest travel weekend of the Swiss year — book months ahead.
You will be in Wengen. **Grindelwald runs a torchlight and cowbell procession from the church to the Eiger Nordwandplatz, free, until about 22:30 — and its page is already dated 1 August 2027.** Alternatively **over 250 farms host a 1 August brunch, 09:00–13:00**, booked directly with the farm; places are limited.

**⚠ Sat 7 August — Sierre-Zinal, confirmed.** Published 2026 closures: Sierre–Zinal shut 04:40–05:25 and 05:40–06:25, Vissoie–Sierre 10:30–12:30, Zinal–Vissoie 11:45–13:30, **Ayer–Chandolin 06:00–14:00**. Grimentz, St-Luc, Chandolin, Zinal and Vissoie are locked down most of the day and the valley is fully booked. **Val d'Anniviers and Lac de Moiry are out of this plan for that reason.** The Zurich Street Parade and the Zermatt Folklore Festival both plausibly land the same weekend — unconfirmed, worth checking.

**Sat 31 July** — Rhine Falls "Fire on the Rocks" at 21:45 with army aviation from 20:00, free. Note the **2026 edition was cancelled for drought and fire risk**, so treat 2027 as tentative. It is not near any of the three bases; mentioned only so it is a known option.

Sunrise is 06:02 on 27 July and 06:19 on 10 August. **First lifts run hours later:**

**Every marquee sunrise requires sleeping high or walking up in the dark.** Four of them need high nights in three different regions: **Stellisee** (Berggasthaus Fluhalp, 15 min away), **Riffelsee** (3100 Kulmhotel Gornergrat or Riffelhaus 1853), **Bachalpsee** (Berghotel Faulhorn, 45 min away, open from 20 June), and **the Aletsch from Moosfluh** (sleep Riederalp, walk up 1h15).

**Book those high nights first, then build the days around them.** Achievable from the valley without a high night: the Zermatt Kirchbrücke (5 minutes' walk, window 05:40–06:25) and Oeschinensee (1h20 walk up from Kandersteg — which also **bypasses the mandatory booking system entirely**, since walkers need no slot).

Full fare from Interlaken Ost **CHF 234.80**; from Grindelwald Terminal CHF 100.60. **Take the Half Fare Card here, not the Travel Pass.** Honest assessment: Schilthorn or Schynige Platte give roughly ninety percent of the view for ten percent of the crowd, and **Männlichen is CHF 62, or CHF 31 with a Travel Pass**.

**The Breithorn is out as a family day.** It was the standout recommendation in this plan and a
10-year-old cannot legally join, so it is either an adults-only morning while one parent does
something else with him, or it comes out. Decide that deliberately rather than discovering it at
the guide's office.

**Weigh and measure him before you book the First package.** Height and weight are checked at
the valley station, the package is non-refundable once bought, and 140 cm for the Flyer is above
the average for his age. If he is under, the **Trottibike (125 cm) and the Cliff Walk (free)**
still work, and the Glider at 130 cm is the more likely of the two flights.

### The scheduling conflict to resolve first

**The Mürren via ferrata cannot start early.** Usage is restricted to **09:00–18:00 for
nature-conservation reasons**, and the last gear issue is 14:30. That collides head-on with this
plan's core rule — *every high day 05:30 to 13:00* — because end of July is the seasonal peak for
afternoon convection.

It is the only activity here that structurally fights the weather strategy. **Put it early in the
Wengen week on a settled forecast**, so a washout still leaves you rebooking days. Note also that
the rental desk **may refuse gear in rain, thunderstorm or slippery conditions even when the route
is officially open** — "open" does not mean you are going.

Everything else on this list can and should run on the 05:30–13:00 clock.

### Zermatt week (5–9 Aug)

**Breithorn 4,164 m — the trip's serious objective.** ZERMATTERS, **CHF 240 pp**, groups of 3–6,
**6 hours total**, back in Zermatt by early afternoon. Crampons, harness and pole supplied; **no
climbing experience required**, min age 18. Lift tickets are **not** included.

Be clear-eyed about why it is called easy: the lift does the vertical to 3,883 m, so **altitude
sickness is the real risk, not technical difficulty.** It needs prior acclimatisation days and a
guide. This is not a casual add-on — book it, then build the week's earlier days around
acclimatising for it.

**Charles Kuonen suspension bridge, Randa** — 494 m long, 65 cm wide, 85 m up, transparent mesh
deck. ⚠ **The Europaweg's Springelboden–Eggenstadel section was still closed as of June 2026 with
rockfall barriers destroyed**, and may be shut for the season. **The bridge itself is unaffected**;
diversion runs Springelboden → Attermenzen → Täsch. No fee listed. **Re-check for 2027, and get the
Randa-to-bridge hiking time from the tourist office, +41 27 967 16 77 — it is still unverified.**

Low-adrenaline by design — this is the jet-lag week. **Rigi, Stanserhorn and Stoos are all free**

## The join — if this is chained to the Dolomites

The Dolomites plan currently ends **26 July driving Bolzano → Venice, 280 km**, which is a **pure backtrack away from Switzerland**.

**The fix: drop the car in Bolzano and go via Innsbruck on 27 July.** Bolzano→Innsbruck 2h02, Innsbruck→Zurich 3h33 — about 5h30 to 6h30 total, and it saves half a day over backtracking to Venice. Cost is an Italian one-way drop fee: Hertz publishes a flat €47.60, Enterprise €5/day capped at €50, but international drops can run far higher — price it before committing.

**Flights become open-jaw: YYZ→Venice nonstop out, Zurich→YYZ nonstop home.** No other pairing gives you a nonstop on both legs. Zurich is the only Swiss nonstop from Toronto — Air Canada seven a week plus SWISS five, eight hours eastbound.

The prettier alternative: **Milan → Tirano → Bernina line → St. Moritz, about 5h30**, which would argue for a Graubünden-first routing and the Engadin guest card. It costs more time and reshuffles the bases.

**Schengen note:** the 90/180 allowance is cumulative across Italy and Switzerland. Two chained trips use roughly 28 of the 90 days. **ETIAS is not open yet and has no confirmed date; 2027 is likely. Plan on needing it.** EES biometrics are collected at the border with no pre-action required.

## Costs, honestly

Switzerland is expensive and the numbers are not close to Toronto. **Zurich runs about 89% above Toronto overall**, Geneva 68%, Lucerne 65%. Restaurants roughly double.

Inexpensive meal **CHF 25** (Zurich 30) · mid-range dinner for two **CHF 100** (Zurich 120) · cappuccino **CHF 5.10** · half-litre of beer **CHF 7.40**. **Budget CHF 90–130 per person per day excluding accommodation**, self-catering lunches.

Cheese is 179% above Toronto and eggs 130% — but milk is actually cheaper. Buy lunch at Coop or Migros and eat it on a mountain.

**Accommodation in peak August is the biggest missing number in the research** — no reliable source could be pulled. Get real quotes early; this is the single largest cost and the fortnight with the least availability.

## Weather and what it means

| Station | July max/min | Rain days | August max/min | Rain days |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Interlaken (577 m) | 24.3 / 13.8 | **12.8** | 23.7 / 13.6 | **12.8** |
| Zermatt (1,638 m) | 20.0 / 8.3 | **9.0** | 19.6 / 8.3 | **9.8** |
| Jungfraujoch (3,580 m) | **+3.4 / −2.1** | — | **+3.5 / −1.7** | — |

**Expect 4–6 of 14 days affected in the Oberland, 3–5 in the Valais — but most with a usable morning.** Interlaken runs 41% wet days against Zermatt's 29–32%, which is why the Valais gets five nights.

**You are sitting on the annual peak of Alpine convection.** Fifteen years of pan-Alpine
lightning data put **convective initiation peaking 16:00–17:00 CEST and cloud-to-ground flash
density peaking 17:00–18:00**, with the quietest window **09:00–11:00** — and the *seasonal*
maximum peaking **at the end of July**. Half of all initiations occur over the mountains, because
in Switzerland the mountains are themselves the trigger: upslope winds pump warmed air over the
ridges until the inversion breaks.

**Operational rule: plan every high day as 05:30 to 13:00.** The German Alpine Club gives the only
explicit clock guidance I could source — plan so that **exposed places, summits and ridges do not
appear in your schedule from midday onwards**. The SAC deliberately refuses to name an hour and
says only that the arrival time and exact area of a storm are *not* predictable even when the
storm itself is. That single 05:30–13:00 rule solves the storms, the crowds and the light at once.

MeteoSwiss only warns at level 3 and above, regional outlooks come 6–36 h ahead, and acute
cell warnings arrive **30–60 minutes** before impact. **Install the MeteoSwiss app.**
Lightning density exceeds 3 strikes/km²/yr on ridges and summits, and is lowest in the dry
inner-alpine valleys — **the Valais is on the quiet side, the Bernese Oberland is not.**

**One more piece of context worth knowing: your dates fall entirely inside the *Hundstage*,
23 July to 23 August, which MeteoSwiss confirms is still statistically the hottest phase of the
Swiss year.** The highest average daily maxima are measured towards the end of July.

**Lapse rate is about 0.65 °C per 100 m.** On a 26 °C valley day, the Jungfraujoch is around 6 °C before wind chill. At 2,500 m — Stellisee, Bachalpsee, Faulhorn — a pre-dawn wait feels like 0 to +3 °C with any breeze. **Gloves and a beanie are not optional.**

**Heat is the under-appreciated risk.** Valleys hit 30–33 °C in a heat spell, Lugano regularly 32–34 with 17.8 °C nights, and **most Swiss hotels have no air conditioning**. Sleeping at 1,300–1,600 m — Wengen and Zermatt both qualify — gives 8–13 °C nights. High bases, day descents.

## Numbers that change decisions

**Mountain food is cheaper than you would guess; the lift to reach it is the real cost.**
On the Schilthorn at 2,970 m: **rösti with vegetables and sour cream CHF 22, a half-litre of
Rugenbräu CHF 7, soup CHF 9.50–12.50, all-you-can-eat brunch until 14:00 CHF 38.** A rösti and a
beer at 3,000 m is CHF 29 against a CHF 25 "inexpensive meal" in the valley. Meanwhile
**Zermatt–Gornergrat return is CHF 132** and **Stechelberg–Schilthorn CHF 115**.
**Budget CHF 200/day for bed and food, then add CHF 80–130 on every day you go up a mountain.**

**⚠ SAC hut prices change for 2027.** Delegates decided in June 2026 to add a **CHF 10 per person
per night reservation fee** at staffed huts from 1 January 2027 — **non-refundable on
cancellation**, which removes the "book speculatively, cancel free" advantage — plus a solidarity
contribution for 2027–2029 of **CHF 3/night for members and CHF 5/night for non-members**.
Concrete example, Täschhütte SAC: **non-member half board CHF 100 against CHF 85 for a member**,
silk liner mandatory (CHF 5 rental), card payment +2%, no-show CHF 50.
Worth checking whether **Alpine Club of Canada UIAA reciprocity** earns the member rate — over
several hut nights that is real money.

**Tourist tax, per person per night, verified:** Zermatt **4.00** (children under 9 free; and the
reporting is blunt that Zermatt gives no discounts in return) · Grindelwald **4.20** ·
Wengen **4.30** · Lauterbrunnen **3.50**. **Plus, in canton Bern only, a further CHF 1.00 per night
for anyone 16+.** Country-wide the range is CHF 0–7; Saas-Fee and Montreux are at the top.

## Two things people get wrong

**⚠ Switzerland is NOT in the EU roam-like-at-home zone.** The regime covers the 27 EU states plus
Iceland, Liechtenstein and Norway — Switzerland is absent, and the EU's own page warns that data
roaming outside it "can be expensive". **A Europe-regional eSIM or an EU carrier bundle may
silently exclude Switzerland. Check the destination list before buying — this is the single most
common and most expensive mistake on a Swiss trip.**
Which network the eSIM rides on matters in valleys. **Swisscom has the largest network at 99%
population coverage. Holafly uses all three Swiss networks — 15 days unlimited, USD 50.50.
Airalo is Sunrise-only — 15 days unlimited, CAD 65.50.** A Canadian carrier day pass at roughly
CAD 15/day is CAD 225 for the trip, four to five times the eSIM. Keep the Canadian line for calls
and SMS, put data on the eSIM.

**⚠ A generic "Europe" plug adapter will not work.** Switzerland uses **Type J (SEV 1011)**,
230 V, 50 Hz. A **Type C two-pin charger fits a Swiss socket perfectly**, but a **German Schuko /
Type F adapter does NOT** — the recessed round body will not seat. Bring Type J or plain Type C.

**Assume Twint is unusable.** It is Swiss-domestic and bank-account-linked. Carry some cash for
huts — Täschhütte charges 2% for card payment, which tells you they take cards but would rather
not.

## Glacier and rockfall conditions — read before choosing high routes

The freezing level in July averages **almost 4,000 m**, can sit under 2,500 m on a cool day, and
has exceeded 5,000 m in each of the recent heat summers. **The SAC's own rockfall trigger is a
zero-degree line above 4,000 m** — which is to say the normal July mean already sits at the
trigger.

The SAC's July 2026 conditions bulletin is worth quoting directly: many glaciers were already
**badly bare — the protective snow layer melted and blank ice exposed, crevasse zones lying open,
large bergschrunds forming — and individual ascents were "not recommended at present."** Their
advice: expect guidebook times and grades to be wrong, and note that **ridge routes and pure rock
climbs are often in better condition than glacier routes.**

Swiss glaciers lost roughly **6% of remaining volume in 2022 alone** and another 4% in 2023 —
about 10% in two years, and nearly 40% since 2000. On the Matterhorn, a pillar on the Hörnli ridge
collapsed in June 2023, traced to meltwater in thawing permafrost; in summer 2026 Zermatt guides
**advised against climbing the Matterhorn from mid-July**, and a section of the south face broke
away on 5 August. The routes were never formally closed — guides simply stopped.

**Old snowfields on marked hiking trails are the under-rated hazard**, and the SAC says so
explicitly. Above about 2,500 m in late July they are plausible. Their protocol: stiff boots,
gloves and long trousers for everyone, probe the edges, avoid protruding rocks because of voids
beneath, cross one at a time, and traverse or ascend rather than descend across.

**Grindelwald Terminal is the trap** — CHF 245 for a week against CHF 93 at Lauterbrunnen for the
same region. The Grund lots are CHF 10 but **ban overnight parking**, so they are useless as a base.

**The links themselves are quick and mostly cheap:**
- **Täsch → Zermatt: 12 minutes, every 20 minutes from 05:55 to 21:55**, then hourly to 01:00, and
  hourly all night Thursday to Saturday. **CHF 17.20 return, or free with a Swiss Travel Pass.**
- **Lauterbrunnen → Wengen: 11 minutes, every 30 minutes**, about CHF 7.20 single.
- **Stechelberg → Mürren: the new direct cableway runs in 3.9 minutes.** Schilthornbahn 20XX is
  complete on all three sections and fully operational for 2027. **CHF 23.60 return, free with a
  Travel Pass or GA.** The old line via Gimmelwald still runs in parallel — Schilthornbahn is
  contractually bound to serve Gimmelwald **at least hourly, 06:00 to 24:00, year-round until 2035.**
- **Aletsch feeder lifts** (valley to plateau) are **CHF 20.40 return, halved to CHF 10.20 with a
  Half Fare Card or Travel Pass, and free with a GA or an SBB day pass.** Note this is separate from
  the *mountain* lifts at Moosfluh, Bettmerhorn and Eggishorn, which the guest card only discounts.
- **Rigi is CHF 84 return to the summit — and free with a Travel Pass or GA, with children under 16
  free when accompanied.** Same for the **Stoos funicular**, CHF 23.20 otherwise.

**Corrections to the usual "car-free Switzerland" lists**, since several widely-repeated names do
not hold up: **Bosco Gurin, Davos Wiesen and Isenthal are all reached by public road and PostBus**,
and **Grächen is restricted-traffic, not car-free**. Genuinely car-free and less known:
**Rasa** in Ticino (cableway from Verdasio, five minutes, no road at all), **Belalp** above Blatten
bei Naters, **Niederrickenbach**, **Gspon**, **Schatzalp** above Davos, **Lauchernalp** in the
Lötschental, and **Quinten** on the Walensee — reachable only by boat or footpath.

## Verify before you go

**Partly resolved — good news:** the **BLM Lauterbrunnen–Grütschalp funicular** was closed
13 April – 10 July 2026 for a rebuild, and I flagged the 2027 dates as the top risk. The
**published 2027 timetable now shows both Mürren routes running on every date across July and
August 2027** (BLM 21 min, Stechelberg 29 min). The Stechelberg line's own 2027 revisions are
26 April – 7 May and 28–29 October, both clear of the window. Re-check in spring 2027, but this
no longer looks like a live threat. **Säntis cable car** closed for renewal from 1 May 2026 with no confirmed reopening. **Hotel Bellevue des Alpes at Kleine Scheidegg** was shut all summer 2026 for roof work; summer 2027 unknown. **Titlis Rotair** had a 10 Aug–11 Dec 2026 maintenance window — check the 2027 analogue. **Europaweg** Springelboden–Eggenstadel has been closed since 2024 "until further notice" — and Randa commune reconfirmed it **closed as of 26 June 2026, rockfall barriers destroyed**, with one source expecting the whole 2026 season lost. **Assume closed for 2027 until proven otherwise.** The **Charles Kuonen bridge itself is unaffected** and reachable via the Springelboden → Attermenzen → Täsch diversion — but **the hiking time and ascent from Randa are still unverified; call Randa tourist info +41 27 967 16 77.**

**Adventure items to re-verify in spring 2027:** the Grindelwald First Adventure Package prices and season (2026 figures were 13 Apr – 25 Oct); the **Mürren via ferrata's actual route features — the operator page confirms neither the zipline nor the Nepal bridge that blogs describe**; First Glider minimum age; and cancellation terms for every operator except paragliding and the ferrata, which are the only two verified.

**Hazard to check two days out:** **Spitze Stei above Kandersteg** — roughly 16 million cubic metres of rock in motion, several metres a year, monitored continuously with a permanent restricted zone. Status is reissued weekly to monthly at gemeindekandersteg.ch. It sits above Oeschinensee.

**Live sources:** MeteoSwiss hazard map (meteoswiss.admin.ch/weather/hazards/hazard-map.html) · the federal all-hazards portal natural-hazards.ch · SwitzerlandMobility closure layer at map.schweizmobil.ch · SAC hut portal · Roundshot webcams at Gornergrat and Riffelberg — **scrub the 05:30–07:00 frames from the previous three mornings to see whether the Matterhorn is clearing at dawn. No forecast tells you that.**

**Rescue — do this, it is the cheapest risk reduction on the trip.**
For a mountain emergency dial **1414 (Rega)**, not 112. That is what the SAC itself prints on its
own card. Also 112 general, 144 ambulance, 117 police, 118 fire, and **161.300 MHz** alpine radio
where there is no signal.

**Rega patronage: CHF 40/year per adult, children free** — so CHF 80 for two. **Foreigners can
join, and it explicitly covers air rescue inside Switzerland for non-resident patrons.** It does
**not** cover repatriation to Canada. Read the wording honestly: Rega is a non-profit foundation,
not an insurer, and *"waives the cost without any legal obligation"* — the conditions say "can
waive". It waived over CHF 14 million last year.
Why the gap exists at all, in Rega's own framing: health insurance pays only **50% of a recognised
rescue, capped at CHF 5,000/year**, and several hundred missions a year rescue people who are
**neither injured nor ill** — lost, benighted, stuck. Those costs are often covered by nobody.
**In the Valais, Air Zermatt or Air-Glaciers usually fly the mission, not Rega.** The
**Air Zermatt Rescue Card is CHF 35/year** for one person and covers residual costs
*"regardless of the rescue organization"* — broader wording than Rega's, which waives only its own
missions. For a Zermatt-heavy trip, buy both.
⚠ **Whether Canadian travel insurance covers helicopter rescue is unverified** — policies commonly
exclude mountaineering and "hazardous sports". At CHF 40 a head, buying patronage is cheaper than
resolving the question. Install the Rega app; it transmits GPS.
