Before you set off

You are visiting a waterfall that has moved

Niagara Falls is not where it started. Since the last ice age the Falls have been eating backwards upstream, cutting the gorge behind them as they retreat. That means the Niagara Glen is not near the Falls — it is where the Falls used to be. When you walk down those stairs you are standing on the floor of a canyon the waterfall dug on its way past, and then abandoned, thousands of years ago.

The Whirlpool you will see from the rim is the strangest part of that story. Long before the last glaciation, an older river had already carved a valley here — St. David's Buried Gorge, about 1,200 m long and 91 m deep. The retreating glacier packed it with silt roughly 12,000 years ago and it never reopened. Then, about 4,200 years ago, the retreating Falls cut backwards straight into that buried valley. Hitting loose fill instead of hard rock, the river tore sideways, flushed the silt out, and has been spinning through the re-excavated ancient channel ever since. The near right-angle turn is not a quirk of the water; it is the river falling into someone else's canyon.

The forest at the bottom is the other surprise. The gorge floor holds Carolinian forest — a zone that properly belongs to the Ohio Valley and the American southeast. The walls trap heat and block wind, so over only 60 m of descent the vegetation changes into something that has no business being this far north. The house-sized boulders scattered through it are roof collapse: hard dolostone caps softer shale, the shale erodes, the cap undercuts and drops. Those blocks are why the Glen is one of Ontario's few real bouldering areas.

The day is built to follow the river downstream — gorge floor, then escarpment top, then the flat delta plain, ending where the Niagara empties into Lake Ontario at sunset. Every stop is further down the same river, and the walking gets easier at every one.

What it costs you, and how to claw it back

The driving penalty is trivial: Pur & Simple → Glen direct is 16.1 km / 19.2 min; via the outlet it is 18.3 km / 22.6 min. The real cost is the 30 minutes in the store, and because sunset is fixed at 7:58 PM that half hour comes straight out of Old Town at the other end of the day.

To protect Old Town, trim as you go: Nature Centre rest 30 → 20 min, and Queenston Heights 85 → 70 min (still fine for the monument, the Bruce Trail cairn and a sit-down). That recovers 25 of the 35 minutes, so Old Town loses only about 10.

Skip it if breakfast runs past 10:30, or if you would rather have the full 90 minutes on Queen Street. Bags in the car through the gorge hike are a non-issue at 22°C.

10:50 – 11:10 AM

The rim first do not skip

Twenty minutes on the upper overlook before you touch the stairs. From up here you get the gorge as a whole shape — the walls, the turquoise water, the rapids, the Whirlpool's impossible turn. Once you are on the floor you cannot see any of it, and the two perspectives are the entire reason this stop is worth three hours.

Why the water is that colour

The green-turquoise is dissolved rock flour — finely ground limestone and shale the river carries after grinding through the gorge. It is most obvious in bright light, which is exactly what a late-morning arrival gives you.

11:10 AM – 1:15 PM

Niagara Glen hike extended

Down the stairs, then Main Loop → River Trail → Cliffside → back up. Two hours rather than the 85 minutes in the draft, so there is time to actually stop at the river instead of route-marching. The River Trail is the payoff — it puts you at water level with the rapids going past at eye height.

Closures, footing, and the way out
  • Closed: Whirlpool Trail entirely; Terrace Trail between the Cobblestone and Trillium Trails (north entrance to Marker 6), for stair repairs. Verified against Niagara Parks' maintenance page on Aug 28.
  • Open: Main Loop (2.1 km), River (0.42 km), Cliffside (310 m), Woodland, Cobblestone, Trillium, Bouldering.
  • Footing: metal stairs, then bare rock, roots and loose talus. Trail shoes or any lugged sole are fine. Smooth-soled sneakers are not — do the rim and skip the descent.
  • The climb out is the same 60 m on tired legs and is where people struggle. Turn around with energy still in reserve.
  • Don't improvise onto a closed trail because it looks open. The Terrace closure is mid-trail, so you can walk into it before you meet the barrier.

Queenston Heights

Eight minutes up the Parkway and you are on top of the escarpment the river just cut through. This is the recovery stop, not a second hike — monument, viewpoints over the lower river, gardens, a bench. Brock's Monument is an 1853 neoclassical column of Queenston limestone carrying a 16-foot statue of Isaac Brock, who was killed here in October 1812.

Put the Niagara Glen parking receipt on the dash here. Niagara Parks lets you re-park free at any other of their lots the same day on one receipt — it is receipt-based, not plate-based. That makes this stop $0. Lose the receipt and it costs ~$12.
Find the Bruce Trail cairn — and a Sunday you're missing

The stone cairn marking the southern terminus of the Bruce Trail — Canada's oldest and longest marked footpath, running 900 km from here to Tobermory — sits about 160 m from the monument, in a parking lot. It is genuinely easy to walk past. Worth two minutes for the photo of where the trail begins.

The draft flagged a free 2–4 PM concert here, but that is Sunday Aug 30. We are here Saturday, which should be the quieter of the two days.

3:15 – 3:45 PM

Niagara Parkway, driven slowly

Do not take the fast inland road. The Parkway follows the river the whole way down to Old Town — orchards, vineyards, big old houses, water on your right the entire time. Churchill supposedly called it the prettiest Sunday afternoon drive in the world; that is marketing, but it is not wrong. There is nothing you have to stop for. The drive is the stop.

Old Town, first pass

Park on the free streets toward Lake Ontario — around Simcoe Park and the Front Street end — rather than feeding a meter on Queen. You are walking to Queen's Royal Park at 7:15 anyway, so it costs no extra steps and saves about $23. Leave the car there for the rest of the night. Queen Street end to end, the Clock Tower cenotaph in the middle of the road, the Niagara Apothecary at number 5 — a restored 1869 pharmacy. Then get off Queen: the residential side streets have the better houses, gardens and trees, and almost none of the crowd.

The parking maths — you pay twice all day, not five times

Two authorities, and a receipt from one is worthless to the other.

Payment 1 — Niagara Parks covers both morning stops. Their policy: guests can re-park the same day for free at any other Niagara Parks paid lot, as long as the original receipt is retained and displayed on the dash. Receipt-based, not plate-based. So ~$24 at the Glen makes Queenston Heights $0.

Payment 2 — Town of NOTL covers the whole evening, because Old Town is walkable end to end. Meters run 10 AM to 8 PM at $5.25–5.75/hour and go free at 8 PM. Ricardo Street is unmetered per Parks Canada's Navy Hall page — north end of Old Town, walkable to Queen Street and Queen's Royal Park both.

Check the boundary before you leave the car: the Town's official paid-parking map is the only authoritative source on which streets are metered. The Town publishes no plain list.

Rejected: the $31.86+HST Niagara Parks day pass (dearer than 3 hours hourly); the $50 annual pass (only wins on a second trip this year); and parking free at the Whirlpool Aero Car lot, which is 1.7 km from the Glen — 25 minutes each way on top of a hike that ends climbing 60 m out of a gorge, and it would forfeit the receipt that makes Queenston Heights free.

Day total: ~$24 with free Ricardo Street, ~$47 if you meter on Queen. Paying every lot separately would be ~$59.

Something small, deliberately

Coffee, a pastry, ice cream — and keep it genuinely light. Dinner is a proper Punjabi meal at 9:50 PM and you will regret arriving full. This is a sit-down-for-45-minutes stop, not a meal.

6:00 – 7:15 PM

The same town again, in gold the good hour

Walk Queen → King → Front a second time. This is not filler. Old Town at 4 PM is bright, flat and busy; at 6:30 the light comes in sideways, the white clapboard goes warm, the shadows get long and the crowd thins as the day-trippers leave. Same streets, a completely different town — and it is the best photography of the trip apart from the sunset itself.

If you are flagging

This is the most cuttable block in the day. If the Glen took more out of you than expected, skip the second lap and sit in Simcoe Park until 7:15 instead. You lose less here than anywhere else — but do not cut the park at the end.

7:15 – 8:25 PM

Queen's Royal Park — the end of the river

Walk here, don't drive. This is where the Niagara River finally reaches Lake Ontario after everything you followed it through today, with Fort Niagara sitting directly opposite on the American bank. The white gazebo was built as a film prop and never taken down; it is now the town's signature photograph.

Be here by 7:15, not 7:55. The best light happens before the sun goes, and then again after — stay through blue hour to 8:26 and the sky does its second act over the lake.
