### Torres del Paine: NO

- **Research verdict: no.** A real visit needs 2-3 nights in Puerto Natales or the park. Getting there is about 350 km from El Calafate, and 5-6 h door to door with the border. The 13 nights are already full.
- **Paperwork for a 2-3 night visit:**
  - two border crossings
  - Chile's rules for minors: long-form birth certificates if both parents travel; a notarised, apostilled and translated letter if a child travels with one parent
  - written authorisation from the rental company to take an Argentine car into Chile (fee GAP), with no one-way drop-off
  - park tickets sold online only at pasesparques.cl, non-refundable. The foreigner price is a GAP: CLP 32,400, 34,000 and an announced 80,000 all appear in the sources.
- **The only swap on offer: the day tour from El Calafate.** About 15 h, US$195-259 plus the park fee, hotel pickup.
  - The day is about 6-7 h of driving and two border passes (about 1 h of formalities on a 2025 bus).
  - What you get: viewpoints (Laguna Amarga, Nordenskjold, Salto Grande, Pehoe), the Cuernos, and one 4.6 km walk to Lake Pehoe.
  - **No Base Torres.** That is 18-20 km and 6-10 h, and impossible on a day tour.
- **What it replaces.** It needs a free full day in El Calafate. The only way to make one: **drop Laguna Esmeralda (Day 5)**, fly Ushuaia to El Calafate on Tue 2 Mar, run the tour on Wed 3 Mar, and drive to El Chalten on Thu 4 Mar. Everything in El Chalten then moves one day later and **the spare day (Day 9) is gone**, so Laguna Torre and Laguna de los Tres have no weather buffer. If you take that Calafate day, I would spend it on the Upsala and Spegazzini cruise instead. It is 7 h with a 6-16 child fare, involves no border, and the Chile research itself rates it the best Argentine alternative for children who like boats and glaciers.

## Orientation

**What this place is.** *(This paragraph is general geology, not from the research files.)*
- **The ice field.** Southern Patagonia is the Andes at about 49-55 degrees south, where the westerly winds come off the Pacific with nothing in their way. They dump snow on the crest. The result is the Southern Patagonian Ice Field, the largest ice mass in the Southern Hemisphere outside Antarctica, about 350 km long. Its outlet glaciers spill east into long glacial lakes: Lago Argentino (Perito Moreno, Upsala, Spegazzini) and Lago Viedma.
- **The steppe.** The air that crosses the ice field arrives on the Argentine side dry and fast. That is why El Calafate sits in brown steppe and why the wind defines every day of this trip.
- **The spires.** Fitz Roy (about 3,400 m) and Cerro Torre (about 3,100 m) are granite intrusions. The ice stripped away the softer rock around them and left the hard cores standing as near-vertical towers.
- **Perito Moreno.** It is fed by a large, high, snowy catchment on the ice field, so it has long held its size while most neighbouring glaciers retreated (recent studies report it has started thinning; GAP). Its front keeps pushing across the lake toward the Magallanes Peninsula. When it touches the far shore it dams the Brazo Rico arm, the water rises, and the ice dam eventually ruptures.
- **Tierra del Fuego.** Further south, the Andes bend from north-south to east-west and run out into the sea. The Beagle Channel is a glacier-carved channel along that bend. Ushuaia sits on its north shore under the Martial range, among the last mountains of the chain.

**Why it is famous, specifically.**
- **The Fitz Roy skyline at sunrise.** Around sunrise the east-facing granite turns red for a few minutes. Photographers call it the "Fitz Roy fire". The classic photographs are taken from Laguna de los Tres, Laguna Capri and the road into El Chalten.
- **Perito Moreno calving.** You watch from metal walkways a short distance across the water from a 5 km-wide ice wall (front width from general knowledge), while blocks fall into Lago Argentino.
- **Ushuaia's end-of-the-road geography.** Ruta 3 ends at Bahia Lapataia, inside Tierra del Fuego National Park.

**What you cannot get elsewhere.**
- **An 11-year-old walking on a glacier with crampons.** Minitrekking takes ages 8-65.
- **Walking among Magellanic penguins.** On Isla Martillo you walk a fixed path a few steps from the birds. Gentoo penguins are possible but not guaranteed.
- **World-class spires as day hikes from a village bed.** El Chalten's two main trailheads are at the two ends of town, so you walk out of the door.
- **The cultural layer** *(general knowledge, not from the research)*:
  - the estancia, Patagonia's sheep-ranch culture; Harberton was founded by the Bridges missionary family and houses the Acatushun marine-mammal museum
  - the asado, the grill-house meal you will be offered everywhere
  - Buenos Aires itself, a European-looking capital 2,500 km north of the ice

### What to know before reading the days

1. **Wind runs the schedule.** In El Chalten, days without wind are rare until about 20 March, and gusts matter more than the average. Wind can cancel boats (Martillo, the Beagle, Safari Nautico), make the last climb to Laguna de los Tres unpleasant or unsafe, and hit cars on RN40. A calm morning in town tells you little about the exposed ground.
2. **Cash is a real problem in El Chalten.** It has one ATM (Red Link, Banco Santa Cruz), often empty, and no exchange office. Card payment fails when the signal drops. Withdraw pesos in El Calafate or Ushuaia. The old "bring dollars for the blue rate" trick is mostly dead: the gap was 1-3% in September 2026.
3. **Park tickets are bought online and shown as a QR code.** El Chalten's trail portals check them, and there is no mobile signal on the trails. Buy on Wi-Fi the night before at the latest.
4. **Domestic flights have a 15 kg checked-bag limit**, and since May 2026 Aerolineas' cheapest fare includes no carry-on, only a 3 kg personal item. Hiking gear for a family breaks that fast.
5. **Children cost adult prices earlier than you expect.** National parks charge full price from age 7. Child fares stop at 11 on the Beagle catamaran, at 12 on the train and at 16 on the Upsala cruise. Get everyone's exact age on the travel dates.
6. **Every peso price here will be higher in 2027.** Budget from the USD figures, not the ARS ones.

**Why B.**
1. **Penguins a week earlier**, on 28 Feb, inside a season that ends 31 Mar.
2. **The walking ramps up.** Ushuaia's walks are 8-9 km before Chalten's 18-22 km days.
3. **El Chalten falls in the first week of March**, when wind is "easing" and Laguna Torre is less crowded.
4. **The last domestic leg leaves El Calafate.** Aerolineas, Flybondi and JetSMART all fly FTE to Buenos Aires, against two carriers out of Ushuaia. That means more ways to rebook a cancelled flight before the international (INFERENCE from carrier lists).

The Calafate-to-Chalten drive is never on a flight day. The one fixed booking in Santa Cruz (minitrekking) sits behind a drive, not a flight.

**The costs of B.**
- The Ushuaia flight on Day 2 may mean a pre-dawn start. The only timed example is Flybondi AEP 05:50 to USH 09:40 (undated).
- If the USH-FTE flight on Wed 3 Mar is cancelled, El Chalten loses a day. The spare day absorbs it, and Chalten's hikes need no booking.

**Rejected: flying EZE to Ushuaia on arrival day.** Aerolineas flies from EZE as well as AEP, so it is possible on paper. I rejected it because the international and domestic flights are separate tickets; a late arrival would strand you and lose the first Ushuaia booking.

**BA at the start or at the end.** The plan uses 1 night at the start and 2 at the end. Putting all of BA at the start would place the last domestic flight on the day before the international, or the same day. Never fly a same-day USH or FTE to BA to YYZ chain: both airports are windy, and the research found no wind-delay statistics (GAP).

### Wind-delay buffer rule

1. **No fixed, non-refundable booking on a flight day.** The only exception is the Day 2 catamaran, which refunds 100% up to 24 h before.
2. **The first fixed booking after each flight starts after midday the next day.** Piratour is at 14:00 on Day 3, so even a next-morning Ushuaia arrival still makes it.

4. **If any domestic leg cancels:** rebook the same carrier first, then try the other carriers on that route. The day dropped is always the softest one in the next base: Esmeralda in Ushuaia, the spare day in Chalten, the city day in BA.

## Ground transport: rental car or buses

**Recommendation: a rental car from FTE airport, Wed 3 to Tue 9 Mar, same-location return.** This assumes a group of four or more; group size is an open question.

**For the car:**
- Perito Moreno is about 80 km (1h15-1h30) with no public bus; the alternative is a tour or transfer.
- Piedras Blancas and Piedra del Fraile trailheads are 16-17 km out of El Chalten.
- The car saves two bus-terminal transfers.
- Cost, 2026 aggregator figures: Sandero about USD 50 a day (Vipcars), Kayak from ARS 72,370 a day, SUV average ARS 138,200 a day. Peak season is higher.

**Against the car:**
- Enterprise takes a USD 2,200 deposit on an SUV or pickup. National's waiver deductible runs up to USD 4,500.
- Basic cover excludes tyres, windscreen and keys. Confirm gravel cover in writing.
- The 37 km of Ruta 41 gravel to Lago del Desierto is the one road to take by tour, not by rental.
- An International Driving Permit is recommended by Global Affairs Canada.
- **There is no fuel in El Chalten.** Fill up in El Calafate. Tres Lagos has fuel; La Leona roadhouse fuel is a GAP.

**The bus alternative:**
- Operators: Cal Tur and Chalten Travel, departures about 08:00 and 17:00, 2h40-3h30.
- Fares conflict: USD 39-40, ARS 35,500, or ARS 50,000 plus ARS 3,000 (Calafate) and ARS 2,000 (Chalten) terminal fees.
- What it costs the plan: the Day 6 arrival moves to about 20:00 on the 17:00 bus, which loses the Condores sunset. The glacier day becomes a tour with pickup (minitrekking includes urban pickup).

**Ushuaia: no car.**
- National park buses leave from Fadul and Maipu in central Ushuaia (timetable GAP).
- The Laguna Esmeralda shuttle leaves the bus terminal at 09:00, 10:00 and 11:00 and returns about 16:00, minimum 3 people (search snippet).
- Remises (cars with drivers) and taxis cover the rest. Piratour includes transport.

### Day 2, Sat 27 Feb: fly to Ushuaia, Beagle Channel catamaran

| | |
|---|---|

- **Morning.** Fly. A 15 kg bag per person, boots on your feet.
- **12:00-14:00** Check in (Centro), lunch, then buy pesos at an indoor ATM. Withdrawal limits are low and fees high (Global Affairs Canada).
- **15:10-16:00 departure** Catamaran. It is on the arrival day because sitting on a boat is what a 04:00-wake day can manage, and the 24 h free cancellation makes it the one safe booking on a flight day.
- **Why the catamaran and Piratour are both here.** The Harberton route to Martillo (Day 3) goes by road and does not pass the lighthouse or the sea lions. *Conflict:* a turismoushuaia.com listing says Piratour's "Navigation to Bridges Islands with landing" (09:30, 15:30, 18:00) passes Lobos and Les Eclaireurs and then lands on Martillo. If Piratour confirms that, one trip covers both and the catamaran can go (GAP: ask Piratour).
- **Plan B (flight late or channel too windy).** Move the catamaran to Day 5 after an early Esmeralda (see Day 5).
- **Weather.** Ushuaia averages 13 C high and 6 C low in February, with 7.3 wet days in the month and mean wind 23.6 km/h. Dress for the boat deck in wind.

### Day 3, Sun 28 Feb: Harberton and the Isla Martillo penguin landing

| | |
|---|---|
| Trip | Piratour, "Harberton + penguin colony with land walk": daily 14:00, about 6 h, bus to Harberton (about 90 km), boat to Martillo, about 1 h walking a fixed path |
| Capacity | About 15-20 per group, about 20 on the island at once, about 50 landing visitors a day (blog). When full you only see penguins from a boat. |
| Cost | GAP for 2027. Blog and reseller figures: about US$140 per person (Feb 2026 visit) to US$300 adult and US$180 child, plus Harberton entry (US$12-35). Civitatis US$220. |
| Harberton (2025-26) | Open 11 Oct to March, 12:00-19:00, **closed Tuesdays**. Homestead tours 13:00, 15:00 and 17:15; museum tours 14:15, 16:30 and 18:45. Entry ARS 50,000 to 31 Jan 2026 (later price GAP). Not suitable for reduced mobility. |
| Light | Sunrise 06:52, sunset 20:39 |

- **Morning (optional).** Sleep, or a short town walk. Glaciar Martial does not fit: its chairlift hours are a GAP and the walk is 3-5 h.
- **14:00** Piratour departs. Why Sunday at 14:00:
  1. It is the first slot that survives a one-day flight delay; a Sunday-morning arrival still makes 14:00.
  2. It is the earliest full day, which leaves Days 4 and 5 as fallbacks if the boat is cancelled.
  3. It falls on 28 Feb, a month before the 31 Mar season end. Gentoo penguins live there all year but are not guaranteed. Magellanic numbers in early March are unverified.
- **On the island.** Stay 4 steps from the birds and keep to the path. Small children reportedly struggle with the rules. An 11-year-old should be fine; Piratour states no minimum age (GAP, confirm).
- **About 20:00** Back in Ushuaia. Book dinner ahead or eat late (restaurant hours GAP).
- **Plan B (boat cancelled for wind).** Rebook Mon 1 Mar at 14:00 and move the park to Tuesday. Esmeralda drops. Do not rely on Tuesday for Piratour: Harberton is closed on Tuesdays, and whether the 14:00 tour still runs is a GAP.
- **Decision rule.** Piratour confirms whether the Bridges Islands navigation lands on Martillo: book the version with the most daily departures and keep the 14:00 Harberton version as the fallback.

| Park hours | March 08:00-20:00 (APN) |

  - Check in 45 min early. Duration GAP; Civitatis sells the ride as 1 h 45 min.
  - **Gotcha:** the official park page says the train reaches the park only as part of an excursion booked through a travel company. Buy the train-plus-park combo (Civitatis from US$66) rather than a bare ticket.
  - With the train, drop the Costera and keep Lapataia.
- **Optional for a strong adult or teen: Cerro Guanaco.** 6 km one way and 900 m, 4 h up, latest entry 12:30. Family alternative: Pampa Alta (4.7 km, 252 m).
- **Plan B (rain or wind).** The coastal forest is partly sheltered (INFERENCE). In heavy rain, take the short Lapataia trails only.
- **Decision rule.** If Piratour was cancelled on Day 3 and rebooked for today at 14:00, walk only the Lapataia trails, 09:00-12:00, then go back to town.

### Day 8, Fri 5 Mar: Laguna de los Tres (default)

|---|---|

- **07:00** Start. The route climbs a steep first kilometre, then goes past Mirador Rio de las Vueltas and Mirador Fitz Roy, over the Rio Blanco bridge (a warning sign follows), and reaches Poincenot camp at the foot of the final climb.
- **By 11:00** At Poincenot (INFERENCE from 7-10 h totals). If the slowest walker is not there by 11:00, everyone turns back via Laguna Capri. That is the rule that makes this day safe with 11-year-olds.
- **11:00-12:30** The final 2.4 km and about 450 m. Use trekking poles and go slowly on the descent. Sources describe it as a steep loose-rock climb, not a technical scramble.
- **12:30-13:30** At the lake: Fitz Roy, Laguna de los Tres, and Laguna Sucia below to the left. Lunch in the shelter of rocks. **Leave by 13:30.**
- **13:30-17:00** Down, then return via Laguna Capri for a different view (recommended by triptins).
- **Fitz Roy at sunrise without a 03:00 start.** A sunrise at the lake itself needs a 03:00-03:30 start from town (4-4.5 h before sunrise). I do not recommend that with children. Instead:
  1. **Recommended: a 05:45 start today.** Reach Laguna Capri for sunrise at 07:28, then carry on. That is about 70 min of headlamp walking up the steep first kilometre, and it gets the Fitz Roy-over-water sunrise. The local site gives Capri "best light in the morning". Timing to Capri is INFERENCE from "less than two hours one way". The cost: a 10-11 h day.
  2. **Low effort: Condores at sunrise on Day 9** (below).
  3. **Optional (added): one night at Poincenot camp.**
     - Walk in on the afternoon of Day 7 or 8, climb the last 450 m at about 05:30, and be at the lake for sunrise (07:28).
     - Booking is required and paid, and every Los Glaciares campsite now charges. You need a tent, sleeping bags and stoves (rental GAP).
     - It is the only way to see Laguna de los Tres at sunrise without the 03:00 start.
- **Family alternative (Track B).** Laguna Capri and the Mirador Fitz Roy loop: 11.7 km, 390 m, about 3.5 h. Same trailhead, Fitz Roy over the lagoon, an outhouse at the lake.
- **Less walking (Track C).** Mirador de los Condores from the visitor centre.
- **Hazards:**
  - exposed slope on the final climb
  - loose rock, worse on the way down
  - weather changes fast
  - this is the most popular hike in town, so expect a crowd on the final climb at midday
- **Evacuation.** One insurer estimates a helicopter evacuation from Fitz Roy at up to EUR 12,000.
- **Decision rule.** Gusty or the summit clouded at 06:30: swap with Day 7's or Day 9's plan, following the 18:00 table.

## Hazards

- **Wind.**
  - Chalten has few windless days before about 20 March, and gusts matter more than the average. The exposed final climb to Laguna de los Tres, Condores and Pliegue Tumbado are the wind-critical places.
  - RN40 has strong crosswinds.
  - Boats (Martillo, the Beagle, Safari Nautico, Lago del Desierto) cancel in wind.
  - Ushuaia averages 22.7-23.6 km/h.
- **Laguna de los Tres final climb.** Steep and loose, about 450 m in 2.4 km, on an exposed slope. Use poles and descend slowly. The turnaround rule is on Day 8.
- **Laguna Torre.**
  - Steep rocky moraine.
  - GLOF warning boards; the river is high and fast.
  - The route beyond the lagoon is closed.
- **Cold and fast-changing weather.** Chalten averages 16 C high and 5 C low in March, and snow is possible at altitude on the same day as sun. Ushuaia is 11 C high and 5 C low in March. Carry a windproof shell, an insulated layer, a hat and gloves every day.
- **Mud.** Laguna Esmeralda has peat bog, beaver dams and a steep muddy third forest.
- **Remoteness and medical care.**
  - No mobile signal on most trails.
  - El Chalten has a basic health facility only. Serious cases go to the SAMIC hospital in El Calafate, 2-3.5 h away, which has basic services and a mixed reputation.
  - A helicopter evacuation from Fitz Roy may cost up to EUR 12,000, often paid upfront. Buy insurance with no evacuation ceiling and with trekking cover.
- **Town dogs.** They follow hikers; rangers ask you not to let them.
- **Buenos Aires.** Pickpocketing and phone snatching. Watch Retiro station and La Boca outside the tourist strip, and Line D at rush hour. Use app rides at night.
- **Emergency numbers.** GAP (not in the research). Look them up before travel.

## Entry and documents (Canadian citizens)

- **No visa** for tourism, up to 90 days (Argentine consulate, Montreal, updated 28 Oct 2025).
- **No reciprocity fee** (suspended from 1 Jan 2018; no reinstatement found).
- **No electronic sworn declaration (DDJJ)**; it was abolished in August 2022.
- **At the border:** biometrics (fingerprints and photo) on arrival and departure. You may be asked for an onward ticket and proof of funds.
- **Passport:** valid for the stay per Global Affairs Canada. 6 months beyond the trip is the safe rule; the official Argentine rule is a GAP.
- **Health insurance:** all foreigners must hold insurance covering hospitalisation and medical care (consulate page). Documents may be in English. How often it is checked is a GAP.
- **Children:** the minor-consent rule applies only to children born or resident in Argentina.
- **Vaccinations:** hepatitis A and B recommended; no yellow fever proof required.
- **Other passports:** any traveller who is not a Canadian citizen needs a separate check (GAP).
