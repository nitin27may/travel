### Step 1. Planner v1

`core/src/planner/v1.ts`, wired behind `firstDraft`:
1. Candidates: catalog rows in the chosen areas (no radius search).
2. Filter (0A.5 step 2): season (`season_months` includes the month, or unknown), eligibility for the youngest and smallest traveller (unknown limits kept with a warning), `house_grade <= max`, `walk_km` and `walk_gain_m` within the per-day ceiling, kid fit when children are present (`yes`, or `unknown` flagged), `adventure_level <= level`, comfort tier within one of the luxury level, `review_status != retired`, `avoid`.
3. Score: `3*tag_match + 2*must_do + 1*freshness` (`freshness` 1 fresh, 0.5 stale, 0.2 raw); ties by id.
4. Bases: the user's S5 bases, else one base per area in area order with nights split by candidate count (minimum 1).
5. Days: per base, candidates spread across that base's nights in score order, at most `pace + 1` items a day, no times; a generic `meal` item at 12:30 and 19:00 (no eatery); pinned items from the current draft stay where they are.
6. Distances: straight line x 1.4 at 60 km/h everywhere, `leg_basis: estimate`.
7. Validate and repair: run the day checks; while a day fails a hard ceiling (`W_CEILING`), drop its lowest-scored optional item, then move an item to a free day in the same base; never exceed the walking ceiling.
8. Output: the trip.json draft (`generated_by: "planner@1"`), plus `explanations` (per item: score parts and the filters it passed) returned beside the document, not inside it.
When the filtered candidates cannot fill the days, leave free days with the note of 0A.5 ("Only 3 days of content for <area> with your filters; Days 4-5 left free. Thin data: ...") and list the area's top backlog items. Never add a row that is not in the catalog.

Tests (`core/test/planner/`): byte-identical drafts for the same inputs and catalog snapshot; every draft passes the day checks or carries an explained accepted warning; property tests (a seeded generator of 200 random inputs over the fixture catalog): no draft exceeds the walking ceiling or `max_house_grade` on Track B or C, no draft contains an id that is not in the catalog, pinned items never move; the Banff case: 7 days, September, family with a 6-year-old, `active-nature`, pace 3 uses only rows with `kid_fit` `yes` or `unknown` (the unknown ones flagged) and stays within 10 km and 500 m a day; a thin area produces free days and backlog items.

## Acceptance criteria

1. Same inputs give byte-identical drafts.
2. Property tests (200 seeded inputs) never exceed ceilings, never invent ids, never move pinned items.
3. The Banff family case meets its constraints; a thin area yields free days and backlog items.
4. Every item has an explanation; every free day has a thin-data note.
5. UI tests and axe pass; `bash tools/check-all.sh` ends with `CHECK-ALL OK`.

## Pitfalls

1. v1 only: no quality score, no crowding penalty, no clustering, no opening-hours fitting, no eateries (0A.5 revised 4). Those are the v2 spec.
2. Determinism: no `Math.random`, no `Date.now()` inside the planner; seeded generators in tests; sort before tie-breaks.
3. Unknown is not "suits": unknown limits are kept and flagged, never presented as fitting.
4. Keep `firstDraft`'s signature; the board depends on it.
5. Never `cd`; never `git add -A`.
