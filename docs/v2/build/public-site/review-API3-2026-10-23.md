# Review of API-3 (independent Opus read, 23 Oct 2026)

Scope: `git diff c6dbae2...HEAD` on `main` (API-3a by Opus, API-3b by Sonnet, merged as 68ed4bb). Read against PHASE-E-START
sections 6, 7 and 15, `store.ts` A25 to A39, the town guard, the new schemas, the server and client changes, the contract cases
K20 to K27, nearby spec 2.4 and 5.6, and the findings this lane was to close (GB-A-8, 10, 12, 13; R-22, R-26).

Method: reading only. No build, server, check-all or e2e was run. One throwaway script called `privacyHits()` on six hand-made
bodies and printed hit counts only (no coordinates, no names); it is cited in R-1 and R-2. Reviewer model: Opus 5.5
(`claude-opus-5-5`).

## Verdict

**Accept with changes.** The contract work is careful and mostly SQL-shaped, the revision change is a real improvement, and GB-A-10,
GB-A-12, GB-A-13, R-22 and R-26 are closed as asked. GB-A-8 is not closed. Two detector problems, both small code changes in
`core/src/privacy/detect.ts`, leave the home town findable by a request. The town guard also leaks more than A33 tells the owner.
All three musts land before META-2 publishes real towns and before LST2b ships `nearTown`. After that the damage is permanent,
because a published layer can be archived.

## Findings

### API3-R-1 (must). The detector turns request echoes into a home-town oracle; GB-A-8 is not closed

`respond()` runs `privacyHits(data)` on every body (`web/lib/server/respond.ts:138`) and answers 500 on any hit. Three outputs echo
the visitor's own words:

- `search` returns `query` (`core/src/schema/api/search.ts:39`);
- `parse` returns `q` and `unused` (`search.ts:68-69`);
- `searchCards` returns `terms` (`core/src/schema/api/place.ts:228`, new in API-3).

None of these keys is in the typed exemption, so the home-town text rule runs on them. Checked with the detector itself: a body
holding the home-town word as `query`, as `terms` or as the parse's `q` gives 1, 1 and 2 hits. So
`GET /api/v1/search?q=<word>` and `GET /api/v1/places/cards?q=<word>` answer 500 for exactly one word. An attacker takes the town
names from the towns layer (or any gazetteer), asks for each one, and the one 500 names the home town. No geometry is needed. D-E3
says "no request is an oracle", and A33 repeats it; that is not true yet.

Ordinary searches fail too: `privacyHits({query: "hotel ideas"})` gives 1 hit, so a visitor searching "hotel ideas", "base camp: x"
or "100 Queen Street" gets a 500.

Fix (API-3 follow-up, about 1 h):
1. `privacyHits(body, limit, { echo })`, where `respond()` passes the request's own text (`q`, `parsed.q`) and the detector lifts the
   TEXT rules (home town, planning note, street address) for strings under `query`, `q`, `unused`, `notYet` and `terms` whose folded
   form is contained in the folded request text. Coordinates and private keys are still checked there. An echo cannot carry data
   the request did not bring.
2. Or drop `terms` from `cardHits` (the client knows its own words) and do (1) for search and parse only.
3. New case K29 (handlers): for the home word taken from `HOME_TOWN_RE` (as `townGuard.cases.ts:11` does), "hotel ideas" and
   "base camp: x", `search`, `parse` and `searchCards` answer 200. Plus a negative case: a planted place summary holding the word
   still fails closed.

### API3-R-2 (must). `nearTown` widens the exemption to every place name in the home town

`TOWN_ID_KEYS` includes `nearTown` and `townId` (`detect.ts:34`), and `townIdsOf()` (`detect.ts:62`) uses those keys to decide that
an object's `name` and `label` are town-label slots. A `PlaceRow` holds both `name` and `nearTown`. Once META-2 fills `nearTown`, any
place whose nearest town is the home town and whose name contains the town name (up to 80 characters, no digit) passes the
home-town rule. Checked: a row `{slug, name: "<Home> Harbour", nearTown: "ca-on-<home>"}` gives 0 hits; the same row without
`nearTown` gives 1. D-E3 says the name may appear in a towns row and a chip, "nothing else may". K26.exemption has no negative case
for this shape.

There is a second, smaller widening: `TOWN_ID_RE` (`core/src/schema/capture/points.ts:54`) accepts any slug of three or more parts
whose first part has two letters (`st-lawrence-river`, `le-...`). Under `id`, `key` or `value`, such a string skips the home-town rule
and makes its sibling `name` exempt when the name holds its tail.

Fix:
- Label owners are only `id`, `key`, `value` and a chip's `set.city`. `nearTown` and `townId` stay id slots for their own string
  only.
- When a towns layer is loaded, pass its id set in `DetectOptions.townIds` and exempt only members. The shape rule is the fallback
  before META-2.
- Add to K26.exemption: the `nearTown` row above must give 1 hit, and so must `{id: "st-<home>-x", name: "<Home> X"}` when `townIds`
  is given.

### API3-R-3 (must). The town guard's trigger is itself the oracle, and A33 understates what it leaks

What an attacker with GeoNames, Wikidata and OSM learns. The rules are home-dependent: the guard acts only when home is near.

- **(a) A changed centre.** A served centre that differs from the public GeoNames centre C means "home is within 2 km of C": a
  circle of at most about 13 km², inside a municipality many times that size. If the served point equals the Wikidata point W, it
  also means "home is more than 2 km from W". If the served point is the 4.5 km offset, it means "home is within 2 km of W" (when W
  exists). The fixed offset (`towns.ts:76-79`) does fix D-E3's original flaw: the point does not depend on where home is, and the
  triangle inequality makes north always succeed. But the act of moving is the signal. The exact 4.5 km due-north displacement is
  also a fingerprint of the rule, and it gives the radius as 4.5 / 2.25.
- **(b) A changed outline.** Rule 3 (`towns.ts:93-110`) nulls every town outline, the home town's and its neighbours', that has a
  vertex within 2 km of home. OSM municipal boundaries are dense, so a nulled outline means "home is within about 2 km of this
  boundary", and a served one means "home is more than 2 km from every vertex of this boundary". Intersect those bands over the
  home town and its neighbours, add the circle from (a), and the area shrinks well below town level. The vertex rule is enough for
  the detector: no served vertex is within the radius. It is not enough for privacy, because which outlines are missing is
  computed from home.
- **(c) No approx flag.** Dropping the flag hides nothing from this attacker; the diff is the flag. It only hides the move from
  ordinary readers. That also means "near <home town>" distances are measured from a point up to 4.5 km off, with nothing on the
  page saying so.
- **`TOWN_POLICY = "exclude"`** (`towns.ts:32`) triggers on the same condition, so a missing town gives the same 2 km fact. The
  missing name is also an obvious diff against GeoNames. It is not a privacy option, only a product one. The spec (5.6 item 6) and
  A33 present it as the safer switch, which is wrong.
- **Names.** `towns.ts` names no town. `detect.ts` takes the word from `home.ts` (allowed). The tests derive it from the pattern.
  Acceptance check 5 holds.

A33 and nearby spec 5.6 item 6 tell the owner the risk is "the town-level fact Q50 accepted". Q50 accepted that "near <home town>"
works as a search, not that the site may reveal which town is home, and the leak is below town level.

Fix (owner decision, Q87 reopened, before META-2 publishes towns). Recommended: **serve public geometry exactly and exempt it by
identity.** A towns row's `lat`/`lon` is served exactly as the gazetteer file holds it. The detector's coordinate rule is lifted for
an object whose town id is in the loaded layer AND whose point equals that town's gazetteer point exactly (and for a `globeCity`
outline whose hash equals the layer's stored outline hash). Nothing is moved or dropped, so a diff against public data shows
nothing, distances stay true, and the flag question goes away. A planted point near home under a town id but not equal to the
gazetteer point still fails closed, so K7 keeps its teeth. Cost: the detector takes `DetectOptions.publicPoints` (a map built at
load), and K26 changes to "served equals gazetteer, no 500". If the owner keeps A33 as it is, correct A33 and spec 5.6 item 6 to the
statement in "Town guard residual risk" below, and drop the Wikidata branch (R-16).

### API3-R-4 (should). The guard is wired only into StubStore; nothing makes META-2 use it

`safeTown` and `safeOutline` are called only in `core/src/catalogue/stub/index.ts:89-94` and `json/legs.ts:24`. JsonStore has no
towns yet, and the web target cannot plant towns (`web/tests/api/targets.ts:94`), so K26 on real data only checks `not_available`.
META-2 owns `tools/towns.py` (Python, start sheet E9), which can write raw centres into `data/reference/towns/**` and from there into
any static file the StaticAdapter reads. The web privacy test would then block the build, or miss the file if it does not scan it.

Fix: export one `guardTownLayer(towns, cities)` from `towns.ts` (or `publicTownLayer()` under R-3's recommendation). StubStore and
`json/towns.ts` both call it. Any static towns file is written from its output, never by the Python step. Make "K26 runs on the json
target with planted towns" a META-2 acceptance check, and extend the web privacy test to `data/reference/towns/**` and any towns
file under `web/public`.

### API3-R-5 (should). A refused leg is a diff oracle for routes that pass near home

`geometryOf()` (`json/legs.ts:22-29`) serves `geometry: null` with km and minutes kept when any vertex is within the radius. In a
`trip-legs/2` file where every other leg has geometry, a null leg says "this ORS route passes within 2 km of home". Anyone can
re-route the same public stops. For a highway that runs through the home town this localises home along that road. Today's legs do
not leave from home (`runsOf()` starts day 1 with no origin, `legs.ts:38`), so the risk is small, but TRP2b fetches every pair. Fix:
TRP2b's fetcher and the loader count refused legs and print the count (never the leg). If it is ever above 0 the owner decides,
with the same identity argument as R-3 applied to route geometry. Add the count to the TRP2b hand-back.

### API3-R-6 (should). Polylines inside an export body are not decoded

The detector decodes a polyline only as a string under the key `geometry` (`detect.ts`, `polylineHits`). The `json` export is one
string body (`web/lib/server/exports.ts`, `json: () => JSON.stringify(...)`), scanned by `NUM_PAIR` text matching only. Lines are
coordinate arrays today, so they are caught. If TRP2b's "Export trip" puts leg polylines into the export model, they pass
`respond()` unread. Fix: when a body string parses as JSON (the `json` format), `privacyHits` walks the parsed value. Or the
`exportModel` schema refuses encoded strings and requires coordinate arrays. One line either way, and it has to land before TRP2b.

### API3-R-7 (should). Two `q` rules; the contract states one and the site uses the other

A16 (`store.ts:66-67`) says `q` is "a substring of sortKey of the row's text fields ... in lists and in search alike". The site's list
uses a different rule: every word, over a wider haystack. 3b keeps free-text states in the browser (`web/lib/client/api.ts:224-226`;
`uncheckedGroups` skips any state with text, `api.ts:238`), which is why server q and list q disagree (20 against 22). So:

- **The contract's rule is A16**, and SupabaseStore would implement substring matching. Nothing in the kit pins the list's rule.
- **The fallback is a hidden second source of truth.** For text states the browser decides membership, counts and "not checked yet",
  over rows the ApiAdapter got by paging every row of a country (`countryRows`, `api.ts:162`). The server's text rule is never used
  by the site, so P9 is met for facets only. When lists move to the server, results change silently.
- The synchronous `countList` path also evaluates locally (`peekPlacesChecked(..., local = true)` in `data.ts`) while the section
  beside it shows server groups, so the count line and the rows can come from two engines.

Fix, before LST2b:
1. Amend A16 now to say which rule is the contract. Recommended: the list's word rule, because that is what visitors see.
2. Move that matcher into `core/src/query` and have JsonStore and StubStore call it.
3. Add the 20/22 query to HYG's K28 corpus.
4. Remove the text exceptions at `api.ts:224` and `api.ts:238`.
5. Make `countList` read the server cache when one exists.

### API3-R-8 (should). Card search parity is unverified, and the rule is held in three copies

The term rule (normalise, split, stop list, stem) lives in `web/lib/search/fulltext.ts:20-40` and again in
`core/src/catalogue/stub/index.ts:63-73`; the scorer lives in `fulltext.ts:76-110` and again in `stub/index.ts:272-292`. JsonSource
uses the web copy (`web/lib/server/source.ts`), which SupabaseStore's loader in core cannot import. A28 leaves out:

- document length (terms after stop words and stemming, repeats counted);
- tags as labels or as ids (the stub uses labels; `cardText` takes `tags: string[]`);
- places with no card are left out of N;
- query terms are distinct before the minimum-match rule.

K24 (`searchCards.cases.ts`) checks the cap, the allowlist, determinism, filters and prefix behaviour, but not the ranking: a store
that returns name matches in alphabetical order passes.

Fix:
1. Create `core/src/search/bm25.ts` holding `terms()`, `buildIndex()` and `score()`. `fulltext.ts` re-exports it, the stub imports it.
2. Write the four definitions above into A28.
3. Add K24.order: a fixed stub query with the expected ref order and scores rounded to 6 decimals.
4. For SupabaseStore, SQL fetches the postings of the query terms in scope and core scores them, so parity is exact by construction
   (no float-sum ordering risk).

### API3-R-9 (should). Body and ETag can disagree: legs and lines are read per request

`revision()` is computed once per process (`json/index.ts`, `revPromise`). `legsFile(slug)` reads the file on every call
(`web/lib/public/timeline.ts:88`, through `source.ts:166`), and so does `readGlobeLine` (`web/lib/public/globe.ts:103-106`). Edit a
legs file under a running server and the body changes while the ETag stays the same, so a client holding the old ETag gets 304 for
stale data. That breaks A8b ("the revision of a response comes from the same snapshot as its data"). TRP2b will rewrite legs files
during development. Fix: read the legs files and line files once at source creation (or memoise them beside the revision), so the
data and the revision come from one snapshot.

### API3-R-10 (should). Build id falls back to `dev` silently; `TRAVEL_BUILD_ID` is documented but gone

`buildId()` reads `.next/BUILD_ID` under `process.cwd()` and returns `dev` otherwise (`web/lib/server/inputs.ts:33-36`).
`revision.ts:84` still names `TRAVEL_BUILD_ID`, which is no longer read. In a deployment whose working directory is not `web/`, or
whose output trace leaves out `BUILD_ID`, every build shares the id `dev`. A code-only deploy then keeps every ETag while the bodies
change, and clients revalidating with `If-None-Match` keep old bodies. Fix: bring back `TRAVEL_BUILD_ID` as an override, set at
build time through `next.config` (`generateBuildId` and `env`). When `NODE_ENV=production` and the id is `dev`, log an error and
fail the revision (it is retried, GB-A-5) instead of serving a constant. Correct the comment.

### API3-R-11 (should). A30's SupabaseStore claim cannot hold

A30 (3) (`store.ts:160-161`) says the SupabaseStore loader computes the same `inputRevision` and that "JsonStore and SupabaseStore
of one build agree". The loader is not the Next build and has no Next build id. Worse, if `catalogue_meta` holds the whole revision,
a code-only deploy does not move a Supabase ETag, because the loader did not rerun. Fix: store a data revision in `catalogue_meta`
(`inputRevision({ buildId: "data", files })`). `SupabaseStore.revision()` returns SHA-256 of the app build id plus that data
revision. Define "agree" on the data revision only, and add that case to SRV-0's target.

### API3-R-12 (should). The unchecked read pulls up to 1,000 full rows to get slugs, and cuts silently

`uncheckedGroups` (`api.ts:237-249`) asks for up to `UNCHECKED_PAGES = 10` pages of 100 full `PlaceRow`s (`api.ts:145`). Nine of
those requests run in parallel on every state change, and only the slugs are used. When `total` is above 1,000 the group counts are
right but the section shows fewer rows, with nothing saying so. Fix: a contract option that returns slugs only per group, capped at
1,000 with `truncated`, in one request. Or the client reads page 1 and pages on demand. At minimum, surface the cut.

### API3-R-13 (should). `tripLegs` has no body budget, and a bad file drops a trip's legs silently

400 legs times 40,000 polyline characters (`limits.ts:32`) is a 16 MB worst-case body. Add `tripLegs.totalChars` (about 400,000)
answered with `too_large`. Also, `legsFile` returns null when the file fails `tripLegsFile` (`source.ts:169`): one leg over 40,000
characters, or any schema slip, and the trip silently has no legs. Validate legs files in `check-all` (or at build) and log at load.

### API3-R-14 (should). Kit gaps found in the spot check

Six cases checked by reasoning. K20, K21, K22 and K25 genuinely exercise the behaviour. K22 is discriminating: case 3 (`q` cleared by
an empty parse) fails on a store that ignores `parsed`. The gaps:

- **K23** checks that every listed row has the gap and is not in the list, but not "fails ONLY on a gap". Add a row that is a gap
  for the selected key and also fails another selected key; it must not be listed.
- **K24**: see R-8.
- **K26** runs for real only on the stub; on JsonStore it checks `not_available` (R-4).
- **K27** on the stub checks geometry the stub made itself (`stub/index.ts:513`). On real data `geometry: false`, so the refusal
  path never runs through a store. Plant a `trip-legs/2` leg with a vertex near home (from `probe.ts`): `geometry` must be null,
  km kept, K7 clean.
- **K10 on the json target** changes a hash inside `revisionInputs()` (`targets.ts`, `makeChanged`), which only proves that
  `inputRevision` hashes its input. Nothing proves that every file the builder reads is in the input set. Add a coverage test: spy
  on `fs.readFileSync` while `createJsonSource()` runs and assert every repo path it reads is in `revisionInputs()`.

Missing for phase E. On the json target: K3, K4, K5 and K17 (`near`, `nearbyForPlace`), K15 (`city=`), K26 with planted towns, and
`towns.clamped` with a real layer. Also R-1's K29, R-2's negative exemption cases and K28 (HYG). The `awaits` mechanism itself stays
in `kit.ts` and `run.ts` with a bookkeeping test that none is left. That is fine, and it is the right tool for META-2 and LST2c to
use.

### API3-R-15 (later). Revision inputs: privacy and hygiene

The `data` tree is walked in full (`inputs.ts:16`), so the private wishlist moves the public ETag (A30 caveat 1, accepted). So do
untracked or ignored files and editor temp files; only `.DS_Store` is skipped. That is harmless within one deployment, but two
checkouts of one commit can differ. Skip every dotfile and `*~`, and build from a clean checkout. Determinism is otherwise sound:
paths are sorted, content hashed, no mtimes, duplicates refused (`revision.ts:98-107`). The 62 ms cold measurement at 10,000 places
meets the 300 ms target.

### API3-R-16 (later). If A33 stays, drop the Wikidata branch

`towns.ts:75` uses the Wikidata point when it is outside the radius. That adds one more home-dependent bit ("home is more than 2 km
from W") over the fixed offset alone. Keep it only if the gazetteer routinely serves Wikidata points, so that the choice looks
normal. Moot under R-3's recommendation.

### API3-R-17 (later). Reserved path words should be derived, not listed

`RESERVED_API_WORDS` (`core/src/catalogue/slugs.ts`) holds `counts`, `unchecked` and `cards`, and `listPlaces.reserved` checks
today's slugs. No other collision exists today: `/trips/{slug}/legs` is one level deeper, and `near`, `towns`, `export` and `globe`
are top-level. A lane that adds `web/app/api/v1/places/<word>/` must remember to add the word. Add a test that reads the static
directory names under `web/app/api/v1/places/` and requires them to equal the list.

### API3-R-18 (later). Caps: what a client can still walk

`cards` (50, no paging), `globePins` (2,000 per country, `too_large` above) and `tripLegs` (400) bound one response, not a client.
Walking the catalogue in bulk still works through `listPlaces` paging and per-country pins (A23, accepted), and the ApiAdapter
itself does it for every country it lists (`countryRows`). `searchCards` and `listUnchecked` add no reach beyond `listPlaces`. The
remaining control is GB-A-9's rate limit, which must cover `/api/v1/*`.

## Town guard residual risk

For the owner, in plain words:

> Today the site never shows a point within 2 km of home. To keep it that way, if a town's official centre falls inside that 2 km
> circle the site shows the centre 4.5 km further north, and any town outline or route line that comes within 2 km of home is
> drawn as a plain circle or a dashed line instead. Nothing on the page marks this. But anyone who downloads the free GeoNames and
> OpenStreetMap files and compares them with our towns can see which town was moved and which outlines are missing. From that they
> can work out that home is within 2 km of that town's centre, and near some town borders but not others. That narrows home to an
> area of a few square kilometres next to a named town: not a street, but much less than "somewhere in the town", and it also says
> which town is home. Switching to "leave the town out" gives the same signal. There is a second, simpler problem: the search box
> answers with an error for exactly one word, the home town's name, so the town can be found without any maps. My recommendation:
> fix the search error now, and show every town exactly as the public maps do, with nothing moved and nothing dropped. The privacy
> check would then accept those exact public points. The site would then reveal nothing that the public maps do not already show.
