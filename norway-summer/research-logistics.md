# Norway — route, transport and logistics

Researched 2026-08-31.

---

## 1. The geography problem, stated plainly

Norway is 1,750 km long. The two things people mean by "Norway" are **1,300–1,500 km
apart by road, about 24 hours of driving**, Bergen to Lofoten. There is no version of a
14-night trip where you drive between them and still have a trip.

So there are exactly three honest shapes:

| Shape | What it is | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Fjords + Arctic, joined by one flight** | ~8 nights Fjord Norway, ~6 nights Lofoten/Senja, one internal flight, two car rentals | ~1.5 days lost to transit; two rental contracts; ~CAD 200–300 pp for the flight |
| **B. Fjords only, deeper** | 14 nights Stavanger → Hardanger → Sogn → Nordfjord → Sunnmøre → Romsdal | No midnight sun, no Arctic; but no transit days and far better hiking density |
| **C. Arctic only** | 14 nights Lofoten + Vesterålen + Senja + Tromsø | Extraordinary, but you skip the fjords Norway is famous for |

**Recommendation: A.** Two weeks is exactly the length at which A stops being greedy and
starts being the right answer — B leaves you with spare days in the fjords by night 11,
and C means flying to Norway and not seeing a fjord.

## 2. Getting there from Toronto

**There is no nonstop YYZ → Norway.** Every routing is one-stop.

| Routing | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Icelandair via KEF** | Only carrier serving Bergen (BGO) on a single ticket from Toronto. Published from-fare CAD 1,268 YYZ–BGO. Free Iceland stopover if wanted. |
| **SAS via CPH or OSL** | Best domestic-connection integration once in Norway. |
| **KLM via AMS** | 47 weekly connections YYZ–OSL; good BGO/AES/TRD coverage. |
| **Air Canada / LH / BA / AF / Finnair / Aer Lingus** | All connect via their hub to OSL. |

**Icelandair's Tromsø route is winter-only** — twice weekly, 23 Oct 2026 to late March
2027. It does not help a summer trip. Coming home from the north in summer means
TOS or EVE → OSL → onward.

**Fly open-jaw.** In → Bergen, out → Evenes or Tromsø. Backtracking to Bergen to fly home
costs a full day and a domestic fare for nothing.

## 3. Getting between the two halves

**Do not use the Bodø–Moskenes ferry as your entry to Lofoten.** Up to 9 sailings a day
in summer, NOK 605 for a car under 6 m (NOK 424 for an EV), but **only part of the
capacity is reservable** and the rest is a first-come queue that genuinely packs out in
July. It is the single most common way a Lofoten trip loses half a day.

**Use Evenes (EVE) instead.** Harstad/Narvik airport is road-connected to Lofoten via
Vesterålen and the E10 — no ferry, no queue, and it puts you on the right side for a
Senja or Vesterålen loop. Widerøe and Norwegian serve it from Oslo, Bergen and Trondheim.

Indicative one-way domestic fares: Bergen–Evenes from ~USD 107, Bergen–Tromsø from
~USD 120, Widerøe average ~USD 170. Cheap relative to everything else in Norway.

**The Havila / Hurtigruten coastal ship is a real option for one leg**, not the whole
route. Bergen → Ålesund runs six times a week, departs mid-afternoon, arrives ~09:45
next morning, 13 h 15 m, roughly USD 140–220. It converts a driving day into a night's
sleep plus a sunrise coastline. Bergen → Kirkenes end to end is 3 days and is a different
holiday.

## 4. Driving — the rules that actually bite

- **Speed cameras have ~3 km/h tolerance and fines are brutal.** NOK 3,350 for 6–10 km/h
  over. Over 110 in a 110 zone by 20 km/h is NOK 10,100 (~EUR 830). **Section control
  (average-speed) is standard in tunnels** and is added to new stretches every year.
  This is not a place to drive European-motorway style.
- Limits: 50 urban (30 residential), 80 rural, 90–100 motorway.
- **Fuel: petrol NOK 22.11/L, diesel NOK 21.41/L** (Aug 2026). Call it CAD 3/litre.
- **Tolls: AutoPASS, automatic.** Rental fleets are registered; tolls are billed back to
  you **plus a handling fee of NOK 30–80 per day** on days you incur any. Budget for it.
- **Ferries** are billed through AutoPASS too. The tag alone gets ~10% off; the **full
  50% private discount needs a separate prepaid AutoPASS-for-ferry agreement**, which a
  short-term visitor in a rental car generally cannot get. Assume full fare.
- **EVs are the default rental now** and Norway has the densest fast-charging network in
  the world (10,000+ fast points, 25,000+ total), at NOK 4–8/kWh. An EV also pays
  materially less on ferries — NOK 424 vs 605 on Bodø–Moskenes, for example.
  Counter-argument: range anxiety on the Sognefjellet and Senja stretches is real if you
  don't plan charges. `GAP:` charger density on the specific mountain routes.
- **One-way drop-off fees are severe.** Historic quotes of >USD 1,000 for Bergen→Ålesund;
  a Bergen→Oslo one-way ran ~+20% over a same-place return. Two separate round-trip-ish
  rentals (one per half of the trip) will usually beat one long one-way.

### The Fiskebøl–Melbu caveat — read before routing Reine → Andenes

The ferry is free and takes 25 minutes, and one source claims it saves "at least two hours"
against a 200 km detour via Sortland and Lødingen. **That claim does not transfer cleanly to
this trip.** The all-road route from Lofoten to Andenes already runs E10 → Gullesfjordbotn →
Rv85 Sortland → Rv82, and does *not* go via Lødingen; the ferry simply cuts the corner around
Hadselfjorden and rejoins the same road near Sortland. The real saving for this pair is
plausibly 45–60 minutes, not two.

**And it sails only every two hours.** Arrive five minutes after a departure and the wait
costs more than the shortcut saves. **Time the crossing before choosing it**, or take the road.

## 6. Trollstigen — treat it as a bonus, never as a plan

This is the biggest correction the research produced.

| Season | What happened |
|---|---|
| 2024 | Landslide; **closed for the remainder of the season** |
| 2025 | Reopened only on **11 July** after stabilisation work |
| 2026 | Opened unusually early, **27–29 April** |
| **2026, 27 Aug 22:43** | **New landslide** on Fv63 in Isterdalen — ten metres long, half a metre high. Nasjonale turistveger currently states the section is **"closed for the rest of the year… closed to all traffic, this applies to all motorized vehicles, cyclists and pedestrians"** |
| Ahead | A **three-season rockfall-securing programme** is approved: road **open 1 June – 15 August, closed from 15 August through winter**, each year of the works |

So for any future summer: Trollstigen is plausibly open in the first half of the window
and near-certainly shut from 15 August, with a standing chance of a weather or rockfall
closure at any moment. Note also that **the road is closed preemptively in heavy rain**
by design.

**Consequence for the plan:** Geiranger must never be reachable only via Trollstigen.
It isn't — **Ørnevegen is open year-round** — but the Åndalsnes↔Geiranger leg must have
a stated fallback, and the plan should place Trollstigen as an optional drive on a day
that works without it.

`GAP:` the exact Åndalsnes→Geiranger detour when Trollstigen is shut. The likely line is
E136 west to Sjøholt, Rv650 to Liabygda, Liabygda–Stranda ferry, then Stranda–Hellesylt
and the Hellesylt–Geiranger ferry — but the ferry times and total duration are unverified.
Confirm on vegvesen.no / Entur before committing a day to it.

## 7. Standing checks before departure

- **175 / vegvesen.no** — live road status. Call +47 22 175 175 for road conditions.
- **Entur** — best combined timetable engine; sells other operators' trains without Vy's
  surcharge.
- **Havspor / fergeruter.info** — ferry timetables by route and year.
- Rail worth knowing even on a driving trip: **Rauma Railway** Dombås–Åndalsnes,
  114 km, ~75 min, past Trollveggen; **Bergen Railway** Oslo–Bergen; **Flåm Railway**
  20 km, Flåm–Myrdal; **Nordland Railway** Trondheim–Bodø.
