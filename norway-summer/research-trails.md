official source disagree, both figures are shown.

**Constraint added 2026-08-31: the group's ceiling is 12 km round trip.** Every hike below is
now judged against that, and two things in the plan failed it — see the table.

**Two consequences for the plan.** Ryten becomes the viewpoint only — going down to the beach
and back is what pushes it to 12–13 km. And **Vidden is out entirely**; the Bergen substitute
is the Ulriken cable car up and back for the same view at zero distance.

**One upgrade the numbers unlocked.** Hesten and Segla are both about 5 km round trip, and
their trailheads sit **600 m apart in Fjordgård** — so both can be done in one day inside the
ceiling. The catch: **they cannot be linked**; each is an out-and-back from its own start.

## The four "trophy" hikes, and why you cannot do all of them

Preikestolen, Kjeragbolten and Trolltunga are the marketed trio — but **all three are in
the far south**, around Stavanger and Odda, 3–6 hours' drive below Bergen. Slotting them
into a Bergen→north itinerary means adding a southern loop of 2–3 days before you have
seen a single fjord.

**The honest trade:** Trolltunga alone is a 20–27 km, 7–12 hour day plus positioning
nights either side. It costs roughly two days. Romsdalseggen and Besseggen each deliver a
comparable ridge day for a fraction of the logistics, and sit on the natural line of the
trip. Take Trolltunga only if the photograph is specifically the goal.

### Trolltunga — Odda / Skjeggedal

| From | Distance | Gain | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| **P2 Skjeggedal** | 27 km return | 800 m | 8–12 h |
| **P3 Mågelitopp** | 20 km return | 320 m | 7–10 h |

- **Season 1 June – 30 September.** Guided only Oct–May.
- **P3 opens 8 June – 30 September only.** P1 Tyssedal and P2 open year-round.
- Shuttles: **Odda→P2, 8 Jun–27 Sep. P2→P3, 8 Jun–30 Sep.** Buses pause midday;
  **last bus up from P2 is 11:00**, **last bus down from P3 is 19:00**, and the **road
  closes at 21:00**. Miss the 11:00 and the day is materially harder.
- `GAP:` 2026/2027 parking fees at P2 and P3, and shuttle fare. Not published on the page
  fetched.
- Odda also has **Via Ferrata Trolltunga and Via Ferrata Tyssedal**, and the
  **Dronningstien** ridge walk — Odda is a genuine adventure base, not just a trailhead.

### Preikestolen — Lysefjord

- **~8 km return, ~500 m gain, 3.5–5 hours.** The most accessible of the three.
- **Parking NOK 250–275 per day**, paid on exit; funds trail upkeep.
- Trailhead is Preikestolen BaseCamp (formerly Preikestolen Fjellstue).
- **The Tau ferry is no longer needed** — the Ryfast tunnel project (14.4 km Ryfylke
  tunnel) connects Stavanger to the Preikestolen side by road. Old guides still send
  people to the ferry. Verify before following any pre-2020 write-up.

### Kjeragbolten — Lysefjord

- ~12 km, strenuous, from Øygardstøl. The boulder is wedged ~1,000 m above the fjord.
- `GAP:` elevation gain, parking fee, and whether the three ups-and-downs are as brutal as
  reputed. Under-researched relative to the other two — close this only if the southern
  loop makes the cut.

## The two ridge hikes that fit the natural route

### Romsdalseggen — Åndalsnes  ← *the best single day in fjord Norway*

- **~10 km, 970 m gain, 7–8 hours**, point-to-point ridge above Romsdalen with Trollveggen
  and the Romsdalsfjord on either side.
- **Solved logistics:** a dedicated **Romsdalseggen bus** runs from Norsk Tindesenter /
  Åndalsnes bus terminal, **platform 5**, to the Vengedalen start, **20 minutes**,
  **daily from 12 June to mid-October**. Tickets go on sale in advance. You walk back into
  town — no car shuttle problem.
- Åndalsnes stacks: **Rampestreken** viewpoint and Nesaksla by foot, the
  **Romsdalen gondola** (NOK **300** one way, **450** return) to the same summit area, and
  **Via Ferrata Romsdalsstigen**, described as the hardest of Norway's via ferratas.
- `GAP:` Romsdalseggen bus fare and 2028 sale-opening date.

### Besseggen — Jotunheimen

- **~14 km, ~1,100 m of ascent, high point ~1,743 m, 6–8 hours.** Norway's most famous
  ridge — a knife edge between green Gjende and blue Bessvatnet.
- Standard method: **Gjende boat from Gjendesheim to Memurubu, 20 minutes, up to 8
  departures a day**, then walk the ridge back to Gjendesheim.
- Officially "for experienced hikers": windy, steep, narrow, with scrambling — but **no
  climbing equipment needed**.
- **This is inland, in the driest part of the country.** It is the right hike to hold as
  a weather-hedge for a bad forecast on the coast.
- `GAP:` 2026/2027 Gjende boat fares and Gjendesheim parking (historically NOK 100/day —
  almost certainly higher now). gjendebaten.no did not resolve; find the current operator.

## Lofoten and Senja

### Reinebringen — Reine, Lofoten

**GAP CLOSED 2026-08-31.** **1.1 km each way — 2 km round trip on the trail itself, about 5 km
round trip if you count the walk from the nearest paid lot.** **1,978 Sherpa-built stone steps**,
~448 m of gain (the summit is 484 m), **1.5–3.5 hours**. Not technical; relentlessly steep.
- **Parking is the entire problem, not the hike.** All of Reine is now a **no-parking
  zone** outside the paid lots, because visitors kept improvising. Options:
  - **Steffenakken** — free, tiny, fills early. The only free option and it is a lottery.
  - **Reine Kulturhus** — **NOK 150 for 3–5 hours**.
  - **Reine harbour / Reine Ytre Havn** — EasyPark, **NOK 25–40/hour** for a car.
- Plan it as a **very early or very late** walk. In late July the light at 23:00 is better
  than the light at noon and the stairs are empty.

### Ryten / Kvalvika — Lofoten

**GAP CLOSED 2026-08-31.**

- **Viewpoint only: 8.7 km round trip, 680 m gain, 3–5 hours.** Some sources give 7 km and
  543 m depending on the start.
- **Adding the descent to Kvalvika beach makes it a 12–13 km loop with ~930 m of gain and
  5–7 hours.** That is at or over the group ceiling — **do the viewpoint, skip the beach.**
- Parking: main lot at the end of Fv806 (the Peat Museum lot); **Innersand is paid, NOK 100**
  (plus NOK 100 for an overnight). The **Torsfjorden trailhead lot has about sixteen marked
  spaces** — treat it as full from mid-morning.

### Segla and Hesten — Senja

**GAP CLOSED 2026-08-31** for the two main routes.

| | Distance | Gain | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hesten** — the viewpoint | **5.3 km** RT (some sources 5 km) | 510–550 m | 2–3 h |
| **Segla** — the summit | **5 km** RT | 610 m | 3–4 h, strenuous |

**The famous photograph of Segla is taken from Hesten, not from Segla.** If the picture is the
goal, Hesten is the hike. Hesten is graded hard despite the modest numbers and **is not for
anyone uneasy with exposure**.

The two trailheads are **600 m apart in Fjordgård** and the hikes **cannot be linked** — each is
its own out-and-back. Both in one day is ~10.3 km total, inside the ceiling. Parking is a large
paid lot at Fjordgård via the EasyPark app. Segla's terrain is rocky and steep near the top but
needs no technical climbing.

`GAP:` **Husfjellet** and **Barden** still have no verified numbers. Only close these if either
gets added to the plan.

## Elsewhere, worth knowing

| Hike | Where | Facts |
|---|---|---|
| ~~**Vidden**~~ | Bergen | **CUT — 13–15 km** (one source says 18), 4–6 hours and commonly 7–8. Over the 12 km ceiling. Ulriken (643 m) cable car **up and back** gives the same view for no distance. |
| **Aksla** | Ålesund | **418 stone steps** from Byparken to the viewpoint over the Art Nouveau town, the archipelago and the Sunnmøre Alps. 30 minutes, and the best cheap view in Norway. |
| **Saksa / Slogen / Skårasalen** | Hjørundfjord, Sunnmøre Alps | Demanding peaks reached from Urke; Saksa's Sørtoppen is the usual summit. Approach by fjord cruise from Ålesund is itself the experience. Prior mountain experience assumed. |
| **Galdhøpiggen** (2,469 m) | Jotunheimen, Lom | Two routes. From **Spiterstulen**, unguided. From **Juvasshytta**, **across the Styggebreen glacier — roped, crampons, guide mandatory**, booked through Juvasshytta. Northern Europe's highest point. |
| **Sherpatrappa** | Tromsø | 1,200 Sherpa-built stone steps up Storsteinen. The move is to start ~23:00 and top out at midnight under the sun. **Fjellheisen** cable car covers the same ground, 50 m → 421 m in 4 minutes, if legs are done. |

## Right to roam — what it does and does not permit

*Allemannsretten* is a legal right, not a courtesy. On **uncultivated land (utmark)** you
may walk, cycle, ski, swim and **pitch a tent** without permission, provided you are
**more than 150 m from any house or cabin** and stay **no more than 48 hours** in one spot.

Three limits that catch people:

- **Open fires are banned 15 April – 15 September** in or near forest and woodland. That
  covers the entire trip window.
- **It applies to people, not vehicles.** A campervan on uncultivated land is illegal
  under the Motor Traffic Act, whatever the right to roam says about tents.
- **Local restrictions override it** in high-pressure areas — and Lofoten now enforces
  exactly that: overnight stays are largely restricted to official campsites and
  designated motorhome stops.
